Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Under Korean law a Yuhan Hoesa does not have a statutory minimum paid-up capital; the incorporated capital is the total amount stated in the notarized Articles of Association, divided into contribution units. All cash contributions must be actually paid into a blocked contribution-protection account at a domestic bank or entrusted to a notary public before the incorporation application is filed, and the deposit must remain blocked until the company is registered. Non-cash contributions must be fully described in the Articles and supported by an independent appraisal where required. There is no separate escrow beyond this statutory blocked-deposit requirement.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable as an industrial permit. If the company leases a general office, the landlord and the company shall maintain standard fire-fighting equipment and the building must have a lawful occupancy certificate under the Building Act, but no Civil Defense fire permit is required for the non-industrial software-only operation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for corporate entities; a Yuhan Hoesa (Korean LLC) must register regardless of turnover. Individual small businesses may use the simplified taxpayer regime if annual taxable sales do not exceed KRW 30,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 24(1) of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, the supply of services to non-residents that are actually consumed in a foreign country qualifies for 0% VAT. For the web-novel subscription platform, all digital subscription fees received from overseas end-users through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are zero-rated when the platform holds conclusive evidence that the purchaser is a foreign non-obligated party, the content is delivered to a foreign IP/device, and the sales proceeds are remitted in foreign currency to the Korean entity's bank account. If the app-store settlement report identifies Korean users, the corresponding revenue is taxable at the standard 10% VAT rate and must be reported separately. The zero rate also applies to overseas digital-content royalties and licenses when the service is physically or electronically consumed outside Korea.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration / export clearance confirmation - required only where the platform ships physical branded goods or media; for pure app-delivered digital web fiction an internal electronic dispatch ledger must be retained
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - applicable only to physical ancillary exports; for digital app subscriptions the functional equivalent is the Apple App Store and Google Play settlement report evidencing digital delivery to foreign storefront accounts
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Purchase Certificate issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, confirming repatriation of foreign sales proceeds
  • Apple IAP and Google Play Billing monthly vendor financial reports disclosing sales amounts by territory and currency
  • IP geolocation, storefront region, device locale, and user-account registration logs demonstrating that the subscription service is consumed outside Korea
  • Commercial invoices and service contracts identifying the Korean Yuhan Hoesa as the seller of exported digital subscription rights

All VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices (e-Tax invoices) through HomeTax or via an NTS-certified ERP/API interface within one business day after the supply date. Each e-Tax invoice carries a mandatory digital signature from the supplier's HomeTax digital certificate, and the invoice data are transmitted to the NTS near-real-time for anti-fraud clearance and digital stamping. For business-to-consumer electronic commerce, including app-store subscription sales, the platform must integrate with the National HomeTax cash receipt API and issue electronic cash receipts when customers request them; non-issuance triggers statutory penalties. All sales ledgers and e-Tax invoice registries must be retained for the Korean statutory five-year tax record period.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service perform risk-based audits on exported digital content. If the zero-rated supply is not substantiated with complete foreign-currency payment evidence within the 180-day repatriation window, the supply is reclassified as taxable domestic supply and the NTS will assess 10% VAT on the full base plus additional tax under the Value-Added Tax Act for failure to issue or correct the tax invoice. For fraudulent or unverified zero-rating, the NTS may impose a penalty equal to 40% of the avoided VAT or of the falsely claimed export value, whichever is greater, and may also apply penalties for false e-Tax invoices. Any foreign exchange proceeds retained beyond 180 days without justifiable cause attract statutory default interest and may trigger separate penalties under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. In addition, the NTS has a clawback mechanism that reverses the zero rate retroactively, adds statutory penalty surcharges, and can extend the audit period to the full statute of limitations for tax fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade settlement, royalties, software license fees, and digital content subscription remittances, provided that underlying documentation and purpose codes are filed with the local authorized FX bank. Capital account convertibility is highly liberalized but not automatic: all capital injections, foreign currency loans, overseas remittances for M&A, and bond issuance require prior reporting or approval under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds; exporters may maintain foreign currency receipts in domestic foreign currency accounts indefinitely, and conversion to KRW is at the market rate. Profit remittance is permissible once corporate tax and dividend withholding tax (typically 20-22% on gross dividends, reduced under tax treaties) have been discharged; the bank will require audited financials, board approval of dividends, tax clearance certificates, and the foreign exchange purchase confirmation. The Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) requires banks to apply a real-demand rule for FX transactions, meaning all FX conversions and derivatives must correspond to genuine underlying cross-border business activity. In practice, digital content platforms with high micro-transaction velocity face enhanced scrutiny because small Apple/Google IAP settlements create many credits; banks must map these to the platform's revenue recognition and underlying author contracts. A Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) with non-resident ownership must register the inward capital transfer under the Foreign Direct Investment procedure with a designated foreign exchange bank, obtain a capital verification certificate, and ensure that subsequent reimbursement of development costs or intercompany licensing fees does not breach the principle of 'one sale, one FX transaction' requiring a single underlying invoice. Overall, Korea does not impose surrender requirements or punitive remittance taxes; the main operational challenge is documentary traceability and meeting bank-specific foreign exchange declaration windows (typically 30 days for receipt and disbursement reporting).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 9 and related enforcement provisions of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds (including digital content and service earnings) must be received and collected within 6 months from the export date or service completion date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; exporters may retain the foreign currency in a domestic foreign currency deposit account or convert voluntarily. If the collection deadline cannot be met, the entity must file an extension application with the Ministry of Economy and Finance through its foreign exchange bank, with extensions granted up to an additional 2 years for bona fide commercial reasons. All receipts must be reported to the bank within the standard reporting timeline, and the bank must issue a foreign exchange purchase confirmation (매매확인증) for any conversion used for capital verification or tax documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as monetary authority and central bank; under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the Ministry of Economy and Finance is the principal foreign exchange regulatory body, with delegated reporting administration to the Bank of Korea, Korea Customs Service, and licensed foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability. The Banking Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reserve cross-border FX brokerage and domestic KRW deposit-taking to licensed banks and licensed foreign exchange banks. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud are not legally permitted to open KRW current accounts or act as an FX intermediary for a Korean entity without obtaining a banking license from the Financial Services Commission (FSC). Using a fintech EMI issuer for Korean entity treasury would fail the capital verification requirement of the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, expose the Yuhan Hoesa to unlicensed money transmission risk, and be rejected by the Financial Supervisory Service audit. EMIs may only support invoicing or payments in their home currency via local partner banks, which does not satisfy the 'authorized foreign exchange bank' status required for purposes of reporting inward direct investment, dividend remittance, or trade settlement. Therefore, all cross-border treasury flows must use the domestic tier-1 bank channels.

Turnover: Under the Korean Act on External Audit of Companies, a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) is statutorily required to appoint an independent certified public accountant (CPA) if its total assets at the end of the preceding fiscal year equal or exceed KRW 10 billion (approximately USD 7.5 million). No separate turnover or employee-headcount trigger applies to a private LLC under the Korean external audit rules for companies not listed and not in the financial sector. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion in annual cross-border related-party transactions for Local File; Master File required when annual revenue exceeds KRW 50 billion and related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion

Economic substance scope: Korea applies the substance-over-form doctrine under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the CFC/GAAR provisions; treaty shopping is restricted by Limitation on Benefits clauses in Korean DTAs, and an entity without Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Korea may be treated as a conduit and subject to anti-abuse reclassification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional Omission of Foreign-Source Revenue

Statutory sanction: Article 9 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: administrative penalty of up to 40% of the evaded tax or 3 times the unpaid amount in certain cases; plus 20% negligence tax under Article 76 of the Corporate Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Civil joint liability: directors who participated or colluded are personally liable for evaded taxes and penalties; criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years, or up to 10 years for aggravated evasion exceeding KRW 5 billion.
Treaty Abuse / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Article 23 of the International Tax Adjustment Act permits denial of treaty benefits and recharacterization of the dividends as taxable Korean-sourced income; a surcharge of 30% of the under-withheld tax may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil under Article 2 of the same act: original shareholders and directors are jointly liable for unpaid Korean tax; directors may also be subject to travel bans and asset seizure orders during criminal investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering / Foreign Exchange Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Act on Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information: penalty of up to KRW 50 million per unreported remittance and up to 10 years imprisonment for willful AML breach.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally responsible for AML compliance; violation leads to civil restitution of illicit proceeds, a denial of corporate veil immunity, and criminal imprisonment terms of up to 5 years.

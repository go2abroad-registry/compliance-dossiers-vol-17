Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act; no blocked escrow account is mandated. However, the subscribed capital must be fully paid into a Korean bank account in the name of the company before registration and evidenced by a Capital Deposit Certificate dated within 14 days of filing. For the separate financial investment business license, the company must satisfy the FSC-prescribed minimum own capital requirements under the Enforcement Decree of the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act and keep that capital available and unencumbered.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense fire permits and special industrial facility permits are not applicable because pure financial trading occupies an ordinary office building. No chemical, assembly, or warehousing civil defense clearance is required.

For a foreign non-resident entity engaging in business in Korea, the statutory procedure to obtain the Business Registration Number (which serves as the unified TIN and VAT ID) is as follows: (1) appoint a resident tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act when no place of business is maintained in Korea; (2) prepare the corporate charter package comprising the issued certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, board resolution authorizing registration and appointing the tax representative, executed power of attorney, and identification documents of the representative; (3) file the prescribed Application for Business Registration with the competent district tax office or electronically through HomeTax; (4) obtain the ten-digit Business Registration Number upon approval, which constitutes the national TIN and VAT registration number; (5) register for electronic tax invoice issuance in the HomeTax e-Tax Invoice system; and (6) open a local settlement and foreign-exchange bank account to receive remittances for the purpose of substantiating zero-rated export transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, the zero rate applies to export of goods, supply of services rendered to a non-resident multinational corporation outside Korea (including qualifying exported financial services), and cross-border shipping. For an algorithmic high-frequency trading entity, zero-rating of service income is accepted only for market-making or exchange-related services supplied to foreign counterparties under a written contract demonstrating that the recipient is a non-resident and the service is performed offshore; proprietary trading for the Korean entity's own account is not zero-rated and is ordinarily an exempt financial supply with no input VAT recovery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate) generated and approved by the Korea Customs Service through its UNI-PASS electronic export clearance system
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical cross-border movement where goods are exported
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificate of Foreign Exchange Acquisition) issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act proving repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order, and export contract identifying the non-resident buyer and the agreed foreign currency consideration
  • Zero-rated e-Tax Invoice and contemporaneous record of service performance (trade blotters, execution confirmations, and access logs) demonstrating that the recipient used the trading service outside Korea

Mandatory electronic tax invoicing through the National Tax Service HomeTax e-Tax Invoice platform. Each VAT-registered supplier must transmit XML-based electronic tax invoices, digitally signed with an accredited certificate, via the NTS API; invoices must be transmitted no later than one day after issuance. There is no physical fiscal printer stamping in Korea, but NTS real-time data matching, third-party exchange of transaction data, and anti-fraud digital audit algorithms enforce compliance; late, missing, or falsely issued e-tax invoices trigger surcharges and penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act.

If the NTS audit determines that the zero-rating is unsupported, fraudulent, or fails the foreign exchange repatriation requirement, it retrospectively treats the supply as taxable, assesses output VAT at the standard rate, denies claimed input VAT credits, and adds statutory surcharges for deficient or fraudulent tax returns. Where forged export documents are discovered, criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may result in imprisonment and fines; in egregious cases, the tax assessment is subject to a fraudulent return additional penalty and the name of the entity is added to a publicly disclosed chronic tax evader list.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized current-account regime but applies a declaration-based capital-account control system. The Korean won is fully convertible on the current account for trade in goods, services, and ordinary remittances; repatriation of dividends and liquidation proceeds is freely permitted once tax clearance and audited financial statements are submitted to the bank. Capital transactions, including inbound foreign investment and outbound loans or securities, require prior reporting to the designated foreign exchange bank or, above certain thresholds, to the Bank of Korea through the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIS). There is no generalized surrender requirement for export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic foreign-currency accounts, but they must repatriate the proceeds to Korea within six months from receipt. For a proprietary high-frequency trading entity, the main constraint is not convertibility but the need to book every cross-border flow under proper foreign-currency purchase/sale purpose codes and to maintain documentary trails to satisfy the 'real demand' test for hedging instruments. Profit remittance by a non-resident shareholder of a Yuhan Hoesa requires a foreign-investment registration certificate, evidence of paid-in capital under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and a tax withholding certificate for dividend income; otherwise the bank may freeze the remittance pending tax-clearance verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Articles 6-7 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, all export proceeds must be repatriated to Korea and must be either deposited into a domestic foreign-currency account or sold for KRW within six (6) months from the date of the export declaration or receipt, whichever is later. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the exporter may keep 100% in a foreign-currency account. Reporting must be filed electronically via FEIS through the handling bank using the appropriate invisible-trade or trade-settlement purpose code. Failure to meet the deadline triggers penalty charges and exclusion from FX priority status; for the algorithmic trading business, any service income denominated in foreign currency is treated as invisible trade proceeds and subject to the same six-month repatriation timeline and documentary reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) jointly with the Financial Services Commission (FSC) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF); FX administration is operated through the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIS) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident founders must first file a Foreign Investment Report (FIRS) under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act with a designated foreign exchange bank or through Invest Korea online. The remittance is wired via SWIFT MT103 to the bank's foreign-currency intermediate account using purpose code A11010 (acquisition of equity), after which the bank converts the funds to KRW on the trade date at the standard exchange rate and credits the foreign-investment capital escrow account. Within 30 days of receipt, the investor must submit the paid-in capital proof (bank certificate of receipt and foreign currency deposit) to obtain the Foreign-investment registration certificate. For a fully non-resident-owned Yuhan Hoesa, the statutory minimum capital is zero, but in practice the bank will require a commercially reasonable minimum, such as KRW 20 million equivalent, to support a robust HFT operation and to cover margin requirements. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Revolut Business, Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex are not authorized under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act to provide resident KRW accounts or domestic clearing services for a Korean-resident legal entity. A Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic entity that must operate through a licensed Korean bank for FX settlement and SWIFT-based clearing. Licensed Korean virtual banks (e.g., KakaoBank, K-Bank) are consumer-oriented and do not provide institutional-grade multi-currency treasury services, sub-account structures, or low-latency payment rail APIs required for HFT cost centers. The viable alternative is a Tier-1 commercial bank treasury unit with an integrated securities settlement account at a licensed brokerage, not an EMI. Any attempt to use an unlicensed EMI for operational treasury would violate FEIS declarations and may trigger AML enforcement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion per fiscal year; Master File is required when the consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country reporting thresholds apply at KRW 1 trillion group revenue.

Economic substance scope: OECD BEPS substance principle applies through Korea's Framework Act on International Tax Coordination and Substance-over-Form doctrine; HFT must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea, including local servers, latency expertise, risk management, board decisions, and employment substance, otherwise tax benefits are denied and income may be reallocated.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act prescribes imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to 3 times the tax evaded; the National Tax Service additionally imposes a 20% negligence penalty under Article 66 of the Corporate Income Tax Act and reassesses the full evaded tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for directors who knowingly implemented concealment: they are jointly and severally liable for the evaded corporate tax under the National Tax Collection Act, face travel bans and asset restriction registration under the Tax Offenders Registration Act, and incur criminal imprisonment up to 5 years; civil restitution can strip personal assets and pass through to insolvency proceedings.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Article 66 of the Corporate Income Tax Act imposes a 20% negligence penalty; Article 62 of the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination authorizes denial of treaty benefits; transfer pricing rules require upward adjustment of taxable income with a penalty surcharge of 10-20% on the unreported amount.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax if they managed or authorized the shell structure with gross negligence; blacklisting and travel ban are imposed; directors face criminal prosecution for tax fraud if the Court finds that the structure was a sham, leading to imprisonment up to 5 years and confiscation of fees paid to the offshore affiliate.
AML/CFT and Foreign Exchange Control Violation

Statutory sanction: Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Article 30 imposes imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to KRW 500 million; the Financial Information Act (AML) provides for license revocation, administrative penalties, and confiscation of illegal trading proceeds; KoFIU may suspend real-time clearing lines and freeze trading accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability and can be sentenced to imprisonment up to 5 years, subjected to travel bans and asset freezing under emergency anti-money laundering measures, and held civilly liable to disgorge profits from unlicensed activity; repeat or systemic violations can trigger personal bankruptcy and disqualification from serving as directors in any Korean financial institution.

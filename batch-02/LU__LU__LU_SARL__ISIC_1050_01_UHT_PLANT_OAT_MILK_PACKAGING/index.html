Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; SARL is an opaque resident capital company (société de capitaux) subject to corporate income tax (17% on profits above EUR 200,000; 15% on the first EUR 175,000 with a 31% marginal bridge rate), 7% unemployment solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax (6.75% in Luxembourg City), giving an aggregate standard rate of 24.94% for the UHT oat-milk plant; resident SARL taxable on worldwide income; no tax-exempt manufacturing zone; non-resident shareholders are taxed in Luxembourg only via 15% dividend withholding tax (reduced or eliminated under EU directives and treaties); not eligible for pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists under Luxembourg law. Available elections: (i) fiscal unity / integral tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Art. 164bis LIR for ≥95% directly or indirectly held resident entities; (ii) IP box election under Art. 50ter LIR exempting 80% of qualifying IP income; (iii) participation exemption election for 100% dividend and capital-gain relief on qualifying holdings of ≥10% (or ≥EUR 1.2M acquisition price for dividends / ≥EUR 6M for gains) held for ≥12 months; (iv) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under Art. 61bis of the VAT Law; hybrid mismatch scenarios are mandatorily neutralised under ATAD implementing Articles 168bis-168ter LIR, leaving no elective hybrid-mismatch planning.

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover (excl. VAT) for the domestic small-business franchise under Art. 57 loi TVA; no threshold for non-established taxable persons or for traders making intra-Community acquisitions/supplies nor for exporters — mandatory VAT registration from the first euro of taxable turnover; the SARL manufacturing and exporting UHT oat milk must register as an ordinary taxable person and cannot use the franchise.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies at 0% to: (a) definitive export of UHT oat-milk products from Luxembourg to non-EU destinations under Art. 44 loi TVA, provided the goods physically leave EU territory and the exporter retains transport evidence; (b) intra-Community supplies of goods to an EU VAT-registered customer under Art. 43 loi TVA, subject to valid customer VAT number, dispatch to another Member State, and EC Sales List filing; ancillary transport, packaging and bottling services provided by the plant in connection with exported goods are also zero-rated; the SARL must hold regular VAT registration, complete commercial books, and may claim periodic VAT refunds of input VAT allocable to zero-rated exports.

  • Electronic export declaration (EXS/Single Administrative Document) filed via the Luxembourg PLDA customs portal with MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and Incoterms-based sale contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice proving payment of export proceeds
  • For intra-EU zero-rating: ECB validation output of customer's EU VAT number and the recapitulative statement (EC Sales List) submitted to AEDT

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime exists in Luxembourg; e-invoicing in B2B is permitted with counterparty consent; B2G e-invoicing to central government via PEPPOL is mandatory since April 2019; VAT returns (monthly/quarterly), EC Sales Lists and Intrastat declarations must be filed electronically through MyGuichet/ACD; the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) reform will impose mandatory structured e-invoicing and digital reporting requirements for intra-EU B2B supplies from 2030, at which point Luxembourg will deploy transaction-level clearance.

The AEDT may claw back the zero-rated treatment and reassess VAT at the 17% standard rate if the documentary bundle is incomplete, inconsistent, or fraudulent, with statutory default interest on the additional assessment; administrative surcharges under the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung) add up to 50% of the evaded tax for negligence and up to 100% for wilful evasion; criminal VAT fraud (Art. 24 loi TVA and AO Arts. 397-405) is punishable by imprisonment of one to five years and fines up to EUR 250,000, with joint and several liability of the SARL's managers; the limitation period of assessment is 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a Eurozone member and applies full euro-denominated current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU on the free movement of capital and payments. There are no exchange-control permits, no tariff-style currency restrictions, and no approval gate for cross-border profit remittance. Dividend withholding tax is the only material fiscal friction: the domestic Luxembourg rate is 15%, reduced to 0% for qualifying EU corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and to lower rates under bilateral tax treaties. Operational hurdles are therefore tax/substance-related rather than currency-related: the SARL must demonstrate substance, transfer-pricing compliance, and proper beneficial-ownership documentation before distributions and shareholder-loan repayments are executed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receivables in EUR, USD, GBP, or other permitted currencies may be retained in full at the SARL's bank accounts. The only compliance overlay is statistical: individual cross-border payments of EUR 12,500 or more are captured in the BCL balance-of-payments direct reporting system, and the SARL must maintain supporting invoices and SWIFT/SEPA records to substantiate the economic purpose and the correct BOP classification of each flow.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as the National Central Bank of the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the competent financial-sector regulator with no separate foreign-exchange administration.

Statutory deadline: 31 March of the year following the fiscal year (Art. 164 LIR); e-filing returns are administratively accepted through 30 June, and extensions of up to 31 December are commonly granted upon ACD reminders; final assessed tax is payable within three months of notification of the assessment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory for a Luxembourg entity whose annual net turnover exceeds €4,000,000 or whose aggregate related-party transactions exceed €1,000,000 (Art. 56bis LIR as amended by the Law of 28 December 2018); Master File and Country-by-Country Report: mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of €750,000,000 or more, with a separate CbC notification filed to the ACD.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) statute, but the ACD applies the EU ATAD GAAR, Art. 6 AO abuse-of-law doctrine, and the ACD's 2017 substance guidance: the UHT oat-milk manufacturing SARL must demonstrate local premises, qualified full-time employees (plant operators, QA engineers, logistics staff), active management decisions taken in Luxembourg, a Luxembourg bank account, and genuine commercial and operational risk. Real plant assets, utility flows, and purchase/offtake contracts executed in Luxembourg provide strong substance; a hollow conduit lacking personnel and physical operations would be recharacterised and denied treaty and Directive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False or incomplete CIT return

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €10,000 and imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years (Art. 217 AO); civil reassessment adds the evaded tax, a 10% surcharge, monthly default interest of 0.6%–0.8%, and taxation d'office disallowing undocumented deductions.

Director personal liability: Gérants and administrators are personally and solidarily liable for the company's resulting tax debt where wilful fault is established; consequences include seizure of personal assets, disqualification from board mandates, travel bans, and up to 5 years' imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Up to 10% surcharge on the ACD transfer-pricing adjustment; denial of interest and expense deductibility; potential double taxation; repeated breaches are escalated to the ACD anti-abuse unit and can trigger criminal tax proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incomplete TP documentation may be held jointly and severally liable for reassessed corporate taxes under ACD enforcement practice, notwithstanding the SARL's limited liability shield.

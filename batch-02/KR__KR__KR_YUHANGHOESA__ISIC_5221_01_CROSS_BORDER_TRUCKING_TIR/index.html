Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Korean Commercial Act for a Yuhan Hoesa; no blocked escrow account is required. However, the full subscribed capital must be deposited in a designated domestic bank account before registration and evidenced by a capital deposit certificate. For the international trucking licence, MOLIT may assess financial solvency and TIR carnet issuance may require a bank guarantee from the national guaranteeing association.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Operation Certificate from the National Fire Agency or local fire station is required for any truck depot, parking building, maintenance workshop, or office; dangerous goods storage permits are required for diesel fuel storage above regulatory thresholds under the Dangerous Substances Safety Act. Fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and evacuation plans must be certified for all physical premises. The Ministry of Employment and Labor must issue or receive a workplace safety and health inspection approval under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including structural load review for any mezzanine, racking, or elevated maintenance platform, and all trucks must carry vehicle inspection certificates and fire suppression equipment compliant with Korea road transport safety regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for a corporation. VAT registration is triggered by business commencement; a Yuhan Hoesa must register within 20 days of starting business. The KRW 80,000,000 simplified taxable person annual turnover threshold applies only to eligible individual entrepreneurs and does not exempt corporate taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for the TIR overland trucking supply is available under the Korean VAT Act where the factual and documentary evidence establishes that: (a) goods were physically exported from the Republic of Korea through a Korean customs office under an accepted Export Customs Declaration; (b) the transport service is performed by the Korean Yuhan Hoesa as part of the international carriage of those goods, or is supplied to a foreign customer as a foreign-currency-earning service qualifying for zero-rating; (c) the TIR Carnet traverses the Korean border under customs seal and is discharged at the foreign destination; and (d) the freight consideration is received and repatriated in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Domestic Korean road haulage that does not leave Korean territory is taxable at 10% unless a specific statutory zero-rating provision applies; the Korean non-sealed domestic leg of an international movement is also taxable at 10% unless adequately evidenced as an international freight service.

  • Export Customs Declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS (Export Declaration Certificate and electronic control number)
  • TIR Carnet with the Korean customs departure-office certificate, green cover, and foreign customs discharge receipt evidencing that the goods exited Korea and arrived abroad
  • CMR international waybill (Convention on the Contract for the International Carriage of Goods by Road) with matching consignor/consignee, goods description, weight, route, and truck registration
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, freight contract, and export sales/purchase documents reconciled to the Export Customs Declaration
  • Foreign exchange collection certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank (inward remittance certificate, being the Korean statutory equivalent to an FIRC) evidencing receipt of the export freight consideration
  • Where applicable, a multimodal transport document, such as a combined transport bill of lading or ocean/air waybill, for the onward sea or air leg after the Korean road section

The Republic of Korea operates an electronic tax invoice (e-Tax invoice) transmission regime through HomeTax; there is no real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory anti-fraud digital stamping engine in Korea. A VAT-registered supplier must issue an e-Tax invoice through HomeTax or an NTS-approved electronic invoicing service provider and transmit it to the NTS by the statutory filing deadline; semi-annual VAT returns then reconcile all e-invoices. For zero-rated TIR exports, the e-Tax invoice must state the zero-rated export nature, and the KCS UNI-PASS export declaration data is cross-checked with NTS HomeTax records. Computerized issuers using bulk, API, or EDI transmission must register their e-invoicing systems with the NTS; the NTS performs post-filing cross-matching and audit rather than real-time fiscal approval.

The NTS and KCS jointly audit zero-rated TIR exports by matching the UNI-PASS export exit record, the HomeTax e-Tax invoice, the TIR/CMR transport trail, and the foreign exchange collection certificate. If any mandatory document is missing or inconsistent, the NTS will retroactively disallow the 0% rate, assess 10% VAT on the freight consideration, disallow input VAT credits attributable to the supply, add late-payment surcharges, and impose underreporting penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes, including up to 40% for dishonest underreporting or evasion and lesser percentages for negligent reporting. Fraudulent use of TIR carnets, false customs declarations, or forged export documents may also be prosecuted under the Customs Act and the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act, exposing the entity and responsible officers to criminal fines, imprisonment, and retrospective reassessment within the statutory limitation period for tax fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a fundamentally liberalised foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with the Korean won (KRW) fully convertible for current account transactions including trade in goods, transportation, travel, and routine service fees. Capital account transactions, such as cross-border equity injections into a Korean LLC (Yuhan Hoesa), are permitted but subject to the outward/inward reporting regime administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Bank of Korea. Foreign investments exceeding a certain threshold require declaration under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act with a Korea Trade Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) or designated foreign exchange bank report. There are no mandatory surrender requirements for export proceeds; a South Korean resident entity may hold foreign currency receipts in domestic foreign currency accounts for up to 18 months without compulsory conversion. Profit remittance from the LLC to non-resident shareholders is treated as current account payment and is freely convertible once a certified tax clearance and audited financial statement confirm the distributable reserves. However, practical friction arises from banks requiring statutory documents, tax completion certificates, and the foreign investor's proof of original foreign exchange transaction certificates to prevent disguised capital flight. Overall, Korea is a low-intervention jurisdiction with no parallel market premium, but the administrative reporting framework creates a compliance burden for any entity lacking robust treasury support.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 12 and Enforcement Decree 7-2 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected or received within 18 months from the date of export customs clearance (the standard settlement period). Extensions are available upon application to the relevant foreign exchange bank if justifiable circumstances exist. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporting entity may retain 100% of foreign currency earnings in a foreign currency account, but quarterly outstanding export proceeds must be reported to the designated foreign exchange bank to ensure compliance with the collection timeline and to facilitate the bank's monitoring of trade financing exposure.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) and non-bank virtual account services have very low feasibility for a Korean-regulated Yuhan Hoesa in the trucking/logistics sector. The FETA and the Korean Banking Act strictly require that all foreign exchange transactions undertaken by a domestic resident entity be executed through a domestic designated foreign exchange bank with a real local branch presence. Offshore EMI platforms cannot obtain a licensed foreign exchange bank status in Korea for the purpose of holding trade proceeds or effecting KRW settlements. They lack access to the FEIN reporting system, which mandates that every foreign exchange transaction be reported on a real-time basis. Fintechs such as Wise or Revolut Business have no legal standing to provide KRW IBANs or local clearing accounts for a Korean LLC and would breach the Korean Government's Foreign Exchange Information Network requirements. Their viability is therefore rated 'Not Viable'; the treasurer must rely exclusively on Tier-1 domestic bank channels for all FX and clearing operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an inbound foreign direct investment into the Korean LLC, the non-resident shareholder shall remit funds via the SWIFT MT103 message through a correspondent bank to the Shinhan Bank or KB Kookmin capital escrow account. The remittance instruction must include the Korean foreign investment declaration number issued by the MOEF/KOTRA and the standard purpose code 'FDI_EQ' or '109' (equity participation). The receiving designated foreign exchange bank verifies the declaration, credits the escrow account, and later reclasses the funds as paid-in capital upon submission of the certified capital subscription agreement and the certificate of foreign currency purchase. For trade-context inbound payments (e.g., freight receivable for a TIR transport), the routing must use the current account purpose code '10-2-03-01' (transportation earnings) and be supported by the corresponding CMR and TIR Carnet. The bank follows the Foreign Exchange Information Network protocol and releases funds to the operating KRW current account. A capital verification deed is required if the LLC is required to undergo statutory auditing or needs to prove capitalisation for tendering contracts.Outbound treasury operations for the Yuhan Hoesa are cleared through a combination of the designated Tier-1 bank's trade portal, SWIFT MT103 for high-value freight refuelling and cargo insurance payments, and documentary channels. For large bonded cargo purchases where the firm acts as a purchasing intermediary, the treasury should open an Irrevocable Documentary Credit (Letter of Credit) against the CMR transport documents and original customs forms; the LC fits the standard UCP600 framework. For lower-risk and time-flexible inventory acquisitions from trucking parts suppliers, an Export/Import Documentary Collection (D/P and D/A) is permissible, though slower, and provides a clean evidence trail required by the Korean Customs Service for the TIR Carnet closure. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) and non-bank virtual account services have very low feasibility for a Korean-regulated Yuhan Hoesa in the trucking/logistics sector. The FETA and the Korean Banking Act strictly require that all foreign exchange transactions undertaken by a domestic resident entity be executed through a domestic designated foreign exchange bank with a real local branch presence. Offshore EMI platforms cannot obtain a licensed foreign exchange bank status in Korea for the purpose of holding trade proceeds or effecting KRW settlements. They lack access to the FEIN reporting system, which mandates that every foreign exchange transaction be reported on a real-time basis. Fintechs such as Wise or Revolut Business have no legal standing to provide KRW IBANs or local clearing accounts for a Korean LLC and would breach the Korean Government's Foreign Exchange Information Network requirements. Their viability is therefore rated 'Not Viable'; the treasurer must rely exclusively on Tier-1 domestic bank channels for all FX and clearing operations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation is triggered when cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; Master File preparation is triggered when Korean revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion; Country-by-Country Reporting is required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above the OECD threshold as implemented by Korea, currently approximately KRW 1 trillion for ultimate parent groups.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but it applies a strict substance-over-form doctrine, the National Tax Basic Act Article 14, the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test, and the Framework Act on International Tax Adjustment Articles 3 and 6. The Yuhan Hoesa must demonstrate that Core Income-Generating Activities for cross-border TIR trucking are performed in Korea: board and management decisions, driver employment, fleet ownership or true leasing, fuel procurement, maintenance, insurance placement, freight billing, and TIR carnet control. If management fee payments are made to a foreign principal while substantive transport operations remain in Korea, profits will be reattributed to the Korean PE and treaty relief will be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham Offshore Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under the Corporate Tax Act, a 10-20% negligence penalty, and a 0.022% per-day late-payment surcharge. Fraudulent evasion is punishable under Punishment of Tax Evasion Act Article 3 with imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to KRW 200 million, plus asset seizure and collection of unpaid corporate tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under Korean Commercial Act Article 401; directors are jointly and severally liable for tortious losses caused by intentional or negligent management. National Tax Service may blacklist the directors, impose travel bans, attach personal bank accounts, collect the corporate tax from personal assets, and recommend criminal indictment. A court can impose imprisonment on the directors who signed the false return.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Inflated Intercompany Deductions

Statutory sanction: The Framework Act on International Tax Adjustment Articles 2 and 4 authorizes the National Tax Service to adjust to the arm's-length price and to make correlative adjustments; understated tax is assessed with a penalty of up to 20%, plus daily late-payment surcharge and, in flagrant cases, criminal referral for tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany agreements without commercial substance and without functional and risk analysis are personally exposed under Korean Commercial Act Article 401. The National Tax Service can pierce the corporate form, attach personal property, deny foreign travel, blacklist the directors, and impose imprisonment of up to 3 years where false or incomplete information is submitted knowingly.
Withholding Tax Failure / Shell Shareholder Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Corporate Tax Act Article 98 makes the Korean payer liable for the un-withheld amount, plus an additional 10% non-compliance penalty and daily late-payment surcharge. The Framework Act on International Tax Adjustment anti-abuse rules and the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act Article 3 apply where the arrangement is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable if the corporation transfers funds abroad and cannot satisfy the withholding liability. Under Korean Commercial Act Article 401 and the tax collection framework, the National Tax Service may pierce the corporate veil, impose personal assessment, asset freeze, foreign-travel blacklisting, restitution orders, and criminal fines; imprisonment of up to 3 years may follow for deliberate failure to withhold and remit.

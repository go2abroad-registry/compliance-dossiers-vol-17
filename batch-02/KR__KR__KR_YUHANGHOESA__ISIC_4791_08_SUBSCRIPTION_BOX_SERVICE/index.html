Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa under Articles 543 through 613 of the Commercial Act. However, the full amount of each member's subscribed contribution must be actually paid in cash or in kind by the time of incorporation, and the cash contribution must be deposited into a designated bank escrow or temporary capital payment account in Korea before registration. The bank-issued certificate of contribution payment must be submitted to the registry court. There is no fixed statutory blocking period, but the deposited funds remain restricted until the incorporation certificate is issued. Where the business seeks certain tax incentives under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, a foreign investment of KRW 100,000,000 or more may be required for those incentives, but that is not a corporate incorporation capital requirement.

A building occupancy permit and structural load inspection are required before installing racking and shelving systems, and the certificate must be displayed at the facility. Under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, the workplace must be registered with the Ministry of Employment and Labor, a safety and health management system established, and a safety and health manager appointed where the statutory threshold of employees is reached.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, Ministry of Economy and Finance; online portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr). Korea Customs Service (KCS) maintains the UNI-PASS clearance portal (https://unipass.customs.go.kr) for export declarations and customs audit evidence.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine registration category. A Korean domestic Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic corporation and must obtain a Business Registration Number (BRN) from the National Tax Service; the registration application is due within 20 days after business commencement and may be filed earlier. A foreign non-resident company without a Korean place of business must first appoint a resident tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes and file the appointment/power of attorney with the NTS. Step 2: Assemble statutory documents: Korean registry corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, shareholder register, board resolution appointing the Korean tax representative or domestic director, lease agreement or proof of place of business, and identity documents of the appointed representative. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not provide a US-style check-the-box or pass-through election. A Yuhan Hoesa is categorically classified as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income. The group consolidation tax regime is available only as a filing measure for qualifying wholly-owned domestic subsidiaries and is not an entity classification election. Anti-hybrid and structural mismatch rules under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs apply as mandatory law and cannot be elected away.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0: there is no general turnover threshold before VAT registration becomes mandatory in Korea. Registration is compulsory upon commencing taxable supplies. The simplified taxable person concession is limited to individual entrepreneurs with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80 million and is not available to a Yuhan Hoesa corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT is the mandatory statutory treatment for exports of goods under the Value-Added Tax Act. For subscription-box merchandising, the Korean entity must prove: (1) the goods physically left Korea under an accepted export declaration to the Korea Customs Service; (2) the recipient is a non-resident customer and title/risk passed outside Korea; (3) the export is recorded in the Korean entity's books as an export sale; (4) the proceeds are paid in foreign currency and repatriated to a Korean foreign exchange bank; and (5) the complete export evidence pack is retained. Digitally supplied services performed in Korea but supplied to a non-resident and actually used overseas may also qualify for zero-rating if paid in foreign currency; however, the physical goods in a subscription box must satisfy the physical export and customs documentation route.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration clearance certificate (UNI-PASS Export Declaration clearance)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) showing the Korean exporter as shipper and a foreign consignee/destination outside Korea
  • Commercial invoice stating FOB/export value of the subscription box
  • Packing list itemizing the contents, quantities, and HS codes of the subscription box
  • Foreign exchange settlement confirmation or Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Korea operates a centralized electronic tax invoice mandate through the HomeTax e-tax invoice service. Business suppliers must issue tax invoices by the statutory deadline; those invoices must be transmitted electronically to the NTS for B2B supplies, and NTS cross-matches invoice data with VAT returns and customs records. Cash receipts are required for consumer cash transactions. Korea does not use a Latin American-style real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping model; there is no transaction-level pre-approval API. Instead, the NTS uses consolidated e-tax invoice submission, pre-refund risk scoring, and automated post-event audits, supported by the KCS UNI-PASS customs data.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-match e-tax invoices, export declarations, and foreign exchange bank settlement records. An exporter that cannot substantiate the zero-rated export within the statutory deadline may have the supply recharacterized as domestic: 10% VAT is assessed, input VAT refunds are recovered by correction order, interest is charged from the date of refund, and additional tax is imposed under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 270-day foreign exchange settlement deadline can also invalidate the zero-rating evidence. False tax invoices, fraudulent refund claims, or deliberate misstatement are referred under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with criminal penalties, retrospective clawback of refunds, and potential suspension or revocation of future export zero-rating privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a market-friendly, largely liberalized foreign exchange regime rather than a strict exchange-control system. The Korean won is fully convertible for current transactions, including trade settlements, travel, and dividend remittances, provided the underlying documentation and bank declarations are complete under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree. Capital and financial account transactions are conditionally liberalized: inbound foreign direct investment into a Yuhan Hoesa must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act or the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and each inward remittance must be declared to the receiving foreign exchange bank for reporting to the Bank of Korea. There is no mandatory surrender requirement, and foreign investors may retain USD, KRW, or other currencies in domestic foreign-exchange accounts. The principal economic friction is documentary, not regulatory: profit remittances require audited earnings evidence, certificate of tax withholding, compensation-related tax receipts, and confirmation that no restrictions on outward remittance apply. Because Korea uses a free-floating regime with FX risk borne by market participants, the treasury must use covered hedging instruments only for genuine underlying exposures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, exporters must collect foreign currency proceeds within six months from the date of export declaration unless an extension is approved by the designated foreign exchange bank or the Ministry of Strategy and Finance. There is no mandatory conversion into KRW; the current statutory surrender ratio is 0% and proceeds may be held, used for foreign-currency payments, converted voluntarily, or deposited into a registered foreign-exchange account. Exporters must submit a receipt report through the receiving bank to the Bank of Korea. Late receipt requires a written explanation and can trigger administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with foreign exchange policy authority coordinated with the Ministry of Strategy and Finance, and transaction-level administration delegated by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act to licensed domestic foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Overall viability for treasury purposes: low. South Korea does not grant cross-border e-money institution (EMI) licenses to foreign fintechs under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act; a foreign EMI cannot hold deposits, provide local IBANs, or clear Korean won settlement for a Yuhan Hoesa unless it establishes a licensed local subsidiary or partners with a licensed bank as its settlement agent. Offshore digital platforms such as Stripe and Authorize.Net can support card collection as acquirers or payment processors, but final net settlement must be into a domestic Tier-1 bank account. Using an offshore EMI as the treasury account would create illegal deposit-taking, unreported foreign-exchange flows, and AML opacity. The correct treasury posture is to treat Stripe and Authorize.Net as payment collection rails only, with automatic daily or T+1 sweep to the Korean operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound paid-in capital must use a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's institutional account to the Yuhan Hoesa's capital escrow account at the designated Korean bank. The remittance instruction must quote the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act filing reference and state the Bank of Korea FX transaction purpose code corresponding to inward direct investment equity contribution. The receiving bank will verify the code against the Foreign Investment Promotion Act report, perform capital verification, issue the foreign-investment registration certificate, and only then release funds to the operating account. Cash deposits, crypto conversions, and interposed fintech wallets are not acceptable. After verification, the funds may be used for subscription-box inventory procurement, warehousing, and merchant service fees. Offshore digital platforms such as Stripe and Authorize.Net can support card collection as acquirers or payment processors, but final net settlement must be into a domestic Tier-1 bank account. Using an offshore EMI as the treasury account would create illegal deposit-taking, unreported foreign-exchange flows, and AML opacity. The correct treasury posture is to treat Stripe and Authorize.Net as payment collection rails only, with automatic daily or T+1 sweep to the Korean operating account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) is required when the aggregate volume of foreign related-party transactions in sales, purchases, loans, royalties, service fees and security subscription transactions equals or exceeds KRW 5 billion in a fiscal year. The International Transaction Report must also be attached to the Korean corporate tax return at that threshold. A Country-by-Country report is separately required when the multinational group's consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the same doctrine is mandatorily applied through Article 15 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, Article 14 of the Adjustment of International Taxes Act, and treaty limitation-on-benefits jurisprudence. The National Tax Service will disregard a shell entity, reallocate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) to Yuhan Hoesa's Korean office, and impute a Korean permanent establishment when subscription-box contract negotiation, customer support, inventory management, order fulfillment, payment collection and strategic decision-making are performed in Korea. A foreign nominee that merely signs paper contracts and records invoices in a zero-tax jurisdiction will not receive treaty protection.

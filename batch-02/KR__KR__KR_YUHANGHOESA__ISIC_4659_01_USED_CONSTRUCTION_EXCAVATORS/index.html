Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea; online portal: HomeTax System (https://www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) in South Korea, the entity must obtain a business registration certificate (사업자등록증) from the local district tax office (세무서) under the jurisdiction of its registered address. The application is submitted via the HomeTax System or in person, with the following documents: articles of incorporation, certificate of foreign investment (if applicable), lease agreement for the registered office, and identification of the representative. After approval, the NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number (사업자등록번호) which serves as the VAT ID and corporate tax ID. For non-resident entities without a local permanent establishment, a simplified VAT registration may be required if they make taxable supplies in Korea. The process typically takes 3-5 business days.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No threshold; any business with taxable supplies must register for VAT. However, a simplified taxpayer regime applies to small businesses with annual revenue below KRW 48 million (for wholesale/retail) or KRW 80 million (for services), which reduces compliance burden but does not exempt registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under Article 21 of the VAT Act. To claim zero-rating, the exporter must prove that the goods were physically exported from Korea and that the export proceeds are received in foreign currency. For used construction machinery, the exporter must provide evidence of the export declaration and the actual shipment. Services directly connected to exported goods may also qualify for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Purchase Certificate

Mandatory electronic tax invoices (e-invoices) must be issued for all B2B transactions via the HomeTax System. E-invoices must be transmitted to the NTS by the 10th of the following month. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, but the NTS uses a digital e-invoice system for cross-matching and anti-fraud analytics. Additionally, cash receipts must be issued for consumer transactions, and a fiscal receipt device is required.

If the export zero-rating is claimed without proper documentation or if the export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, the NTS will assess VAT at 10% on the export value, plus an additional tax of 40% of the VAT as a penalty. In cases of fraud, criminal penalties may apply, including fines up to KRW 100 million and imprisonment. The NTS conducts post-export audits and may claw back any refunds previously granted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade payments, service fees, royalties, and dividend remittances, subject to submission of underlying evidence to a licensed foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, including direct equity investments, foreign borrowing, and cross-border loans, operate under a declaration-and-registration rather than a blanket prohibition, but require filing under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and, for foreign direct investment, reporting under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. A foreign investor injecting equity into a Korean limited liability company must open a temporary Foreign Currency Deposit Account with a domestic Korean bank, deposit the inward remittance, convert it into Korean won, and complete a post-investment capital verification process before the funds can be used. Profit remittance is permitted after corporate tax returns and withholding tax settlement, with the bank requiring certified financial statements, tax payment certificates, board-approved dividend resolution, and tax treaty residence confirmation to support the outward remittance. Because Korea maintains a free-floating regime but restricts speculative non-trade capital flows, all large wire transfers require explicit FX purpose codes, and the bank will reject remittances lacking a clearly documented underlying transaction. The practical hurdle for a used heavy machinery wholesaler is not convertibility itself but the evidentiary burden of proving asset valuation and the commercial reality of cross-border trades with countries posing sanctions-related or trade-based money laundering risks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Korea does not impose a mandatory surrender-to-central-bank ratio or a forced KRW conversion percentage. Exporters must repatriate and settle export proceeds in foreign currency through a licensed foreign exchange bank within six months from the date of the export declaration, either by converting to KRW or by retaining the funds in an approved Foreign Currency Deposit Account. If payment is legitimately delayed by buyer default or documentary discrepancies, the exporter must apply for an extension through the settlement bank before the six-month deadline lapses. The applicable statutory principle is full repatriation and settlement, with 0% forced conversion, but 100% of export receipts must be reportable and traceable through the Korea Customs Service export declaration and bank settlement records.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), acting in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Korea Customs Service, under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and regulations promulgated by the Foreign Exchange Administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail. Offshore fintech EMI platforms cannot service this entity as a principal account bank because they do not hold a Korean banking license and are legally barred from accepting KRW deposits, issuing local IBAN-like settlement accounts, or performing foreign investment capital verification under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. They may be used only as ancillary payment aggregators that settle through a licensed Korean bank, never as the receiving institution for FDI capital injections, export proceeds repatriation, or statutory capital verification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must first file a foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act through KOTRA or a licensed foreign exchange bank, then remit the equity contribution via SWIFT MT103 to the Korean bank's Foreign Currency Deposit Account established in the name of the Korean entity or the foreign investor. The remittance must clearly identify the investor, the target entity, and the foreign exchange purpose code for equity acquisition or capital increase; after receipt, the bank converts the foreign currency to KRW at the market rate into a temporary capital escrow account, verifies the paid-in capital, and issues the foreign investment certificate. Capital verification is completed by comparing the subscribed capital in the notarized articles of incorporation, the bank credit advice, and the balance on the escrow account, after which funds are released to the operating account.For imports of used excavators and spare parts, the preferred rails are SWIFT MT103 for open-account settlements, commercial letters of credit issued through the Korean bank's trade finance desk for high-value confirmed goods, and documentary collections D/P or D/A for goods financed under conditions where an LC is not commercially justified. All outbound trade payments must be matched against the Korea Customs Service import declaration, the commercial invoice, packing list, Bill of Lading, and inspection certificate, with the bank applying the appropriate foreign exchange trade purpose code. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% national corporate income tax plus 10% local income tax surcharge (maximum combined 27.5% for taxable income above KRW 30 billion)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge = 22% on gross dividends paid to a nonresident shareholder. Treaty rate reductions generally apply (commonly 5% for substantial shareholdings, 15% for portfolio dividends; US-Korea treaty 15% portfolio / 10% for 10% shareholders).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 100 billion (approximately USD 76 million) in gross revenue, or a cross-border related-party transaction volume of KRW 50 billion; a Local File is also mandatory when any intercompany transaction category exceeds KRW 5 billion, and a Master File is required for groups with consolidated revenue above KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies the economic-substance and substance-over-form doctrines under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and Article 52 of the Corporate Tax Act. A company with no real office, no core income-generating activities in Korea, and with profits shifted to a low-tax offshore entity will be recharacterized and subjected to the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR). Although Korea does not apply OECD Economic Substance Regulations directly, the BEPS provisions and case law require functional and factual substance for both treaty benefits and intercompany pricing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Cross-border tax evasion / willful omission of sales from used excavator exports

Statutory sanction: Article 109(1) of the Corporate Tax Act imposes imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine up to KRW 50 million; Article 34(2) of the Framework Act on National Taxes adds a 40% fraudulent understatement penalty and 20% unfaithful-report penalty; overdue interest of 0.022% per day accrues on the unpaid tax, capped at 36 months.

Director personal liability: Under Article 39(1) of the Framework Act on National Taxes, a director who materially participated in the evasion is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax if corporate assets are insufficient; the Special Act on Aggravation of Punishment for Specific Economic Crimes raises the imprisonment term to 5 years and mandates travel-restriction (blacklisting), asset seizure and personal bank account freezing.
Fraudulent transfer pricing / abuse of a low-tax offshore intermediary

Statutory sanction: Under Article 4(4) of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the National Tax Service adjusts the taxpayer to the arm's-length range and imposes an additional tax of 10% of the understated tax; if contemporaneous documentation is not submitted the penalty rises to 30%; a secondary adjustment applies, treating the difference as a deemed dividend subject to 22% withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or signed the defective pricing are personally liable for the adjusted tax and penalty; article 11 of the same law creates a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment up to 2 years or a fine up to KRW 20 million; Korean courts pierce the corporate veil when the entity is a mere façade, and the court may attach the director's personal overseas real estate and assets.
Anti-money laundering failure on unreported cash and split payments

Statutory sanction: Article 13(1) of the Act on Reporting and Use of Specific Financial Transaction Information punishes failure to report financial transactions with imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine up to KRW 30 million; if proceeds are criminal in nature, the Act on Confiscation and Regaining of Criminal Proceeds permits seizure of the company's assets and the proceeds of the machines; repeat offenders face up to 5 years of imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Article 14(2) of the AML Act imposes individual criminal liability on the responsible director unless he or she proves the transaction was concealed from management and unauthorized; directors involved in the structuring are subject to immediate travel bans, account freezes, permanent criminal records, disqualification from board service for regulated activities, and, if convicted of underlying bribery, forfeiture of personal assets and civil damages under the Korean Commercial Act and Foreign Bribery Prevention Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that creates a Korean domestic place of business must file the corporate business registration application with the district tax office that has jurisdiction over that place of business within 20 days of business commencement, obtaining an 11-digit business registration number (TIN) which also operates as the VAT registration number. The application can be submitted through the HomeTax portal, by a certified tax agent, or at the district tax office; supporting documents include foreign incorporation/registration certificates, proof of the Korean place of business (lease agreement or office registration), the appointment of a domestic tax representative where required, and board resolution or branch head authorization. For the newly formed domestic Yuhan Hoesa (LLC), registration is performed by filing the standard Application for Business Registration together with its Korean certificate of incorporation, and the NTS issues the Business Registration Certificate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Korea requires VAT registration from the first taxable supply for an incorporated entity, so the effective threshold is zero. For unincorporated/simplified businesses, KRW 80,000,000 annual tax-exclusive taxable turnover separates the simplified taxable person regime from the general taxpayer regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment is available for goods supplied to a foreign purchaser and physically exported from Korea under a valid customs export declaration, and for exported services actually performed or used outside Korea as specified in the VAT Act Enforcement Decree. For the online automotive parts retail scope, zero rating requires that the parts leave Korea under customs supervision and that legal title and physical delivery pass to an overseas customer.

  • Export Customs Declaration (Declaration of Exportation) approved through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill showing the foreign port/destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract / purchase order with the foreign buyer
  • Bank-issued Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation (or an Inward Remittance Certificate / FIRC if accepted by the relevant exchange bank) evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Korea Customs Service export clearance log/UNI-PASS printout

The Yuhan Hoesa must issue electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) for ordinary B2B supplies and transmit them to the National Tax Service through the HomeTax E-Invoice system by the statutory tax invoice deadline. The NTS provides bulk transmission APIs and EDI for high-volume taxpayers, assigns electronic numbers to received invoices, and reconciles seller/buyer filings for anti-fraud detection. There is no separate third-party pre-clearance digital stamping requirement for export-only movements, but the e-tax invoice must be linked to the export declaration to substantiate zero rating.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly verify that the export declaration, shipping documents, and foreign currency collection evidence are consistent. If the zero-rated claim is not substantiated by the required customs and FX documentation, the NTS will retroactively assess output VAT on the supply, deny or claw back any VAT refund or input tax credit, and impose additional tax penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act. Serious or fraudulent abuse can result in criminal referral, fines, and personal liability for directors and representatives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a market-based current-account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is fully convertible for trade-related current transactions and ordinary service payments; residents and non-residents may freely convert through designated foreign exchange banks within reporting thresholds. Capital-account operations, however, remain regulated: foreign direct investment must be notified through the Korea Trade Investment Promotion Agency or a designated foreign exchange bank, and portfolio flows are subject to reporting and, in stress periods, prudential limits. Exporters do not face a surrender requirement; proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted at market rates. Profit remittance is permitted but subject to evidenced tax residency, audited financial statements, withholding tax at 20% or treaty-reduced rate, and, for dividend outflows, prior foreign exchange bank reporting. Transfer-pricing documentation may be requested under Korea's global anti-base-erosion rules. Therefore, convertibility is robust on the current account, but treasury plans must build time buffers for capital reporting, tax waivers, and beneficiary verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds must generally be collected within three months from the date of export declaration, with extension possible up to six months or longer for deferred-payment, shipbuilding, or plant-export contracts. There is no mandatory conversion ratio in South Korea; exporters may hold 100% of export receipts in a designated foreign-currency deposit account at a foreign exchange bank. No surrender to the Bank of Korea is required. Late collection or non-collection must be reported and may trigger a foreign-exchange violation review.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF), the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS), and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low and legally constrained. Offshore fintech EMIs cannot serve as the primary treasury account for a Korean-resident entity unless licensed under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and authorized by the Financial Services Commission. Non-resident digital banks such as remote EMI providers do not have the legal right to open KRW-denominated corporate accounts or issue a Korean business IBAN for the Yuhan Hoesa. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a compliant inbound equity injection, the non-resident investor should file a foreign investment notification through the Korea Trade Investment Promotion Agency or the designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The remittance must be sent via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's own account to the Yuhan Hoesa's foreign-currency capital escrow account. The wire must state a qualified FDI purpose code and be supported by the investment declaration, corporate flowchain pack, and purpose-of-remittance explanation. The receiving domestic bank will credit the foreign-currency deposit, issue a capital verification certificate, and permit conversion into KRW only after the entity registration and share issuance are completed. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File and related disclosure; Master File and Country-by-Country Report are triggered for multinational groups with consolidated global revenue of at least KRW 1,000,000,000,000

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime like the UAE; however, the National Tax Service applies the OECD core income-generating activities analysis in determining treaty entitlement, beneficial ownership, permanent establishment presence, and anti-abuse recharacterization; a Korean LLC lacking real office, employees, business decisions, and operational control over auto-parts e-commerce activities may be denied DTA benefits as a mere shell or conduit, with the NTS recharacterizing the transactions under the substance-over-form principle and the Framework Act on International Tax Adjustment

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act imposes imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine of up to 5 times the evaded tax; the Framework Act on National Taxes imposes a 40% fraudulent understatement penalty plus a 0.022% daily late-payment surcharge on the unpaid corporate tax; the NTS may simultaneously assess additional corporate tax, local income tax, value-added tax, and all related penalties, and may publish the taxpayer and director as habitual tax evaders

Director personal liability: Under the National Tax Collection Act, a director who willfully or grossly negligently fails to collect or pay corporate taxes is personally and jointly liable to satisfy the unpaid corporate tax from personal assets; the director may face overseas travel restrictions and immigration blacklisting for tax arrears exceeding KRW 500 million, and if the evasion scheme is proved, imprisonment of up to 5 years through criminal prosecution
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The Framework Act on International Tax Adjustment authorizes the NTS to deny tax treaty benefits where the principal purpose of an arrangement is treaty shopping; the NTS may recharacterize the Korean LLC as a direct taxable presence of the foreign parent, disallow the reduced treaty rate, impose the full 22% withholding together with a 40% understatement penalty and interest, and apply domestic anti-abuse rules under the general substance-over-form doctrine

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false beneficial ownership certificates or tax residency statements in support of treaty shopping are exposed to joint personal liability for the shortfall, plus monetary penalties; criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may result in imprisonment of up to 5 years; a director who is also the architect of the shell chain may face civil damages, asset seizure, and travel blacklisting pending tax collection
AML / Unreported Foreign Financial Accounts and Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Act on Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Information and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, failure to report foreign financial accounts may result in penalties of up to 20% of the unreported balance for severe infractions, criminal fines, and imprisonment of up to 3 years; the unreported funds are also subject to full Korean corporate income tax, value-added tax, 40% fraudulent understatement penalty, and daily delinquency charges; financial institutions are obligated to report suspicious transactions, and Stripe/PayPal merchant records are routinely demanded through tax information exchange requests

Director personal liability: A director who controls, benefits from, or orders the concealment of foreign payment accounts is personally liable for the civil restitution of unpaid taxes and penalties; the NTS and prosecution may request departure prohibitions, freeze director and shareholder assets, and obtain extradition in international mutual legal assistance; upon conviction, the director faces imprisonment of up to 3 years for AML non-compliance and up to 5 years for tax evasion, with the corporate veil pierced to the extent of personal benefit received from the concealed payment flows

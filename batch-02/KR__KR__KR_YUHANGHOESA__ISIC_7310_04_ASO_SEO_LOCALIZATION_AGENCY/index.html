For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) in South Korea, the statutory procedure to obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number is as follows: (1) Prepare the required documents, including the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, passport copies of the representative director, and a power of attorney if applicable; (2) Submit the application for business registration (사업자등록신청서) to the local district tax office (세무서) having jurisdiction over the entity's registered address, either in person or via the HomeTax online portal; (3) Upon approval, the NTS issues a Business Registration Certificate (사업자등록증) containing the 10-digit TIN, which also serves as the VAT registration number; (4) The entity must also register for the electronic tax invoice system (e-Tax Invoice) and, if applicable, for the cash receipt system; (5) The entire process typically takes 3-5 business days, and no minimum capital is required.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a legally floating but administratively anchored convertibility regime. The current account is highly convertible for services, including app store optimization, digital marketing, localization fees, and other intangible trade activities. Such payments are freely made through designated foreign exchange banks provided the underlying transaction is genuine and the proper purpose code is declared in the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). The capital account is also substantially liberalized for inward direct investment; a non-resident shareholder may inject capital into a Korean Yuhan Hoesa without prior BOK approval, but must complete the requisite investment declaration and report through a domestic bank to obtain a certificate of foreign exchange banking. Profit remittance is permitted through dividend distributions, yet the outflow must be supported by audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, board resolutions, and a corresponding FEIS report; the remitting bank will screen for underlying economic substance and anti-money laundering risks. While there are no blanket surrender requirements for export receipts, all export and service proceeds must be repatriated within the statutory settlement window and are subject to transaction-level monitoring. The Korean won is not a fully freely convertible offshore currency; non-residents cannot freely trade it through unlicensed offshore venues, and onshore FX derivatives are limited to hedging purposes rather than speculative positions. Overall, capital suffices for routine treasury management, but compliance officers must maintain rigorous supporting dossiers for every cross-border wire to avoid automatic rejection or regulatory referral.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Direct fintech EMI service such as Wise Business, Payoneer, or similar neobank rails are not legally permitted to act as standalone bank account providers for a resident Korean corporate entity. Korea requires any entity providing foreign exchange business to be authorized as a foreign exchange bank or hold a limited payment service license with strict regulatory scope; EMIs are generally barred from opening domestic won-denominated operational accounts or foreign currency settlement accounts for a Yuhan Hoesa without partnering with a licensed Korean bank. The practical viability is Low to Medium: Wise and Payoneer may be used only as auxiliary payout channels for outbound contractor or software vendor payments, but the primary corporate treasury and capital verification must happen through a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank. Any attempt to route statutory equity contributions through an offshore EMI will fail the capital verification and bank onboarding process because the source of funds cannot be transparently matched to an FEIS investment declaration. Therefore, the treasury blueprint should treat all fintech EMIs as subordinated disbursement utilities, never as a substitute for the Korean banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity injections must be received by a designated foreign currency account at a licensed Korean bank using SWIFT MT103 with the investor's full legal name, purpose of remittance clearly stated as capital contribution, and the FEIS transaction identification number generated after the investment declaration. The wire narrative should reference the Yuhan Hoesa name, the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reporting reference, and the specific Korean corporate registration number to avoid quarantine. For a Korean LLC with no minimum statutory capital, the shareholder can contribute a practical working capital amount typically between KRW 100 million and KRW 300 million; an amount higher than the operational demonstrator will trigger additional source-of-funds review. After receipt, funds must remain in escrow until the bank issues a capital verification certificate (납입증명서), which is then submitted to the Korean court registry upon company registration; the same certificate is needed for the corporate bank account opening and later tax filings. The receiving bank will route the transaction through the BOK Foreign Exchange Information System, and the status must remain matched with the investment approval form. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Revenue Suppression

Statutory sanction: Under Article 76(1) of the Corporate Tax Act, fraudulent failure to report or underreporting carries a 40% additional tax on the unpaid national tax, plus the 10% local surcharge and 0.025% daily late-payment interest; under Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, willful evasion is punishable by imprisonment up to three years or a fine not exceeding twice the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the representative director caused the concealment or false invoicing; the director becomes jointly and secondarily liable for the entire corporate tax deficiency through the National Tax Collection Act, personal bank accounts and residences can be seized, the director may be placed on a departure-restriction blacklist with a travel ban, and criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to three years with personal fines.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service will recharacterize the arrangement under the substance-over-form doctrine and the integrated international transaction rules, disallow the artificial deductions, reclassify the amounts as constructive dividends, impose a 40% fraudulent underreporting additional tax, and deny the benefit of Korean tax treaties under domestic anti-abuse provisions; treaty relief can be refused even where a Tax Residency Certificate exists.

Director personal liability: A director who signs false accounts for a shell company is exposed to civil asset restitution and personal secondary tax liability; the tax authority can freeze personal assets and impose a departure-restriction travel ban; in aggravated cases, the director is subject to criminal prosecution for tax evasion using a sham entity, with imprisonment risk and joint responsibility for penalties.
Failure to Withhold Tax on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is personally responsible for the uncollected tax under Article 97 of the Corporate Tax Act; Article 76(6) of the Corporate Tax Act imposes an additional tax of 10% of the undeposited withholding amount, plus daily late-payment interest of 0.025% from the due date; the foreign recipient also remains liable for the under-withheld tax through the collection process.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize dividend remittance without lawful withholding are treated as responsible withholding agents and can be assessed personally for the unpaid withholding tax and penalties; repeated willful non-withholding is treated as embezzlement of state tax revenue and can lead to criminal fines, imprisonment, personal asset attachment, and a travel-ban blacklist preventing the director from leaving Korea.

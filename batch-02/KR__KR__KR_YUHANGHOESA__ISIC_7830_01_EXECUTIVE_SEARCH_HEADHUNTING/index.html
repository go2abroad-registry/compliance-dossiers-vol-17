Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korean law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. The members may fix the capital amount in the Articles of Incorporation, but all declared contributions must be paid in full before the incorporation registration is filed. No blocked or escrow account is required beyond the bank-issued capital deposit certificate; the deposited subscription funds are released after the registration is completed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating through district tax offices and the online HomeTax Service at hometax.go.kr; the competent district tax office is the tax office that has jurisdiction over the registered Korean head office of the Yuhan Hoesa. Customs export clearance is administered separately by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system.

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Korean Yuhan Hoesa: (1) complete commercial registration with the Korean Commercial Registration Court; (2) within 20 days of business commencement, file the business registration application with the NTS district tax office using HomeTax or direct filing; (3) submit the articles of incorporation, certificate of commercial registration, foreign shareholder identification or passport and applicable notarized translations, proof of the Korean place of business such as a lease or title document, and the corporate resolution appointing the representative; (4) the NTS will then issue the Business Registration Certificate containing the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number used for corporate income tax, VAT, payroll withholding, and electronic tax invoices; (5) for a foreign non-resident entity that has no domestic Korean establishment but makes taxable supplies in Korea, the foreign entity must separately register as a foreign VAT business operator with the NTS through HomeTax or the competent tax office and, where required by the NTS, appoint a Korean tax representative to receive the tax ID certificate, file VAT returns, and receive official communications; (6) the BRN must be quoted on all tax invoices issued and on all customs declarations where goods are cleared.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a newly incorporated Korean Yuhan Hoesa: (1) complete commercial registration with the Korean Commercial Registration Court; (2) within 20 days of business commencement, file the business registration application with the NTS district tax office using HomeTax or direct filing; (3) submit the articles of incorporation, certificate of commercial registration, foreign shareholder identification or passport and applicable notarized translations, proof of the Korean place of business such as a lease or title document, and the corporate resolution appointing the representative; (4) the NTS will then issue the Business Registration Certificate containing the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number used for corporate income tax, VAT, payroll withholding, and electronic tax invoices; (5) for a foreign non-resident entity that has no domestic Korean establishment but makes taxable supplies in Korea, the foreign entity must separately register as a foreign VAT business operator with the NTS through HomeTax or the competent tax office and, where required by the NTS, appoint a Korean tax representative to receive the tax ID certificate, file VAT returns, and receive official communications; (6) the BRN must be quoted on all tax invoices issued and on all customs declarations where goods are cleared.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa is a separate legal person and is mandatorily classified as a Korean domestic corporation subject to entity-level Korean corporate income tax on its worldwide income. Korea has no check-the-box election, no pass-through LLC election, and no domestic hybrid-entity election. A foreign jurisdiction may classify the Yuhan Hoesa differently for foreign tax purposes, but the NTS will disregard any such foreign classification for Korean tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 - there is no statutory economic turnover threshold. A Korean corporate entity that begins business activities is required to register as a VAT business operator from business commencement regardless of expected revenue. The simplified taxable person scheme is limited to eligible individual proprietors below the statutory turnover ceiling and does not exempt a corporate Yuhan Hoesa from VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, exports of goods and qualifying foreign-currency-earning services are zero-rated. For the Yuhan Hoesa executive-search and headhunting business, zero-rating is available when the service is supplied to a non-resident client and the service is not consumed for the benefit of a Korean domestic business. The zero-rated export treatment must be evidenced by a contract with the overseas client, a fee charged in foreign currency, actual receipt of the fees from outside Korea within the statutory foreign-exchange collection period, and an electronic tax invoice issued under the zero-rated code. If the foreign-invoiced service in fact benefits a Korean operating company or a Korean resident employer, the NTS will recharacterize the supply as domestic and assess 10% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate) issued via the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system - applicable only to goods exports, not to pure service export
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - applicable only to physical goods movement, not applicable to executive-search services
  • Executed executive-search or headhunting services agreement with the non-resident client, identifying the parties, scope, fee, and place of benefit or use of services
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice transmitted through HomeTax, marked as export/zero-rated under the Korean VAT Act
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of the foreign currency consideration
  • Bank statement or SWIFT credit record showing the foreign currency receipt and demonstrating compliance with the statutory 180-day repatriation/collection deadline

Corporate VAT payers are mandatory electronic tax invoice users. The electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) must be issued through the NTS HomeTax system or through an NTS-approved e-invoice service provider integrated with HomeTax APIs. The e-tax invoice data must be transmitted to the NTS by the day after the invoice is issued. Korea does not operate a Latin-American-style real-time pre-issuance fiscal clearance or digital stamping system; anti-fraud integrity is enforced through digital-certificate authentication, mandatory issuance for B2B supplies, automatic cross-matching of purchase-side and sales-side VAT records, and NTS audit powers. For a B2B executive-search and headhunting agency, e-invoicing is the required fiscal path for all domestic supplies, and zero-rated export supplies must also be reported through the VAT return with the export evidence retained.

If export zero-rating is claimed without the required export documentation, foreign-exchange receipt evidence, or customer documentation, the NTS may retrospectively disallow the zero rate, reassess the full 10% VAT on the invoiced amount, and impose statutory additional taxes for underpayment, late payment, and negligent or fraudulent returns. Fraudulent or intentionally unsubstantiated export zero-rating claims can trigger the criminal tax-evasion penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes, including imprisonment and fines up to three times the evaded tax, together with extensions of the statute of limitation up to 10 years for false or fraudulent returns. The taxpayer bears the burden of producing and retaining all export service contracts, electronic tax invoices, foreign exchange receipts, and placement evidence for at least the full statutory retention period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised, largely market-based current account convertibility regime. The won is not freely convertible outside the country; official settlement and client FX conversion must occur through Korean financial institutions. Capital account transactions are generally permitted but require report to the Ministry of Economy and Finance on a post-transaction basis or prior report for foreign direct investment and offshore loans under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account payments, including service fees, royalties and executive-search retainer fees, are freely convertible through foreign exchange banks subject to transaction evidence. There are no pooled surrender obligations; the export proceeds receipt rules impose a collection deadline and route through a domestic foreign-exchange bank, although there is no mandatory conversion ratio. Profit remittance barriers are minimal for a properly documented dividend distribution; withholding tax rates apply according to the relevant tax treaty. For a headhunting export-of-services model, retainers invoiced abroad received in USD may be held in foreign-currency deposit accounts, or converted to KRW for payroll, without prior approval, provided the corresponding underlying contract and invoice documentation are retained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from both goods and services must be collected through a domestic foreign-exchange bank. Under the Foreign Exchange Transaction Regulations, a services exporter such as an executive-search firm must normally obtain collection of foreign currency proceeds within 180 days from the invoice date or completion of services; extensions up to two years are available at the discretion of the designated foreign-exchange bank in cases of force majeure, litigation or demonstrable collection delays. There is no mandatory conversion ratio in the sense of a surrender requirement to the Bank of Korea; the statutory ratio is effectively 0% of export proceeds required to be converted to won. Exporters may retain the foreign currency in a designated foreign-currency deposit account or convert to KRW based on operational treasury needs. Evidence such as the underlying export contract, service completion report, invoice, and receivable ledger must be maintained and presented to the handling bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the primary foreign-exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA); the Bank of Korea acts as the central monetary supervisory authority and oversees the reporting and clearing infrastructure for the won and foreign-currency accounts held through domestic foreign-exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low and legally restricted. Wise B2B and other offshore digital EMIs are not licensed as foreign-exchange banks under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and cannot act as the primary settlement rail for export service proceeds. Under the statutory framework, all export proceeds from a Korean LLC must be received through a Korean foreign-exchange bank, and corporate operating accounts for a resident Korean entity must be settled at a licensed domestic bank. A foreign EMI can only function as a non-bank payment initiation interface for low-risk discretionary disbursements, while the actual clearing and settlement still runs through a Korean-licensed bank. Relying on a foreign EMI as the exclusive cross-border treasury rail would create an FX booking gap, violate the export-proceeds receipt discipline, and likely lead to a bank compliance rejection at the account-opening stage.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All foreign capital injections into the Korean LLC must be routed through a designated foreign-exchange bank. Before remittance, the foreign investor must complete a Foreign Investor Registration with the handling bank or Kotra. The remittance itself must be a SWIFT MT103 marked with the correct Bank of Korea transaction purpose code for foreign direct investment, identified as share subscription or capital increase. Upon receipt, the bank issues a foreign-investment deposit confirmation and the investor must file a Foreign Investment Report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, normally within 30 days. For capital verification, the bank provides a Capital Deposit Certificate for the corporate registry; the LLC then completes the paid-in-capital registration with the Seoul District Court. If the funds will be converted to KRW, the conversion must be executed through the same handling bank on a spot basis with proper trade-evidence retention. Parent-company loans above the regulatory threshold must be reported to the Ministry of Economy and Finance through the handling bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act using the relevant external borrowing purpose code.For external service payments such as subcontractor placement fees, software subscriptions, marketing purchases or repayment of shareholder loans, the LLC instructs its bank to execute SWIFT MT103 transfers with the full invoice number, contract reference, and tax certificate attached. Comfort letters or documentary evidence for each beneficiary relationship must be pre-filed with the bank. Traditional Commercial Letters of Credit and Documentary Collections are not typical for executive-search headhunting, but they remain available to the LLC if it engages in international procurement of technology assets, office equipment purchases, or trade-related transactions with Korean or offshore vendors. Domestic payroll, social security, pension and tax payments are cleared through the local Korean banking and Hometax systems, not through SWIFT. The LLC should maintain monthly reconciliation between cross-border wires and the corresponding export/service invoices to avoid a pattern of broken payments.Onshore deliverable forward foreign-exchange contracts against real current-account exposure, specifically USD/KRW forwards with maturity aligned to the invoicing and settlement cycle of the headhunting retainers, supported by the underlying service contracts and receivables schedule.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) entered into with a licensed Korean bank for hedging the KRW/USD exposure of a non-resident parent or foreign institutional borrower, only when the underlying transaction is a Korean foreign-investment asset valuation hedge to protect the capital account, and only through the handling bank as an acceptable substitute for a deliverable forward when offshore settlement is contractually required.Cross-currency swaps and buy/sell currency swaps permitted strictly for asset-liability management on foreign-currency loans or capital account items, for example swapping USD principal borrowed from a foreign parent into KRW for the LLC's operating needs, provided the swap is booked with a licensed Korean bank and the maturity does not exceed the underlying debt maturity.Forwards and options are not permitted for speculative uncovered positions; all hedging must be documented with a clear treasury and risk-policy memorandum signed by the statutory director and filed with the bank's FX department.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low and legally restricted. Wise B2B and other offshore digital EMIs are not licensed as foreign-exchange banks under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and cannot act as the primary settlement rail for export service proceeds. Under the statutory framework, all export proceeds from a Korean LLC must be received through a Korean foreign-exchange bank, and corporate operating accounts for a resident Korean entity must be settled at a licensed domestic bank. A foreign EMI can only function as a non-bank payment initiation interface for low-risk discretionary disbursements, while the actual clearing and settlement still runs through a Korean-licensed bank. Relying on a foreign EMI as the exclusive cross-border treasury rail would create an FX booking gap, violate the export-proceeds receipt discipline, and likely lead to a bank compliance rejection at the account-opening stage.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Local File is generally required when annual revenue is at least KRW 50 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; Master File is generally required when annual revenue is at least KRW 100 billion and related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion; any executive-search LLC with foreign related-party transactions should maintain a contemporaneous functional analysis, benchmark study, and written transfer-pricing policy from the first transaction even below these thresholds to substantiate arm's-length pricing

Economic substance scope: Korea applies substance-over-form and beneficial-ownership tests derived from the International Tax Coordination Act and OECD BEPS. Economic substance requires core income-generating activities to be performed in Korea through local staff, management, and offices; for an executive-search agency, CIGA include candidate sourcing, evaluation and vetting, client negotiation, fee setting, and contract execution. A Korean LLC acting only as a pass-through invoice shell for a foreign headhunting network will be disregarded, treaty benefits denied, and profits reallocated to the foreign principal.

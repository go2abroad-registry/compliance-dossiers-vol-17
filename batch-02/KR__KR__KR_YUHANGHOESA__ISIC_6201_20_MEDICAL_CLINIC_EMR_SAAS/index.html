Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum capital and no blocked escrow account are prescribed for a Yuhan Hoesa. Zero paid-up capital is permissible; however, if capital is paid, the full subscribed amount must be deposited into a bank subscription account or paid before the commercial registration application is submitted. The deposited capital is released to the Company after the Commercial Registration Certificate is issued. For commercial credibility and banking due diligence, a voluntary paid-up capital of at least KRW 1,000,000 is recommended, but this is not a government mandate.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no civil defense fire permit or structural load clearance is required for virtual software operations. If a future leased office is established, the lessor must ensure building compliance with the Framework Act on Fire-Fighting Services and the Building Act, and the Company must obtain an annual fire and electrical safety inspection certificate from the local fire station, together with a workplace safety statement under the Occupational Safety and Health Act applicable to ordinary office work.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea through the HomeTax System (www.hometax.go.kr); for customs-related export declarations, the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Korean LLC (Yuhan Hoesa), upon registration under the Commercial Act, the company must, within 20 days of commencing a business, file a 'Business Registration Application' (and, for VAT, a 'Tax Invoice Registration Application') with the National Tax Service's local district office, online via HomeTax. The NTS will assign a 10-digit Business Owner Registration Number (TIN) which also serves as the VAT registration and electronic tax invoice number. For a foreign non-resident enterprise (not a domestic Korean entity) supplying goods or services in Korea, the enterprise must appoint a tax representative in Korea and submit a 'Non-Resident Business Operator Registration Application' to the NTS via HomeTax, attaching the foreign entity's incorporation certificate, a tax representative power of attorney, and evidence of a place of business or a transaction creating future VAT liability; the NTS then issues a foreign business operator registration number, also to be used for VAT and e-tax invoice mandates.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No zero-economic-turnover threshold. All persons (domestic entities and certain foreign non-resident enterprises) who supply goods or services in Korea, whether for profit or not, are statutorily required to register for VAT within 20 days after the business commencement date. A small-business (Sujeopja) with annual taxable supply turnover below KRW 84 million may elect simplified taxation (Korean: 간이과세), but remains registered and receives a Business Owner Registration Number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under VAT Act Article 21 and its Enforcement Decree, the supply of exported goods is zero-rated when the goods are actually exported and leave Korea with a customs export declaration. Services qualify for zero-rating if they are 'exports of services to a foreign customer' – meaning the counterparty is a non-resident with no place of business in Korea, the payment is received in convertible foreign currency, and the service is actually used, consumed, or enjoyed outside Korea. For digital and cloud-based electronic medical record (EMR) SaaS, zero-rating applies where the overseas client accesses the software remotely, the contract merely provides an intangible service executed abroad, no Korean-resident employee or domestic-based personnel performs events in Korea to supply the service, and the subscription fee is settled in foreign currency abroad; the service cannot be zero-rated if the server or digital infrastructure is placed at the disposal of a Korean resident for use within Korea.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Korean Customs Service 'Export Declaration Certificate' / 'Bill of Entry') issued via UNI-PASS, including HS code and FOB value
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Airway Bill (air) evidencing actual movement of goods to a foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (with description of exported goods and purchaser details)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Certificate of Acquisition of Foreign Exchange (외국환 매입확인증) issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank evidencing that the export proceeds have been actually repatriated to Korea in convertible foreign currency
  • Standard Service Contract / Work Order / Digital Order Form identifying the overseas counterparty, detailed scope of the EMR SaaS subscription, and the foreign-currency billing terms (for cross-border digital services)

Electronic Tax Invoice (e-Tax Invoice) mandate under VAT Act Article 32, as amended: all registered taxpayers must issue and transmit via HomeTax an electronic tax invoice through the national e-Document network for every supply of goods or services (B2B and B2C on request) by the 15th day of the month following the supply month, or within 1 day after the date of payment depending on invoicing date rules; non-electronic invoices are only permitted for cash-based micromerchants under simplified taxation. The NTS operates a real-time matching and cross-referencing system with Korean Customs Service import/export data, corporate bank account data, card acquiring data, and Cash Receipt System data. Foreign e-commerce suppliers must also transmit a simplified e-Tax invoice through HomeTax. There is no separate real-time fiscal clearance/stamping API; instead, the HomeTax system automatically reconciles issued and received e-tax invoices, and large enterprises (annual revenue over KRW 100 billion) are subject to mandatory intra-group aggregate reporting and enhanced NTS analytical validation.

NTS routinely audits export zero-rating claims. If the required documentation bundle is incomplete or the zero-rating is found to be unlawful or fraudulent, the NTS will retroactively assess the 10% VAT on the entire export supply, disallow any related VAT refund, and impose additional penalties under VAT Act Article 60: a negligence penalty of 20% of the unpaid or erroneously refunded tax amount, or a fraud penalty of 40% if the zero-rating was based on intentional misrepresentation, forged evidence, or fabricated export documentation. In addition, where a false e-tax invoice or false export declaration is filed, the NTS may impose an administrative penalty up to KRW 100 million (or 3% of the supply value for certain invoice violations) and may refer the matter to the Korea Customs Service or the Public Prosecutor for criminal liability for customs and tax evasion, possibly including imprisonment up to 5 years or fines up to five times the assessed tax amount.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a highly liberalised but strictly monitored current account system. Software exports, EMR service fees, licensing royalties, and dividend remittances from a Korean limited liability company are generally free from exchange control approval, provided the transaction is reported to a designated foreign exchange bank through the Foreign Exchange Information System. Capital account transactions, notably non-resident equity injections, acquisition of a Korean entity, cross-border loans, and investments in local banking products, require prior reporting or approval depending on underlying amounts and counterparty substance. The Korean won is fully convertible for trade and service payments, but settlement must flow through licensed domestic banks. There is currently no general statutory requirement to surrender a fixed percentage of export proceeds; however, all export receipts must be collected by an onshore foreign exchange bank, reported under the relevant purpose-of-payment code, and any conversion into Korean won must be executed at market rates with proper tax identification. Profit remittance from the Korean LLC is permissible after the company files audited financial statements, obtains a tax clearance certificate, and settles final withholding tax on dividends. During a severe balance-of-payments crisis, the Minister of Economy and Finance retains legal powers to impose temporary surrender or conversion directives, but such measures are not in force today.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act regulations, export proceeds from software and EMR licensing services must be collected within 180 days of the invoice date or service completion date and repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange bank. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio under the current free-floating regime; exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in a foreign currency account only if the bank classifies the receipt as a lawful export collection. The minimum requirement is that 100% of the export value must be reported via the Foreign Exchange Information System using the relevant software export code, and any KRW conversion needed for settlement of wages, VAT, corporate tax, or operating costs must be made through the same bank. Non-performance of the 180-day collection rule triggers a fine and a compliance review unless the exporter obtains an extension from the bank or the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. South Korean regulatory practice requires corporate settlement accounts and all KRW clearing transactions to be conducted through a licensed bank under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Foreign digital EMI institutions, such as offshore neobanks or non-bank payment providers, are not permitted to open a primary operating account for a Korean-domiciled LLC or execute KRW transfers on behalf of the entity without a licensed domestic bank partner. A medical EMR SaaS business handling clinic subscription fees is better served by a Tier-1 domestic bank in order to comply with Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reporting, PIPA data protection expectations, and bank underwriting standards. An offshore EMI could be used only for non-KRW expense payments outside Korea, but it cannot lawfully handle the local medical receivables or capital escrow balances.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% national corporate income tax plus a 10% statutory local income tax surcharge, producing a combined headline statutory rate of 24.2% for consolidated taxable income above KRW 20 billion; lower national brackets of 10% up to KRW 200 million and 20% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion apply before adding the local surcharge

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% on outbound dividends paid by a Korean corporation to a non-resident shareholder, composed of 20% national income tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surcharge; a tax treaty may reduce the rate to 5%, 10%, or 15% depending on the recipient's shareholding percentage and treaty partner

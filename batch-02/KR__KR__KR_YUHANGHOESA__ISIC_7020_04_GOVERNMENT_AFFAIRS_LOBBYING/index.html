Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is strongly disfavored and generally rejected by Korean tax authorities and financial institutions for an active government-affairs lobbying and advisory company. Although the Korean Commercial Act does not explicitly prohibit a virtual registered address, the National Tax Service requires a fixed place of business for business registration, and banks conducting customer due diligence will expect a physical office where the directors and employees actually work, where books and records are maintained, and where regulatory inspections can be conducted. For a lobbying entity with government-audience engagement, a virtual office creates a high risk of being classified as a sham entity, triggering administrative sanctions, tax reassessment, criminal penalties for violation of the Act on External Registration of Names, or denial of corporate bank account opening. Therefore, a leased physical office in Seoul or another business district is operationally mandatory to demonstrate genuine commercial substance.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Yuhan Hoesa under the amended Commercial Act; the amount of contribution is fixed by the Articles of Incorporation. No blocked escrow account is required by law. Capital contributions must be deposited into a Korean bank account opened by or on behalf of the incorporators, and a deposit certificate or balance confirmation from the bank must accompany the registration application. The initial contributed capital may be as low as one Korean Won, but practical bank account opening and legal formality costs require a nominal working capital amount.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable at the company license level for a pure office-based consulting and lobbying entity. The building owner must maintain a completed fire protection inspection and civil defense fire safety approval under the Framework Act on Fire-Fighting Services, and the lessee must pass an ordinary workplace safety review under the Occupational Safety and Health Act. For offices with fewer than the statutory threshold of workers and with no hazardous equipment, no separate KOSHA registration or civil defense fire permit is required; the company should retain the landlord fire safety certificate and conduct a basic ergonomic and electrical safety checklist for compliance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS); electronic filing portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr). Export clearances and customs declarations are administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) via UNI-PASS (https://unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine Korean tax presence. If the foreign non-resident entity has a domestic place of business in Korea, it is treated as a foreign corporation with a Korean business establishment and must file a business registration application with the district tax office having jurisdiction. Step 2: Appoint a Korean tax representative who is jointly liable for tax filing and payment, or an authorized agent through a valid power of attorney. Step 3: File the Foreign Business Registration application with the responsible district tax office or via HomeTax, attaching the foreign certificate of incorporation (translated and, where required, apostilled), articles of association, proof of the Korean place of business such as a lease agreement, the tax representatives identification, and the power of attorney. Step 4: The NTS reviews the application and issues the statutory 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as the entity TIN and VAT registration number under the Value-Added Tax Act. None for U.S.-style check-the-box or elective pass-through classification. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily taxed as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Income Tax Act on its worldwide income. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - no general turnover-based VAT registration threshold; every business operator, including a foreign non-resident operator supplying VAT-liable services in Korea, must register within 20 days of commencement of a taxable business. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, goods are zero-rated when physically exported from Korea under a customs export declaration, and services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident or foreign place of business, used or consumed outside Korea, and paid for from abroad with foreign currency. For the Yuhan Hoesa cross-border government-affairs advisory and lobbying-consulting services, the services must be supplied to a non-resident beneficiary, the actual performance and benefit must occur outside Korea, and the consideration must be received through the Korean foreign exchange banking system. Qualifying offshore digital services, such as online policy research, digital advisory deliverables, or electronic data-analytics reports, may also be zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient and the place of use is demonstrably outside Korea; the taxpayer must retain evidence of the recipient's non-resident status, foreign place of use, and foreign-currency inward remittance.

  • Validated Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate) issued by the Korea Customs Service showing the exporter, consignee, HS code, quantity, and FOB value
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List signed by the exporter
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight, Air Waybill for air freight, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical departure from Korea
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice issued under the Value-Added Tax Act referencing the 0% VAT rate and the statutory zero-rating provision
  • Foreign Currency Receipt Certificate or Foreign Exchange Remittance Confirmation issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank proving collection of export proceeds
  • For service exports: written advisory contract, service delivery report, and documentation demonstrating the recipient is a non-resident and the place of use or benefit is outside Korea

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory under the Value-Added Tax Act; all VAT-registered business operators must issue tax invoices, and businesses above the statutory e-tax invoice threshold, or those requested by a counterparty, must issue electronic tax invoices through HomeTax or an approved EDI/API service provider. E-tax invoice details are transmitted to the NTS at issuance, after which the NTS assigns a national e-tax invoice serial number and automatically cross-matches the data against VAT returns, customs declarations, and corporate income tax records. Korea does not use a physical fiscal stamp or digital stamp device; anti-fraud integrity is achieved through near-real-time e-invoice clearance, mandatory electronic transmission, third-party data matching with the Korea Customs Service and foreign exchange banks, and penalties for failure to transmit or for issuing false e-tax invoices.

The Korea Customs Service and the National Tax Service jointly audit zero-rated export claims by cross-matching customs export declarations, e-tax invoice transmissions, and foreign exchange bank remittance records. If the required documentation is missing, inconsistent, or untimely, or if export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day deadline (or the approved extended deadline), the zero-rating is revoked retroactively and the NTS assesses output VAT at 10% on the gross export consideration for the relevant VAT return period. Additional penalty taxes are imposed for late or non-filed returns, understated taxable amounts, and failure to issue a valid zero-rated e-tax invoice. In cases of fraudulent false tax invoices, false export declarations, or fraudulent foreign-exchange documentation, criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Value-Added Tax Act apply, including imprisonment and fines, and the NTS may pursue retrospective clawbacks for all periods within the statutory tax assessment window.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but supervision-heavy foreign exchange regime. The Korean won is fully convertible on the current account for genuine trade, service fees, and profit remittances after tax clearance, while capital account transactions are generally permissible if reported to a foreign exchange bank or the Bank of Korea. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds; exporters may retain earnings in Foreign Currency Accounts. However, certain restitution and reporting obligations apply: equity injections require advance filing under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and inbound remittances above USD 50,000 must pass through an authorized foreign exchange bank with stated purpose codes. Dividends and branch profits may be repatriated after audited financial statements and withholding tax settlement, subject to the foreign investor registration certificate. Non-resident shareholders engaging in government affairs or lobbying activities will attract enhanced scrutiny because the underlying business is politically sensitive. Practical convertibility risk arises at the clearing level: KRW is an onshore currency with no offshore window, so any offshore counterparty must convert through an authorized dealer. The Bank of Korea monitors large or unusual transactions and may request underlying contract evidence before an exchange bank releases settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current Korean foreign exchange regulations, exporters are not required to convert foreign currency export proceeds into KRW; the statutory surrender ratio is 0%. However, the exporter must obtain receipt of the foreign exchange within the contractually agreed settlement date and, where the period is not agreed, within one year of the export declaration date for goods or service completion date for services. Any delayed receipts must be reported to the designated foreign exchange bank through a Delayed Receipt of Export Proceeds report, and amounts exceeding USD 50,000 per transaction need full supporting documentation such as bill of lading, commercial invoice, service contract, or a retained advisory agreement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and Korea Customs Service under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and associated Regulations on Foreign Exchange Transactions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability for any offshore digital EMI acting as the primary treasury or clearing rail. Under the Banking Act and Electronic Financial Transactions Act, only institutions licensed by the Financial Services Commission and the Financial Supervisory Service may receive deposits and provide foreign exchange services in the Korean jurisdiction. Foreign EMIs such as Revolut or Wise are not licensed as Korean banks, cannot issue local IBANs denominated in KRW, and cannot provide the required Foreign Investment Promotion Act reporting or capital verification certificates. Such EMIs may be used only as a pre-funded supplementary payment gateway after a compliant bank account is established, never as the main account for inbound equity, outbound trade, or FX hedging.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound equity, the foreign investor must first submit a foreign investment report through the chosen foreign exchange bank or KOTRA before the remittance is transmitted. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability for any offshore digital EMI acting as the primary treasury or clearing rail. Under the Banking Act and Electronic Financial Transactions Act, only institutions licensed by the Financial Services Commission and the Financial Supervisory Service may receive deposits and provide foreign exchange services in the Korean jurisdiction. Foreign EMIs such as Revolut or Wise are not licensed as Korean banks, cannot issue local IBANs denominated in KRW, and cannot provide the required Foreign Investment Promotion Act reporting or capital verification certificates. Such EMIs may be used only as a pre-funded supplementary payment gateway after a compliant bank account is established, never as the main account for inbound equity, outbound trade, or FX hedging.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% on taxable income up to KRW 200,000,000; 19% on taxable income between KRW 200,000,001 and KRW 20,000,000,000; 21% on taxable income exceeding KRW 20,000,000,000; plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national corporate income tax, producing effective marginal rates of approximately 9.9%, 20.9% and 23.1%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax on outbound dividends paid to non-resident shareholders under Article 98 of the Corporate Income Tax Act, plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national withholding tax, resulting in an effective aggregate withholding rate of 22%, unless reduced by an applicable bilateral Double Tax Agreement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50,000,000,000 of aggregate cross-border transactions with foreign related parties in the fiscal year triggers compulsory Master File and Local File preparation and filing with the annual corporate income tax return; MNE groups with prior-year consolidated revenue of KRW 100,000,000,000 or more are also required to file a CbC report notification. Economic substance scope: South Korea does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime in the form of certain Middle Eastern offshore jurisdictions, but the substance-over-form doctrine is codified through the place of effective management test, general anti-avoidance rules, controlled foreign company rules and treaty limitation-on-benefits clauses. For a Yuhan Hoesa engaged in government affairs and lobbying, the National Tax Service will examine whether the Core Income-Generating Activities are performed by actual employees in Korea, whether the entity controls its bank accounts, maintains a physical office, pays Korean employment taxes, and makes independent business decisions; a merely immigrant-taxed shell without real economic presence will be denied DTA benefits and may be recharacterized as a permanent establishment of the foreign parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act imposes imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to 3 times the evaded tax; under the Corporate Income Tax Act, unlawful omission triggers an additional tax of 40% of the unreported amount, failure to file triggers 20% of the unpaid tax, and under-declaration triggers 10%; the National Tax Service may immediately assess the tax, seize bank accounts and cancel business registrations.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the director transfers corporate assets into personal accounts, uses the LLC as a mere fraud instrument, or is the de facto controlling person: the director may be held secondarily liable under the National Tax Collection Act, may have personal assets attached, may be placed on the NTS customs/immigration blacklist with a travel ban, and may face imprisonment up to 5 years; civil restitution is ordered for the full amount of evaded tax plus interest.
Money Laundering, Foreign Exchange Violation and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act penalizes unregistered foreign exchange brokerage and undeclared inbound payments with fines up to KRW 50,000,000 and imprisonment; the Financial Transaction Reports Act requires suspicious transactions above KRW 10,000,000 to be reported, and failure can lead to criminal penalties and asset freezing orders; the Anti-Money Laundering authorities may issue a freezing order on the corporate bank account and revoke the entity's foreign direct investment registration.

Director personal liability: A director who personally directs the shell arrangement is civilly liable for fraudulent conveyance and restitution of all illicit flows; personal bank accounts are subject to freeze orders, the director is placed on international travel blacklists, and criminal imprisonment up to 3 years applies under the AML framework; if corporate assets are insufficient, the court may attach the director's personal property under the alter ego doctrine recognized in Korean civil execution practice.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Fatal DTA Treaty-Shopping Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Adjustment Act, the National Tax Service may make a transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length amount, impose a 30% international tax adjustment penalty on the adjusted understated tax base, disallow all treaty benefits under the anti-avoidance and principal purpose provisions, and calculate delayed-payment interest at the statutory rate; the Corporate Income Tax Act additionally allows correction assessments with additional taxes of 20-40% for false or unjust returns.

Director personal liability: The director who signs false transfer pricing schedules and DTA certificates may be personally assessed for penalties where the limited liability company is used as a mere tool for tax avoidance; the director faces secondary tax collection, asset seizure, blacklisting by customs, immigration and public procurement agencies, travel restrictions, and criminal prosecution for producing false official documents, with imprisonment up to 5 years if the transfer pricing adjustment is accompanied by fabricated invoices, back-dated agreements or inflated royalty payments.

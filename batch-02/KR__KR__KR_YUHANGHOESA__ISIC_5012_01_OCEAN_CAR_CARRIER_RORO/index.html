A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not wholly prohibited by the Korean Commercial Act for registration purposes, but it is generally rejected by the National Tax Service, customs authorities, and corporate banks for a Deep-Sea Ro-Ro vehicle transport enterprise. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal Home Tax Service (Hometax) at https://www.hometax.go.kr. Export declarations and customs clearance are processed through the Korea Customs Service (KCS) electronic UNI-PASS system at https://unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

For a Korean Yuhan Hoesa, the unified TIN and VAT registration number is the Business Registration Number issued under the Framework Act on National Taxes. The company, through its legal representative or local tax agent, must file an Application for Business Registration with the competent district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered head office or domestic place of business, generally within 20 days from business commencement. Required submission documents include the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, register of directors, representative identification documents, proof of the domestic place of business such as a lease contract or ownership certificate, and for a foreign non-resident shareholder, a certificate of foreign incorporation and the foreign entities tax identification number. If a foreign non-resident entity has no domestic place of business but makes taxable supplies in Korea, it must appoint a Korean tax representative and register under the statutory simplified VAT registration procedures for foreign businesses before the taxable supply occurs. Upon successful review, the NTS issues the Business Registration Number, which also serves as the corporate entity TIN and the VAT ID. Amendments to business status, location, or representative must be filed with the NTS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary turnover threshold triggers VAT registration in Korea. VAT registration is mandatory for every business operator, including a Yuhan Hoesa, from the date taxable business activity commences in Korea. The optional simplified taxpayer regime for individual businesses with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000 is not available to a limited liability company; therefore no KRW threshold applies for this entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value Added Tax Act, zero-rated supplies include exported goods and international transportation services by sea or air. A Yuhan Hoesa deep-sea Ro-Ro vehicle carrier qualifies for the 0% VAT rate for the international ocean transport of vehicles, provided the supply is genuinely rendered in connection with international carriage and supported by documentary proof of the foreign supply, vessel movement, cargo carriage, and export or foreign exchange realization where applicable. Qualifying offshore digital services are not relevant to this maritime entity.

  • Korea Customs Service electronic Export Declaration through UNI-PASS, together with the cargo loading report or ship s manifest for vehicles loaded in Korea
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading (or sea waybill) issued by the Ro-Ro carrier showing vessel name, voyage number, VIN-level vehicle details, ports of loading and discharge, and the shipper and consignee
  • Commercial invoice and contract of carriage, charter party, or booking confirmation evidencing the international transport supply
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation or a foreign exchange bank receipt issued in the Republic of Korea evidencing receipt of freight, charter hire, or export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Cargo manifest, port-call certificates, or ship agent certificates substantiating the actual international movement of the vehicles and the Ro-Ro voyage

All corporations and all registered business operators in Korea must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS Home Tax System or through an NTS-approved electronic invoice service provider using API integration. Electronic tax invoice data must be transmitted to the National Tax Service under the statutory VAT filing schedule. Korea does not currently operate a real-time government fiscal clearance or anti-fraud stamping regime; instead, the NTS performs post-issuance electronic validation and cross-matching of buyer and seller VAT returns through the Hometax e-invoice database.

The NTS will scrutinize any zero-rating claim against the VAT return, jury invoices, customs declaration database, vessel movement records, and foreign exchange receipt records. If the required export documentation or foreign exchange realization evidence is missing or unverifiable, the NTS may disallow the zero-rating retroactively, assess 10% VAT on the supply value, claw back any previously refunded input VAT, and impose statutory additional tax penalties for underreporting, unfounded refunds, or failure to preserve supporting evidence. Fraudulent or deliberately false zero-rating claims can also lead to criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment or monetary penalties, together with enhanced civil penalty rates on the suppressed tax base.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a highly liberalised but statutorily monitored foreign exchange system. Current account transactions, including international ocean freight income derived from Ro-Ro vehicle transport and ordinary operating expenses, can generally be executed without pre-approval, subject to mandatory reporting to the Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank and the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). The Korean won is freely convertible on the current account, and there is no requirement to surrender export proceeds into KRW; the entity may retain foreign currency receipts in designated Foreign Exchange Deposit Accounts. Capital account convertibility remains more conditional: equity injections, shareholder loans, and cross-border financial arrangements by foreign residents require declaration or prior approval in specified cases, but ordinary repatriation of dividends and profits by foreign-invested companies is permitted after corporate tax settlement. Profit remittance hurdles typically involve submission of audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, board resolutions authorising the dividend, and the filing of the corresponding foreign exchange transaction report. In practice, the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires all resident-nonresident settlements above prescribed thresholds to pass through a domestic Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank, which makes a local banking relationship indispensable for compliant treasury operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds, including ocean freight earnings from non-resident charterers and cargo interests for Ro-Ro transportation services, must be collected and settled through an Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank within the statutory settlement period, generally 180 days from the date of service completion or the contractual payment date stated in the bill of lading or freight invoice unless a longer period is approved by the bank or the Ministry of Economy and Finance. There is no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio; the company may retain the entire foreign currency export receipt in a Foreign Exchange Deposit Account. All receipts must be reported in FEIS through the authorised bank, and the payment must be supported by freight invoices, charter party agreements, and vessel operational documentation to evidence the underlying current account transaction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (central bank) acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with operational execution through Authorized Foreign Exchange Banks and the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise Business, Revolut, Payoneer, and similar non-bank international money service businesses are not licensed by the Financial Services Commission of Korea as domestic financial institutions and are not Authorized Foreign Exchange Banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Consequently, they cannot lawfully service the treasury settlement and FEIS reporting obligations of a Korean resident corporate entity. They may be used only as auxiliary payment cards or collection wallets outside Korea, but all statutory KRW and foreign currency settle-governance, including freight receipt collection, capital injection, and FX hedging, must be executed through a domestic Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank. Relying on a fintech EMI as the primary clearing rail would create regulatory non-compliance and immediate underwriting rejection at the bank onboarding stage.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital injection must be executed through the Foreign Investment Promotion Act route. The company first files a foreign investment declaration at a designated domestic bank or KOTRA, receives a foreign investment registration acknowledgment, and then the shareholder transmits funds via SWIFT MT103 into a dedicated Foreign Investment Capital Deposit Account at the Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank. The remittance must use the FEIS purpose code designated for foreign direct investment equity subscription and not a general loan or trade purpose code. After the funds are credited and the bank issues a capital entry/verification certificate, a licensed certified public accountant conducts paid-in capital verification based on the corporate registry amendment for the capital increase or initial incorporation. Once verified, the funds may be used for working capital, vessel acquisition payments, charter deposits, and operational expenses; transfer of the balance to a general operating account is recorded as a foreign-exchange-purpose disclosed transaction for FEIS and tax reporting.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax headline rate, producing an effective 26.4% rate after the 10% local income tax surcharge; the progressive national brackets are 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% up to KRW 20 billion, 21% up to KRW 300 billion, and 24% above KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% corporate income tax withholding plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the national tax withheld, making the effective statutory withholding rate 22% on gross dividends paid by the Korean Yuhan Hoesa to a non-resident shareholder without a Korean permanent establishment; treaty rates of 5% or 10% may apply depending on the DTA and the ownership threshold.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File is mandatory when annual cross-border related-party transactions reach KRW 100 billion; Local File is mandatory when the same transactions reach KRW 50 billion; and additional documentation is required when transactions with a related party resident in a tax haven reach KRW 30 billion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but it applies substance-over-form doctrine through the GAAR in Article 17 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and through the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs; for a deep-sea RoRo vehicle carrier, Core Income-Generating Activities include vessel ownership or chartering, flag registration, commercial management, cargo booking, crewing, bunkering, and maintenance decisions, and a Korean Yuhan Hoesa claiming tonnage tax or treaty benefits must demonstrate real management and operations in Korea rather than acting as a shell or nominee for non-resident principals.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Article 9(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act authorizes imprisonment up to three years and a fine up to ten times the amount of evaded tax; the Framework Act on National Taxes applies a 40% fraudulent understatement penalty and a 20% negligent understatement penalty, plus daily delinquency surcharges until the amount is paid.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced when the Yuhan Hoesa is used as a sham vehicle or a mere instrumentality, making directors jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties, and civil restitution; the NTS may blacklist the directors, impose travel bans, freeze personal assets, and criminal conviction will result in imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs permits the NTS to re-determine the arm's-length price, adjust taxable income, and impose penalty taxes together with non-payment surcharges; the Framework Act on National Taxes further imposes up to 20% understatement penalties, or 40% if the TP misstatement is fraudulent, and denial of the deduction for the excessive portion of the controlled payment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false transfer pricing documentation are treated as active participants in the offshore tax scheme; they can be held personally liable for the additional corporate tax and penalties, subjected to NTS blacklisting and travel restrictions, and prosecuted criminally for falsification of tax documents and attempted tax evasion.
Shell Company, Economic Substance Deficit, and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The GAAR under Article 17 of the Framework Act on National Taxes allows the tax authorities to disregard the sham entity and attribute the income to the true controlling person; the Principal Purpose Test under Korea's tax treaties and the OECD Multilateral Instrument denies treaty relief; the omitted or mischaracterized income is subject to the 40% fraud penalty and statutory interest charges.

Director personal liability: Courts and tax authorities may pierce the corporate veil and impose joint and several liability on the directors and shadow directors who exercised actual control; remedies include civil restitution of diverted corporate assets, personal bank account seizure, NTS blacklisting, travel ban, and a term of imprisonment for criminal tax evasion arising from the sham structure.

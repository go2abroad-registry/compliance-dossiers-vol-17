Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None: Korean law does not impose a minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. Nevertheless, the total declared capital in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid in or fully covered by non-cash contributions before registration. For cash contributions, the funds must be deposited into a Korean bank account and evidenced by a certificate of capital payment issued by the bank. Korean law does not require a separate blocked escrow account or court-supervised escrow deposit for Yuhan Hoesa formation.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for industrial or civil-defense fire permits; a standard commercial office must nevertheless satisfy basic fire safety requirements under the Korean Fire Service Act, including maintenance of fire extinguishers, unobstructed exits and an evacuation plan, and must comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act for office-based software employees. No structural load certificate, civil-defense clearance, or special factory fire-safety certification is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic tax portal at hometax.go.kr, together with the Korea Customs Service for customs clearance through UNI-PASS at unipass.customs.go.kr

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity engaged in Korean-source taxable supply must obtain a Korean Business Registration Number (BRN) by filing an application with the local district tax office of the National Tax Service (NTS) via HomeTax. The procedure requires (1) completing the business registration application form; (2) submitting the foreign certificate of incorporation or equivalent registry extract; (3) providing the statutory representative or tax representative's power of attorney and identity documentation; (4) declaring the actual place of business in Korea or confirming the absence of a fixed place and appointing a tax representative for VAT compliance; (5) submitting the lease contract or free-use letter for the Korean office where a fixed place exists; and (6) obtaining the 10-digit BRN certificate, which is simultaneously the Korean VAT identification number. For foreign online or digital service providers without a domestic fixed place of business, the NTS requires VAT registration through the foreign non-resident business track of HomeTax, with the same BRN issuance, and the foreign entity must aggregate all Korean web sales and report VAT under the Value-Added Tax Act. Electronic filing is mandatory through HomeTax or an approved electronic tax invoice integration service.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for VAT registration; every business supplying taxable goods or services in Korea must register for VAT regardless of annual turnover. Reduced compliance for simplified taxpayer status applies for an individually owned small business with annual taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, but the simplified taxpayer is still registered and must issue cash receipts; a foreign incorporated PropTech company will not qualify for simplified taxpayer status when operating as a Yuhan Hoesa and must maintain full VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act of the Republic of Korea, a zero rate of VAT applies to (1) export of goods where the goods are removed from Korea under an export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service; (2) services rendered to a foreign client in connection with exported tangible goods; and (3) digital services or electronically supplied services provided by a Korean VAT-registered business to a non-resident overseas recipient for use wholly outside Korea, provided the contract, place-of-supply rules, and business reality demonstrate that the benefit is consumed outside Korea. For PropTech SaaS subscriptions sold to foreign real estate landlords or overseas platform users, zero-rating is available only when the supply is not regarded as a domestic supply under the statutory place-of-supply rules and the exporter retains full documentation proving both export and foreign-exchange recovery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export bill of entry / export declaration certificate) accepted by the Korea Customs Service under the Customs Act
  • Bill of Lading for marine freight or Air Waybill for air freight showing the Korean shipper and foreign ultimate consignee
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the exported digital service or the physical media/goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign exchange purchase or collection certificate) issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank or receipt certificate under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act demonstrating repatriation of export proceeds
  • Service contract or order agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and confirming the offshore place of supply for digitally supplied SaaS services
  • Bank statement or SWIFT/telegraphic transfer advisory matching the invoice value and remittance period

Korea operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-invoice) system under Articles 32 and 32-2 of the Value-Added Tax Act. Registered VAT businesses must issue e-invoices through HomeTax, an approved electronic tax invoice service provider, or direct EDI linkage, and transmit the invoice data to the NTS under the statutory electronic reporting timeline. All tax invoices for supplies subject to VAT must include the BRN, the recipient's BRN, supply value, VAT amount, and supply date. The system includes near-real-time digital validation and anti-abuse checks; retail supplies to final consumers are supported by a mandatory cash receipt and credit card receipt system that feeds transaction data to the NTS, substantially reducing VAT leakage. No physical digital stamp is required for ordinary B2B SaaS invoices, but e-invoice records must remain auditable and transmitted through the NTS-approved protocol.

The National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service conduct document-based and post-clearance audits to verify export zero-rating claims. If an exporter fails to prove physical export, foreign exchange collection within the statutory 180-day period, or the offshore consumption of digital services, the zero-rated amount is recharacterized as taxable domestic supply and assessed VAT of 10% plus interest and additional tax. Penalties include negligence penalties for false or unsubstantiated tax invoices ranging up to 40% of the supply value, separate aggravated penalties for issuance of fictitious electronic tax invoices, and criminal referral for tax evasion where fraudulent documentation is identified. Foreign exchange non-repatriation is also subject to penalty under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, including the revocation of export acceptance and clawback of tax privileges.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a lightly managed KRW float with full convertibility for current account transactions and ordinary operational service payments, while the capital account remains regulated and transaction-specific. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act permits almost all current account disbursements such as software royalties, SaaS subscription fees, and dividend remittances to be executed upon presentation to a foreign exchange bank of invoice, contract, tax certificate, and board resolution evidence. Capital account inflows such as foreign direct investment (FDI), share subscriptions, trustman type capital contributions or financial borrowings must be declared to KFEIS or, for direct investment exceeding regulatory thresholds, reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA). There is no absolute Ban against profit repatriation: non-resident shareholders may remit dividends once audited retained earnings are confirmed, withholding tax is paid, and the bank receives a dividend distribution resolution plus tax records. However, if accumulated impairment losses exist, dividend payments are prohibited. Export proceeds including SaaS/PropTech subscription receipts must be repatriated to South Korea and either deposited into a domestic foreign currency account or sold for KRW; the Central Bank does not ration dollars, but all settlement receipts must carry permissible transactional purpose codes and be screened against the bank's AML surveillance. Capital inflow to the Korean LLC is treated as FDI and, upon registration and report completion, subsequent repatriation of the invested capital is generally unrestricted. The regime is therefore commercially accessible but documentary compliance and purpose-code integrity are strictly enforced, and Korean banks will not service flows that circumvent or evade these reporting duties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, exporters of SaaS and software services are required to receive export proceeds through a certified foreign exchange bank within 18 months of the invoice date. The statutory surrender duty is satisfied by repatriating 100% of proceeds into a domestic foreign-currency operating account or by selling 100% to the bank for KRW; there is no fractional conversion ratio. If the bank permits an overseas receipt account, that structure must be pre-declared and reported to KFEIS.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange administrative authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, with certified foreign exchange banks delegated execution and reporting powers via the Korea Foreign Exchange Information System (KFEIS)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection should be routed as an SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's bank to the Korean Tier-1 bank with purpose code for direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; prior to wiring, the investor must file a foreign-investment report with a designated foreign exchange bank or the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency. The receiving bank will open a foreign-investment registration account, verify the entity documentation, collect the capital verification certificate after attorney or CPA confirmation, and supply the company with the Approval of Foreign Investment notice. All KRW conversions must be executed onshore at the prevailing spot rate and reported to KFEIS.For service and SaaS export disbursements, use SWIFT MT103 with the appropriate Korean standard currency code for software/IT services and maintain a database of invoices and proof-of-performance. For physical equipment or real-estate-related ancillary hardware purchases, commercial letters of credit and documentary collections may be used, but the primary cross-border trade settlement for PropTech SaaS is direct MT103 to vendors, service providers, and dividend beneficiaries.

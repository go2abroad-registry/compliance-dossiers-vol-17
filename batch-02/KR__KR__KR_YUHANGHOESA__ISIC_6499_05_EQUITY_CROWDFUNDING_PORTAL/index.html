Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 24 of the Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods and services supplied and consumed outside Korea are zero-rated. For an equity crowdfunding portal, qualifying cross-border digital intermediary services provided to non-resident issuers and investors are zero-rated when the place of supply is outside Korea, the service is not used for a domestic establishment, and the Korean supplier receives foreign-currency consideration through foreign exchange repatriation to Korea. Services connected to domestic Korean startups or Korean resident users remain standard-rated at 10%.

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory under the Value-Added Tax Act. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but precisely monitored convertibility regime. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, current transactions such as trade settlement, royalties, fees, and dividends are generally freely convertible through registered foreign exchange banks, subject to submission of underlying transaction evidence and foreign exchange reporting forms. Capital account transactions are not fully unrestricted; they are governed by a positive-list approach where market participants obtain approvals, declarations, or notifications depending on the transaction type. Foreign direct investment into a Korean entity enjoys a streamlined notification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and effectively requires conversion at a domestic FX bank with proper investor identification. Profit remittances by foreign shareholders are permissible under current-account rules, but a Korean withholding tax, normally 20% plus local surtax unless reduced by tax treaty, must be settled via the local bank; the bank requires board resolutions, audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and evidence of paid-in capital to ensure the remittance is not a disguised capital flight. Export proceeds must be received in foreign currency through an FX bank, converted immediately if the exporter has no matching won obligations, and declared through the Foreign Exchange Information System. In sum, capital controls remain layered, bank-mediated, and surveillance-heavy, not market-totalitarian.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export claims must be settled as prescribed by Korea's Foreign Exchange Regulations. An exporter must deliver the export proceeds through a domestic foreign exchange bank and may be required to sell or deposit the foreign currency within 30 days from the due date or expected receipt date. The statutory settlement period for export receivables is generally within 1 year of the export date; longer terms require advance approval. In practice, when export proceeds are collected, if the bank account is a won account with automatic conversion, the won amount is credited immediately; if kept in foreign currency, the proceeds count as a foreign currency deposit and conversion is not mandatory if they will be used for future foreign currency payments. For follow-on compliance, exporters must file the export declaration through Korea Customs Service and the bank subsequently reports the settlement through the Foreign Exchange Information System.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), operating in tandem with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the Foreign Exchange Authority and supported by the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections are processed as foreign direct investment through a domestic foreign exchange bank. The Korean LLC must file an FDI notification with the Ministry of Trade, Industry, and Energy or Invest KOREA before remittance or within the statutory filing window. The foreign investor wires funds to the dedicated Foreign Investment Account or ordinary current account of the LLC using SWIFT MT103, clearly quoting the FDI notification number and the bank-compliant foreign exchange purpose code. On receipt, the bank verifies the notification number, value of remittance, and investor identity; the LLC then completes foreign currency verification and capital tax treatment. Funds are credited to the company's corporate account in KRW or foreign currency. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: LOW / NOT VIABLE AS STANDALONE. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, or Currenxie cannot open local clearing accounts in Korea in their own name as third-party EMIs unless they obtain an FSC e-settlement license or partner with a licensed banking institution under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. The Korean entity may receive remittances from foreign EMIs only if those EMIs route through an intermediary or licensed bank; however, using them as the primary clearing rail is untenable because they are not treated as domestic foreign exchange banks and cannot facilitate Korean cash-management products, won settlement, or statutory tax and FX filings. Domestic Tier-1 banks must be used for the platform's cash custody and FX rails; EMIs may be used only as last-mile collection for small foreign investors and with full traceability.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax; aggregate top effective rate 26.4% including the 10% local income tax surtax (lower brackets: 9% up to KRW 200 million; 19% up to KRW 20 billion; 21% up to KRW 300 billion)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate domestic statutory withholding on outbound dividends to non-resident corporate shareholders (20% corporate income tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surtax); treaty relief may reduce the rate to 5%-15% depending on ownership and the applicable double tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when aggregate cross-border related-party transactions for the fiscal year exceed KRW 5 billion; Master File is required when the multinational group has consolidated revenue of at least KRW 100 billion; different or simplified thresholds may apply to small entities and CbC reporters.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply Caribbean-style ESR, but applies statutory substance-over-form rules under the Corporate Tax Act and beneficial-ownership requirements under tax treaties; the equity crowdfunding portal must keep its core income-generating activities (issuer due diligence, KYC/AML, escrow settlement, investor communication, board oversight) onshore in Korea with locally employed directors/managers and physical records, otherwise the National Tax Service may disregard the Korean entity for treaty/TP purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unregistered financial investment business / crowdfunding license violation

Statutory sanction: FSCMA Article 444: imprisonment up to 3 years or a criminal fine up to KRW 100 million; the FSC may issue cease-and-desist orders, revoke registration, and impose administrative fines.

Director personal liability: Directors face individual criminal imprisonment/fines, civil joint and several liability to reimburse misappropriated crowdfunding proceeds, FSC blacklisting and travel bans, and personal asset seizure upon court order when the corporate veil is pierced for fraud or willful misconduct.
Corporate tax evasion, false non-resident declarations, and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Tax Offense Punishment Act Article 9: imprisonment up to 2 years or a fine up to twice the evaded corporate tax; additional 10%-40% tax penalties under CTA Article 76; aggravated sentencing for large-scale evasion under the Act on Aggravated Punishment for Specific Economic Crimes.

Director personal liability: National Tax Collection Act Article 39 authorizes collection from directors who intentionally or grossly neglect their duties when corporate assets are insufficient; directors may also face civil restitution, travel blacklisting by the Ministry of Justice, and criminal imprisonment that can substantially exceed the base 2-year term if the evaded amount is large.
AML/CDD failure and false suspicious-transaction reporting for crowdfunding escrow flows

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Act on Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information (Article 4 suspicious transaction reporting duty and related penalty provisions) can produce criminal fines up to KRW 50 million or imprisonment up to 5 years, along with FSC license revocation and disgorgement orders.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, civil damages for investor losses, a prohibition on re-entering financial services, travel bans/blacklisting, and piercing of the Yuhan Hoesa veil in cases of deliberate AML compliance failure or knowing participation in money laundering.

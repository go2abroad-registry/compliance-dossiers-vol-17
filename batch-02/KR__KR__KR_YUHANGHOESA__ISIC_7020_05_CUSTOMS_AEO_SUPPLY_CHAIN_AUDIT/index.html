Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). Export and customs declarations are administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the Unipass electronic customs portal.

E-government filing portal

The Korea National Tax Service (NTS) operates the HomeTax electronic portal. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa obtains its statutory tax identity by registering for a 'Business Registration Number' with the NTS district tax office within 20 days of commencing business. For a foreign non-resident shareholder, no separate shareholder VAT registration is required; the Korean entity is the registered taxpayer. The registration application is filed on HomeTax (or at the district tax office) under the Framework Act on National Taxes; the following are required: certificate of incorporation and articles of incorporation, corporate registration certificate, proof of the Korean registered business address, identity documents of directors/representative, power of attorney appointing a Korean tax representative (if management is overseas), and evidence of any foreign investment. Upon acceptance the NTS issues the entity's 10-digit Business Registration Number, which simultaneously functions as the Corporate Tax TIN, VAT registration ID, and business identifier for e-invoicing and customs declarations. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that supplies taxable digital services to Korean customers without a Korean establishment, the statutory route is the separate NTS simplified business registration procedure for foreign non-resident operators, with a mandatory Korean tax representative, but that does not replace the domestic Yuhan Hoesa registration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for a domestic corporation: VAT and business registration are mandatory from business commencement. For individual traders, the simplified-taxpayer revenue threshold is KRW 80 million per year, but that threshold is not available to the Yuhan Hoesa corporate entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under VAT Act Article 24 and Enforcement Decree Article 40, zero-rating applies to exports of goods and to qualifying services supplied to non-residents, including offshore services and foreign-currency-earning services. Yuhan Hoesa's ISIC 7020 AEO certification and supply-chain security auditing services are zero-rated when the client is a non-resident, the service is performed, delivered, and consumed outside Korea, and payment is received in foreign currency from an overseas source. For digital content or other offshore digital services, the VAT law requires evidence that the service is not used or enjoyed in Korea; a Korean resident client or Korean place of use disqualifies the zero rate. The supplier must retain sufficient export and offshore-service performance evidence to satisfy NTS and Korea Customs Service audit requirements.

  • Customs export declaration number and export clearance confirmation issued by Korea Customs Service through the Unipass system
  • Original or authorised copy of the Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the Korean Yuhan Hoesa as shipper/exporter
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and the underlying AEO supply-chain shipment records
  • Foreign exchange inward remittance certificate or foreign exchange settlement certificate issued by a Korean designated foreign exchange bank evidencing collection of the export proceeds

All VAT invoices in business-to-business supplies must be electronic tax invoices transmitted to NTS through HomeTax. The supplier must transmit the e-invoice in the HomeTax system no later than the statutory invoice and VAT return deadline for the relevant taxable period; the NTS system auto-matches buyer and seller data and clears the invoice against tax authority records. The system supports API/EDI integration for high-volume filers. There is no physical revenue stamp; anti-fraud control is achieved through mandatory e-invoice reporting, automatic cross-validation, and periodic VAT liquidation on HomeTax. For exported goods, electronic customs clearance in Unipass is a pre-condition for claiming the zero rate.

NTS and Korea Customs Service may reopen a zero-rated export within the statutory assessment period and claw back the 0% VAT if the export declaration is cancelled, the transport document does not match the exporter, the foreign exchange proceeds are not received within the statutory 180-day collection deadline unless an extension is granted by a designated foreign exchange bank, or the services are actually consumed in Korea. Penalties include assessment of unpaid VAT at 10% plus interest and statutory additional tax under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes; fraudulent or intentionally false zero-rating claims may trigger 40% additional tax and criminal sanctions for tax evasion or false e-invoices. The AEO supply-chain audit is also a customs compliance point; Korea Customs Service can revoke AEO certification and impose trade-law penalties for supply-chain record failures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with an administrative controls overlay under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is not freely deliverable or fully convertible offshore; all onshore KRW conversion must be executed through a designated foreign exchange bank. Current account transactions, including business-service audit fees, profit dividends, and royalties, are generally permitted once the underlying invoice, contract, tax settlement, and service-delivery evidence are presented. Capital account transactions, however, are subject to a prior reporting or approval matrix, including foreign direct investment deposits, equity inflows, cross-border loans, and capital outflows, and must be routed through a Korean foreign exchange bank. There is no blanket mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency in an approved foreign-currency account or sell it to the bank for KRW, but every transaction must pass through the statutory reporting system. Profit remittance hurdles arise mainly where the bank cannot verify corporate capacity, foreign investment registration, or corporate income tax settlement; withholding tax clearance, audited financial statements, and board minutes are the standard proof required to repatriate profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds must be collected by a designated foreign exchange bank within 180 days of the export declaration unless a longer collection period has been approved by the Ministry of Economy and Finance through the bank based on the underlying sales contract. No fixed statutory surrender ratio applies under the current regime; an exporter may maintain the proceeds in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account, sell them for Korean won at the bank's prevailing market rate, or use them for permitted foreign-currency disbursements. Each collection must be recorded using the applicable Bank of Korea transaction purpose code, and the customs export declaration, invoice, bill of lading, and supporting contract must be retained to substantiate the transaction in the event of an AML or FX audit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank and monetary authority, with foreign exchange administration supervision shared with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Korea Customs Service under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low legal viability. South Korea does not recognise an standalone electronic-money institution licence equivalent to UK EMIs or Singapore MPI licences. A foreign digital EMI, including Wise, cannot hold a corporate KRW treasury mandate, cannot execute FDI capital verification, and cannot act as a primary foreign-exchange counterparty for an onshore Korean entity. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only designated Korean foreign exchange banks may provide current and capital account foreign-exchange clearing services for corporate treasury. Wise or similar providers may only be used as an ancillary payment interface if they settle through a licensed bank, but for the Yuhan Hoesa treasury blueprint they are not recommended for institutional inbound investment, profit repatriation, or export settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign investment into the Yuhan Hoesa must be reported before or immediately upon subscription using the Foreign Investment Promotion Act route through Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) or the designated Korean foreign exchange bank. The foreign investor should send a SWIFT MT103 from its home bank to the temporary foreign-investment deposit account registered with the Korean bank, using the Bank of Korea-compliant purpose code for inbound foreign direct investment in a domestic corporation. The Korean receiving bank will verify the investor identity, confirm the remittance against the equity/membership subscription contract, issue a Foreign Investment Certificate, and complete the capital verification process. After verification, the funds may be moved into the entity's operational current account. The statutory capital verification dossier must include the bank's inward remittance certificate, foreign investment report confirmation from KOTRA, the investment contract, board resolution of the recipient, and evidence of beneficial ownership; failing this, the bank will freeze the escrowed funds under AML requirements.Outbound treasury disbursements for the AEO audit and security supply-chain consulting practice should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 for direct service payments, licence fees, and subcontracted vendor settlements. Each MT103 must carry a valid Korean foreign-exchange purpose code and be accompanied by the invoice, service contract, and proof of performance to satisfy the bank's current-account reporting obligations. For any import of surveillance hardware or documentation technologies, the company may open an irrevocable commercial letter of credit under ICC UCP600, with documents cleared against payment through the Korean bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation must be prepared and maintained where the aggregate amount of cross-border transactions with each foreign related party exceeds KRW 5,000,000,000 for the relevant fiscal year. Master File documentation is mandatory where the Korean entity's annual turnover exceeds KRW 100,000,000,000 and the aggregate foreign related-party transaction amount exceeds KRW 50,000,000,000. A related-party transaction disclosure schedule must also be attached to the annual corporate tax return once the statutory threshold is met.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply a separate Free Zone ESR/CIGA filing regime, but Korean anti-avoidance law gives effect to the economic substance doctrine through substance-over-form interpretation and the general anti-avoidance rule in Framework Act on Taxes Article 14. For an AEO supply-chain audit entity, substantive economic presence in Korea is essential: Yuhan Hoesa must maintain real premises, full-time professional staff, management decision-making, client-facing AEO certification work, data security infrastructure, board meetings in Korea, and core income-generating activities in Korea. A shell company or conduit without these functions faces recharacterization, disallowance of claimed benefits, CFC attribution, and sanctions for unlawful tax avoidance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act and Articles 47-2 and 52 of the Framework Act on Taxes, the entity is liable for the unpaid tax, additional tax equal to 20% to 40% of the understated tax, daily late-payment surcharges, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to KRW 100 million. NTS may also conduct a special tax investigation, seize assets, and void fraudulent transfers.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced when the Yuhan Hoesa is used as a sham or alter ego for tax evasion. Directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax damages under the Korean Commercial Act Article 401 and may face civil asset restitution, personal confiscation of hidden profits, overseas travel bans and blacklisting under the National Tax Collection Act, and criminal imprisonment for intentional evasion.
Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting and Deficient Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (Korean International Tax Adjustment Act), the National Tax Service may recompute taxable income using arm's-length methods, deny deductions for inflated intercompany payments, impose deemed dividend treatment, and add transfer-pricing penalties of up to 30% of the adjusted tax amount. Failure to produce required documentation separately exposes the taxpayer to documentation penalties and adverse evidentiary presumptions.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately engineer artificial transfer-pricing structures can be held civilly liable for restitution of diverted corporate assets to the Korean entity and for damages suffered by minority shareholders and creditors. Criminal prosecution may follow where the pricing scheme is accompanied by false invoices or concealment; personal bank accounts and overseas remittance records are subject to investigation, and managing directors may be blacklisted and prohibited from leaving Korea until tax liabilities are settled.
AML, Cross-Border Remittance, and AEO Certification Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, carrying on a foreign exchange business without a license is punishable by imprisonment and fines; under the Specific Financial Information Act, failure to report suspicious transactions and cross-border wire transfers attracts AML penalties and criminal sanctions. Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in unlicensed money transmission, false AEO certification, or AML lapses are exposed to personal criminal imprisonment, personal fines, confiscation of illicit gains, and civil liability to defrauded clients. Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil where the entity is a thin façade for the director's personal business, leading to personal asset seizure, an overseas travel ban and blacklisting by customs and immigration authorities, and disqualification from holding managerial positions in licensed audit or customs-compliance firms.

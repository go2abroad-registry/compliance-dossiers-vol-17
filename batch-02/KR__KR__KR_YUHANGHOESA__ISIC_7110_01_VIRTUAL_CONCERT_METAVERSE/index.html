Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Commercial Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, a Yuhan Hoesa must have a registered place of business in Korea that can receive legal service. Virtual-office and commercial-mailbox addresses are not categorically prohibited, but the National Tax Service and commercial banks generally reject them for VAT and bank account opening when the principal activity is virtual concert ticketing and digital ticket tokenization involving USDC, because e-commerce and virtual asset enterprises must establish actual business substance. A serviced-office lease with a dedicated desk, a visible company sign, and the ability to produce original corporate records on site is the minimum accepted arrangement; a pure mailbox provider creates adverse tax and banking risk and may trigger deemed business-place deregistration.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under Article 287-2 of the South Korean Commercial Act. The Articles of Incorporation must state the amount of capital and the number of contribution units, and each member must fully pay or contribute in-kind the full value of the subscribed contribution before the incorporation registration application is filed. For a Yuhan Hoesa, no blocked escrow account, notarial verification of paid capital, or external auditor capital certificate is legally required; a bank certificate of deposit or an in-kind contribution appraisal is sufficient. The registry fee is ad valorem on the stated capital, so the USD amount shown assumes a normal small-capital formation and may vary with the actual capital amount.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS), Republic of Korea; primary electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); cross-border goods clearance and export declarations are handled by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

The Korean Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company) obtains its Tax Identification Number (TIN) in the form of the Korean Business Registration Number, issued automatically by the competent District Tax Office upon incorporation under the Commercial Act and registration under the Framework Act on National Taxes. If the company has not yet registered a place of business, the legal representative must file the statutory business registration application with the District Tax Office having jurisdiction over the Korean address within 20 days from the date of business commencement or the first taxable event. For a foreign non-resident entity with no Korean fixed establishment, the National Tax Service requires the appointment of a domestic tax representative, filing of the statutory business registration application for a foreign business operator, submission of the foreign corporation certificate of incorporation, overseas business license/registration proof, articles or constitutional document and a notarized power of attorney, resulting in a VAT registration number embedded in the business registration certificate. Where a non-resident supplier has no Korean place of business, registration is filed with the Director of the National Tax Service through HomeTax; the foreign entity must use the same registration number for VAT invoicing, customs, and export transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not provide a check-the-box election or a hybrid mismatch election. A Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic corporation per se and is taxed at entity level on its worldwide income; it cannot make pass-through elections. Hybrid mismatch rules under Korean taxation are applied as mandatory statutory adjustments rather than as electable mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory turnover threshold denominated in Korean Won (KRW) triggers VAT registration. Any supplier of taxable goods or services in Korea, including non-resident digital service suppliers, must register from the moment of the first taxable supply. The KRW 80,000,000 annual turnover simplified taxpayer threshold for small operators is a VAT return simplification, not an exemption or registration threshold, and does not apply to a corporation or to foreign digital service suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies, including digital services supplied to recipients outside Korea, are zero-rated under the Value Added Tax Act. A Korean LLC hosting virtual concerts and selling digital ticketing tokenization in the metaverse may zero-rate the service if the customer is a non-resident/foreign consumer and the service is actually performed or used outside Korea. Qualifying intangible/digital services recognized by the NTS include software, IT-enabled digital content, gaming, video/event streaming, audiovisual works, advertisement, and similar metaverse event services. The zero-rated supply must be evidenced by the contract, invoice, payment, and delivery arrangements; a domestic Korean ticket buyer is subject to 10% VAT on the same virtual concert supply.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration (Export Bill of Entry) filed through UNI-PASS for any physical goods; for pure digital virtual-concert services, a service-export declaration or the customs status record if required by Korean export procedures, showing the 0% VAT supply and foreign recipient.
  • Commercial invoice and Korean National Tax Service electronic tax invoice (or statutory tax invoice) annotated with the zero-rated export supply, the foreign buyer, and the service description.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical goods; for virtual concert/metaverse services, the digital delivery evidence such as server access logs, virtual event platform reports, tokenized virtual ticket records, or blockchain/ledger ticketing transaction history.
  • Korean foreign exchange receipt certificate issued by a licensed foreign exchange bank evidencing collection of export proceeds in foreign currency; this is the Korean equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), and Korean banks do not issue an India-style FIRC.
  • Executed purchase/service contract, SWIFT payment credit notice, or other remittance instruction proving that the virtual event ticketing and metaverse service was supplied and paid from without Korea, with use outside Korea.

All VAT-registered suppliers are required to issue a Korean tax invoice; for B2B supplies the invoice must be an electronic tax invoice transmitted to the National Tax Service through the HomeTax electronic tax invoice system no later than the day after supply. The system operates via NTS-approved XML/JSON-API integration for software vendors, with no pre-clearance approval from the NTS and no anti-fraud digital stamping requirement. In parallel, B2C virtual concert and metaverse ticket sales must be recorded through the cash-receipt system when payment is made in cash or no other tax invoice is required; NTS accepts card/POS or gateway metadata as API evidence. The obligation to issue an accurate invoice under the Value Added Tax Act remains on the supplier.

The National Tax Service audits zero-rated export claims and requires the complete documentation bundle to be retained by the supplier. If the 0% export VAT claim is unsupported, false, or the foreign exchange proceeds are not collected, the NTS will claw back the zero-rated input VAT previously refunded and assess 10% VAT on the entire sale amount, plus delinquency surcharges and interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Fraudulent export zero-rating, including fabricated export declarations, false digital delivery logs, or fake bank receipts, subjects the company to the penalty charge of 40% of the underdeclared tax for intentional concealment, 20% for gross negligence, and possible criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act; the statutory assessment/payment collection period extends to 10 years in cases of fraudulent tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the Korean won is largely convertible on current account transactions; remittances for dividends, royalties, and current business payments are permitted if properly reported through a licensed foreign exchange bank. However, capital account operations such as equity subscription, loans, external guarantees, and non-resident equity transfers remain subject to declaration or prior approval via the Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance. The won is not freely convertible offshore; onshore KRW can be converted to major currencies within licensed banks, while offshore won trading is heavily restricted and mainly conducted through non-deliverable forwards. Export proceeds are required to be collected within six months from the shipping date. There is no blanket mandatory surrender ratio for export receipts; exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in licensed foreign currency deposit accounts at domestic banks, but any settlement of domestic transactions must be executed in KRW, requiring conversion through a licensed foreign exchange bank. All transactions are captured by the NEDIAS electronic reporting platform. The key profit remittance hurdles for a foreign-owned Yuhan Hoesa include a statutory dividend distribution ceiling based on audited retained earnings, withholding tax at a rate of between 5% and 22% depending on the relevant tax treaty, and the need to provide a prior bank-filed foreign exchange report demonstrating that the remittance is traceable to an original capital importation certificate, foreign investment declaration, or valid trade-backed invoice. Banks may also impose additional source-of-funds scrutiny if the remittance pattern shows round-tripping or no underlying economic substance. For the virtual concert metaverse model, ticket presale revenue from foreign platforms entering through online card rails must be repatriated with clear purpose codes to avoid re-characterization as unlicensed cross-border money transfers. Overall, the regime is current-account liberalized but capital-account controlled, requiring strict documentation, reporting, and a stable onshore local banking relationship.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received within 180 days of the export declaration date; no mandatory conversion ratio is imposed, but all export foreign currency receipts must be reported through NEDIAS and either held in a licensed foreign currency account or converted to KRW through a licensed bank, with any extension of the collection period requiring prior bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and Ministry of Economy and Finance (Foreign Exchange Policy Division), with Korea Customs Service's NEDIAS foreign exchange reporting system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Onshore fintech EMIs are not legally viable for servicing the Yuhan Hoesa because South Korea requires all foreign exchange transactions for domestic legal entities to be executed through licensed domestic deposit-taking banks operating under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and the Anti-Money Laundering Act. Unlicensed offshore EMIs such as Revolut, Payoneer, and Wise cannot open KRW a/c accounts, cannot issue dollar accounts in the name of a Korean company, and cannot provide licensed FX clearing onshore. Stripe can be used only as a payment collection aggregator for the metaverse ticketing platform, but the underlying settlement must be routed through a Korean licensed acquiring bank and ultimately credited to a local Tier-1 bank operating account. Therefore, any treasury architecture that relies on a fintech EMI as the primary clearing hub is legally blocked and poses KYC underwriting rejection risk.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation is triggered when annual gross revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and cross-border related-party transaction value exceeds KRW 5 billion. The International Transaction Information Report must be filed with the annual corporate tax return for material related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the Korean tax authorities apply substance-over-form and OECD BEPS principles. A non-resident virtual concert operator must demonstrate that Core Income-Generating Activities are performed in Korea if claiming a permanent establishment or seeking treaty benefits. Artificial offshore arrangements lacking local employees, offices, and real operational control will be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years or a fine of up to 3 times the amount evaded. The Korean National Tax Service also imposes a 40% fraudulent under-reporting penalty under the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced when the LLC is an alter ego of its directors. Directors are personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties, face civil asset restitution, are subject to NTS blacklisting and travel bans, and may receive criminal imprisonment for tax fraud.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the National Tax Service may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the intercompany transaction, and impose transfer pricing penalties of up to 20% of the adjusted income. Abusive tax avoidance also triggers a 40% penalty on the resulting under-reported tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure the shell arrangement can be held jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency. Piercing the corporate veil permits seizure of personal assets, criminal fraud prosecution, director disqualification, and inclusion on the NTS compliance blacklist with travel restrictions.
AML and Foreign Exchange Reporting Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Transaction Reports Act, failure to report suspicious virtual-asset transactions carries criminal liability of up to 5 years' imprisonment or a fine up to KRW 50 million. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, unreported outbound transfers may be penalized up to 20% of the unreported amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize crypto settlement or offshore remittances may be prosecuted as accomplices, subject to personal asset forfeiture, passport revocation and travel bans, and permanent debarment from acting as corporate directors in Korea.

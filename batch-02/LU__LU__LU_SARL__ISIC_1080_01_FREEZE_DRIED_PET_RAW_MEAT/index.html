Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT matters; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct tax matters; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) for entity registration. Official portals: guichet.lu, MyGuichet.lu, eTVA.

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL receives a 13-digit business registration number (numéro de matricule d'identité) upon RCS filing. The VAT identification number (numéro d'identification à la TVA) is issued automatically by the AEDT after RCS registration for resident entities. Foreign entities without a permanent establishment in Luxembourg must submit a non-established taxable person registration form to the AEDT, attaching the foreign business registration certificate, constitutional documents, identification of the Luxembourg fiscal agent (if required), and, where applicable, the EU VAT number and a declaration of planned activities. Approval may take 1 to 4 weeks; an ACD declaration may also be required if the activity creates a Luxembourg permanent establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (resident SARL subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment) — A Luxembourg SARL receives a 13-digit business registration number (numéro de matricule d'identité) upon RCS filing. The VAT identification number (numéro d'identification à la TVA) is issued automatically by the AEDT after RCS registration for resident entities. Foreign entities without a permanent establishment in Luxembourg must submit a non-established taxable person registration form to the AEDT, attaching the foreign business registration certificate, constitutional documents, identification of the Luxembourg fiscal agent (if required), and, where applicable, the EU VAT number and a declaration of planned activities. Approval may take 1 to 4 weeks; an ACD declaration may also be required if the activity creates a Luxembourg permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident SARL subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment)

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT group election under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for financially, economically and organically linked entities; (ii) corporate tax consolidation (régime d'intégration fiscale) for resident parent companies holding at least 95% of a qualifying subsidiary for at least five years; (iii) the simplified flat-rate VAT exemption scheme for small businesses within the domestic threshold; (iv) limited hybrid mismatch adjustments under ATAD navigation, including opting into the UE provisions and defensive rule elections via bilateral treaty relief; (v) joint taxation of spouses for personal income tax, inapplicable to SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-business VAT exemption for domestic established taxable persons; EUR 0 for non-established suppliers making taxable supplies in Luxembourg; EUR 10,000 EU-wide OSS threshold for intra-Community distance sales of goods and cross-border digital services

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of finished freeze-dried pet treats and raw-meat kibble physically exported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the European Union, provided the goods leave the EU by the end of the month following the supply and are supported by transport/customs evidence. Intra-EU supplies to taxable customers may be zero-rated when the customer communicates a valid EU VAT identification number and the goods are dispatched to another Member State. Ancillary freight and insurance services may be zero-rated if directly related to an export supply. Packaging and processing services performed on goods destined for export may also qualify under the merchanting and tolling provisions if properly evidenced.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A / common transit declaration T1) validated by Luxembourg Customs with the assigned MRN
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR transport document evidencing physical dispatch from Luxembourg and arrival outside the European Union
  • Commercial invoice and packing list consistent with the customs declaration and VAT invoice
  • Purchase order or distribution contract with the foreign buyer showing Incoterms 2020 (FCA, CPT, or CIP) and the place of delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt confirming payment from the foreign customer within the contractual and statutory periods
  • Delivery confirmation signed by the foreign importer or customs release documentation evidencing arrival in the destination country

No mandatory real-time continuous transaction controls (CTC) or digital stamping is currently in force. B2B electronic invoicing is optional but accepted. Invoices to Luxembourg public-sector entities must be submitted electronically via the Peppol BIS 3.0 network or an equivalent public procurement e-invoicing portal. All VAT-registered businesses must file periodic VAT returns electronically via eTVA and retain underlying records for ten years. The AEDT may require SAF-T or structured data files during audit for cross-checking; no real-time clearance is imposed.

The AEDT may conduct documentation audits and physical tracing of exported goods. If zero-rating is claimed without genuine customs clearance, transport evidence, or payment repatriation, the VAT becomes immediately due and is clawed back with delay interest at 0.6% per month (or such statutory rate from time to time applicable) plus administrative fines of up to EUR 25,000. Fraudulent documentation or intentionally false customs declarations may trigger criminal prosecution and imprisonment. Repayment of temporally claimed VAT may also be reversed if the exporter cannot demonstrate the goods left the EU within the statutory deadline. Retrospective adjustment periods vary from three years generally to ten years for intended fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg has full capital-account and current-account convertibility under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the general EU legal framework for cross-border payments. There are no exchange-control approvals, no mandatory conversion obligation, and no regulatory barrier to profit repatriation for a resident SARL. Dividends and shareholder-loan repayments may be paid abroad provided the corporate-law solvency tests under the Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies are met. The main frictions are AML/KYC source-of-funds verification, balance-of-payments statistical reporting thresholds administered by the BCL, and the Luxembourg 15% domestic dividend withholding tax, which is generally reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a qualifying EU corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the SARL share capital for an uninterrupted two-year period.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation, no minimum retention in Luxembourg, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds in USD, GBP, CNY, or other freely convertible currencies may be retained indefinitely in a Luxembourg foreign-currency account or converted into EUR at the prevailing commercial bank exchange rate. The only related obligations are the SARL's record-keeping duty, standard SWIFT reference/purpose information, BCL balance-of-payments reporting where the transaction exceeds statistical thresholds, and EU cash-control declarations for physical movement of currency above EUR 10,000.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL). Luxembourg does not maintain a separate foreign-exchange administration; exchange-control and payment-system oversight is shared between the BCL, the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance, and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking and AML supervision. The BCL is the Eurosystem central bank, operator of TARGET2-T2 participation, and national compiler of balance-of-payments statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate but strictly limited. A Luxembourg or other EEA-authorised e-money institution (EMI) may provide payment accounts, SEPA and SWIFT-style transfers, issuer/payment acquiring services, and FX conversion under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the CSSF e-money framework. An EMI is not a credit institution: it cannot accept deposits, issue a blocked capital-escrow certificate, grant credit, or provide the full treasury security of a BCL-licensed bank. A non-EEA digital EMI should be treated as high-risk and unsuitable for principal SARL treasury operations because of weaker supervisory reach, absence of deposit protection, and potential restriction by Luxembourg banks on receiving funds from unregulated or thin-capitalised payment firms. For the Freeze-Dried Pet Raw Meat project, an EMI may be used only as a secondary collection/payout rail for Platform_Pay and small e-commerce sales, while the primary operational and escrow accounts must remain with a Luxembourg credit institution.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound equity injection for the LU_SARL must be routed by SWIFT MT103 or equivalent high-value transfer from the foreign shareholder to a blocked capital-escrow account opened in the name of the SARL-in-formation with a Luxembourg credit institution or the BCL. The payment reference must state clearly: 'Equity capital contribution to LU_SARL [name] under Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915'. The Luxembourg bank will verify receipt of funds and, after notarisation of the incorporation deed, release the blocked funds to the company's operational account. For later capital increases, the same blocked-account procedure applies and the BCL balance-of-payments statistical classification should be 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital'. The inbound transfer narrative should include the legal entity identifiers of both remitter and beneficiary, the ultimate beneficial owner screen, and the source-of-funds documentation. Foreign-currency inbound amounts may be converted to EUR at the receiving bank's market rate; no exchange-control permit is required.Outbound trade disbursements should use SWIFT MT103 for non-urgent supplier payments and documentary clearing, MT700 for issuance of a Commercial Letter of Credit and MT707 for amendments when procuring bulk raw meat or animal-derived ingredients from third countries. Within the euro area, SEPA Credit Transfers are acceptable for low-value EU supplier payments. For Platform_Pay e-commerce operations, daily or periodic settlement payouts should be redirected from the payment platform to the SARL's Luxembourg operational account, with reconciliation files retained for audit. All disbursements must identify the underlying commercial invoice, Incoterms 2020 rule, HS classification of freeze-dried pet food and raw pet-food inputs, and be backed by EU veterinary import documentation. A single BCL-supervised bank should act as the clear treasury hub to reduce counterparty clearance failures and ensure liquidity forecasting.Deliverable FX forward contracts with a Luxembourg credit institution or EU/EEA investment firm, documented under an ISDA or EVS master agreement, used to fix the EUR cost of confirmed USD/GBP/CNY raw-material payables for up to twelve months.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market currencies where a deliverable interbank forward is unavailable in Luxembourg; settlement is made in EUR, must be supported by documented underlying trade exposure, and is subject to ISDA margin provisions where the counterparty is a financial entity.Cross-currency swaps for long-term shareholder loans or intercompany treasury balances, permitting conversion of fixed-rate foreign-currency liability into EUR floating or fixed interest streams, with credit support annexed and EMIR clearing thresholds assessed for each counterparty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a Luxembourg SARL with a fiscal year ending 31 December, the corporate tax return must be filed by 31 December of the subsequent year. A six-month extension is available upon written application to the Direct Tax Administration (Administration des contributions directes, ACD). The assessment (rôle) is issued after filing and payment of the estimated tax instalments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg applies an objective threshold requiring a Luxembourg resident entity (including SARL) to prepare and file with its corporate income tax return a transfer pricing documentation report (Annex 740) for intragroup transactions exceeding EUR 250,000 in aggregate per transaction category in a tax year. There is no separate local file/master file threshold under domestic law; however, for entities belonging to an MNE group with a consolidated turnover above EUR 750 million, Luxembourg implements the OECD Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting requirement, together with mandatory Master File and Local File obligations in line with BEPS Action 13, when the Luxembourg entity is the ultimate parent or a constituent entity of such a group.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies the OECD-integrated economic substance doctrine through several statutory layers. For a SARL engaged in freeze-dried pet food manufacturing, the company must demonstrate real, locally embedded substance: an office or production facility, employees with decision-making authority, board meetings held in Luxembourg, and operational risk management. The Luxembourg register of beneficial owners (RBE) imposes a transparency obligation; failure to file or maintain RBE data is a criminal offence. The anti-abuse provisions (GAAR under Article 6 of the Luxembourg tax code and the 2019 Law on tax abuse), together with ATAD interest restriction rules, deny tax advantages for artificial arrangements lacking commercial substance. In cross-border payment scenarios, the 2023 Luxembourg Administrative Circular concerning the beneficial ownership requirement for DTA application and the EU's 'DAC 6' mandatory disclosure rules for potentially aggressive cross-border arrangements further enforce substance. The Luxembourg common law company (société commerciale) status is never sufficient alone; the manufacturing scope with freeze-drying equipment, local warehouse, veterinary food compliance approvals, product liability insurance, and payroll in Luxembourg constitute the required economic substance. A shell company with no production activity but generating royalty or service fee income would fail the substance doctrine and be subject to requalification, denial of treaty benefits, and deemed dividend treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 242 to 244 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law, fraudulent tax evasion is punishable by a fine of up to EUR 25,000, which may be multiplied per taxable year of the offence. The tax administration may additionally impose an administrative penalty of 10% of the evaded tax plus a 50% surcharge for wilful tax evasion (indices sérieux d'intention). Prosecution occurs before the Luxembourg criminal courts (tribunal correctionnel) and can lead to confiscation of goods and closure of business premises.

Director personal liability: Directors and the duly appointed manager (gérant) of the SARL face joint personal liability for intentionally or negligently committed unlawful acts. In tax evasion, criminal liability extends directly to the natural-person manager: imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years under Article 244 LIR possibly combined with fines. The corporate veil is pierced where the SARL is used as a mere facade or where the manager personally assisted or procured the evasion. For unpaid corporate tax liabilities resulting from fraud, the tax authority may issue a director liability notice against the gérant holding him civilly liable for the company's unpaid taxes, interest, and penalties. Additionally, the Luxembourg Criminal Code sanctions abuse of company assets and fictitious dividend distributions, enabling creditors and tax authorities to claim civil restitution from directors.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg GAAR (Article 6 LIR) and the 2022 Council Directive (EU) 2016/1164 implementation, any artificial arrangement or series of arrangements put in place for the essential purpose of obtaining a tax advantage is disregarded and recharacterized. The tax administration will reallocate profits to where the substance is located, impose an upward adjustment, and apply an interest charge of 0.6% per month plus a possible intentional-neglactence penalty of 10% to 50%. Additionally, non-cooperative intermediary and user arrangements require disclosure under DAC 6, with fines up to EUR 250,000 for non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors who manage the shell company as a conduit face liability under Luxembourg civil law for misrepresenting corporate substance when applying for VAT, bank accounts, or export licences. Directors may be sued for fraudulent misrepresentation by investors or trade creditors and ordered to pay restitution out of personal assets. In the context of abuses of the domestic dividend withholding exemption or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, the Luxembourg tax administration can — under the 'look-through' anti-abuse doctrine — treat the SARL as transparent and attribute profits directly to the ultimate non-resident shareholders. The gérant may face a travel ban to Luxembourg if judicial ex parte freezing orders are issued, though imprisonment arises only for criminal fraude fiscale.
AML and Unregistered Beneficial Ownership (RBE)

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg Law of 13 January 2019 establishing the RBE register, non-compliance is a criminal offence: fines from EUR 1,250 to EUR 1,250,000 plus imprisonment of 6 months to 5 years for the SARL and its directors, and the possibility of publication of the administrative decision. Non-compliance also triggers a blocking of dividends and further legal acts, because financial institutions must refuse to execute corporate transactions until the RBE registration is compliant.

Director personal liability: The gérant and each director who knowingly participates in an AML breach face joint and several liability under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended) for civil damages caused to third parties. The CSSF and Luxembourg police Judiciaire can levy personal fines, impose a prohibition against managing a Luxembourg entity, and for severe breaches involving predicate crimes (e.g. laundering of funds from prohibited meat-import smuggling), arrest and imprisonment of the responsible directors. A travel ban from the Schengen area can be issued as part of coercive criminal procedure.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Where documentation is absent or false, the Luxembourg tax administration applies the best-method judgment and makes a transfer pricing adjustment. The adjustment is subject to an interest charge on the additional tax assessed. A specific administrative penalty of up to 25% of the tax deficiency may be imposed for failure to cooperate in the audit. In cases where transfer pricing mispricing conceals a distribution of profit to shareholders, the excess may be recharacterized as a constructive dividend subject to 15% withholding tax and administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: The gérant who signs the tax return containing knowingly false transfer pricing documentation may be treated as having concluded an unlawful taxable agreement with the treasury. Directors can be held civilly liable under Article 110-1 of the Luxembourg Law on commercial companies for the company's audit and tax adjustments if the tax deficiency results from a serious personal fault (faute grave). If the transfer pricing scheme is used to evade corporate income tax intentionally, the criminal fraude fiscale regime with imprisonment provisions applies to the individual director who directed the scheme.

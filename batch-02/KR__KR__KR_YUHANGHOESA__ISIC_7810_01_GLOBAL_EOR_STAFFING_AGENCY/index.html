Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS), Republic of Korea; online portal HomeTax at www.hometax.go.kr; export customs and clearance declarations filed through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity that incorporates a Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company) or establishes a domestic place of business must file the prescribed Application for Business Registration with the competent district tax office before the commencement of business or within 20 days thereafter. The application should be accompanied by the certificate of incorporation or a notarized copy of the foreign entity register, articles of incorporation, lease agreement or evidence of the domestic place of business, evidence of appointment of the representative, and power of attorney for the local tax agent if one is appointed. NTS issues the 10-digit registered business number that serves as the VAT identification number, employer registration number, and corporate tax account. A foreign enterprise with no Korean domestic place of business that wishes to claim input VAT refunds must separately apply for Foreign Entrepreneur Business Registration under the Value-Added Tax Act through the competent tax office and may be required to designate a tax manager in Korea. The exact statutory form is the business registration application set out in the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act enforcement decree; no proprietary NTS form code is required.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable turnover is the simplified taxable person threshold, not a registration exemption; every business with a domestic place of business must VAT-register within 20 days of commencement of business, regardless of expected turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act for exported goods and for services supplied to a non-resident that are actually used or consumed outside Korea. An EOR or staffing services provider must demonstrate that the contracting counterparty is a non-resident without a Korean permanent establishment, that the remuneration is received in foreign currency, and that the services are not performed or consumed in Korea. The exporter must retain a written or electronic contract, service delivery reports, payroll records where relevant, and foreign-exchange transaction evidence. If the service is deemed supplied in Korea or the customer is a Korean entity, standard 10 percent VAT applies.

  • For goods exports: the Korea Customs Service export declaration acceptance file (Export Declaration) generated through UNI-PASS, together with commercial invoice and packing list.
  • Transport document evidencing physical departure: an original bill of lading for sea freight, airway bill for air freight, or a postal/courier dispatch receipt.
  • Foreign-exchange repatriation record: the foreign exchange transaction report or certificate of acquisition of foreign exchange issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, showing payment of export proceeds in convertible currency.

All tax invoices for transactions between registered businesses must be electronic tax invoices issued through the NTS HomeTax e-invoice channel or through an NTS-certified ERP/API interface. The electronic invoice must be issued no later than the day following the supply date and the invoice data is transmitted to NTS for central reconciliation. Korea does not operate a physical fiscal stamp or per-invoice pre-clearance server; anti-fraud control is achieved through mandatory centralized e-invoice storage, real-time validation against VAT returns, and risk-scoring algorithms. Failure to issue or transmit a valid electronic tax invoice triggers an unfaithful tax invoice penalty and may also result in denial of input VAT deduction by the recipient.

If NTS or Korea Customs Service finds that the goods did not physically leave Korea, the services were not actually supplied to or used by the non-resident, or the foreign-exchange proceeds were not received within the statutory 180-day window (or an approved extension), the zero-rated supply is recharacterized as domestic supply. The taxpayer must remit the unpaid VAT plus interest and is exposed to inaccurate-return penalties and unfaithful tax invoice penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act, while intentional fraudulent export zero-rating may be prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act. Assessment clawback for fraudulent VAT evasion is generally available for up to 10 years from the calendar year in which the tax was due.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a largely liberalised but prudently regulated foreign exchange system. Current account transactions, including service fees, royalties, and payroll outsourcing payments, are freely convertible and require only a bank-reporting or post-event declaration where no capital element is involved. Capital account transactions, especially inward foreign direct investment and equity injections, are subject to the FETA and FIPA registration, notification to the MOEF, and opening through designated foreign exchange banks. The Korean won (KRW) is not subject to a mandatory surrender of export earnings. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in onshore foreign-currency deposit accounts or convert the proceeds into KRW at market-determined exchange rates. Profit repatriation by a foreign-owned Yuhan Hoesa is permitted once the foreign investment is properly registered and after paying corporate tax and dividend withholding tax and obtaining the bank's verification of the tax clearance certificate under the Korea Tax Treaty law. In practice, remittance of dividends requires submission of the registered foreign investment certificate, audited or statutory financial statements, board minutes declaring the dividend, and a tax withholding certificate. Capital gains repatriation is also permitted but may be scrutinised against the original FIPA registration and external debt reporting thresholds. There is no universal conversion cap, but banks apply anti-money-laundering and real-name verification protocols to every cross-border transaction, and any suspicious structuring across multiple accounts would trigger a mandatory suspicious transaction report to the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU). Overall, the regime is open for recurring EOR and payroll flows, but full pre-registration of the parent-guarantee capital and strict evidentiary discipline on underlying employment contracts and tax payments are mandatory to avoid delays in outbound remittance clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, an exporter must collect and receive the export proceeds within one year from the date of export declaration or the bill of lading/airway bill date, unless an extension is approved by the MOEF or the designated foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters are allowed to hold the foreign currency receipts in a domestic foreign-currency deposit account at a licensed Korean bank or sell the proceeds in the spot or forward FX market at the prevailing bank exchange rate. If collection exceeds the statutory one-year window, the bank requires a stamped confirmation of the receivables status, a default certificate, or a MOEF extension approval. All export proceeds must be reported through the bank within five business days of receipt where required by the foreign exchange information reporting rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting as the central bank, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the competent foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability for offshore digital EMI operators seeking to become a full primary banking layer in South Korea. Fintech payment providers such as Wise hold an Electronic Financial Transaction or small-sum money transfer registration with Korean authorities, but they are not commercial banks and are legally barred from issuing KRW account numbers or independently servicing domestic payroll clearing rails in a manner equivalent to a bank under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. An offshore EMI can only function as a supplementary remittance instruction channel by partnering with a licensed Korean bank or a licensed Money Exchange Business (MEB) registrant. For a Yuhan Hoesa holding payroll regulatory licenses, the practical compliant path is to maintain the primary operating account at a Tier-1 domestic bank and use the EMI solely as an optional final-mile payout saddle for cross-border contractors, provided that the contractor's receiving bank is permitted to accept the funds and the underlying transfer purpose is disclosed. Any narrative suggesting EMI as a stand-alone cash management vehicle for the Korean entity would fail a bank's compliance review because it would bypass domestic real-name account obligations, suspicious transaction reporting, and the mandatory FX reporting infrastructure. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must treat fintech EMIs as non-bank add-ons, not as substitutes for Tier-1 domestic banking relationships.

Workers where applicable, monthly projected payroll volumes and destinations, agreed payment rails, and a written AML/CTF risk control policy for the Korean entity, together with bank confirmations of the downstream payroll disbursement methodology.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability for offshore digital EMI operators seeking to become a full primary banking layer in South Korea. Fintech payment providers such as Wise hold an Electronic Financial Transaction or small-sum money transfer registration with Korean authorities, but they are not commercial banks and are legally barred from issuing KRW account numbers or independently servicing domestic payroll clearing rails in a manner equivalent to a bank under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. An offshore EMI can only function as a supplementary remittance instruction channel by partnering with a licensed Korean bank or a licensed Money Exchange Business (MEB) registrant. For a Yuhan Hoesa holding payroll regulatory licenses, the practical compliant path is to maintain the primary operating account at a Tier-1 domestic bank and use the EMI solely as an optional final-mile payout saddle for cross-border contractors, provided that the contractor's receiving bank is permitted to accept the funds and the underlying transfer purpose is disclosed. Any narrative suggesting EMI as a stand-alone cash management vehicle for the Korean entity would fail a bank's compliance review because it would bypass domestic real-name account obligations, suspicious transaction reporting, and the mandatory FX reporting infrastructure. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must treat fintech EMIs as non-bank add-ons, not as substitutes for Tier-1 domestic banking relationships.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Progressive national headline Corporate Income Tax (CIT) of 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 19% on income between KRW 200 million and KRW 20 billion, 21% on income between KRW 20 billion and KRW 300 billion, and 24% on income above KRW 300 billion, each bracket subject to a 10% Local Income Tax surcharge, producing a combined statutory headline range of 9.9% to 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national Corporate Income Tax withheld at source plus 2% Local Income Tax surcharge, being 10% of the 20% national withholding) on gross dividend distributions made by a Korean Yuhan Hoesa to a non-resident corporate shareholder. Bilateral DTA rates commonly reduce the combined Korean dividend WHT to 5%-15% (e.g., Korea-US 10%; Korea-Japan 5%-15%; Korea-China 5%-10%), provided the beneficial-owner test is met.

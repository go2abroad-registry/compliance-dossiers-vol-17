Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Commercial Act; the initial members may fix any capital amount in the Articles. However, the full subscribed capital must be paid in before the incorporation registration is filed. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr), with export declaration linkage through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity commencing a Korean trade or business must register its domestic place of business with the district NTS office that has territorial jurisdiction over that place of business. The registration application is filed through HomeTax or at the tax office and must be accompanied by the corporate registration documentation from the home jurisdiction, articles of incorporation, a power of attorney for the local fiscal or tax representative where applicable, and proof of the domestic place of business (e.g., lease agreement or property ownership). Upon acceptance, the NTS issues a Business Registration Certificate and assigns a 10-digit registered business number, which serves as the corporate Korean TIN and VAT identification number. For a domestic Yuhan Hoesa LLC, the same registration is mandatory before any supply is made; there is no minimum capital threshold and non-resident ownership does not alter the entity-level registration requirement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 for corporate entities — VAT business registration is compulsory before the commencement of any taxable supply. A KRW 48,000,000 annual turnover simplified taxpayer threshold exists for individual proprietors only and is not available to a Yuhan Hoesa corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Korean VAT Act for exported physical goods. The supplier must prove that the dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper goods were actually moved out of Korea under an accepted export declaration, with custody and title passing to a non-resident buyer, and the proceeds must be received through a Korean foreign exchange bank using the declared payment terms (SWIFT LC or TT wire).

  • Export Declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill, as applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation issued by the Korean bank receiving the export proceeds

All registered VAT taxpayers must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax electronic tax invoice system and transmit the e-invoice data to the NTS on or before the day following the statutory issuance date. Korea does not impose per-transaction real-time fiscal clearance, external invoicing software approval, or anti-fraud digital stamping for ordinary B2B exporters; the compliance architecture is based on centralised e-invoice capture in HomeTax, cross-matching with Korea Customs Service export declarations transmitted via UNI-PASS, and periodic VAT return scrutiny.

The NTS routinely audits export zero-rating by matching customs declarations, shipping records, bill-of-lading weights, and foreign exchange receipts. If export status is disproved or documents are false, the zero-rated supply is recharacterised as a taxable domestic supply and VAT at 10% is assessed retrospectively, together with statutory additional taxes and interest under the VAT Act. Deliberate evasion or false tax invoice conduct may extend the assessment limitation period to 10 years and may lead to criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, as well as aggregate penalties under the Customs Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a liberalized current account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree. Korean won is fully convertible for genuine trade settlement, dividend remittances, and service payments, subject to documentary verification by authorized foreign exchange banks. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections, intercompany loans, and portfolio investment, are largely liberalized under notification-based regulations, but require filing through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) via a reporting bank. Export proceeds are mandated to be repatriated within a statutory time frame and may be retained in foreign currency accounts without an immediate surrender requirement; however, the domestic won conversion must be executed through an authorized currency exchange bank and reported under the relevant trade settlement codes. Profit remittance hurdles are modest but real: a certified audited financial statement, tax clearance certificate, evidence of paid corporate tax, and a board resolution approving dividends are required. The authorities retain residual powers to impose temporary market-stabilization measures in severe volatility, and all cross-border transfers above USD 10,000 equivalent must be reported. Anti-money laundering verification (CDD) is enforced by the Financial Intelligence Unit of Korea under the Financial Services Commission, and the Korea Customs Service screens export documents for invoice and price manipulation risks. Overall, the won is fully convertible for trade and investment, yet the compliance overhead is stringent, requiring precise purpose codes and supporting dossiers for each clearing event.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 18 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Trade Settlement Regulations, exporters must repatriate foreign currency export proceeds to Korea within 180 days from the date of shipment, except where an extension is approved by the Bank of Korea or the delegated foreign exchange bank due to delayed collection, open account terms exceeding standard tenor, or force majeure. There is no mandatory surrender ratio requiring conversion into Korean won; eligible exporters must bring the receipts into a foreign currency account with an authorized foreign exchange bank in Korea and may freely hold, convert, or use the funds, provided the conversion into KRW is booked through an authorized currency exchange bank. Prior to repatriation, exporters must register the export contract with the FEIS through the reporting bank and obtain a Trade Settlement Expectation Confirmation for advance receipt cases. The domestic settlement of the inbound remittance must be matched with the corresponding export customs declaration and invoicing records within 5 business days of the credit to the foreign currency account; discrepancies or overdue proceeds attract penalty interest and administrative fines. The statutory threshold for late filing triggers a penalty not exceeding 20 million KRW in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting through the Foreign Exchange Information System operated by the Korea Customs Service and authorized foreign exchange banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border digital EMI-only solutions are not viable as a primary banking rail for this Korean LLC. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations require any entity resident in Korea that engages in cross-border trade settlement to maintain its foreign currency and won accounts with an authorized foreign exchange bank licensed by the MOEF and the Bank of Korea. Korean law does not permit unlicensed foreign fintech EMIs to hold customer funds, issue KRW IBANs, or execute Korean won clearing. They may only serve as ancillary invoicing or data-orchestration tools, never as the account-holding institution. Any attempt to use a non-licensed EMI for settlement of export proceeds is a statutory violation exposing the entity to penalties, confiscation of unsettled trade proceeds, and adverse KYC rating. Therefore, the financing structure must open a domestic tier-1 Korean bank account first; a licensed overseas EMI with a genuine banking license in Hong Kong or Singapore may act as a supplementary collection tool for freight prepayments or inspection fees only if funds are immediately swept to the Korean operating account and full FEIS reporting is maintained.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection for the Korean LLC will route via the SWIFT international network using the bank identifier (SWIFT BIC) of the chosen tier-1 Korean bank, with field 50K identifying the paying shareholder and field 57D indicating the Korean beneficiary account. The remittance must carry purpose code 100001 for direct investment capital contribution, or 100003 for additional paid-in capital when a second tranche follows registration. Before execution, the Korean bank will require the Investment Registration Certificate issued by the MOEF and the Certificate of Capital Injection from the foreign investor; the funds must be credited to the Capital Escrow Account first, then officially converted to won after the bank verifies the amount against the registered equity amount. The Foreign Exchange Information System must receive the notification within 3 business days, and a capital verification report from an external Korean auditor must be filed with the court and tax authorities once the entire subscribed capital is paid. No lock-up period applies to the capital account; however, future disposal of shares by a non-resident requires a separate Investment Disposal Notification through the same FEIS channel.Trade disbursements for bulk dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper will predominantly settle under the documentary credit rail. The Korean exporter mandates an irrevocable, confirmed letter of credit (ISBP 745-compliant) issued by a prime overseas bank and advised through the Korean bank; payment is triggered by presentation of the full set of clean-on-board ocean bills of lading, phytosanitary certificate, certificate of origin, packing list, commercial invoice, and certificate of inspection. The standard clearing rail will be SWIFT MT700/MT707 for the documentary credit issuance and amendment, with the subsequent reimbursement under MT103 or MT202 after negotiation of the presentation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Korean-resident Yuhan Hoesa is required to prepare and submit a Local File if its cross-border related-party transactions for the fiscal year exceed KRW 50 billion; a Master File is required if the Korean entity's annual revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion; a Country-by-Country Report is required for the ultimate parent or surrogate entity when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion. A related-party transaction report must be filed even below these Local File thresholds, and failure to submit the Mandatory TP documentation on time triggers separate penalties and may be used to reverse the burden of proof in a transfer pricing audit.

Economic substance scope: Korean tax law applies the principle of substantive taxation under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, reinforced by the general anti-abuse rules and Article 4 of the International Tax Coordination Act. A Korean Hoesa engaged in the trading of bulk dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper must show that its core income-generating activities in Korea include actual purchase order negotiation, inventory ownership and risk absorption, contract execution, shipping and logistics control, credit risk management, and day-to-day decision-making. Where the company is merely an invoicing entity and the real trading functions are performed abroad by the parent or a non-resident group company, the NTS may reallocate income to the actual performer, deny DTA benefits, treat the Korean entity as a commissionaire or agent, and disregard the limited-liability structure as a pure shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through False Export Proceeds Reporting

Statutory sanction: This violates the filing and record-keeping obligations under Article 60 of the Corporate Income Tax Act and the truthful-reporting rule in Article 15 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. Additional tax is 20% of the unpaid tax for fraudulent non-filing/underreporting under Article 47-3, plus daily interest surcharge; criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act can impose imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine up to three times the evaded amount, with aggravated punishment where forged official documents are used.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced if the director used the LLC as a façade to hide export proceeds; the director becomes jointly liable for the unpaid national and local corporate tax under Article 202 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, may face civil asset restitution and confiscation of personal accounts, is subject to the National Tax Service blacklist and overseas travel ban for serious tax delinquency, and can be imprisoned for tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting and Shell Company Structure

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service is authorized under Article 4 of the International Tax Coordination Act to make an arm's-length adjustment and reallocate the Korean entity's taxable income; Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and Articles 19/19-2 of the Corporate Income Tax Act permit disallowance of excess related-party interest and denial of the deduction. Penalties include TP adjustment taxes, an additional tax of 10% or 20% of the adjusted understatement, separate documentation penalties for failure to file TP reports, and, in egregious cases, criminal indictment for attempted evasion.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly implemented the sham transfer-pricing arrangement may be held personally liable for the adjusted taxes where the limitation of liability is abused and the corporation is unable to satisfy the assessment. Statutory sanction: This violates the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act provisions covering all cross-border capital transactions and payment/receipt obligations, as well as the Customs Act export declaration accuracy requirements; the Act on Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Transaction Information imposes AML compliance duties.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korean law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company). Each founding member must subscribe for and fully pay the value of capital contributions before incorporation registration. The paid-in capital amount must be deposited into a dedicated bank deposit account in the company's proposed name, maintained as a blocked deposit until registration is completed, and verified by a balance certificate. No government escrow account is required; any commercial bank chartered under the Korean Banking Act may hold the deposit.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate under the Fire-Fighting System Installation and Maintenance Act, obtained from the local fire station for the premises. If the leased premises contain a warehouse or showroom with total floor area above the local threshold, a civil-defense shelter designation and approved evacuation plan are required. Workplace clearance requires registration of each workplace with the Ministry of Employment and Labor, completion of a workplace hazard assessment, installation of fire extinguishers and emergency lighting, mandatory occupational accident insurance subscription, and appointment of a workplace safety supervisor if the workplace has five or more employees, all in accordance with the Occupational Safety and Health Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of South Korea (NTS) via the Home Tax Service (HTS) electronic portal at www.hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity or a Korean entity with foreign ownership must apply for a Korean business registration certificate (Sa-eop-ja-deung-luk-jeung) at the local NTS district office having jurisdiction over the domestic place of business or the authorized domestic agent. For a Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company), the representative or a duly authorized tax agent must file the registration application under Article 168 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, supplying the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, foreign investment report or notarized ownership evidence, a domestic registered address or place of an effective fixed place of business, proof of the domestic agent's appointment (where applicable), and a copy of the passport or foreign corporate registration certificate. The NTS then issues a 10-digit business registration number, which functions as both the TIN and VAT registration number. Effective from an operational standpoint, VAT registration is automatic upon business registration for a VAT-entrepreneur; however, a non-resident without any domestic place of business but required to collect Korean VAT on cross-border supplies of services shall separately register as a foreign entrepreneur within six months of the date of supply via the HTS portal.

Available election forms

None – Korean tax law does not allow a Yuhan Hoesa to make a check-the-box partnership pass-through election for its entire Korean tax profile. Hybrid mismatch and similar elections recognized under the OECD BEPS Action 2 recommendations are not generally available for standalone VAT purposes. The only relevant statutory election within the VAT framework is the ordinary taxpayer (general method) versus simplified taxpayer election; however, given the corporation's wholesale scale and cross-border distribution of ophthalmic frames, the simplified taxpayer regime is unavailable because the entity's revenue exceeds the statutory threshold, and no alternative entity-level classification election exists under the VAT Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general exemption threshold for VAT registration. Any entrepreneur, including a foreign entity making taxable supplies in Korea through a fixed place of business or a domestic agent, must register with the NTS from the date of business commencement. A simplified taxpayer threshold exists for individually owned small businesses receiving annual taxable turnover below KRW 80 million (KRW 48 million for most service industries, revised thresholds apply depending on the business sector); however, this does not apply to a Yuhan Hoesa carrying on international wholesale of optical frames. Foreign non-resident businesses without a domestic place of business are required to register only when making supplies subject to Korean VAT, particularly electronically supplied services or imported services that reverse-charge, but no statutory minimum turnover exemption is available for such registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export of goods applies under Article 0 of the VAT Act when goods are sold to a foreigner or a foreign enterprise and are delivered from Korea to an overseas destination, whether through direct export, consignment sale through overseas bonded warehouses, or licensed local foreign trade transactions. For wholesale distribution of ophthalmic eyeglass frames, the seller must keep evidence that ownership of the goods has passed to a foreign buyer, that the goods have physically departed Korea, and that the sales proceeds are received in foreign currency. Digital services and intangible supplies are zero-rated only when actually supplied to a non-resident user outside Korea and supplied domestically only in an ancillary manner. A Yuhan Hoesa must also ensure the Korean customs officer approves the export declaration and the entity reports the export within the statutory VAT return periods.

  • Electronic export declaration receipt and Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry / Permitted Export Customs-Clearance Completion Certificate) issued via UNI-PASS by Korea Customs Service
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing shipment to the foreign buyer
  • Contract or purchase order from the foreign buyer
  • Foreign Exchange Bank certificate evidencing repatriation of export proceeds, e.g. Inward Remittance Certificate or Export Foreign Exchange Sales Confirmation
  • Certificate of Origin only where required by trade agreement, not generally required for VAT zero-rating
  • Overseas bonded warehouse or customs transit records if applicable

All VAT taxpayers must issue tax invoices (Seong-gum-gye-san-seo) electronically through the National Tax Service's electronic tax invoice system (Jeonja Seonggumgyesanseo) hosted on Home Tax Service. Issuance of an electronic tax invoice is mandatory for any transaction exceeding KRW 1,000,000 (including VAT) in the case of an electronic tax invoice obligation and for all registered corporations regardless of amount. The E-Tax Invoice must be transmitted to the NTS through the recommended API using the NTS-certified business accounting software or an open API using the certificate-based integrated authentication (Hanbo Geumseung Injeung). There is no independent fiscal clearance stamp or government real-time digital stamping server; instead, real-time anti-fraud validation is achieved through NTS e-tax invoice XML schema validations, electronic tax invoice issuance deadlines, and mandatory periodic VAT returns filed semi-annually. For exports, additional electronic customs clearance occurs via UNI-PASS of the Korea Customs Service (KCS), electronically linked to the NTS VAT export documentation system.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service may jointly audit export zero-rating claims. Under the VAT Act and Framework Act on National Taxes, any export zero-rating improperly claimed results in immediate assessment of unpaid VAT with a 10% additional tax for non-payment or understatement; up to 20% additional tax for negligent underreporting; and up to 40% additional tax for fraudulent non-reporting or false claims. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory foreign exchange collection period does not automatically revoke zero-rating if the exporter proves receipt through a deferred settlement authorized by a foreign exchange bank, but failure to keep the mandatory documentation or submission of false export declarations may trigger criminal sanctions under Article 276 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, including fines up to KRW 100 million and imprisonment up to three years, plus retrospective clawback of VAT refunded for up to five years from the original return filing date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates an open capital account under the framework of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and its Enforcement Decree. The Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, dividends, and royalties, provided that supporting documents are filed with the designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions are largely liberalized but remain subject to a prior declaration or reporting regime, particularly for inward direct investment, external borrowing, and acquisition of domestic securities by non-residents. There is no mandatory surrender requirement for export proceeds; Korea eliminated forced conversion in the 1990s, allowing exporters to hold foreign currency in designated foreign currency accounts (USD, EUR, JPY, etc.) domestically or abroad. Profit remittance is permitted through the 'trade settlement' or 'non-trade settlement' route, but the remitting bank will require an audited financial statement, tax clearance certificate from the National Tax Service (NTS), and a board resolution declaring dividends. Foreign exchange banks must report suspicious transactions to the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU) under the Financial Information Act. In practice, the convertibility is highly accessible for legitimate commercial entities, but banks conduct strict beneficial ownership identification, end-use verification, and source-of-funds checks for both inbound equity investments and outbound trade payments. For an optical eyewear wholesale entity, there are no industry-specific FX restrictions, but invoice authenticity and customs declaration matching (via the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system) are key validation points for bank compliance. The strategic risk is not non-convertibility but rather administrative friction from documentary mismatch and reporting thresholds, which can delay settlement unless treasury operations maintain meticulous trade documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, exporters must receive payment for exported goods within 6 months from the date of the export declaration, subject to extension by up to 2 years for capital equipment or projects. The full amount of foreign currency received must be either deposited into a domestic foreign currency account (to be used freely) or reported to the designated bank through the foreign exchange settlement system. Exporters are free to convert to KRW at any time or hold the proceeds in foreign currency; however, all receipts must be recorded with the underlying export declaration number (D/O No.) to satisfy the Korea Customs Service and the Bank of Korea's reporting requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting through the Foreign Exchange Information System operated by the Korea Customs Service for trade-related foreign exchange transactions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility and legally constrained. South Korea does not recognize third-country electronic money institutions (EMIs) or digital bank licenses issued in the EU/UK as valid payment service providers for domestic corporate accounts. Global EMIs such as Revolut Business, Wise, or Airwallex cannot open a local KRW account or provide a domestic account number for Yuhan Hoesa. Foreign EMIs may only operate as money transfer businesses under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act if they register with the Financial Services Commission (FSC), which effectively requires a local subsidiary and full licensing, a process unrealistic for a low-risk wholesaler. Domestic internet-only banks (KakaoBank, Toss Bank) are restricted to consumer and small business banking; they do not robustly support cross-border letters of credit, documentary collections, or foreign investment capital verification. The correct treasury rail remains a tier-1 traditional commercial bank with a dedicated corporate foreign exchange desk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from a foreign shareholder must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the designated foreign currency account of the Korean entity at a domestic bank. The remittance must carry a proper purpose code under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act—typically 'A4-1 (Investment)' or 'A4-2 (Acquisition of shares)'—and must be accompanied by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank Certificate of Foreign Currency Purchase. The entity must first obtain a Foreign Investment Certificate from the receiving bank by submitting the investor's share acquisition application, power of attorney, and certificate of incorporation. Within 30 days after the receipt of funds, the entity must register the foreign investment with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy or its delegated authority. Capital verification is done through the bank's issuance of a 'Certificate of Paid-in Capital' after confirming the converted KRW deposited into the corporate account; this certificate serves as evidence for future dividend repatriation and for the commercial registry update.For payment to overseas frame suppliers, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 for open account settlements under a purchase invoice, using purpose code 'A2-2 (Import settlement)'. The bank requires the import declaration receipt (D/O) from the Korea Customs Service, an import license for medical devices if applicable, and the original commercial invoice. For high-value or first-time transactions, the entity may open an irrevocable Letter of Credit (Documentary Credit) via the bank's trade finance desk, cleared through SWIFT MT700; settlements under LC are executed via MT202/MT103 after presentation of compliant shipping documents. Documentary collections (DP/DA) are also permitted but require the remitting bank to process export bills through the national electronic platform. All outbound wires must be pre-notified to the bank's compliance division if the transaction exceeds USD 10,000 equivalent, with supporting documents retained for five years for audit.Plain-vanilla forward exchange contracts (USD/KRW) with a domestic commercial bank, limited to maturities of 12 months and based on real underlying trade exposure (i.e., the wholesale purchase/sale of acetate eyewear frames).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permitted only through the onshore interbank market for non-trade capital flows; for trade-based hedging, the Korea Financial Supervisory Service encourages deliverable forwards instead. A corporate entity may execute NDFs with a recognized bank if it qualifies for such transactions under the FETA, but they are not the default instrument for a low-risk wholesaler.Cross-currency swaps are permissible strictly for hedging long-term foreign currency liabilities (e.g., an overseas facility) but are restricted for speculative purposes; a treasury operation would need to document the underlying exposure and obtain the bank's FX derivative eligibility approval.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility and legally constrained. South Korea does not recognize third-country electronic money institutions (EMIs) or digital bank licenses issued in the EU/UK as valid payment service providers for domestic corporate accounts. Global EMIs such as Revolut Business, Wise, or Airwallex cannot open a local KRW account or provide a domestic account number for Yuhan Hoesa. Foreign EMIs may only operate as money transfer businesses under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act if they register with the Financial Services Commission (FSC), which effectively requires a local subsidiary and full licensing, a process unrealistic for a low-risk wholesaler. Domestic internet-only banks (KakaoBank, Toss Bank) are restricted to consumer and small business banking; they do not robustly support cross-border letters of credit, documentary collections, or foreign investment capital verification. The correct treasury rail remains a tier-1 traditional commercial bank with a dedicated corporate foreign exchange desk.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the Commercial Act of Korea, a Yuhan Hoesa has no statutory minimum paid-up capital. However, because the entity will operate as a licensed investment brokerage under the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act, the Financial Services Commission prescribes a minimum initial paid-up capital of KRW 5,000,000,000 for retail securities brokerage, or any higher amount set by the FSC at the time of application. This capital must be fully paid before the FSC license is issued and must be maintained in a blocked escrow deposit account at a domestic bank; a balance confirmation certificate issued by the bank must be attached to both the court incorporation registration and the FSC license application. The escrow funds cannot be released until the incorporation registration has been completed and the FSC license has been approved, except as expressly permitted under insolvency or statutory void-ab-initio rules.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for industrial civil defense fire permits or warehouse fire approval, because no physical factory or storage facility is operated. As a standard office premises, the building owner must obtain the ordinary Fire Safety Inspection and Occupancy Certificate from the local fire station before occupancy. The company must comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act by appointing a workplace safety manager, conducting emergency evacuation drills, maintaining extinguishers and clear exit paths, and recording workplace accident statistics. No additional chemical, structural or industrial civil defense clearance is required for this regulated financial services entity.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS); online portal: Home Tax Service (Hometax) at https://www.hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

Within 20 days after business commencement, the Yuhan Hoesa (domestic LLC) must file the business registration application with the local NTS tax office or through Hometax, attaching the court-certified corporate registration, articles of incorporation, and foreign investment notification certificate. NTS then issues the Business Registration Number (BRN) which functions as the VAT identification number. For foreign non-resident entities without a Korean establishment supplying VAT-liable services, registration is effected through a tax representative under the mandatory branch/land-based registration rules; the application is submitted with the foreign entity's home-country registration certificate, name and address details, and the designated Korean tax representative's identification. The tax representative must be a Korean resident or domestic corporation authorized to receive NTS notices.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration from the first business supply (no turnover threshold); simplified taxpayer scheme for small businesses with annual revenue not exceeding 80,000,000 KRW (as adjusted by law).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to exported goods and to services supplied to a non-resident where the service is either performed outside Korea or is confirmed as consumed outside Korea. For offshore securities brokerage, the commission/service fee is zero-rated when the client is a non-resident, the execution of the trade is confirmed abroad, and the service shows evident overseas consumption; the brokerage contract and order records must evidence the overseas supply. Otherwise, financial brokerage services are VAT-exempt if performed in Korea.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Cargo Documents)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing export from Korea
  • Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean bank confirming repatriation of export proceeds
  • Signed offshore brokerage contract, overseas client identification files, and execution confirmations

Every VAT-registered business must issue a Korean electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) through the NTS Home Tax Service electronic invoicing system; invoice data must be transmitted to NTS within 1 day after issuing the invoice. There is no separate real-time fiscal clearance stamp, but the NTS electronic invoice matching system validates transmitted data against compliance criteria and flags anomalies. Anti-fraud digital stamping is integrated into the NTS e-invoice platform.

NTS cross-validates export declarations with customs manifest data and bank foreign-exchange remittance certificates. Zero-rated sales lacking the required documentation or remittance proof are recharged with 10% VAT plus penalty tax; fraudulent claims attract added penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes (up to 40% of avoided VAT) and criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalised current-account and capital-account regime for the Korean won. The won is convertible at market rates through authorised foreign exchange banks for trade, service, investment, and treasury transactions. Current-account payments do not require prior exchange control approval, but every foreign-exchange transaction should be supported by an underlying document and reported to the Bank of Korea as part of balance-of-payments statistics. Capital-account transactions operate under a negative-list approach: inward foreign direct investment, cross-border loans, overseas investment, and financial derivatives generally require reporting or prior approval depending on category and size. Profit repatriation via dividends is freely permitted after tax clearance and audit certification, but the handling bank will require audited financial statements, board resolution, tax payment receipts, and a purpose code. The practical hurdle for a high-risk offshore securities brokerage is not exchange control prohibition, but documentation completeness and bank de-risking. The BOK may impose macro-prudential measures, such as aggregate foreign-exchange derivative position limits, which indirectly affect hedging availability. FATCA/CRS confirmation and anti-tax-avoidance declarations remain unavoidable. Overall, there is no material convertibility restriction on legitimate commercial flows, but the foreign-exchange bank has full compliance discretion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and related BOK enforcement rules, resident exporters must collect export proceeds within four months from the date of the export declaration. The handling foreign exchange bank can grant an extension of up to two months when justifiable circumstances such as documentary discrepancies are evidenced, and the extension is reported to the Bank of Korea. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; exporters may keep the foreign currency in a domestic foreign-currency account at an authorised bank or convert it into KRW at market rates. All export proceeds must nevertheless be received through a licensed foreign exchange bank and cannot be settled through offshore EMIs or unlicensed payment intermediaries.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as central bank, with foreign exchange administration jointly exercised through the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and BOK Foreign Exchange Trading Regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability rating: Non-Viable for any standalone bank-like onboarding. South Korean law only recognises foreign exchange banks authorised under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act for FX settlement, corporate deposits, and SWIFT clearing. Offshore digital EMI providers have no legal ability to open KRW accounts for a Korean entity, issue local IBAN numbers, or perform capital verification. A fintech that holds an Electronic Financial Transactions Act licence may act only as an electronic payment/prepaid service in KRW, not as a corporate treasury clearing bank. For this high-risk securities brokerage, a Korean Tier-1 commercial bank is mandatory for compliant segregated client wiring and FX.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

21% top statutory bracket for taxable income above KRW 20 billion plus a 10% local income tax surcharge, producing a 24.2% aggregate top headline rate; lower brackets are 9% and 19%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% statutory withholding on outbound cash and deemed dividends paid by a Korean corporation to non-resident shareholders, plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the withholding, producing a 22% aggregate domestic rate. A Korean permanent establishment remitting branch profits after tax is additionally exposed to a 22% branch profits tax, as applicable under the Corporate Tax Act.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; Country-by-Country Reporting is triggered at KRW 100 billion consolidated group turnover. The National Tax Service may nonetheless require contemporaneous documentation below these thresholds for offshore brokerage structures bearing treaty, conduit, or captive-intermediary indicia.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies a substance-over-form and beneficial-ownership anti-avoidance doctrine under the Income Tax Act, the Corporate Tax Act, and the International Tax Adjustment Act. Although Korea is not a Crown Dependency subject to the OECD ESR template, an offshore securities brokerage Yuhan Hoesa must prove Core Income-Generating Activities including client contracting, order execution, trade booking, risk management, valuation, and compliance oversight. A shell entity lacking local employees and decision-making control will be denied treaty benefits, its income re-attributed to the non-resident principal, and its intercompany fees recharacterised as constructive dividends.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act additional taxes of 40% of evaded national tax plus 10% local surtax penalty; Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3(1) imposes imprisonment not exceeding five years and/or a fine not exceeding KRW 100 million, increased for evasion of KRW 500 million or more.

Director personal liability: Korean courts pierce the corporate veil where directors engaged in intentional concealment; directors are jointly and severally liable to the National Tax Service for the unpaid tax, penalty surcharges, and interest. The director becomes a delinquent taxpayer subject to travel ban and passport invalidation, and criminally liable to imprisonment up to seven years with court-ordered restitution of the hidden profits.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Shell Conduit Entity

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 4 and 5 permit the National Tax Service to recompute taxable income on an arm's length basis; Article 26 penalises failure to submit transfer pricing documents by up to 30% of the adjusted tax amount; the Framework Act under-reporting penalty adds 40% for fraudulent transfer-pricing concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute sham pricing are personally liable under the Korean Commercial Act for damages to creditors and the tax authority; personal assets may be attached for the tax deficit, the director is disqualified from managing any regulated Korean financial company, and a criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act can impose imprisonment or a fine when the arrangement is a cloak for concealment.
Unlicensed Securities Brokerage and AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: FSCMA Articles 44 and 448 impose imprisonment up to five years or a fine up to KRW 200 million for unregistered financial investment business; the AML Act separately penalises failure to report suspicious transactions and can result in asset freezes, corrective orders, and licence revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the unlicensed brokerage are jointly and severally liable for client losses, subject to travel bans and passport invalidation, and criminally liable to imprisonment up to five years; foreign directors can be prosecuted in absentia with enforcement pursued through mutual legal assistance and extradition channels.

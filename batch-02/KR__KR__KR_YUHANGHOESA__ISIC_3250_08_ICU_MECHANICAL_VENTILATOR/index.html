Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. Contributions must be fully paid by the subscription-close date and evidenced by a bank-issued capital payment certificate annexed to the court filing. There is no blocked escrow account rule under Korean company law. However, for the ISIC 3250 invasive ICU ventilator facility under the Korean Medical Devices Act, the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety and counterparty banks will evaluate capital adequacy; a paid-in capital of at least KRW 100,000,000 is recommended, and the official registration fee estimate above is based on that recommended capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the National Fire Agency or delegated local fire station under the Act on Fire Prevention and Safety: the facility must obtain a civil defense and evacuation plan approval, fire alarm and suppression system acceptance, and a permit for storage of medical oxygen cylinders and any compressed gases. Structural load inspection by the local district building authority is required for cleanroom mezzanines and rooftop HVAC and air handling units under the Building Act. Workplace safety clearance from the Ministry of Employment and Labor is required before fabrication begins, including submission of an occupational safety and health management plan, chemical hazard communication training, local exhaust ventilation verification, gas cylinder restraint inspection, emergency egress plan, and periodic atmospheric monitoring for cleaning agents, sterilants, and respirable particles used in the ventilator production line.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea and its online electronic portal HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); export declarations and customs processing are administered by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS system (unipass.customs.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

After the Yuhan Hoesa is duly incorporated in the Korean Commercial Registration Court, the entity must file a business registration application with the district NTS tax office having jurisdiction over its registered place of business, before commencing the manufacture of medical ventilator equipment. Under Article 168 of the Value-Added Tax Act and Article 34 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, business registration is mandatory regardless of turnover. The application is filed by the statutory representative either in person at the district tax office or electronically through HomeTax. Required substantiation includes the corporate registry extract issued by the court registry, articles of incorporation, evidence of ownership or lease of the registered business premises, identity documents of the statutory representative and beneficial foreign shareholders, foreign investment reporting confirmation under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and the appointment certificate of a resident tax representative, which is mandatory for non-resident shareholders or non-resident representatives under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Upon satisfactory submission, the NTS issues a Business Registration Certificate containing the entity's ten-digit business operator registration number, which serves simultaneously as the Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number and the Value-Added Tax registration number. The same number is used for Korean VAT, corporate income tax, and withholding tax accounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a domesticly incorporated Yuhan Hoesa is treated as a domestic corporation subject to Korean corporate income tax on worldwide income; no pass-through taxation is available)

Available election forms

None. The Korean tax system does not provide a check-the-box election or a general hybrid mismatch entity-classification election. A Yuhan Hoesa is taxed as a domestic corporation at the entity level as a matter of law. No consolidated or group taxation election exists for ordinary industrial manufacturers, and the tax year must be the calendar year unless the Commissioner of the National Tax Service separately approves a different business year.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic manufacturers: no turnover threshold; every business operator supplying goods or services in Korea must obtain VAT registration before commencing business. Non-resident electronic service providers: a special mandatory registration threshold of KRW 300,000,000 per year applies to supplies of electronic services to non-registered Korean consumers under the Special Value-Added Tax Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods is zero-rated under Article 6 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act when the goods physically leave Korea under a valid Korean Customs export declaration and title and risk pass to a foreign buyer. Qualifying offshore services, including certain digital services, are zero-rated when supplied to non-resident recipients and used or consumed entirely outside Korea. For the manufacture and sale of ICU invasive mechanical ventilators to overseas healthcare institutions, zero rating is available only where there is actual physical export from Korea, the export shipment is covered by a customs export declaration in the seller's name, and the export proceeds are repatriated to Korea through formal banking channels.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry / export clearance receipt from the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Korea
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Original export sales contract or dated purchase order from the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued Foreign Exchange Transaction/Receipt Confirmation evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Company tax invoice and business registration certificate; where applicable, certificate of origin for trade agreement benefits

For all business-to-business supplies, a seller must issue an electronic tax invoice through the HomeTax e-invoice system and transmit the e-invoice data to the National Tax Service within one day after issuance. Korean VAT thus operates on mandatory near-real-time electronic reporting rather than pre-clearance fiscal stamping for general manufactured goods. Cash receipts must be issued to individual consumers on request through the mandatory cash receipt device network. No anti-fraud digital stamp is required for ICU mechanical ventilators. Export shipments are documented and cleared through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic declaration system, which feeds export evidence used in the VAT zero-rating review.

The National Tax Service scrutinizes export zero-rating claims by matching VAT returns with Korea Customs Service export declarations and bank foreign exchange receipts. If any claimed export documentation is missing, forged, or inconsistent, the NTS will disallow the zero rating and assess 10% VAT on the original supply. Under the Framework Act on National Taxes, the additional tax for underreported or fraudulently unreported tax can reach 20% or 40% of the assessed VAT amount, and fraudulent tax refunds or unreasonable VAT refund claims are subject to mandatory additional tax plus interest and may trigger criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, including imprisonment or fines for wilful evasion. The retrospective clawback period is generally five years, extended to ten years in cases of false documents or fraudulent intent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates an open-market, OECD Article 8-liberal regime in which the Korean won is fully convertible for current-account trade and service transactions, but capital-account transactions, including inbound foreign direct investment, equity subscriptions, loans, and derivative contracts, remain on a filing/notification or prior-approval track under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Export proceeds, import settlement, and service remittances can flow through designated foreign exchange banks without mandatory exchange surrender; the exporter may retain foreign currency in a domestic foreign exchange bank account. Profit remittance, represented by dividends and licence fees, is lawful after corporate tax payment and an external transaction supporting-document review at the receiving bank; the remitting bank must verify the shareholder register, board dividend resolution, audited financial statements, tax payment certificate, and FDI report number. A common hurdle is mismatch between the original FDI route and the payee bank/investor name, so remittance is delayed until the foreign investment report is amended. Further, the won is not an internationally fully deliverable currency offshore; settlement for non-resident counterparties is generally quoted in USD or KRW onshore through Seoul branches, which creates an implicit NDF valuation market for hedging. The Bank of Korea and the MOEF monitoring system also create a documentation trail for every Korean won transfer above the reporting threshold, requiring banks to submit clear records to the Korea Foreign Exchange Information System. As a result, the entity can receive inbound capital and remit profits freely, but compliance with the reporting thresholds and the substance-to-payment documentary discipline is the real convertibility constraint.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For general goods exports, export proceeds must be collected and credited through a designated foreign exchange bank within one year from the export declaration date unless a longer settlement period is pre-approved by the bank. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; no statutorily fixed percentage of export foreign currency proceeds must be surrendered into Korean won. The exporter may hold receipts in a domestic foreign-currency account, but each receipt must be matched to the underlying export declaration, commercial invoice, proof of shipment, and freight document in the Korea Foreign Exchange Information System. Late, unreported, or mismatched receipts trigger administrative fines and AML scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of the Republic of Korea); the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) holds the principal foreign exchange policy authority and jointly administers the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Foreign Exchange Regulations through designated foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Not viable as primary banking. South Korea does not recognise any foreign electronic money institution or offshore fintech EMI as a full deposit-taking bank. Foreign EMIs are not permitted to open resident Korean won accounts or conduct cross-border money transfer services unless separately licensed as a payment institution under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and registered with the Financial Services Commission. Even a licensed non-bank would require a physical registered place of business, local AML/KYC capacity, Financial Supervisory Service examination, and cannot offer trade-loan, LC advising, import/export settlement, or foreign-exchange hedging infrastructure. Therefore, a Yuhan Hoesa should use a domestic Tier-1 bank for all capital, trade, and liquidity rails and avoid offshore EMI routing for medical-capital equipment transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must file a foreign direct investment report and open an FDI capital escrow account at the chosen designated Korean bank. The overseas shareholder remits cash in foreign currency by SWIFT MT103, using the bank-assigned foreign investor reference and the FDI capital-investment purpose code. The bank credits the non-resident capital account in Korean won or in the original foreign currency, updates the Korea Foreign Exchange Information System, and the funds may only be applied to the eligible business use stated in the FDI report. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10,000,000,000 (10 billion Korean Won) in cross-border intercompany transactions in a tax year triggers mandatory Local File and Master File documentation; group consolidated revenue of KRW 100,000,000,000 triggers Country-by-Country Report.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not use the OECD ESR label, but Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes requires substance-over-form application, Article 17-2 provides general anti-avoidance rules, the Korean Courts routinely pierce sham entities without local offices or board authority, and the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs contains CFC rules treating offshore profits as distributed through attributed income if the foreign entity lacks business substance and is operated for Korean commercial benefit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion in the Corporate Income Tax Return (False Sales Omission)

Statutory sanction: Under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-3(2), unfaithful under-reporting is penalized at 40% of the evaded tax amount, plus daily overdue interest at 0.022%; criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act Article 3 include up to 2 years in prison or a fine up to KRW 100 million, escalated to 5 years for amounts above KRW 100 million under the Specific Tax Crimes Aggravated Punishment Act.

Director personal liability: The veil will be pierced under the Korean Commercial Act Article 401, making the director who signed the false return liable for the unpaid tax and damages; the NTS can issue a travel-ban (departure restriction) on the responsible director and freeze personal bank accounts under the National Tax Collection Act; if the Korean Yuhan Hoesa was a mere instrumentality of the director, the court will treat its assets and the director's private assets as a single pool and may impose imprisonment up to 10 years for aggravated tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation Through a Shell Intermediary

Statutory sanction: The NTS will reallocate the arm's-length profit under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 60 plus impose a 30% to 40% unfaithful underreporting surcharge under Framework Act Article 47-3; failure to submit the Local File or Master File for intercompany transactions above KRW 10 billion carries additional penalties of up to 30 million KRW per file and the denial of evidence in an audit.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved and signed the false intercompany contracts are jointly and severally liable for the Korean tax deficiency under Commercial Act Article 401 and may suffer contract criminal liability for document forgery under Criminal Act Articles 231-234, with imprisonment up to 10 years; travel-ban and blacklisting will be imposed by the NTS while administrative restitution remains unpaid, and the Korean court will disregard the shell entity due to fraus legis.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Non-Resident Dividend / Royalty Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is liable for the uncollected tax under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 19, plus a 10% penalty for non-remittance and daily overdue interest at 0.022%; the Korean Tax Offense Punishment Act authorizes a criminal fine of up to KRW 100 million for intentional failure to withhold, and the illegal distribution of profits to non-residents subject to a court order to pay the tax with interest.

Director personal liability: The responsible director who ordered the wire transfer without employment of withholding tax is treated as a co-debtor for the unpaid corporate tax under the joint-responsibility provisions of Framework Act Article 39; personal assets of the director can be seized, a blacklisting and departure-cancelation order is issued to prevent exit from South Korea, and the director faces imprisonment up to 2 years for willful violation of the withholding obligation. If external shareholders profited from the non-compliance, the court can pierce the Korean entity and restore the subsidiary shareholder's receivables for the withholding tax, thereby causing personal liability to be covered out of the director's private estate.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office or commercial mailbox is rejected for a remote BPO call center if the address serves as the registered address for service of process and tax authority correspondence. The Korean Commercial Act requires each corporation to maintain its head office at a definite, physically accessible location, and the National Tax Service will refuse electronic registration if the address is merely a virtual workspace without a resident staff member authorized to accept tax documents. A serviced office in Seoul or an approved business center providing a physical office space, a lease agreement, a dedicated mail handling service, and a resident director's office is permissible. A non-resident BPO operator cannot rely on a commercial mailbox provider without office space; the Korean tax authority will classify the arrangement as an artificial substance and impose a 2% annual penalty on gross fees for the unregistered place of business, or deny the head office registration entirely.

None under the Korean Commercial Act; no minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or blocked-account license capital is required for a remote BPO call center. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for ordinary VAT registration: any business supplier in Korea must register. A simplified taxable person election may apply to small businesses with annual revenue at or below KRW 80,000,000; above that turnover full VAT returns and standard electronic tax invoice duties are mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (1) goods exported outside the Republic of Korea, evidenced by a Korea Customs Service export declaration acceptance and shipment; and (2) services supplied to foreign non-residents where the service is actually performed/consumed outside Korea, the consideration is received in foreign currency from outside Korea, and the supply is outside the scope of domestic consumption. For the remote BPO call-center model, the outsourced helpdesk and tele-sales services must be rendered to a foreign principal for a foreign end-customer base, not to a Korean resident, and the contract must demonstrate the offshore B2B service nature. Documentary proof must include the signed service contract, foreign currency invoice, and foreign-exchange receipt certificate. The statutory deadline for repatriating export consideration is 180 days from the export/service date.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration acceptance certificate (export Bill of Entry / clearance certificate) showing HS classification and FOB value
  • Original or certified copy of the Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill) evidencing shipment of the goods to a foreign destination
  • Foreign exchange bank Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent foreign currency receipt certificate evidencing receipt of the export consideration in a foreign currency

Mandatory electronic tax invoices under the Korean VAT Act, transmitted through the NTS Home Tax Service or an NTS-approved Electronic Tax Invoice solution. A registered taxpayer must issue an e-invoice for taxable B2B supplies as statutorily provided and transmit the e-invoice data to the NTS by the statutory deadline through Hometax submission. The system assigns an NTS-received reference number upon successful validation; there is no separate QR-code stamping or real-time fiscal clearance. For B2C payments, the Cash Receipt System is mandatory for card and online transactions unless exempt or zero-rated, and cash receipts are reported to the NTS.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service may jointly verify export declarations, logistics records, and foreign-exchange receipts. If zero-rating is claimed without complete documentation, or if export consideration is not received within 180 days and the failure is not remedied, the export is reclassified as a domestic taxable supply and VAT at 10% is assessed on the transaction value. The VAT refund originally obtained is clawed back with interest and statutory punitive additional tax; fraudulent export documentation may result in criminal referral under tax-evasion enforcement rules and, in serious cases, export licensing restrictions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is broadly convertible for current account purposes, consistent with South Korea's Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement. Cross-border service payments, trade settlements, ordinary professional fees, and dividend remittances derived from genuine business activity are executable through domestic foreign exchange banks upon submission of the underlying transaction documentation. Current account receipts and payments do not require standalone central bank pre-approval in most cases; instead, the relevant foreign exchange bank verifies the documentation and reports the transaction through BOK's Foreign Exchange Information System. However, the capital account remains systematically controlled and subject to a reporting-and-licensing framework under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and its Enforcement Decree. Inward direct investment by a foreign person or company into a Korean Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA); the funds can be wired into a bank-designated foreign investment account and then converted into KRW for operational use. De minimis transactions may be aggregated for reporting, while any capital inflow structured as a shareholder loan, convertible instrument, or other security is separately reportable and may be treated as a restringent capital transaction. Profit remittance of an attributable dividend is treated as current account, but the bank will require proof of corporate income tax settlement, audited financial statements, withholding evidence, and signed board minutes. Surrender of export proceeds is not mandatory in the sense of a fixed legal ratio, but BPO export receipts must be repatriated into Korea and either held in a foreign currency deposit account or converted to KRW within the statutory collection window. Early-stage entities, especially those with opaque beneficial ownership or with payment rails routed through non-bank fintech intermediaries, will face additional documentary scrutiny and may be asked to sign a heightened monitoring undertaking as a condition of account activation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, proceeds from BPO service exports must be collected and brought into Korea within 180 days from the date of service exportation or the contractual due date reasonably attributable to the BPO delivery milestone. The statutory window can be extended by up to 12 months only upon application to the foreign exchange bank with a written commercial justification, such as a disputed invoice or contractual milestone delay. There is no mandatory conversion ratio under current Korean reserve management policy; the legacy 100% export surrender obligation has been abolished. The LLC may retain collected foreign currency in a domestic foreign currency deposit account and decide separately whether and when to sell it into KRW at the interbank market rate. Nevertheless, each collection must be associated with the corresponding export invoice and service contract, reported to the bank through the electronic foreign exchange settlement system, and placed in the accounting records while preserving the underlying SWIFT MT103 or telex confirmation trail. Uncollected proceeds beyond the regulatory timeline are a red flag that can trigger BOK investigation, customs notification, and potential seizure or administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), operating as delegated foreign exchange agent under the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), with the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KFIU) responsible for AML/CFT reporting and targeted financial sanctions administration

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50 billion of annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers full Local File/Master File obligations; KRW 100 billion group consolidated revenue triggers the Master File; KRW 5 billion in related-party service transactions triggers simplified contemporaneous documentation for routine remote BPO services.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not use the Caribbean ESR label but applies equivalent substance-over-form and anti-treaty-shopping principles through the Corporate Income Tax Act and the International Tax Adjustment Act. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa performing offshore BPO call-center services must maintain a real Korean office, local management, employees, client contracts, call-center systems, script ownership, and risk-bearing capacity; if not, the tax authorities may recharacterize the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, apply CFC rules, and treat the Korean entity as a conduit taxable at the ultimate shareholder level.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act provides imprisonment up to 10 years or fines up to ten times the evaded tax; Article 76 of the Corporate Income Tax Act imposes an additional tax of 20% of the under-reported tax and up to 40% for fraudulent false returns; National Tax Service criminal referral and blacklisting are mandatory in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties; civil asset restitution, seizure of bank accounts, corporate-veil piercing, travel bans, exit blacklisting, and imprisonment of responsible individual directors are available where corporate funds were commingled with director assets or where invoices were signed with knowledge of falsity.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's-Length Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: The International Tax Adjustment Act permits the tax authority to adjust income to the arm's-length price and impose interest; failure to submit transfer-pricing documentation triggers penalties up to 20% of the adjusted cross-border transaction amount, daily surcharges, and concurrent additional-tax penalties under Article 76 of the Corporate Income Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false transfer-pricing documentation are personally liable for restitution of misstated profits, may be fined under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act for unreported outward payments, and may face blacklisting by the National Tax Service and criminal indictment if the transfer-pricing misconduct is intentional.
Shell Company, Treaty Shopping, and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Korea will deny treaty benefits under limitation-on-benefits or principal-purpose-test clauses; statutory withholding of 22% on dividends and royalties and 16.5% on interest is reinstated; Article 76 of the Corporate Income Tax Act penalties and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act fines apply to unauthorized outbound remittances; false substance declarations trigger blacklisting and anti-avoidance assessments.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the shell entity has no independent business purpose; directors are personally exposed to restitution of unwithheld expatriated amounts, travel bans, court-ordered asset freezes, disqualification of foreign directors, and imprisonment up to five years under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act for false declarations and unauthorized capital outward remittances.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa. The Articles must specify the authorized and subscribed capital. Before registration the full subscribed capital must be paid into a blocked deposit or escrow account at a licensed Korean bank, and a certificate of deposit must be presented to the court registry. Under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, if foreign ownership is used, funds may remain in an escrow account until incorporation is completed.

Funding & unlock path: Mandatory fire safety certificate from the local fire station under the Fire Services Act; structural load inspection and occupancy certification by the competent municipal building authority; workplace safety and health approval from KOSHA (Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency), including hygiene licensing, ventilation clearances, and food preparation worker medical certificates. High-risk kitchen operations must secure a Civil Defense inspection for refrigerant gas, fire suppression systems, and emergency egress routes.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax Service (Hometax) at https://www.hometax.go.kr; export and import clearance support is handled by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS portal at https://unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

1) After incorporating the Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act, the entity, or its foreign non-resident parent, must apply for statutory business registration with the district tax office of the NTS that has jurisdiction over the registered head office. 2) The application must be accompanied by the company's articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation, foreign company registration extracts if applicable, personal identification of the representative director, and, if no physical Korean office is yet established, a power of attorney appointing a domestic fiscal representative for NTS procedure. 3) The NTS verifies the corporate existence and then issues a Business Registration Certificate containing the 10-digit Business Registration Number; that same number is the VAT registration number and functions as the legal TIN for corporate tax, e-Tax Invoices, customs export declarations, and foreign-exchange reporting. 4) To carry on export business, the Yuhan Hoesa must additionally complete the foreign trade business registration with the Korea Customs Service in UNI-PASS, using the NTS Business Registration Number as the underlying identifier for the customs exporter ID. 5) For non-resident entities without a Korean business presence that are nonetheless making taxable supplies in Korea, the NTS will accept registration through a designated tax representative; the issued Business Registration Number remains the sole VAT ID.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for corporations: a Yuhan Hoesa must register for VAT from its first taxable supply. The KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable turnover threshold only determines eligibility for simplified taxable person status of individual sole proprietors, and does not exempt a company from standard VAT registration and e-invoicing obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Korean VAT law, goods supplied for export to foreign countries and certain services rendered overseas are zero-rated. For airport bonded in-flight catering, the following criteria must be met to claim 0% VAT: (i) the meals are manufactured in an airport bonded catering production facility or licensed airport supply area; (ii) the meals are physically delivered onto and uplifted by an aircraft operating an international route from Korea; (iii) an aircraft stores/catering manifest or equivalent flight document proves the quantity and destination; (iv) a zero-rated e-Tax Invoice is issued to the airline or its local agent; and (v) the export proceeds are received in foreign currency in Korea within 180 days from the export declaration/customs clearance. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied by the entity to non-resident customers would also be zero-rated when consumed outside Korea, but any B2B digital services supplied to a Korean VAT-registered recipient remain taxable at 10%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate) filed with and accepted by the Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS, identifying the catering supplies as export, bonded uplift or aircraft stores shipment
  • Air Waybill, export cargo manifest, or international aircraft stores list, including a signed catering uplift/loading manifest from the airline or ground handling agent evidencing that the in-flight meals were loaded onto the specified international flight
  • Foreign Exchange Repatriation Certificate or Foreign Exchange Purchase Certificate (foreign currency purchase confirmation) issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, evidencing receipt of export proceeds within the 180-day period, or an approved netting/settlement certificate where application is accepted
  • Zero-rated e-Tax Invoice bearing the Yuhan Hoesa VAT registration number and the 0% zero-rated code, together with its NTS transmission acceptance record
  • Sales agreement or airline purchase order with the non-Korean airline or its approved ground handling subsidiary, and the payment rail confirmation (aviation contract wire or monthly ACH)

The e-Tax Invoice (electronic tax invoice) is mandatory for every taxable supply of goods or services made by the Yuhan Hoesa. The invoice must be transmitted electronically to the NTS HomeTax system, or through an NTS-integrated ERP/electronic invoice service provider via NTS Open API or certified ASP channels, no later than the close of the day following the supply date. Korea does not employ hardware-based real-time transaction clearance or digital stamping in the sense used in some EU countries; instead, the NTS administers online integrity controls for e-Tax Invoices, including a certified corporate certificate, unique sequential invoice numbers, NTS certification and a machine-readable QR code on printed representations. Invoice data is matched centrally against VAT returns, cash receipts, and customs export data for anti-fraud purposes. E-invoices are also mandatory for zero-rated export sales, and the Yuhan Hoesa must issue and transmit the zero-rated e-Tax Invoice without any paper-only exception.

NTS and Korea Customs Service will cross-check export declarations, aircraft departure status, load/catering manifests, e-Tax Invoices, and banking import/export proceeds records. If the exporter cannot provide a complete statutory file proving physical export and foreign-exchange repatriation, the 0% rate is reversed and the supply is recharacterised as domestic taxable supply: the Yuhan Hoesa must then pay 10% VAT, plus statutory overdue interest and additional tax under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes, including daily late-payment additional tax and short-reporting additions for negligent understatement. Any VAT refund paid on the basis of the disallowed zero-rating is clawed back together with interest. A false or sham export, forged customs export documents, or non-repatriated export proceeds beyond 180 days without an approved extension may expose the entity and responsible officers to criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, including imprisonment and fines calculated on the tax evaded or the amount of foreign exchange withheld.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a fundamentally liberalised current-account regime, permitting routine foreign exchange transactions for trade, services, dividends, and royalties with minimal administrative friction. For airline in-flight catering services, Korean-registered legal entities may receive cross-border wire transfers through domestic banks under standard receipt notifications to the designated foreign exchange bank. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), current account transactions are freely permitted, while capital account transactions such as direct equity investment, loans, and securities transactions require statutory declaration or reporting through banks. A foreign-invested Yuhan Hoesa must file a Foreign Investment Report with KOTRA or a delegated domestic bank before inbound capital can be wired. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is permitted on an annual basis once audited financial statements are adopted, but the remittance will trigger dividend withholding tax at 22% absent treaty reduction, and banks will require proof of the foreign investment report, tax clearance, and board resolutions. Korea does not impose a compulsory conversion of export proceeds into KRW, but export proceeds must be collected within the statutory inward collection period of six months from shipment unless an exemption is granted. The Won is freely convertible; however, banks must report suspicious cross-border flows and aggregate FX transactions to the Bank of Korea and Korea Customs Service. This is therefore an open but dokumentarily rigorous regime for treasury operations, and no exchange-certificate approval is needed for standard aviation catering contracts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from the in-flight catering business must be received within six months from the date of shipment under FETA Article 14 and related Bank of Korea regulations. There is no mandatory minimum conversion ratio; export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted to KRW at the exporter's discretion. Inward collection extensions require a documented application to the designated foreign exchange bank, typically with airline payment terms, reason for delay, and revised settlement schedule. Exporters must report the inward receipt to the designated bank or customs within the prescribed timeline, and failure to collect within six months without authorisation can trigger administrative penalties and possible foreign exchange offence review.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and delegated authority from the Korean Ministry of Economy and Finance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Pure offshore fintech EMIs and digital-challenger payment service providers are not viable as primary operating banks for a Korean Yuhan Hoesa. Korean banking law reserves domestic foreign-currency account opening and Foreign Investment Report handling to licensed banking institutions. Internet-only banks such as KakaoBank and Toss Bank may provide KRW accounts to domestic corporations, but their appetite and operational readiness for foreign-invested companies with cross-border aviation receivables is moderate at best, and they do not offer the full array of trade finance instruments, MT103 processing, or foreign exchange hedging desks required for airline catering operations. Offshore non-bank EMI entities such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Airwallex cannot provide a compliant Korean settlement account or act as the designated foreign exchange bank for FETA reporting. They may be used only as auxiliary collections channels where a licensed Korean bank is used as the treasury anchor, and even then the treasurer should be aware that Korean beneficiary account details will not include offshore EMI IBANs. The prudent blueprint is to rely exclusively on a top-tier Korean commercial bank as the settlement anchor and treat any fintech EMI as a dismissed option.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Unexplained beneficial ownership chain with foreign shareholders hiding behind an intermediate offshore entity.

Root cause: Unexplained beneficial ownership chain with foreign shareholders hiding behind an intermediate offshore entity.

Defense evidence: A full corporate ownership chart from the final individual human beneficial owner to the Korean Yuhan Hoesa, showing each intermediate entity's jurisdiction, registration number, and constitutional documents. This must be supported by audited financial statements of the ultimate shareholder, a sworn beneficial ownership declaration, passport copies of the ultimate beneficial owner with legalisation, and internationally accepted background checks or sanctions screening results from a reputable screening provider.
Adverse media or potential PEP status of a Korean statutory director or ultimate shareholder.

Root cause: Adverse media or potential PEP status of a Korean statutory director or ultimate shareholder.

Defense evidence: A personal bank reference letter from a Tier-1 financial institution in the director's domicile, a comprehensive CV or business biography, extraction from national corporate registry showing other legitimate board positions, and a formal written declaration of politically exposed person status with detailed source-of-wealth documentation including salary contracts, real estate registration extracts, stock sales records, and tax statements covering the last five years.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be routed through a designated foreign exchange bank in Korea. Before funds are wired, the foreign investor must submit a foreign-investment report to KOTRA or a delegated bank branch, specifying the total investment amount, shareholding percentage, and intended business scope. After report acceptance, the investor opens a temporary foreign currency receipt account under an escrow arrangement or a dedicated capital account with the Korean bank, and the overseas bank remits the equity contribution via a SWIFT MT103, referencing the foreign-investment report number. The Korean bank will verify the incoming funds, attribute them to the capital account under the Capital Account Liberalisation framework, and issue a Certificate of Foreign Capital Investment, which is essential for subsequent dividend remittances, repatriation of capital, and legal review by the Ministry of Economy and Finance. The funds must remain son the dedicated account until the corporate registration and Korean FDI notification procedures are complete; once documented, the funds may be transferred into the operating KRW account and used for licensed business expenditures. All post-transaction foreign exchange statements must be archived to satisfy potential audits by the National Tax Service and the Bank of Korea.For trade-related disbursements, the Korean Yuhan Hoesa will use its designated foreign exchange bank to execute SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for low-value transaction payments and recurring annuity-style payments to overseas raw material suppliers. For high-value or documentary trade settlements, the bank will issue Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits) to the foreign seller, with terms requiring presentation of the air waybill, invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and phytosanitary certificates for inbound food ingredients. For open-account settlements with established overseas suppliers, the treasury can utilise documentary collections (D/P and D/A) through the Korean bank's correspondent banking network, though the airline's monthly settlement cycle is more efficiently handled via clean MT103 remittances tied to the monthly ACH reconciliation of meal counts. Domestic treasury obligations, such as kitchen payroll, airport ground handling fees, and refrigeration bills, should settle through the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute (KFTC) retail or corporate ACH rails, with the appropriate corporate business day cut-off. The bank will require a written instruction authorising the use of the specific purpose code for each outward payment, especially for food procurement, freight, royalties, and related services, to ensure alignment with FETA reporting requirements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Pure offshore fintech EMIs and digital-challenger payment service providers are not viable as primary operating banks for a Korean Yuhan Hoesa. Korean banking law reserves domestic foreign-currency account opening and Foreign Investment Report handling to licensed banking institutions. Internet-only banks such as KakaoBank and Toss Bank may provide KRW accounts to domestic corporations, but their appetite and operational readiness for foreign-invested companies with cross-border aviation receivables is moderate at best, and they do not offer the full array of trade finance instruments, MT103 processing, or foreign exchange hedging desks required for airline catering operations. Offshore non-bank EMI entities such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Airwallex cannot provide a compliant Korean settlement account or act as the designated foreign exchange bank for FETA reporting. They may be used only as auxiliary collections channels where a licensed Korean bank is used as the treasury anchor, and even then the treasurer should be aware that Korean beneficiary account details will not include offshore EMI IBANs. The prudent blueprint is to rely exclusively on a top-tier Korean commercial bank as the settlement anchor and treat any fintech EMI as a dismissed option.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national top marginal CIT rate (progressive brackets: 9% up to KRW 200m, 19% up to KRW 20bn, 21% up to KRW 300bn, 24% over KRW 300bn) plus 10% local income tax surtax, yielding a combined top effective rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax) for dividends paid to non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000

Economic substance scope: While Korea does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing analogous to the UAE, the substance-over-form doctrine is embedded in the Corporate Income Tax Act and the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LICAA). An inflight catering entity must demonstrate actual decision-making, procurement, production, and ramp delivery in Korea through active employees, physical kitchen facilities, and airline contracts. Where a shell vehicle is used only to hold treaty benefits and bonded customs privileges, the NTS will disregard the vehicle, reallocate profits to the real taxpayer, and apply CFC rules to penalize offshore passive income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, Article 3, the company is liable for a fine up to 3 times the evaded tax, plus administrative negligence tax of 20%–40% of the unreported amount; the NTS may additionally revoke the bonded warehouse license under the Customs Act.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced because the tax offense is committed through deliberate acts of the directors. They become jointly and severally liable for the assessed taxes and fines, and may be subject to asset seizure, entry refusal (travel blacklist) if the evaded tax exceeds KRW 300 million, and criminal imprisonment up to 5 years. All directors of a Korean LLC are personally accountable for intentional tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Offshore Profit Leakage

Statutory sanction: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LICAA), Articles 4 and 5, the NTS adjusts taxable income to the arm's length price, imposes a 10% penalty on the understated income, and levies a KRW 50 million fine for material documentation failure; treaty mutual agreement procedure relief is denied for transactions tainted by intentional manipulation.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized non-arm's length pricing are personally responsible for the adjusted tax, additions, and penalties if the corporate entity cannot pay; their participation constitutes joint tortious conduct under the Commercial Act. They may be blacklisted from leaving Korea, their Korean bank accounts may be frozen, and each director can face up to 5 years' imprisonment for deliberate false reporting.
Shell Company Abuse / Treaty Shopping and AML-Connected Evasion

Statutory sanction: The Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Economic Offenses, Article 3, sanctions embezzlement/misappropriation with imprisonment up to life for extremely high amounts; the Customs Act allows import duty evasion penalties up to 10 times the evaded duties and seizure of imported kitchen equipment; the Financial Transactions Report Act and AML regulations impose administrative fines up to KRW 100 million per violation and criminal sanctions up to 5 years for money laundering.

Director personal liability: The lack of substance is used to pierce the corporate veil under Korean Supreme Court precedent: the director's personal assets and overseas assets traceable to the shell structure are subject to civil restitution. The director is placed on the financial blacklist, receives a travel ban, and can be imprisoned up to 7 years. If the shell is used to evade foreign taxes on income diverted from Korea, extradition procedures may also be initiated.

A virtual office or commercial mailbox-only arrangement is not permissible for a Yuhan Hoesa manufacturing sterile medical examination nitrile gloves. Under the Commercial Act, the registered head office must be a genuine physical location in the Republic of Korea capable of hosting board records, general meeting minutes, and statutory books. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. However, the full subscribed capital must be actually deposited into a temporary capital subscription account in Korea and remain blocked until after incorporation registration. No escrow bank or government escrow requirement is mandatory for a domestic LLC; any foreign-invested portion must be remitted to Korea through a licensed foreign exchange bank and reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act within the prescribed window.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr); customs-related tax clearance and export validation are handled separately by Korea Customs Service (KCS) via UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Korean Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) with the competent court registration office and obtain the Commercial Registration Certificate under the Commercial Act. Step 2: If foreign investors exist, file a Foreign Investment Notification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act with KOTRA or through the designated foreign exchange bank before or immediately after initial capital contribution. Step 3: Within 20 days of business commencement, file an Application for Business Registration (Business Registration Form) with the competent district tax office or through HomeTax, attaching the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, lease or ownership evidence of the Korean place of business, and shareholder/investor identification documents. Step 4: The district tax office will issue the Business Registration Certificate containing the ten-digit Business Registration Number (BRN); this BRN is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and simultaneously serves as the VAT registration number for indirect tax purposes. Step 5: To perform export customs clearance, the Korean LLC must additionally register as a trader with Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS under the Foreign Trade Act and the Customs Act, obtaining a KCS trader identification code and submitting a daily/each-shipment electronic export declaration. Step 6: A foreign non-resident entity that merely establishes a Korean permanent establishment must also appoint a domestic tax representative and obtain a BRN for that PE; however, the Korean LLC itself is a resident domestic corporation with its own independent tax identity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not permit a US-style check-the-box election for a Yuhan Hoesa. The LLC is per se treated as a domestic corporation taxed at entity level on worldwide income. A qualifying consolidated corporate tax filing under the Corporate Tax Act is not an incentive available for a standalone formation; group consolidated filing is limited to certain domestic wholly-owned subsidiaries that meet statutory combined-entity conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary threshold applies: every business operator making taxable supplies in Korea must register under VAT Act Article 5 regardless of turnover. The simplified taxpayer threshold of KRW 80,000,000 annual supplies applies only to individual proprietors and not to a Yuhan Hoesa LLC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Korea to a foreign consignee qualify for 0% VAT under VAT Act Article 21(1)(1), provided that the goods are actually shipped and exported under an accepted Korea Customs Service Export Declaration, that legal title passes to the overseas buyer, that the supply is not deemed a domestic transaction under VAT Act Article 4, and that the export proceeds are received in conformity with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The zero-rating is available on export sales of sterile examination nitrile gloves under ISIC 3250. Input VAT on raw materials, packaging, labor-associated service supplies, and export-related costs may be refunded via the quarterly/term VAT return to the extent of the zero-rated export ratio.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Clearance Statement) issued through UNI-PASS, containing the export declaration number, exporter name, consignee, HS codes, quantities, FOB value, and shipment route
  • Original transport document: Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air), evidencing that the goods crossed the Korean border to the foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Sales contract, purchase order, or LC/TT evidence establishing the bona fide cross-border sale between the Korean LLC and the overseas buyer
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation Certificate issued by the designated foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in the contracted foreign currency

Electronic Tax Invoices (eTIs) must be issued through HomeTax for B2B supplies and must be transmitted to the National Tax Service by the end of the business day following issuance. There is no separate blockchain-based fiscal stamp, but each electronic tax invoice is authenticated with a unique HomeTax invoice number and electronic signature to prevent fraud. Exporters must obtain an accepted electronic Export Declaration through KCS UNI-PASS before physical shipment; the UNI-PASS validation of the export declaration and KCS seal is the real-time clearance gateway for 0% VAT export zero-rating.

NTS and Korea Customs Service audits will reconcile the Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, commercial invoice, bank wire/LC records, and Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation. If zero-rated export documentation is missing, inconsistent, or fraudulent, the export supply is retrospectively reclassified as a domestic taxable supply; the tax office will assess VAT at 10% plus statutory interest and order recovery of any previously refunded input VAT. Penalties under the VAT Act and Framework Act on National Taxes include an additional 20% of the amount for failures to file or pay, 10% for negligent underreporting, and 40% for fraudulent underreporting or fraudulent refund claims. Criminal liability and imprisonment may be imposed for tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Additionally, violation of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act 180-day inward-remittance deadline can result in customs penalties and administrative fines, even if the export itself was legitimate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised foreign exchange system under Article 4 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, where the Korean won (KRW) is freely convertible for current account transactions provided the transaction is supported by genuine trade or service documentation. Capital account transactions, however, are subject to a prior reporting or licensing regime administered through authorised foreign exchange banks and the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Profit repatriation by a foreign-invested entity is permitted once the firm has filed annual corporate tax returns and obtained a tax clearance certificate, but remittances exceeding USD 10,000 must be reported through the bank with a designated remittance purpose code. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds for KRW; exporters are allowed to hold foreign currency in domestic foreign currency accounts, referred to as 'Foreign Exchange Resident Accounts', without an obligation to convert into KRW within a statutory timeframe. In practice, a foreign entity establishing a local LLC (Yuhan Hoesa) will need to obtain a Foreign Investment Registration Certificate from MOFE or a designated bank before injecting capital, and the capital verification process requires bank confirmation of the actual remittance. Dividends can be remitted abroad with proof of audited financial statements, tax payment receipts, and a board resolution. The overall convertibility is high, but bureaucratic reporting, strict source-wealth verification, and the requirement of a real economic nexus for trade settlement create friction for shell-style structures. The government has WTO-consistent capital controls and a transaction monitoring system through Korea Customs Service and BOK's Foreign Exchange Information Network, making any unusual round-tripping or off-market settlement behaviour a red flag. In short, KRW is convertible for operational purposes, but every cross-border flow must be tagged with a legitimate purpose code and authenticated trade documentation to avoid administrative sanctions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 1998 reform and fully abolished in 2012, South Korea imposes no statutory surrender or conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts in foreign currency accounts and choose whether to convert to KRW. However, receipts from exports exceeding USD 50,000 must be reported to the designated foreign exchange bank within 30 days of receipt, and supporting underlying contract or invoice documentation must be retained for five years for verification by the bank or customs authorities.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) under the delegated authority of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) via the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, non-bank fintech EMI providers (including Revolut, Wise, and similar EU/UK licenced EMIs) are not permitted to open direct KRW current accounts or provide domestic settlement services for a local Yuhan Hoesa unless they operate through a licensed local bank as the sponsor. Even then, the entity would ultimately need direct bank onboarding. Offshore EMIs can only act as a supplementary remittance channel for foreign-currency to foreign-currency flows, but cannot receive inbound capital injections classified as 'capital receipts' nor facilitate outbound KRW conversions because those require an authorised foreign exchange bank reporting. Legal constraints also include KFTC (Korea Financial Telecommunications & Clearings Institute) settlement rails that mandate direct membership or correspondent sponsorship; no realistic path exists for an EMi to service this entity without a domestic banking partner. Practically, a treasurer should engage only a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank for core account structure and use a licensed payment gateway for trade ancillary services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The preferred routing for a foreign investor injecting share capital into a Yuhan Hoesa is as follows: through the investor's home bank, execute a SWIFT MT103 remittance to the designated Foreign Investment Escrow Account (often a 'Foreign Currency Settlement Account' held by the Korean branch of the receiving bank) using purpose code 'KR-01' (capital goods and equity) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The remitting bank must include the company entity ID and the Foreign Investment Certificate serial number in the remittance narrative. Upon credit, the Korean bank will issue a Foreign Investment Verification (or a certificate of receipt) which is filed with the Ministry of Economy and Finance within 30 days. After the company is registered with the Korean Trade Registry, the escrow funds are transferred to the ordinary KRW operating account upon presentation of the share issue report to the tax authority. For working capital loans or shareholder loans, separate purpose codes apply (e.g., KR-09 for long-term intercompany debt) and a Finance Transaction Reporting Form must be filed with total value exceeding KRW 30 million. All inbound wires over USD 10,000 require the bank to record the Korea Customs Service electronic embassy data. Non-compliance with the reporting deadline can result in administrative fines of up to KRW 50 million. The company must also issue share certificates and submit a capital increase registration within three months to the local registry office.

The audit report must be filed with the FSS within one month after the shareholders' meeting approves the financial statements, but no later than April 30 for December fiscal-year-end entities. $0 USD Failure to submit the external audit report or submitting materially false audit reports can subject the company and responsible officers to administrative fines, criminal penalties under the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies (up to criminal imprisonment for egregious violations), sanctions on certified public accountants and audit firms including suspension or fines, and possible administrative enforcement orders compelling corrective filings. Continued non-compliance can lead to designation as an uncooperative company, publication of non-compliance, and restrictions on financing from financial institutions.
Annual Business License Renewal / Business Place Confirmation (Periodic Business Registration Update) The business registration certificate does not require an annual renewal filing per se; however, any change in business status must be reported to the local tax office within 15 days of the change. For medical device manufacturing entities, the manufacturing business license itself must be renewed or confirmed in compliance with the Medical Devices Act, typically subject to periodic inspections/performance reports by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS). Annual re-reporting of business facts may be required in connection with the annual income tax filing and local tax declarations. $0 USD Failure to report changes in business registration information triggers a penalty of up to 20% of the relevant tax amount under the Framework Act on National Taxes, plus late payment interest. Operating without a valid medical device manufacturing license for sterile nitrile examination gloves can trigger criminal penalties including imprisonment up to 5 years or fines up to KRW 50 million under the Medical Devices Act, administrative closure orders, product recall orders, and suspension of business operations.
Compliance with Foreign Investment Reporting / Annual Foreign-Invested Company Status Declaration (if foreign-owned) Foreign-invested enterprises must report changes in foreign investment status to the competent authority within 30 days of any reportable event, including changes in shareholding percentage, capital increases, transfers of shares, or dissolution. There is no separate annual confirmation filing for foreign-invested status, but the annual external audit report must be submitted to the relevant authorities under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act where applicable. Filings are tied to transaction events rather than a fixed annual calendar. $0 USD Failure to report foreign investment changes or submit supporting documents within 30 days may result in administrative fines up to KRW 3 million under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and potential disadvantageous treatment concerning foreign investment benefits, tax incentives, and repatriation privileges. Continue non-compliance may lead to revocation of foreign-invested status benefits.
Annual Local Tax Return (Property Tax, Corporate Tax Surcharge, Local Income Tax) Local corporate income tax is filed with the local government where the head office is located within the same deadline as the national corporate income tax return (by March 31 for December-year-end companies, or April 30 for audited foreign-invested companies with shareholders' meeting approvals). Property taxes are paid annually in two installments: June (for buildings) and September (for land); vehicle taxes and other local taxes follow separate schedules. $0 USD Late filing or underpayment of local corporate income tax triggers a 20% non-filing penalty and daily late payment interest of 0.022% per day on unpaid amounts. Property tax delinquency triggers a 3% penalty and additional 0.75% per month. Persistent non-payment leads to public auction or forced collection of the delinquent property.
Annual Manufacturing License Inspection and Quality Management System (QMS) Compliance Report for Medical Devices Manufacturers of sterile medical devices must register the manufacturing facility and product with MFDS before commencing production. Annual quality management system audits are conducted by MFDS or delegated bodies; facility inspection intervals depend on risk class (Class II sterile single-use nitrile examination gloves are typically subject to routine periodic inspection). Any change in manufacturing processes, packaging, sterilisation methods, or facility relocation must be notified/re-approved in advance. Annual reporting of manufacturing performance is due pursuant to MFDS schedules (often within 30 days after the end of each fiscal year). $0 USD Failure to undergo required QMS inspection or submit annual manufacturing performance reports may trigger administrative fines up to KRW 3 million, suspension of manufacturing license, cancellation of the license, product distribution suspension, product recall orders, and criminal penalties under the Medical Devices Act. Operating without a valid licence after cancellation may lead to imprisonment up to 5 years or fines up to KRW 50 million.
Annual Customs and Foreign Exchange Transaction Reporting (if importing/exporting raw materials or finished goods) Exporters/importers must file customs declarations for each transaction; there is no separate annual return for ordinary trade transactions. However, export/import performance confirmation, foreign exchange transactions involving capital (SWIFT LC/TT wire receipts), and settlement of export proceeds must be reported by the due date prescribed under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act: export proceeds must be converted into Korean won or received within 18 months from the date of export, unless extension is granted. The annual Customs compliance audit (if selected) follows KCS schedules. $0 USD False or omitted customs declarations trigger penalty taxes up to 40% of the evaded amount plus daily late payment interest. Failure to report foreign exchange transactions or receipt of export proceeds can trigger fines up to KRW 100 million under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and criminal sanctions for intentional violations. Trade restrictions, suspension of customs clearance, and exporter status revocation may also apply.
Annual return deadline

Corporate income tax returns for fiscal year ending December 31 must be filed by March 31 of the following year; for fiscal years ending on a date other than December 31, filing is due within three months after the end of the fiscal year. For foreign-invested companies with statutory audit requirements, the filing deadline extends to April 30 for December-year-end entities when financial statements are approved by the shareholders' meeting.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50 billion for manual cross-border transaction documentation (Master File and Local File) per Article 6 of the International Tax Adjustment Act (ITAA); KRW 100 billion for a concurrent 'Country-by-Country Report' filed with the NTS. For smaller transactions, a simplified summary document must be retained if intercompany material or service cross-border transactions exceed KRW 300 million per fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Under the Korean Economic Substance Doctrine applied through the ITAA and the 'substance over form' principle in Article 14 of the Corporate Tax Act, a Korean LLC supplying sterile examination nitrile gloves must demonstrate that its core income-generating activities (CIGA) — such as production planning, procurement of raw latex, quality certification (ISO 13485), and final inspection — occur in Korea. The NTS will deny treaty benefits if the LLC functions as a mere 'dummy' shell, if no actual staff or facilities are present, or if the risk of inventory and foreign exchange is borne by the foreign parent without contractual alignment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Underreporting Intercompany Revenue (Transfer Pricing Fraud)

Statutory sanction: Article 42-3 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act: severe tax fraud criminal fine of up to KRW 50 million, and surcharge of 40% of the understated tax under the Framework Act on Local Taxes. In addition, penalty of 10% for international transaction non-disclosure under ITAA Article 14-2.

Director personal liability: If the director of the Korean LLC knowingly authorized underpricing, the corporate veil is pierced under Article 389(4) of the Commercial Act and Article 31 of the Corporate Tax Act. Directors may be personally liable for the full amount of unpaid corporate tax, interest, and penalties if assets are mixed or the LLC is undercapitalized. A criminal conviction can result in up to 3 years' imprisonment and a personal travel ban (blacklisting) preventing departure from Korea pending tax investigation and enforcement.
Failure to Withhold Dividend Withholding Tax on Outbound Repatriation to Non-Resident Shareholders

Statutory sanction: Article 98(3) of the Corporate Tax Act: 20% withholding tax for national tax and 2% under Article 103(1) of the Local Tax Act. Non-payment triggers a collection charge of 10% of the withheld tax, plus daily late payment interest at 10.95% per annum, and a 30% penalty on un-withheld amounts. The NTS can also assess a 20% penalty on the LLC for failure to file the withholding return.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the withholding tax and penalties under Article 8 of the Framework Act on National Taxes (joint secondary liability). A court may impose a criminal fine of up to KRW 10 million and imprisonment of up to 2 years for violation of the Tax Act. Repeated non-compliance leads to an individual 'high-risk taxpayer' designation, extension of statute of limitations, and asset freezing orders.
Shell Entity Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance to Claim Treaty Benefits

Statutory sanction: Article 15 of the Corporate Tax Act (substance-over-form) combined with Article 24 of the ITAA allows the NTS to re-determine the taxpayer as the actual economic owner and deny treaty benefits. The NTS may impose a 30% penalty for unjustified tax benefits and additional corporate tax of up to 22% plus 1.2% per month underpayment interest. If the shell is used to bypass Korean foreign exchange controls, the Foreign Exchange Transaction Act imposes a fine of up to KRW 50 million.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly establish a sham entity to defraud tax authorities are liable under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act for up to 5 years in prison and a fine of KRW 100 million. The court may order the disgorgement of all profits. A travel ban and criminal arrest are possible if the director attempts to flee. Additionally, the director's personal assets can be attached for the tax deficiency under the doctrine of 'alter ego' liability when corporate formalities are consistently ignored.

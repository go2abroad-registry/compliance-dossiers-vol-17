Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital exists under the Commercial Act for a Yuhan Hoesa. The organizers may declare any capital amount sufficient for the stated fertilizer wholesale business. However, the entire declared subscription price must be fully paid into a blocked pre-incorporation bank account in the name of the proposed company before the court registration application is filed. The bank must issue a capital blocking certificate, and the funds cannot be withdrawn by the organizers until the court issues the Certificate of Incorporation Registration. No mandatory escrow amount is imposed by statute, but the fertilizer wholesale business may require a higher operational capital commitment depending on bank lending and vendor credit expectations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety and Civil Defense approval is required before stocking fertilizer inventory: the Company must obtain the Fire-Fighting System Completion Inspection Certificate from the local fire station under the Firefighting Facilities Installation, Maintenance, and Safety Control Act, and a civil defense fire safety certificate for the storage building. Structural load inspection is required for palletized urea and potash storage, with a minimum documented design floor load of 2.5 metric tons per square meter. The warehouse operation must be registered with the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency, and workers must complete new-hire safety training and chemical handling orientation for fertilizer dust and ammonia release.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online filing and registration through the HomeTax electronic tax portal at hometax.go.kr. Export clearance and customs verification are administered jointly by Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Korean Yuhan Hoesa, the statutory identifier is the 10-digit Business Registration Number issued under the VAT Act, which also functions as the corporate TIN. Procedure: complete Korean incorporation under the Commercial Act; obtain the court registry certificate of incorporation; file a Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office via HomeTax or in person; attach the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, evidence of a domestic place of business, and identification of the representative director. If the foreign investor has no domestic residence or office representation, the Korean entity must appoint a domestic tax representative by notarized power of attorney to receive NTS notices, file VAT returns, and maintain statutory records. For a foreign non-resident entity that does not incorporate in Korea but still makes VAT-taxable supplies in Korea, the same Business Registration Number regime is applied through a domestic tax representative, who files the simplified foreign business operator registration with HomeTax. The NTS issues the Business Registration Number after verification, typically within several days, and this number is used for corporate income tax, VAT, and electronic tax invoices.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for an incorporated Yuhan Hoesa; VAT registration is mandatory within 20 days of business commencement. For individual small business operators, annual taxable turnover of KRW 80,000,000 marks the simplified taxable person classification boundary. For foreign non-resident suppliers of electronic services to Korean consumers, registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act and Enforcement Decree, a supply of goods is zero-rated when title to the goods passes to a foreign buyer and the goods are actually exported from Korea under an export declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service. For qualifying services supplied to non-residents and paid for in foreign currency, zero-rating may also apply if the foreign-exchange earning requirements are met; for this fertilizer wholesale scope, zero-rating is based on physical export of prilled urea and potassium chloride. The exporter must report the zero-rated export in the VAT return and retain complete proof that the goods departed Korea and that the export proceeds were remitted to and collected in Korea.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS Export Declaration (Export Declaration Confirmation) showing the physical export of the goods
  • Negotiable Bill of Lading or equivalent ocean transport document evidencing loading at a Korean port and discharge at the foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the prilled urea and potassium chloride shipment
  • Export Sales Contract or Letter of Credit (Swift MT700) establishing the commercial terms of the offshore sale
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation or Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds

Mandatory electronic tax invoice issuance through HomeTax for all VAT-registered businesses. The e-tax invoice must be transmitted to the National Tax Service no later than the day after issuance, and HomeTax returns a unique electronic tax invoice issuance number used for validation. There is no pre-issuance fiscal clearing block and no QR anti-fraud digital stamp requirement; however, the NTS performs real-time validation of registration numbers, cross-matches purchase and sales e-tax invoices, and reconciles them against Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declarations. HomeTax provides EDI/API channels for ERP integration so that invoice data can be filed programmatically.

The NTS denies the zero-rating and assesses 10% output VAT if the export declaration, B/L, and foreign exchange receipt evidence are incomplete, inconsistent, or fraudulent. The assessment is accompanied by penalty taxes under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the VAT Act: up to 40% additional tax for intentional or fraudulent underreporting, 10% for negligent understatement, a separate false e-tax invoice penalty where applicable, and statutory delinquency surcharges on late or reversed payments. Refund amounts previously paid are retrospectively clawed back with interest from the date of refund, and the statute of limitations is extended to ten years in cases involving fraud or false documents. Serious cases are referred for criminal tax evasion prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Divident, interest, trade settlement, and service payments are freely remittable through designated foreign exchange banks, subject to documentary verification and reporting thresholds. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, equity contributions, and inbound loans, are generally liberalized under the negative list system but require filing with the designated bank using prescribed purpose codes. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds into KRW; exporters may retain foreign currency in domiciled foreign currency accounts, provided the collection is reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System and the Customs export declaration is closed out within the statutory period. Profit remittance for a foreign-owned Yuhan Hoesa requires audited financial statements, board resolution declaring dividends, and evidence of tax withholding and tax clearance; remittance is typically executed within 3-5 business days through the reporting bank. For an agrochemical commodity wholesaler, the bank will require underlying trade documentation for each cross-border payment to prevent trade-based money laundering and to verify the economic substance of the transaction. The government imposes strict anti-abuse measures on trade mis-invoicing and misclassification of chemicals; all transactions must reflect customs declarations and arm's-length pricing. There are no prohibitive conversion queues or surrender requirements, but the Financial Action Task Force-aligned AML framework requires banks to aggregate all cross-border transactions for reporting and to block any payment that lacks a valid foreign exchange transaction reason code. The practical barrier is documentary compliance rather than quantative control; a well-kept KYC file and trade evidence package will allow seamless KRW/USD conversion and international settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 16 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Operational Regulations on Foreign Exchange Transactions, export proceeds must be collected within 15 months from the date of export declaration unless an extension is approved by the designated foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio of export receipts into KRW, but all collected foreign currency must be reported to the Korea Customs Service and the Foreign Exchange Information System through the designated bank. Uncollected export proceeds beyond the deadline may trigger penalty assessments and require a non-collection notification to the tax and customs authorities. The exporter may keep the proceeds in a foreign currency account, sell them to the bank for KRW, or use them directly for approved trade-related payments.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) through the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Korea Federation of Banks operates the Foreign Exchange Information System for transaction reporting and monitoring.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-negligible for a fertilizer wholesale entity physically operating in Korea. The Electronic Financial Transactions Act requires any institution receiving or transmitting cross-border funds for Korean residents to be licensed as a financial company or a registered foreign exchange business. Offshore digital EMI platforms without a Korean banking license cannot maintain a won-denominated account, cannot issue a domestic IBAN, cannot clear KRW through the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute (KFTC), and cannot legally act as the primary settlement partner for trade-related payments. Some global non-bank payment providers may support cross-border collection, but the final onshore settlement leg must pass through a designated Tier-1 Korean bank for the Foreign Exchange Information System and tax reporting. Prudent treasury practice for ISIC 4669 commodity flows is to use only domestic regulated banks, reserving an EMI only if the bank itself offers an integrated digital payment channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder must remit the capital injection through a SWIFT wire transfer to the Yuhan Hoesa's designated foreign exchange bank account. The payment must be referenced with the Korean purpose code for direct investment or 'capital increase' in the wire narrative; failure to include the code can cause the funds to be held in a suspense account. The bank will register the transaction in the Foreign Exchange Information System and issue a foreign investment verification report. For a KRW or USD-capital escrow account, the receiving bank will verify the corporate registration certificate and the capital verification certificate from a Korean CPA. The company must then notify the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy or the Bank of Korea through the designated bank within 30 days under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. All funds must maintain a clean audit trail from the beneficial owner's bank account to the Korean receiver account; any interposition of an offshore EMI or personal account will trigger an automatic AML freeze.Trade disbursements for fertilizer imports and re-exports should use irrevocable documentary letters of credit (MT700) for large shipments, with a confirming bank in the supplier's country to mitigate seller's risk. For low-value or repeat transactions, SWIFT MT103 bank-to-bank wires are acceptable after the bank has seen a minimum of three reconcillied trade contracts and customs declarations. The company should also employ documentary collections (DP/DA) for established counterparties with a stable payment track record, but the bank will require the same underlying shipment evidence. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-negligible for a fertilizer wholesale entity physically operating in Korea. The Electronic Financial Transactions Act requires any institution receiving or transmitting cross-border funds for Korean residents to be licensed as a financial company or a registered foreign exchange business. Offshore digital EMI platforms without a Korean banking license cannot maintain a won-denominated account, cannot issue a domestic IBAN, cannot clear KRW through the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute (KFTC), and cannot legally act as the primary settlement partner for trade-related payments. Some global non-bank payment providers may support cross-border collection, but the final onshore settlement leg must pass through a designated Tier-1 Korean bank for the Foreign Exchange Information System and tax reporting. Prudent treasury practice for ISIC 4669 commodity flows is to use only domestic regulated banks, reserving an EMI only if the bank itself offers an integrated digital payment channel.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal national corporate income tax rate (applicable to taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion), plus a 10% local income tax surtax, producing a 26.4% aggregate headline marginal rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate domestic withholding tax on outbound dividends (20% national income tax plus 2% local income tax surtax); bilateral tax treaties generally reduce the national tax component to 5% to 15% depending on ownership percentage and treaty provisions, with the reduced treaty rate subject to beneficial ownership and substance requirements.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS), official electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); export customs side: Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident that intends to operate through or invest in a Korean Yuhan Hoesa LLC, the Korean company itself must first complete Korean commercial registry incorporation, then submit the statutory business registration application to the competent NTS district office via HomeTax. The application includes the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, proof of the foreign investor's existence, power of attorney for tax matters, and evidence of the registered place of business in Korea. The NTS assigns a 10-digit Business Registration Number which serves as the taxpayer VAT and corporate tax identifier; the same registration is used for VAT and is mandatory before any taxable supply commences. A foreign non-resident company that has no Korean place of business but makes direct taxable supplies into Korea must separately obtain a VAT business registration for foreign electronic-service suppliers through HomeTax, generally through a domestic tax representative, unless a treaty or NTS published procedure grants exception. No general monetary pre-registration threshold exists; registration is triggered by commencement of business activity or by the making of taxable supplies in Korea.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, 0% VAT applies to goods physically exported out of Korea after Korean Customs Service clearance. Services qualify for 0% where they are supplied to a non-resident recipient and are performed or consumed outside Korean territory, including qualifying exported digital content and software-related services, provided the Korean supplier proves that the place of use/benefit lies outside Korea and that the consideration is receivable in foreign currency from abroad. For a Yuhan Hoesa publishing social casino/slots simulations, zero-rating is available only in respect of the offshore supply element - i.e. sales credited to customers located outside Korea or, in B2B chains, services wholly used by non-resident business customers outside Korea. Sales to Korean domestic users through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing remain standard-rated Korean domestic VAT at 10%; the app stores' own VAT/settlement reporting must be reconciled. Export earnings must be collected through authorized Korean foreign-exchange channels by the statutory 180-day deadline.

  • Export declaration processed through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system, carrying the export clearance number and zero-rating supporting data
  • Commercial invoice and, for digital services, a signed service agreement or statement of work identifying the cross-border nature and the non-resident recipient
  • Bill of lading or air waybill for physical exports; for digital/software exports, offshore download or server usage logs and platform settlement reports from Apple/Google showing foreign end-user purchases
  • Foreign exchange settlement confirmation issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank evidencing the inward receipt and repatriation of export proceeds in convertible currency
  • Documents establishing the overseas place of consumption, such as customer billing address, IP address geolocation, tax registration or VAT ID, and end-use certificate

Korea's NTS operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) regime integrated with HomeTax. VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices for each taxable supply and transmit them to the NTS through HomeTax or an NTS-approved electronic tax invoice service provider; transmission must be completed by the following day after issuance. The NTS system validates transaction data against VAT returns and payment records, and flags mismatches in near real time. There is no blockchain or analogue digital stamping requirement for general software publishers, but app-store/IAP settlements from Apple and Google are reconciliation evidence. For foreign electronic-service suppliers, a simplified electronic VAT filing and e-invoice/upload channel is available via HomeTax.

During audit, NTS cross-checks zero-rating claims against Korea Customs Service export declarations, foreign-exchange bank settlement records, app-store payout reports, and customer location data. Missing or inconsistent documentation leads to disallowance of the 0% rate and the assessment of 10% VAT on the supply value, plus additional tax penalties and interest under the Value-Added Tax Act and Framework Act on National Taxes. Material false zero-rating or fraudulent refund claims are subject to criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, and the NTS may claw back refunds and disallow historical claims within the statutory assessment limitation periods applicable to national taxes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized, monitored foreign exchange regime. The Korean won is fully convertible for bona fide current account transactions, including software exports, royalty payments, and profit distributions, subject only to transaction reporting and documentary retention. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity subscription and related-party loans, are generally permitted but require filing through designated foreign exchange banks, and some large or non-bank transactions require advance approval or onboarding to the MOEF reporting system. There is no blanket surrender requirement for export proceeds; Korean exporters must receive export consideration through a licensed foreign exchange bank within statutory timeframes, but they may retain those proceeds in resident foreign currency deposit accounts or convert them to KRW at market rates. For a non-resident-owned Yuhan Hoesa engaged in digital content monetization via Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the key constraint is that every inbound platform payout must be traceable to the entity's registered foreign exchange bank account, with supporting platform settlement statements retained for at least five years. Profit remittance to a foreign parent or shareholder requires certification that corporate income tax and dividend withholding tax have been settled with the National Tax Service; the foreign exchange bank will require the tax payment receipt, financial statements, board resolution approving the dividend, and a beneficial ownership declaration. Overall, South Korea is not a stringent exchange control jurisdiction in the sense of mandatory conversion or central bank rationing, but the administrative burden rests on full reporting, bank intermediation, and documentation to prove the underlying commercial purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Regulations, export proceeds arising from digital services and software publishing must be received through a designated foreign exchange bank within 18 months of the export performance date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio requiring the export proceeds to be changed into KRW; proceeds may be held in a foreign currency account, deposited, or converted at the prevailing market rate. If the 18-month collection deadline is missed, the Yuhan Hoesa must seek an extension through its settlement bank, supported by a documented reason such as ongoing platform payment cycles, legal disputes, or agreed trade credit terms. All inbound wire transfers from Apple and Google must be identified with the correct foreign exchange transaction purpose code and settled in a manner that permits audit trail reconciliation with the platform payout statements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK); foreign exchange administration is jointly governed by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with delegated licensing and reporting executed through foreign exchange banks supervised by the BOK and the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs as principal treasury accounts. South Korean corporate banking, foreign exchange reporting, and KRW clearing are governed by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and are serviced only through licensed domestic financial institutions. An offshore EMI such as a digital banking service based in Hong Kong, the EU, or the UK cannot open a fully compliant KRW settlement account for the company without a licensed Korean bank partner, nor can it reliably provide the inward remittance reporting certificate needed for export proceeds and equity capital verification. EMIs may be accepted only as auxiliary non-KRW wallets for developer payouts or contractor disbursements, provided transfer rationale is documented; however, the statutory treasury spine must remain with a Tier-1 Korean bank. Any effort to process Apple or Google IAP outflows through an EMI without direct domestic bank intermediation will likely trigger AML inquiries and audit deficiencies from Korean regulators.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital for the Korean LLC should be wired by the foreign shareholder via SWIFT MT103 to the foreign exchange bank's collection account, using the investor's registered corporate account and a remittance narrative identifying the payee as the Yuhan Hoesa, the purpose as equity subscription, and the foreign investment intention. The designated foreign exchange bank will file the foreign investment report with the Ministry of Economy and Finance through the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) electronic system or the bank's delegated reporting portal. After the inward remittance clears, the bank will issue a foreign exchange purchase certificate and a capital verification confirmation that can be registered with the court for paid-in capital proof. The KRW conversion should be executed at the bank's market rate and credited to the company's domestic operating account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs as principal treasury accounts. South Korean corporate banking, foreign exchange reporting, and KRW clearing are governed by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and are serviced only through licensed domestic financial institutions. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

21% national corporate income tax top bracket plus 10% local income tax surtax, producing an aggregate headline rate of 23.1%; lower national brackets are 9% up to KRW 200,000,000 and 19% up to KRW 20,000,000,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge) on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; treaty rates may reduce the dividend withholding rate, commonly to 10% or 15% depending on shareholding size and applicable DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is required for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding KRW 5,000,000,000 in an annual accounting period; Master File preparation is generally required for multinational groups exceeding KRW 100,000,000,000 in consolidated revenue or assets, as adjusted by the International Tax Coordination of Tax Adjustment Act (ITCA) and its Enforcement Decree. Even below the thresholds, a taxpayer must maintain contemporaneous arm's length evidence in response to an NTS adjustment request.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the NTS enforces substance-over-form under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the ITCA. A Yuhan Hoesa must demonstrate genuine decision-making, employees, servers, risk-bearing, and operational control in Korea; foreign affiliates must not merely invoice or hold intangible property without performing core income-generating activities. For social casino gaming, CIGA-type substance principles govern whether user contracts, game servicing, and high-value software-development risks are actually borne by the Korean entity, which also underpins DTA beneficial ownership claims.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Disguised real-money gambling / illegal game operation

Statutory sanction: Under the Game Industry Promotion Act, illegal game-content production and gambling inducement may be punished by imprisonment for up to 5 years and fines up to KRW 50,000,000; Criminal Act gambling provisions and game-industry administrative orders also apply, with confiscation of illegal proceeds and forced game registration cancellation.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize convertible chip ecosystems or secondary-market activities can be criminally prosecuted as principals or co-conspirators, face personal asset seizure/restitution, be named in the Ministry of Culture and NTS blacklisting and travel-restriction system, and may be imprisoned for up to 5 years; the corporate veil will be pierced for fraudulent operation of the legal entity.
Transfer pricing manipulation and economic-substance sham

Statutory sanction: The NTS may, under the ITCA, recharacterize or disregard non-arm's length transactions and impute income; penalties include up to 30% additional tax on the adjusted underreported income, documented-transaction surcharges, and daily late-payment interest, with criminal referral for willful false apportionment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer-pricing returns or destroy documentation may be held jointly liable for the corporation's unpaid tax under Korean tax statutes, may have their assets attached through secondary collection, may be placed on the NTS delinquent taxpayer blacklist with exit restrictions, and can face imprisonment under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for deliberate underreporting of corporate income.
Non-resident withholding and dividend tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Withholding penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes include a 10% additional tax on the withheld amount plus daily late-payment surcharges; intentional evasion or failure to withhold after service of a demand exposes the company to an assessment of the full under-withheld tax, interest, and criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: The representative director who causes the company to pay untaxed cross-border distributions becomes a secondary liable person for the unpaid corporate and withholding tax, with personal property collection, travel ban and tax-defaulter blacklisting under the National Tax Collection Act, and imprisonment for up to 5 years or more where the amount evaded is aggravated under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

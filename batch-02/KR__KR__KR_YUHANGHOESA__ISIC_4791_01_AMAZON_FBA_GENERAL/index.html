Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. The chosen stated capital must be fully paid into a temporary deposit account opened in the Company's name at a domestic bank before the registration filing; the bank-issued deposit balance certificate is filed with the court. No government escrow account or independent accountant verification is required. The state registration tax is calculated on stated capital; the USD fee figure assumes a nominal capital of KRW 10,000,000.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable at formation - no physical industrial property is owned or occupied by the Company. If a leased warehouse or fulfillment depot is operated directly, the Company must obtain Civil Defense fire safety clearance from the local fire department including fire suppression equipment, evacuation plan, and occupancy load approval, a structural load inspection for racking systems, and occupational safety approval under the Occupational Safety and Health Act before receiving inventory. For Amazon FBA inventory stored in third-party fulfillment centers, the fulfillment center operator is responsible for fire and structural compliance; the seller remains responsible for product safety labeling and restricted-product clearance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea, using the HomeTax electronic tax portal (www.hometax.go.kr) and the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS Export Clearance portal for customs-linked tax administration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must first appoint a South Korean tax representative with a domestic place of business. The tax representative then files the foreign company's VAT/business registration application with the applicable District Tax Office through the HomeTax non-resident business registration menu. Supporting documents include the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation authenticated or apostilled, a notarized power of attorney appointing the tax representative, proof of identity of the tax representative, proof of the representative's domestic place of business, a statement of intended economic activity, and a Korean bank account for VAT refunds. Upon approval, NTS issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which operates as the unified TIN and VAT ID for the Yuhan Hoesa. The foreign entity must also open a designated foreign currency purchase/repatriation account with a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank to evidence inward remittances and to support export zero-rating documentation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic resident suppliers, VAT registration becomes compulsory when annual taxable turnover exceeds KRW 80,000,000; for foreign non-resident suppliers of cross-border e-commerce services and goods supplied through an online platform into or from South Korea, there is no monetary threshold and compulsory VAT registration is required from the first taxable supply, mediated through the appointed Korean tax representative on the HomeTax portal.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is allowed for exported goods and for qualifying services supplied to a non-resident overseas user from or through the Korean territory, including goods sold via Amazon FBA where the goods are physically exported from Korea to an overseas fulfillment center or overseas end consumer. The claim requires legal export documentation proving removal of the goods from the Korean customs territory, title transfer to a non-resident buyer, the use or enjoyment of qualifying services outside Korea for zero-rated services, and receipt of hard-currency foreign exchange proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate / Export Bill of Entry) issued by Korea Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean and/or Air Waybill), including a House Bill of Lading or Fulfillment Center Receipt showing delivery to an overseas Amazon fulfillment warehouse
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Foreign Exchange Repatriation Certificate issued by a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank
  • Commercial Invoice and Pro-forma Invoice
  • Export Sales Contract / Customer Purchase Order
  • Certificate of Origin (where applicable)
  • Proof of Delivery to the foreign Amazon facility or final non-resident consumer
  • Foreign currency receipt ledger entries prepared by the Korean tax representative

All B2B taxable supplies must be issued through the NTS-mandated electronic tax invoice system, transmitted in real time through HomeTax 'Electronic Tax Invoice' service, with a unique electronic approval number and NTS receipt validation. Anti-fraud and fiscal clearance requirements apply via the NTS digitized invoice authenticity verification, mandatory cash register linkage for retail sellers, and the online e-commerce overseas seller data reporting obligation. Foreign non-resident suppliers using Amazon FBA must maintain the Korean tax representative's HomeTax account for VAT return filing, electronic invoice issuance where applicable, and cross-border transaction records.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to NTS and Korea Customs Service audit. If documentary evidence does not substantiate physical export and foreign exchange repatriation within 180 days, the claimed zero rating is reversed and the full 10% VAT is assessed with interest. Incorrect refunds arising from fraudulent export declarations or forged customs or remittance documents trigger a penalty tax, additional collection, and criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act; NTS also imposes retroactive clawback assessments under the Framework Act on National Taxes, with a general statute of limitations of five years and up to ten years for fraudulent evasion.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains Article VIII current account convertibility standards under the IMF, meaning trade-related payments and ordinary service fees are freely convertible and remittable through banks licensed as foreign exchange banks. Capital account transactions are also significantly liberalized, but they remain subject to declaration, registration, or reporting obligations under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is not a fully internationalized currency; settlements must flow through authorized foreign exchange banks, and non-resident capital injections require a capital transaction report or inward direct investment report to be filed with the Bank of Korea through the local bank. Export proceeds do not carry a broad statutory surrender requirement; exporters may retain foreign currency in a domestic foreign currency account, but any conversion into KRW must be executed at the prevailing bank exchange rate. Profit remittance from a Korean limited company is permissible once the underlying inward investment is properly reported and the paying bank verifies the transaction against tax clearances, audited financial statements, and dividend distribution resolutions. The principal practical hurdles include the need to demonstrate that export proceeds originate from genuine cross-border e-commerce transactions, consistency between Korea Customs Service export declarations and FX settlement records, and the prohibition on using unofficial channels, payment aggregators, or crypto intermediaries for KRW settlement. As a result, the convertibility framework is fully functional for compliant Amazon FBA operations provided every cross-border movement carries the correct declaration and banking trail.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected within six months from the date of export declaration or cargo departure, unless an extension is approved by the Bank of Korea governor. There is no general mandatory percentage of export receipts that must be converted into Korean won; exporters may lawfully maintain USD, EUR, JPY, or other foreign currency in authorized foreign currency accounts. However, all export proceeds must be received directly into a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank account, and any conversion to KRW for domestic operating expenses must be settled at the bank's published exchange rate. Late collection, offsetting of receivables, or transfer to a third party without supporting trade documentation requires prior reporting or bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and coordinated with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MoEF) and the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) for enforcement and reporting supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low direct viability for offshore digital EMIs. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, a non-bank overseas EMI cannot independently open or hold KRW current accounts for a Korean resident entity, nor act as the primary settlement or clearing bank for local corporate treasury operations. Offshore EMIs such as Airwallex, Stripe, and Payoneer may be engaged only as supplementary payment gateways or cross-border payout engines that ultimately route cleared funds through a licensed Korean commercial bank. The Korean legal framework requires the actual KRW clearing, capital verification, customs reconciliation, and FX reporting to take place at a domestic authorized foreign exchange bank. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must maintain a Tier-1 Korean banking relationship as the anchor, with EMIs limited to Amazon marketplace receivable aggregation and secondary payout acceleration.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top national corporate income tax rate under a progressive schedule of 9%, 19%, 21% and 24%; aggregate top effective headline rate is 26.4% after adding the 10% local income tax surcharge.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate statutory rate, composed of 20% national withholding plus 2% local income tax surcharge (10% of the national withholding); bilateral DTAs may reduce the rate to 5%, 10% or 15% depending on the shareholder jurisdiction, ownership percentage and holding period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory International Transaction Report when cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 500 million; Local File when such transactions exceed KRW 10 billion; Master File when such transactions exceed KRW 100 billion; all thresholds are applied for the current or immediately preceding business year under Article 11 of the International Tax Adjustment Act.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a zero-tax economic substance regime, but Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes demands economic substance over legal form; a Korean Amazon FBA entity that performs only formal functions while the actual digital commerce value-adding activities, pricing decisions and inventory risk are held abroad will be re-attributed profit; the OECD Core Income-Generating Activities doctrine applies through permanent establishment profit attribution rules in the International Tax Adjustment Act, and a shell Korean entity without CIGA is exposed to sham re-characterisation, denial of treaty relief and controlled foreign company treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate tax evasion and unreported cross-border e-commerce revenue

Statutory sanction: Violation of Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for tax evasion by fraudulent or unlawful means; the National Tax Service recalculates the full tax base, imposes a 20% to 40% unfaithful return surcharge plus daily late-payment surcharge, and the directors are exposed to criminal fines up to five times the evaded tax and imprisonment up to two years, increased in egregious cases involving concealment of foreign assets.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced through the secondary liability rules of the Framework Act on National Taxes; a director who act as the actual controlling mind or who knowingly diverted cross-border sales revenue is personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, local income tax and penalties; the NTS may request the Ministry of Justice to impose an overseas travel ban, place the director on the high-value tax delinquent blacklist, and pursue criminal indictment with civil asset restitution of all diverted Amazon proceeds.
Transfer pricing evasion and sham profit allocation to foreign IP or fulfillment entities

Statutory sanction: The NTS re-determines income under Article 4 of the International Tax Adjustment Act using the most appropriate OECD arm's length method, denies deductions for undocumented cross-border charges, imposes the false return penalty up to 40%, and applies the Article 11 documentation failure penalty for missing or false international transaction declarations, with monetary penalties up to KRW 100 million in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany agreements lacking commercial substance, approve artificial transfer prices or cause false transfer pricing disclosures are jointly liable for the adjusted tax and surcharges if the corporate entity is stripped of assets; personal exposure includes civil restitution of shifted profits, asset seizure, travel blacklisting, and criminal liability for aiding and abetting tax evasion, with possible disqualification from serving as foreign-invested company directors.
Non-resident withholding tax default and abusive treaty shopping

Statutory sanction: The payer becomes jointly liable for the uncollected 22% withholding tax, plus unfaithful withholding penalties and daily late-payment surcharges; abusive treaty claims are denied under the anti-abuse provisions of the International Tax Adjustment Act and the relevant double tax treaty limitation-on-benefits clause, requiring repayment of the preferential amount with interest; criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act imposes imprisonment up to three years and fines for the intentional failure to collect and remit withholding taxes.

Director personal liability: The responsible representative director and any foreign director who executed the dividend, interest or royalty remittance without tax are personally liable for the withheld amounts and penalties; the Ministry of Justice may impose departure restrictions, the NTS may blacklist the director as a serious tax delinquent, and criminal conviction carries imprisonment, civil asset restitution and disqualification from future corporate management.

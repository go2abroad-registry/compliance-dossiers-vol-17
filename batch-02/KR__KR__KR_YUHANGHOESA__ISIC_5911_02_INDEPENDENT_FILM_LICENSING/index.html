South Korean law requires a Yuhan Hoesa to have a registered business address within the court's jurisdiction. A virtual office is conditionally permissible for registration, but commercial mailbox addresses and non-physical registered agent solutions are generally rejected by the National Tax Service and by banks when opening a corporate account. If a serviced-office company is used, it must provide dedicated desk space, meeting room access, and a tenant directory entry in the company's registered Hangul name.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the Korean Commercial Act, there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. The company must issue at least one share or contribution unit, and the full subscription amount must be paid into a blocked domestic bank account in the name of the company-in-formation. The deposit may not be withdrawn or released until the court registry issues the commercial registration certificate. If the paid-in capital is exceptionally large, the subscribers must obtain an independent appraiser's or inspector's certificate confirming the valuation of contributions.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity carrying on film-licensing activity in Korea must file a business registration application with the local District Tax Office within 20 days after business commencement (or, with no Korean place of business, use the Simplified Registration function for foreign suppliers on HomeTax). The application dossier includes the certificate of foreign registration or incorporation, notarised corporate constitution, appointment of a Korean tax representative by notarised power of attorney, the home-country tax identification number, and evidence of the Korean place of business or agent. NTS then issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the payer's VAT identification number and enterprise TIN for all tax filings and e-tax invoices.

Korea does not require pre-clearance or real-time anti-fraud digital stamping on ordinary export invoices; enforcement is achieved through e-tax-invoice matching, NTS Cash Receipts records, and NTS audit checks. Foreign non-resident simplified registrants supplying electronic services to Korean consumers must keep transaction logs and issue simplified receipts rather than formal tax invoices.

If NTS or Korea Customs Service determines that the export is fictitious or improperly documented, or that foreign-currency settlement was not received and repatriated within the applicable deadline (180 days), the zero-rating will be clawed back; VAT at 10% is assessed on the gross amount, and the taxpayer becomes liable for statutory additional tax on false invoices and underdeclared VAT, statutory interest, and late payment surcharges. Deliberate fabrication of export documents or tax invoices is prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Evasion and Tax Offenses Act, carrying imprisonment and fines of up to three times the evaded tax, in addition to public listing and credit penalty consequences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized current-account regime and a partially controlled capital account. The Korean won trades in a managed floating regime, and current-account transactions such as film licensing royalties, trade fees, and cross-border content distribution settlements can be freely converted through a licensed foreign-exchange bank. Profit and dividend remittance is permitted after corporate tax clearance, withholding tax payment, and documentary proof of distributable earnings. Capital-account flows, including foreign direct investment capital subscription, offshore borrowings, and cross-currency funding, require classification and reporting to BOK-backed systems under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. There is no mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds; foreign currency may be retained in domestic foreign-currency deposit accounts. Effective convertibility is determined by the quality of KYC documentation and the underlying commercial reality of each remittance. The bank, BOK, and the reporting system will treat unexplained round-tripping, mismatch between contract parties, or non-transparent beneficial ownership as suspicious and may defer, suspend, or refuse conversion. For a Korean LLC engaged in ISIC 5911 independent film licensing, the paper trail must therefore include executed license agreements, certification of copyright/title, royalty mathematics, tax compliance evidence, and traceable shareholder source of funds before high-value won conversion and cross-border settlement can be cleared.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export and trade proceeds, including income from film copyright licensing and distribution, must be collected through a licensed foreign-exchange bank and recognized within the statutory collection period, in practice no later than five months from the export declaration/shipment for goods, while service and royalty contracts follow the express payment terms in the underlying agreement. Extensions are available through the foreign-exchange bank with prior reporting to BOK where justifiable. The mandatory conversion/surrender ratio is zero percent; Korea does not currently obligate the resident to convert to Korean won, and proceeds may be credited to a retained foreign-currency account. Where collection is delayed beyond the contracted due date, the bank must be notified and the delayed receipt report must be maintained for BOK/KoFIU supervision.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), discharging forex regulatory authority alongside the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is low and legally barred as a primary clearing agent: overseas fintech electronic money institutions are not licensed banks under the Korean Banking Act and do not qualify as foreign-exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Such an EMI cannot open a domestic KRW operating account, verify paid-in capital, execute surrender/collection reporting, or participate in BOK/KoFIU reporting for a Korean resident LLC. The corporate treasury should therefore use a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank for all regulated rails; an offshore EMI may only act as a non-licensed partner within a licensed Korean bank's scheme, and even then solely for minor auxiliary collection and never as the statutory account for capital verification or profit remittance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder loans should be remitted by SWIFT to the designated Tier-1 Korean bank account in the legal name KR_YUHANGHOESA, with the remitter named as the actual non-resident shareholder. The remittance message should carry the Korean foreign investment report number issued through the FEIS/KOTRA portal for direct investment, contract name, and the underlying purpose classification for paid-in capital, while physical supporting documents must be presented to the receiving bank before any KRW conversion. After credit, the bank performs capital verification confirmation, the company obtains the Foreign Investment Enterprise report/registration, and a Korean certified public accountant issues the capital verification certificate for corporate registration. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is low and legally barred as a primary clearing agent: overseas fintech electronic money institutions are not licensed banks under the Korean Banking Act and do not qualify as foreign-exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Such an EMI cannot open a domestic KRW operating account, verify paid-in capital, execute surrender/collection reporting, or participate in BOK/KoFIU reporting for a Korean resident LLC. The corporate treasury should therefore use a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank for all regulated rails; an offshore EMI may only act as a non-licensed partner within a licensed Korean bank's scheme, and even then solely for minor auxiliary collection and never as the statutory account for capital verification or profit remittance.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% (highest marginal national corporate income tax rate for taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion); plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the national tax, resulting in a combined headline rate of 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents, plus 2% local income tax surcharge (10% on the national tax), for a combined aggregate rate of 22%. This rate applies unless reduced under an applicable income tax treaty (DTA); a treaty may lower the rate to 5%, 10%, or 15%, depending on the holding percentage and the treaty partner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 100 billion (master file required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding KRW 100 billion); local file required if the Korean entity's cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion in the fiscal year, or if intangible-related payments to foreign related parties exceed KRW 1 billion. Country-by-Country (CbC) report is required if the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies economic substance and substance-over-form principles through statutory anti-abuse rules (i.e., Corporate Tax Act §88-2 and International Tax Coordination Act §§2, 11-12). A film licensing entity must demonstrate real activities in Korea: local management, board decisions, employees, office premises, and contractual operational control over the film distribution rights. If the NTS determines the entity lacks economic substance and is merely a conduit, it can recharacterize the transaction, deny DTA benefits, impose penalties of up to 20%, reassess taxes by treating the income as belonging to the non-resident principal, and apply Korean CFC rules to attribute undistributed profits to Korean shareholders if the offshore recipient is in a low-tax country.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Withholding Tax Evasion on Outbound Dividend and Royalty Payments

Statutory sanction: Imposition of a deficiency tax equal to the unpaid withholding amount, plus a penalty surcharge of 10% for mistake or 40% for fraudulent under-withholding. Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (Article 9), intentional non-withholding is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding KRW 100 million.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly order or participate in the non-withholding are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties, notwithstanding the corporate limited liability shield (piercing the corporate veil under Korean Civil Act §2 and Corporate Tax Act §91). The NTS can attach personal assets, including bank accounts, real estate, and vehicles, and may impose a travel ban (blacklisting) on the director for tax debts exceeding KRW 500 million. Directors found to have misappropriated withheld amounts face criminal prosecution for embezzlement and tax evasion, with imprisonment up to 5 years and restitution of the full evaded amount.
Tax Evasion and False Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, fraudulent tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years (up to 7 years when the evaded amount exceeds KRW 1 billion) or a fine up to KRW 30 million (increased up to KRW 100 million in aggravated cases). An administrative penalty tax of 40% of the evaded tax is also imposed, plus a daily interest of 0.03% on underpaid taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false tax returns with intent to evade taxes become personally liable for the entire tax deficiency, penalties, and interest; the corporate veil is pierced to reach personal stock holdings, pension plans, and other assets. The NTS may issue a criminal complaint, resulting in public indictment and potential imprisonment of the director between 1 and 7 years. A travel ban and director disqualification are mandatory for tax debts exceeding the statutory threshold, and civil liability allows the tax authority to seek recovery from the director's personal estate in private litigation.
Shell Company Abuse and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Coordination Act (Article 12) and Corporate Tax Act (Article 88-2), the NTS can disregard the corporate entity as a sham, reattribute the income to the actual non-resident principal, deny treaty benefits, and impose a 20% penalty surcharge on the assessed tax. If the shell scheme was used to conceal the true beneficial owner, the entity becomes subject to anti-money laundering penalties under the Financial Transaction Reports Act, and the parent may be assessed as having a permanent establishment in Korea.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly established or managed the shell company are exposed to piercing of the corporate veil: the NTS can attach personal assets of directors who had control over the sham entity, regardless of formal separation. Under Korean law, the court may find that the company was the alter ego of the foreign shareholder, allowing judgment creditors (including the NTS) to seize personal property, freeze bank accounts, and attach overseas assets. Criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion may lead to imprisonment of up to 7 years and restitution of every tax benefit realized, including interest and penalties. Directors are also subject to administrative blacklisting, preventing them from obtaining government licenses, permits, or serving as directors of any Korean company for up to 5 years.

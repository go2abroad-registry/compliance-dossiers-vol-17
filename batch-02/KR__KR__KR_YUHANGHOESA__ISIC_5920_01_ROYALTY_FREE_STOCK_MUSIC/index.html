Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is mandated by the Commercial Act. The articles of incorporation must state the total capital and each member's contribution amount; all contributions must be fully paid or performed before the corporate registration is made. The paid-in capital may be kept in a Korean bank contribution deposit account established in the name of the proposed company or its representative director. This account is not a blocked escrow, but the funds must remain deposited and dedicated to the Company until the incorporation registration is completed. After registration, the Company has unrestricted use of the contributed capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating through the HomeTax electronic portal at www.hometax.go.kr for business registration, e-Tax Invoice issuance, VAT returns, and tax retrieval services.

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company): file a Business Registration Application with the district tax office that has jurisdiction over the registered head office before commencing taxable business. Attach the articles of incorporation, proof of shareholder identities, lease or ownership evidence of the place of business, and a corporate bank account certificate. The NTS issues a Business Registration Certificate containing a ten-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the unified Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), VAT registration number, and corporate tax reference number. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Korean domestic place of business that intends to make taxable supplies in Korea: appoint a statutory tax representative who is a Korean tax accountant or tax attorney, pursuant to the Framework Act on National Taxes. The tax representative must file the same Business Registration Application with the attached power of attorney, the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation or equivalent official company extract in English or certified translation, and a board resolution appointing the tax representative. Once NTS validates the application, the foreign entity is registered in HomeTax and receives a Business Registration Number for VAT purposes. The Business Registration Number must be quoted on all e-Tax Invoices issued in Korea. Registration must occur before the first taxable supply; no economic threshold applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. A Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily classified as a Korean domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and as an independent VAT taxable person. The Korean tax system does not offer any check-the-box, pass-through, or hybrid mismatch election for a limited liability company. A foreign shareholder may claim treaty benefits on dividends, but the entity level taxation and VAT identity remain fixed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold. Any person supplying taxable goods or services in Korea, including foreign non-residents, must register for VAT regardless of turnover. A simplified VAT taxpayer status applies only where annual taxable revenue does not exceed KRW 80,000,000, but this status still requires issuance of simplified tax invoices and does not remove the registration mandate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value Added Tax Act, the supply of royalty-free stock music licensing and audio subscription services qualifies for zero-rating when the service is classified as an 'export of services'. Statutory conditions include: (i) the recipient is a foreign business or a non-resident individual whose habitual residence or place of establishment is outside Korea; (ii) the licensed copyright or music content is exploited outside the Korean customs territory; (iii) the supply is not physically performed at a domestic place of business for Korean-type consumption; and (iv) the consideration is received in a freely convertible foreign currency or through an international payment settlement method, and such foreign exchange receipts are substantiated with approved export documents. For B2B digital services the place of supply is the customer's registered business establishment; for B2C digital services the place of supply is the customer's habitual residence, supported by payment-rail billing address data and device/IP metadata. The supplier must issue a zero-rated e-Tax Invoice in HomeTax, which acts as the export sales declaration for VAT purposes.

  • Zero-rated export e-Tax Invoice issued through HomeTax, explicitly marked as export and including the foreign buyer's name and tax identifier
  • Digital licensing agreement or purchase order establishing the foreign counterparty, scope of commercial music licensing rights, and the currency-denominated royalty or subscription consideration
  • Foreign Exchange Acquisition Certificate (Oehoehoekdeukjeungmyeong) issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank certifying actual receipt of the foreign currency
  • SWIFT wire transfer advice, foreign bank statement, or PayPal/Stripe settlement report evidencing cross-border inflow and the net amount credited to the Korean entity's foreign currency account
  • For B2B transactions, a copy of the overseas business registration certificate or VAT registration number of the recipient
  • For B2C transactions, customer billing address and payment method evidence (credit card issuer country, device/IP geolocation report) confirming overseas consumption
  • No customs export declaration is required for purely intangible digital deliveries; if physical media were included, a full Customs Export Declaration and Bill of Lading or Airway Bill would become mandatory

Mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) regime via NTS HomeTax. Every VAT-taxable business must issue an e-Tax Invoice through the HomeTax API or an accredited e-Document provider and transmit it to NTS no later than the day following the supply date. Paper tax invoices are invalid for input VAT deduction. The e-Tax Invoice contains a unique invoice number, tax classification code (including zero-rated export codes), and digital signature. NTS performs post-clearance automated matching and anti-fraud data mining; there is no real-time fiscal stamping device or in-line clearance API, but all invoices must be lodged in the centralized national electronic tax fleet system. Cash receipts are also mandatory for B2C cash transactions.

The National Tax Service may recharacterize zero-rated export supplies as taxable domestic supplies at 10% VAT if the supporting documentation is incomplete, the foreign-exchange receipt is not substantiated, or the repatriation occurs after 180 days from the supply date. A 'bad claim' penalty of 20% of the incorrectly zero-rated VAT is imposed under the Framework Act on National Taxes, in addition to a reporting breach surcharge of 5% to 10% of the tax amount. For fraudulent or fictitious export claims, the aggravating penalty rises to 40% of the unpaid tax, the refunded VAT is clawed back with statutory interest, and criminal liability may be pursued under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. All export evidence must be retained for at least five years from the filing deadline of the relevant VAT return.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is freely convertible for current account transactions, including digital content licensing and subscription service exports. Payments for such services may be received in foreign currency and retained in a domestic foreign-currency account without mandatory surrender. Capital account transactions, by contrast, are subject to reporting or prior approval under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; a foreign investor subscribing to shares of a Yuhan Hoesa must file an FDI report through KOTRA or an authorized foreign exchange bank and obtain a foreign investment registration certificate before the capital remittance is considered compliant. Dividend and royalty profit remittance is permitted, but the bank will require evidence of tax clearance, audited financial statements, and a board resolution confirming distribution. The practical hurdle for a new digital music entity is not government exchange rationing but bank risk appetite: recurring micro-royalty inflows from Stripe and PayPal can generate automatic transaction monitoring alerts unless the company maintains a clean audit trail of the underlying license agreements and end-user records. For a digital audio business, the convertibility regime is favorable provided all current account inward remittances are settled through a licensed foreign exchange bank and all capital movements are pre-notified to the Bank of Korea or the delegated foreign exchange bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory percentage of export receipts must be surrendered into Korean won; the mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 0%. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export and service proceeds must be received within one year from the date of export or service performance, and this collection window may be extended by up to six months with foreign exchange bank approval. Foreign currency receipts may be held in domestic foreign-currency deposit accounts. For the royalty-free stock music business, subscription and licence fees collected through Stripe or PayPal must be aggregated and repatriated through a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank within the statutory window.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea, operating as monetary and central banking authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the policy direction of the Ministry of Economy and Finance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary banking engine. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business, and Airwallex are not licensed foreign exchange banks under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and therefore cannot satisfy the statutory settlement, reporting, and foreign investment escrow requirements for a domestic Korean entity. Their viability is medium only as auxiliary collection or disbursement vehicles that funnel funds into a licensed domestic bank account; all final settlement and outward remittance must be executed through a Tier-1 domestic foreign exchange bank. EMIs are legally permissible for ancillary payment aggregation, but they are not a substitute for a Korean corporate bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign capital contributions must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act through KOTRA or an authorised foreign exchange bank before the inbound wire is credited to a dedicated foreign-invested enterprise foreign currency account. The wire instruction should reference the foreign investment report number and use a corporate capital contribution purpose code; after receipt, the bank issues a foreign investment registration certificate and a Korean certified public accountant performs capital verification. For operating revenue from audio licensing, inbound SWIFT MT103 wires must be settled as current account service export payments with the underlying licence agreement attached; capital codes must not be used for commercial subscription receipts. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary banking engine. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business, and Airwallex are not licensed foreign exchange banks under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and therefore cannot satisfy the statutory settlement, reporting, and foreign investment escrow requirements for a domestic Korean entity. Their viability is medium only as auxiliary collection or disbursement vehicles that funnel funds into a licensed domestic bank account; all final settlement and outward remittance must be executed through a Tier-1 domestic foreign exchange bank. EMIs are legally permissible for ancillary payment aggregation, but they are not a substitute for a Korean corporate bank account.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; combined with the 10% local income tax surcharge the top effective marginal rate is 26.4%. Lower brackets apply at 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, and 21% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate statutory dividend withholding tax on remittances to non-resident shareholders: 20% national corporate income tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surcharge.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion for cross-border related-party controlled transactions triggering the Local File; KRW 10 billion for domestic related-party transactions; Master File is required when the consolidated worldwide group revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country Report obligations follow the International Tax Adjustment Act thresholds for groups exceeding KRW 100 billion in consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies a substance-over-form doctrine under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the International Tax Adjustment Act. A Yuhan Hoesa licensing marketplace must maintain actual office space, employees, servers, contracts, invoicing, and income-generating decision-making in Korea; if the company is merely a shell for licensing royalties from foreign stock-music assets or for shifting profit to foreign shareholders, the National Tax Service will disregard the entity, reattribute its income, and deny treaty benefits under the anti-abuse doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Crimes Act Article 3: imprisonment up to 2 years or a fine not exceeding twice the tax evaded; corporate income tax deficiency reassessed together with a 40% penalty surcharge for unfaithful filing and daily overdue interest; the entity also becomes ineligible for tax clearance certificates and bank transaction credits.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed the concealment face joint and several civil liability for the unpaid corporate tax and surcharges under the Commercial Act; criminal indictment for tax fraud may include imprisonment, permanent bar from corporate management, and placement on the National Tax Service departure restriction blacklist.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Treaty Shopping Shell Conduit

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 4 and 12 authorize the National Tax Service to recharacterize the intercompany transaction at an arm's-length royalty rate and reassess tax; additional taxes of 20% for deficient documentation and 40% for false or undocumented transactions apply; the DTA benefit is denied and 22% statutory withholding is assessed retroactively.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign sham intercompany agreements or knowingly maintain an economic-substance shell breach their duty of loyalty and due diligence under the Commercial Act; a court may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for restitution, impose travel bans, and impose criminal imprisonment for fraudulent treaty shopping.
Anti-Money Laundering / Cross-Border Payment Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Financial Transaction Reports Act Article 12 and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Article 28: failure to report suspicious transactions or unlawful cross-border settlement is punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years and/or a fine up to KRW 100 million; the National Tax Service may reconstruct omitted Korean-source revenue and assess corporate tax plus penalties and interest on all unreported settlement amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who control payment routing and approve foreign-exchange settlement may be criminally prosecuted, jointly liable for the unreported funds and tax deficiencies, placed on the departure blacklist, and disqualified from continuing corporate management because the entity is treated as their alter ego.

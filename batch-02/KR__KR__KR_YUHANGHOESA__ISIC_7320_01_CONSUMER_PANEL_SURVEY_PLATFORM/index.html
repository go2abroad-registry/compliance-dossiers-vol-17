Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or dedicated commercial mailbox is legally permissible in the Republic of Korea only if it is a fixed, physical address situated within the same district court jurisdiction that is notified to the registry as the company's registered office and where all official correspondence can be lawfully served. However, for the ISIC 7320 market research SaaS segment, the National Tax Service and domestic banks will reject a pure PO Box or non-accessible virtual address where no on-site staff, server room, or operational records exist. For corporate banking onboarding, a lease agreement covering at least 12 months for a serviced office, a workplace safety and hygiene certificate, and a utility bill in the company name are expected. For immigrant investors, the secured independent workplace requirement (Article 58 of the Immigration Act) means a virtual office will not satisfy the commercial substance test for a D-7 or D-8 visa. Consequently, the recommended approach is a high-grade coworking suite with a dedicated mail-handling service, a government-recognized building address, and a visible company sign; otherwise tax registration and bank account opening are likely to be challenged.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS) with electronic portal HomeTax at hometax.go.kr; customs export controls are administered separately by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS single-window system

E-government filing portal

A domestic Yuhan Hoesa must obtain a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which is simultaneously its TIN and VAT identification number, from the district tax office with jurisdiction over its registered domestic place of business before commencing operations. The application is made on the official tax registration form under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, director identification, board resolution, evidence of the Korean place of business, and, where relevant, foreign investor documentation. The entity is classified as a domestic corporation and cannot elect pass-through treatment. A foreign non-resident entity with no Korean place of business that is required to collect Korean VAT on electronic or digital services supplied to Korean consumers must instead complete the simplified foreign business operator registration through HomeTax under the Value-Added Tax Act and its Enforcement Decree. That registration is fully electronic, results in assignment of a 10-digit TIN/VAT number, and requires designation of a Korean tax representative where the NTS so requires. The application dossier for the simplified foreign registration must include the foreign entity's certificate of good standing, board authorization, tax representative appointment, evidence of Korean supply activities, and supporting calculation of Korean turnover.

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for the domestic Yuhan Hoesa; VAT registration is mandatory from the commencement of any business activity. For a foreign non-resident supplier of digital or electronic services with no Korean place of business, simplified VAT registration is triggered when the annual Korean B2C supply exceeds KRW 10,000,000 in the relevant period, and registration must be completed through HomeTax by the month following the month in which the threshold is reached.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Article 24 of the Value-Added Tax Act is available for exported goods and services. Goods qualify when physical export is evidenced by a validated Korea Customs Service export declaration. Services qualify when supplied to a non-resident recipient, paid for in foreign currency properly remitted through foreign exchange banking channels, and consumed or used outside Korea. For a cross-border market-research SaaS platform, the supply must be made to an overseas counterparty for a valid business purpose, and the digital service must be actually accessed or used by the non-resident counterparty outside Korea; zero-rating will not apply if the service is treated as supplied or consumed within Korea.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic export declaration and acceptance number (customs export declaration / bill of entry equivalent)
  • Original commercial invoice and signed service contract or purchase order identifying the non-resident customer and scope of services
  • Bill of lading or air waybill for physical goods, or equivalent electronic evidence of service delivery for SaaS/digital services
  • Inward remittance certificate / foreign exchange purchase certificate issued by a licensed foreign exchange bank showing receipt and conversion of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Bank statements matching the invoice value, payment dates, and remitter identity
  • For digital services: IP access logs, session records, platform user location data, and consumption records demonstrating actual use outside South Korea

B2B supplies must be documented through the Korean electronic tax invoice system. Electronic tax invoices issued under the e-Hometax system must be transmitted to the NTS by the day following the supply or payment date, whichever applies. There is no real-time cryptographic fiscal stamping requirement, but the NTS requires digital submission through HomeTax, supports machine-to-machine API-based transmission for approved software, and expects complete tax invoice data to be captured online. VAT returns are filed semi-annually through HomeTax, with interim prepayment returns due during the year; retail cash receipts are required for cash purchases where requested, with the receipt data transmitted to NTS.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-check export declarations, electronically transmitted tax invoices, and foreign currency remittance records. If zero-rating is claimed but the export cannot be substantiated, the NTS reverses the claim, assesses the standard 10% VAT on the supply, and imposes statutory additional tax penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act for late payment, underdeclaration, and failure to issue or transmit electronic tax invoices. In cases involving fraud or intentional concealment, the Framework Act on National Taxes permits a ten-year retroactive assessment window, and criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may result. The taxpayer must retain the full export documentation bundle for at least five years, and ten years where fraudulent misstatement is alleged. Fictitious or circular foreign exchange repatriation will be treated as evidence of domestic supply, triggering full VAT clawback, penalties, and possible criminal charges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade settlements, service fees, dividends, and royalties, subject to documentary reporting thresholds. Capital account transactions, such as foreign direct investment inbound equity subscriptions and cross-border loans, are generally permitted on a notification or declaration basis through a foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, but Korean banks require exporters to report export earnings and to repatriate proceeds within a statutory period, typically within 18 months from the export date for receipt into a foreign currency account or conversion into KRW. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal for properly registered foreign-invested enterprises: a Foreign Investment Report (FIRC) issued under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act is required to evidence the initial capital injection, and subsequent dividend remittances are processed through the bank upon submission of audited financial statements and tax clearance certificates. For a market research SaaS platform receiving cross-border consumer panel survey payments, the main operational FX consideration is that inbound cross-border service receipts should be reported as export proceeds under the code for 'Service Trade - Other Business Services (Market Research)'. The lack of a dedicated capital control regime for small service receivables means the treasury operation can use standard SWIFT MT103 credits with the correct foreign exchange purpose code (e.g., code 910000 or specific service category code) without needing central bank pre-approval. Nevertheless, the bank will apply anti-money laundering screening and may request underlying contracts and invoices for amounts above the equivalent of USD 10,000. Given the regulatory framework, the entity should maintain a KRW operational account and a USD or multi-currency foreign currency account at a domestic tier-1 bank. A fintech EMI is viable only as a downstream payment collection tool supported by a licensed domestic bank settlement account, not as a primary regulated banking rail for corporate FX obligations under Korean law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must report export earnings to the designated foreign exchange bank and repatriate the foreign currency proceeds into the exporter's foreign currency account or convert into KRW within 18 months from the date of export; the full amount must be received, and no mandatory conversion ratio is imposed, although submission of the Customs export declaration and the related service contract is required for service exports such as market research subscriptions.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting through the Foreign Exchange Information System operated by the Korea Customs Service and the Bank of Korea's Foreign Exchange Administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Fintech EMIs such as Wise and PayPal operate in South Korea under limited payment gateway arrangements and are not licensed as primary commercial banks under the Banking Act. They are legally barred from accepting vanilla corporate deposits or providing FX clearing services that would replace the banking system. However, the platform may use PayPal Mass Payout and Wise as peripheral collection and mass payout rails, as long as all onboarding funds are routed through a licensed domestic bank settlement account and that the EMI is integrated via open-banking APIs. The legal basis for this restriction is the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, which requires payment service providers to obtain a license from the Financial Services Commission (FSC). Since a foreign EMI typically lacks the required license, the entity should maintain a domestic corporate bank account as the settlement anchor and treat EMI collections as pre-settlement receivables subject to daily sweep into the licensed bank. Using an unlicensed EMI as the sole banking rail would constitute a compliance failure and expose the treasury structure to regulatory sanction by the FSC and the Bank of Korea.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% national top marginal corporate income tax rate for taxable income above KRW 300 billion, plus 10% local income tax surtax on national corporate tax producing a 24.2% top combined statutory burden; lower brackets are 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% above KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, and 21% above KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion

20% national withholding tax on outbound dividends plus a 2% additional local income tax surtax, producing an effective statutory rate of 22%; the rate may be reduced under a bilateral income tax treaty

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory for a Korean company when cross-border controlled transactions with a foreign related party exceed KRW 5 billion in the fiscal year; Master File documentation is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion, and Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated group revenue of at least KRW 100 billion; when a lower transaction threshold exists for a specific industry or tax-haven jurisdiction, separate reporting schedules govern.

Economic substance scope: The Republic of Korea is not an Economic Substance Regulations jurisdiction of the Gulf model, but its substance-over-form jurisprudence, permanent establishment rules, treaty beneficial-ownership requirements, and general anti-avoidance doctrines require a Korean entity to demonstrate real offices, employees, bank accounts, board minutes, risk management, and customer contracting activity; for a consumer panel survey SaaS platform, the Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in Korea or attributed to a Korean permanent establishment, including platform development, data hosting, client contracting, panelist management, invoicing, and after-sales support, otherwise the National Tax Service will disregard the Korean entity or deny treaty and incentive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Fraudulent Tax Evasion and Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, fraudulent evasion is punishable by imprisonment for up to five years or a fine of up to three times the tax evaded; the National Tax Service also imposes a 40% negligence penalty on the fraudulently under-reported amount and can publish the company as a tax evader.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced when the entity is a sham or when directors intentionally cause the company to defraud tax authorities; directors are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act if they actively manipulate funds, must make civil restitution of diverted corporate assets, face exit bans and blacklisting by the tax administration, and remain exposed to criminal imprisonment for tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Abuse and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: The Framework Act on International Tax Adjustment requires arm's-length pricing and permits the National Tax Service to adjust the tax base, impose a secondary adjustment declaring the excess remittance as a deemed dividend, and assess penalty taxes for failure to submit transfer-pricing documentation or for filing a return inconsistent with the arm's-length result.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute sham intercompany agreements or certify false transfer-pricing studies can be held civilly liable for the resulting tax and penalties, be required to restore the company's lost assets, be named on the tax-delinquent blacklist, be restricted from international departure, and may face imprisonment for willful preparation of false tax filings.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Abuse / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service can disregard the shell entity under substance-over-form principles, deny treaty reductions under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and the Principal Purpose Test, revoke foreign-investment tax incentives retroactively, assess corporate income tax on undistributed profits, and apply the 40% fraudulent-return penalty where the shell structure was knowingly concealed.

Director personal liability: Directors will be personally liable for unpaid tax and interest where the corporate form has been abused to evade tax, may be ordered by Korean courts to pay restitution to the tax authority or injured creditors, are exposed to exit bans and public blacklisting, and can be criminally prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for deliberate use of a no-substance conduit, carrying up to five years' imprisonment.

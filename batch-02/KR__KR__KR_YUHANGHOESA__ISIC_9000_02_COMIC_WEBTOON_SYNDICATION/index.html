Competent corporate registry

Commercial Registration Section (Deunggi-gwa) of the Registration Office of the District Court having jurisdiction over the registered office; in Seoul, the Seoul Central District Court Registration Office, under the Supreme Court of Korea.

Governing statute

Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea (Act No. 1000, as consolidated to January 2025), Part III 'Companies', Chapter 4 'Yuhan Hoesa' (Limited Liability Company), Articles 287-2 through 287-74 inclusive; together with the Commercial Registration Rules and the Registration Fee Schedule of the Supreme Court of Korea.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of Establishment of a Limited Liability Company (Yuhan Hoesa) accompanied by the certified Articles of Incorporation, capital payment certificate, office-use consent, and officer appointment statements; registration is evidenced by the Certificate of Company Registration (deunggi-sahang-jeungmyeongseo).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Yuhan Hoesa. The incorporators may choose the capital amount freely. However, all subscribed capital must be fully deposited into a blocked escrow or provisional subscription account at a licensed Korean bank before the incorporation registration is filed; the bank's capital payment certificate must accompany the registration application. The deposited capital is frozen and may not be released until the court issues the Certificate of Company Registration. For a digital webtoon publishing and syndication company, KRW 10,000,000 (approximately USD 7,500) is recommended to establish bankability and satisfy Apple and Google settlement counterparty requirements.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for an industrial Civil Defense fire permit because no manufacturing or warehousing operations exist. For the leased commercial office, the building owner must maintain a valid building fire safety certificate under the Fire Prevention and Safety in Buildings Act, and the company must comply with standard office workplace safety obligations under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including evacuation signage and first aid provision. No Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency factory approval is required for ISIC 9000 digital services.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with the statutory electronic portal being HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr), supported by the NTS English website (https://www.nts.go.kr) and, for export clearance, the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS portal (https://unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident investor forming a Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company), the company must first complete Korean commercial registration at the district court-level registration office; the court issues the unique Korean corporate registration number. The statutory tax registration with the National Tax Service occurs separately: within 20 days of commencement of a domestic taxable business the representative director (or a duly appointed tax representative for foreign-owned companies) must file the standard Business Registration Application to the NTS district tax office with territorial jurisdiction over the registered office. Supporting documents include the certificate of incorporation, commercial registration certificate, articles of incorporation, foreign investor passport/director ID, power of attorney, and local office lease evidence. The NTS validates the application and issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the company's TIN and VAT registration number for all electronic tax invoices, VAT returns, withholding tax administration and corporate tax filings. The same procedure applies for a foreign corporation registering a Korean-sourced taxable business through a permanent establishment; in that case the foreign head office must appoint an NTS-recognized tax representative and attach its home-country tax residence certificate. After registration, the company should procure a recognized joint digital security certificate for HomeTax electronic filing and e-tax invoice transmission. No separate VAT ID is required; the Business Registration Number is the single tax identity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid-entity elective classification regime for a Yuhan Hoesa; a Korean limited liability company is statutorily a domestic corporation subject to entity-level corporate taxation. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are corrected under the law's OECD BEPS Action 2-aligned provisions, not by taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None in local currency terms: a Korean Yuhan Hoesa must register for VAT from the first taxable supply; there is no economic turnover threshold excusing a corporation from registration. For individual sole proprietors only, a simplified taxable-person computation is available under the VAT Act for small turnover, but that concession is not available to a Yuhan Hoesa, which remains under the standard credit-invoice regime regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment is available under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act for (i) goods that are actually exported from Korea through customs, and (ii) services supplied to non-residents, including digital webtoon content, where the place of supply is outside Korea and the services are not consumed or enjoyed in Korea. The Yuhan Hoesa's multilingual webtoon chapter unlocking transactions sold through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing to non-Korean end-customers qualify for 0% VAT if the company retains platform-level transaction evidence proving that the purchaser's App Store or Play Store country of account and the consumer location are outside the Republic of Korea, the sale is concluded with the overseas store entity or the ultimate non-resident consumer, and the proceeds are received in foreign exchange by Korea from an authorized settlement channel rather than merely invoiced to a domestic entity.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic export declaration (the statutory customs export clearance record for physical merchandise; functionally equivalent to a Bill of Entry, and mandatory for any ancillary physical goods or collectible items shipped from Korea; for pure digital webtoon supply with no physical goods this declaration is replaced by a digital service export ledger).
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill) evidencing actual physical transportation out of Korea; not applicable to consumable digital chapter unlocks but mandatory for tangible comic-book merchandise.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or, for Korea, a bank-issued Foreign Exchange Transaction Receipt evidencing that the export value was repatriated into a Korean foreign-exchange bank account in convertible currency within the statutory export-proceeds deadline.
  • Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Console settlement reports showing the transaction, country of delivery, currency and net proceeds remitted by the offshore platform entity.
  • Customer-territory evidence: App Store or Google Play account country, IP-address geolocation audit logs, and the digital license metadata substantiating that each chapter unlock was delivered outside Korea.
  • Export sales contract, Webtoon content licensing or end-user terms, zero-rated electronic tax invoice marked as export, and the foreign-currency purchase order or payment file.

All B2B supplies and export transactions must be documented with an electronic tax invoice issued through the NTS HomeTax e-Tax Invoice system or a certified electronic tax invoice service provider. The electronic tax invoice must be transmitted to the NTS at or immediately after issue, and no later than the next day after issuance, in the mandated electronic data set; the system validates VAT numbers, supply value, tax amount and transaction parties, and retains an electronic audit trail. Korea does not issue a per-transaction CFDI-style fiscal stamp or require government API pre-clearance of every invoice, but the HomeTax platform provides machine-readable e-filing APIs and the mandatory electronic tax invoice dataset must contain all statutory fields. Failure to transmit, late transmission, or issuance of false electronic tax invoices makes input VAT non-deductible and subjects the issuer to additional taxes under the VAT Act.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service will deny zero-rating if the supporting customs declaration, shipping documents, platform settlement reports, FIRC-equivalent bank receipt, or end-user location evidence are false, missing, or internally inconsistent. A denied export zero-rating is clawed back retrospectively by computing 10% VAT on the full uncollected export consideration, adding the standard additional tax for understated VAT and failure to issue valid tax receipts, and applying daily or monthly delinquency charges from the original filing deadline. If the export proceeds are not physically repatriated in the required 180-day foreign-exchange window, the NTS treats the supply as having enjoyed unjustified zero-rating and foreign-exchange penalties may also apply. Fraudulent falsification of export documents or deliberate use of shell app-store transactions to fabricate an export qualifies for criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including fines and imprisonment, together with the extended reassessment period for fraudulent non-disclosure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized but regulated foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with Bank of Korea and Ministry of Economy and Finance as the primary regulators. The won is broadly convertible for current account activities such as trade settlements, royalties, and service fees, although every transaction above defined thresholds must be processed through a designated foreign exchange bank and reported for statistical and supervision purposes. Capital account operations, including foreign equity injections, loans, and repatriation of invested capital, have moved toward free notification in principle, but remain subject to declaration or approval depending on the sector and counterparty jurisdiction. There are no daily conversion ceilings for corporates, yet all receipts and payments must be justified by underlying commercial documentation. Profit remittance in the form of shareholder dividends is permitted after payment of corporate income tax and dividend withholding tax, followed by submission of audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and the relevant foreign exchange business form to the bank. In practice, the bank performs an enhanced review to prevent disguised capital flight. The Republic of Korea maintains real-time transaction monitoring through the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must collect export proceeds in foreign currency within six months from the date of export declaration or the date of loading, unless a justifiable extension is granted by the designated foreign exchange bank or Bank of Korea. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may hold foreign currency in domestic foreign-currency accounts or convert to KRW at prevailing market rates. Uncollected export proceeds must be reported and may incur administrative penalties if the exporter fails to obtain legitimate credit guarantee or delay justification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as foreign exchange policy regulator and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KOFIU) as AML supervisor

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut Business, Wise, and Payoneer have low viability as primary corporate banking suppliers in South Korea. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, a foreign exchange business license from the Ministry of Economy and Finance is required to debit and credit KRW corporate accounts or to execute domestic clearing. Unlicensed EMIs cannot provide Korean settlement accounts, cannot receive app-store KRW payouts from Apple and Google, and may not sign up as the entity's designated foreign exchange bank. They could be used only for auxiliary USD/EUR collections outside Korea, but local payroll, VAT payments, and capital verifications still require a licensed domestic bank. The risk exists that EMIs are legally barred from servicing Korean domestic entities for local-currency treasury operations.

Turnover: Under the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies, as applied to a Yuhan Hoesa LLC, mandatory independent CPA audit is triggered where the company at the end of the immediately preceding fiscal year has total assets of at least KRW 12 billion (approximately USD 8.9 million). For ordinary unlisted private LLCs, turnover and headcount are not standalone audit triggers; however, where the company qualifies as a public-interest entity or financial company, the audit mandate also applies if at least two of the following three thresholds are exceeded: (i) total assets of KRW 12 billion; (ii) annual revenue of KRW 100 billion; (iii) average headcount of 300 or more employees. Any statutory audit must be performed by a certified public accountant registered with the Financial Supervisory Service. The revenue and headcount tests are supplemental to, and not substitutes for, the statutory asset test; otherwise the LLC is exempt from mandatory external audit unless the articles of incorporation or financing covenants require one.

Box 1 - Business Revenue / Gross Sales (Webtoon Digital Content, Coin Top-ups, Chapter Unlocks, Syndication Royalties) Aggregate gross revenues from digital comic chapters, in-app purchase coins, platform subscriptions, advertisement, IP licensing and export syndication fees. For a foreign corporation with a Korean permanent establishment (PE), report only domestic-source revenues attributable to that PE. For a Korean-resident Yuhan Hoesa, report worldwide revenue. Apple IAP and Google Play Billing collections must be reported at gross level; platform fees are not netted against revenue. Box 2 - Cost of Sales (Creative Production Costs, Artist Royalties, Cloud Hosting, Platform Commission Costs) Direct costs of producing webtoon chapters, creator royalties, translator fees, server hosting, and channel store commission costs recognized as an expense for tax accounting. Costs paid to foreign affiliates must meet the arm's length principle under Article 5 of the International Tax Coordination Act; contemporaneous Local File documentation is required if the annual aggregate foreign related-party transaction amount exceeds KRW 5 billion. For a foreign corporation's PE, only expenses directly attributable to the Korean business and substantiated with invoices are deductible; apportioned head-office overhead requires a tax office approval and cannot reduce income below zero in a manner inconsistent with treaty nondiscrimination.
Box 3 - Gross Profit / (Loss) Automatic computation of Box 1 minus Box 2. No entry input is required; however, the gross margin in webtoon syndication must be checked against benchmark data, especially if the content is licensed to an overseas affiliate. A chronic low-margin position may trigger an income adjustment.
Box 4 - Selling, General and Administrative Expenses (Marketing, Depreciation, Employee Compensation, Insurance, Cloud Services) Operating expenses, including director remuneration, marketing, depreciation of developed-IP and servers, software amortization and corporate insurance. For a non-resident corporation, only expenses incurred for the PE's business inside Korea are deductible. Management fees paid to a foreign parent are disallowed unless the parent can prove concrete rendering of services (ITCA Article 95) and the fees are at arm's length. The webtoon entity must not book a residual profit to the parent through excessive SG&A fees.
Box 5 - Add-back and Tax Adjustment Items (Non-deductible Expenses, Entertainment Limits, Reserves, Tax Penalties) Enforceable statutory additions for items such as corporate entertainment above the legal cap, fines, tax penalties, non-deductible reserves, and undisclosed income amounts. Enter all non-deductible fines and penalties; for a year with gross revenue exceeding KRW 10 billion, only 50% of entertainment expenses exceeding the statutory cap is deductible. If a transaction is conducted with a tax haven affiliate without economic substance, the adjustment is made under ITCA Article 80, disallowing the related expense or including undeclared foreign undistributed income.
Box 6 - Taxable Income / Assessable Income (과세표준) Final income subject to corporate income tax after the application of adjustments and permitted income deductions (loss carryforwards et al.). The Korean-sourced net income of a non-resident with a PE is limited to domestic-source items enumerated under ITCA Article 93. Treaty income attribution rules apply, and taxpayers must attach a PE income attribution schedule. Loss carryforward of 15 years is recognized only if the prior year return was filed timely and the fiscal year has lasted at least 6 months.
Box 7 - Tax Reduction and Credits (Foreign Tax Credit, R&D Tax Credit, Investment Tax Credit) Subtract tax credits from the calculated gross tax, including foreign tax credit under ITCA Article 81 and credits under Article 10 of the Restriction of Special Taxation Act. A resident Yuhan Hoesa can claim a foreign tax credit for actual taxes paid to a foreign country on foreign-source income; the credit amount is capped at the Korean tax attributable to that foreign income. For non-residents, no credit is granted against the WHT of the foreign shareholder. R&D credits for digital content development are enumerated; if unused, the credit carryforward is 10 years.
Box 8 - Final Corporate Income Tax Payable (including Branch Profits Tax and Local Income Tax Charge) Gross corporate tax less credits equals the final tax payable; for a foreign PE, add the branch remittance tax payable upon actual repatriation of after-tax branch profits; aggregate local income tax is declared in a supporting schedule. For a domestic subsidiary, the branch tax does not apply. The 10% local income tax surcharge is a declared amount attached to the main form. For any distribution to foreign shareholders, 22% dividend withholding tax must be accrued and paid. If the Yuhan Hoesa makes an outbound dividend while loss carryforward is unused, the dividend WHT is still payable.
Box 9 (supporting schedule) - Withholding Declaration for Outbound Distributions, Interest and Royalties to Non-Residents Declaration of all payments to foreign shareholders, foreign lenders and foreign licensors subject to withholding under Article 67-2 and Article 67-3 of the Corporate Tax Act, with a treaty relief attachment. A separate withholding return must be lodged before the 10th day of the month following payment to a non-resident. Statutory rates are 20% plus 2% local surcharge = 22% for dividends, interest and royalties. To claim the relevant bilateral DTA rate, the payor or recipient must file a treaty-application schedule, attach the Tax Residency Certificate and Beneficial Ownership Statement, and prove economic substance in the foreign jurisdiction.

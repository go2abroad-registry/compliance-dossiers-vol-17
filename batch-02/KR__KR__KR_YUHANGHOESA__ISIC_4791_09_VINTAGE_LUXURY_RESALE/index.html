Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox-only address will be rejected for this industry by the National Tax Service and almost all regulated Korean banks. The income tax and VAT audit posture for cross-border resale and luxury consignment treats a mailbox address as a red flag for the sham office doctrine and the substance-over-form principle. The Korean court requires a real domestic office address for the company to serve as the venue for service of process, board meetings, books and records, and physical license inspections. Because ISIC 4791 involves high-value secondhand luxury goods, the National Tax Service will use the absence of a physical inventory or consignment warehouse to re-characterize the entity as an engagement of a foreign enterprise place of business, resulting in withholding and permanent establishment exposure. A registered office must have leasehold rights evidenced by a building owner certificate, an outlined inventory storage area or receiving facility, and at least one employee accountable for the daily business operations. Banks will require a physical premises verification visit before opening the corporate account, and the premium brand consignors will also demand an operational office to satisfy their own compliance program.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act; however, the capital amount stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully subscribed and deposited into a provisional Korean bank account in the name of the proposed company before registration. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic tax portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr); customs trade data and export/import declaration portal: Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated domestic Korean limited company (Yuhan Hoesa), file the Business Registration Application with the competent NTS district tax office (based on the registered place of business) within 20 days from business commencement, either through HomeTax or by in-person submission. The application must attach the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, shareholders and officers registry, copy of the legal representative's identification, and evidence of the registered business address (lease agreement or ownership certificate). NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) and Business Registration Certificate; the BRN is simultaneously the corporate tax registration number and the VAT registration number. A foreign non-resident entity with no Korean place of business must appoint a domestic tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes, submit the Business Registration Application in the non-resident business operator category through HomeTax or the tax representative's district office, and attach the foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, apostilled or legalized power of attorney appointing the tax representative, foreign tax residence certificate, and a description of the Korean taxable supplies. NTS then issues a non-resident BRN/VAT ID. When the entity exports or imports goods, it must also register with Korea Customs Service as an exporter/importer on UNI-PASS and obtain the customs filer code to use in export declarations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None (no monetary threshold for general VAT registration). A business operator must register within 20 days of commencing Korean taxable business. Small individual businesses with annual taxable sales under KRW 80,000,000 may be eligible for simplified taxpayer status, but they are still required to register for VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The sale of goods by a Korean VAT registrant is zero-rated when the goods are physically exported from the Republic of Korea and the export is declared and cleared in the exporter's name through Korea Customs Service. The seller must be the actual exporter; the goods must leave Korea under an export declaration; the export proceeds must be received in foreign currency or through a documentable settlement method consistent with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the seller must issue and transmit a zero-rated export e-Tax Invoice to HomeTax; and the seller must retain the complete export and payment documentation for audit. Qualifying offshore services and certain digital services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated when the place of supply is outside Korea, the services are consumed outside Korea, and payment is received under the foreign-exchange requirements. For vintage luxury goods sold domestically under the margin scheme, the 10% VAT applies to the consignment margin rather than the full retail price; exports, however, are zero-rated on the full supply value with input VAT recovery available.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (export cargo declaration) filed and released through UNI-PASS
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, multimodal waybill, master air waybill, or house airway bill issued by a licensed carrier or courier
  • Zero-rated export e-Tax Invoice transmitted to HomeTax, showing the export declaration number and 0% VAT rate
  • Foreign exchange purchase certificate or bank inward remittance certificate issued by a licensed foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act
  • Consignment agreement or proof of lawful acquisition and ownership of the vintage luxury goods, retained for customs valuation, margin-scheme audit, and AML/KYC verification

Mandatory e-Tax Invoice issuance through HomeTax for all VAT-registered businesses; invoice data, in XML/JSON format, is transmitted to NTS through the HomeTax portal or NTS Open API and is continuously reconciled with VAT returns, customs declarations made on UNI-PASS, and bank/foreign-exchange settlement records. Korea does not operate a real-time pre-supply fiscal-clearance stamping system; fiscal integrity is achieved through authenticated electronic invoice transmission, digital signatures, and ex-post cross-visibility by NTS. For B2C retail transactions, the seller must also comply with the electronic cash receipt system when the buyer requests a cash receipt or when cash receipts are statutorily required.

If NTS or Korea Customs Service determines that the export was not substantiated, the zero-rating is denied, the 10% VAT on the gross supply is assessed under the Value-Added Tax Act, and the refunded input VAT is clawed back with statutory additional tax in the form of underreporting penalties, non-filing penalties, late-payment surcharges, and interest. Fraudulent or concealed transactions may trigger criminal tax penalties, including fines and imprisonment, under Korean tax crime legislation and the Framework Act on National Taxes. The authorities maintain a joint data-exchange mechanism between HomeTax, UNI-PASS, and banking/foreign-exchange settlement systems, enabling retrospective assessment within the statutory limitation periods, with extended periods for fraud and transactions with foreign elements.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a registered and negative-list foreign exchange system rather than a stringent surrender-based model. Current account transactions, including export and import payments, service fees, and cross-border trade settlements, are generally freely convertible and can be routed through any authorized foreign-exchange bank. Capital account transactions are subject to reporting and registration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, while foreign direct investment is separately regulated under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The Korean won is not an offshore deliverable currency in practice, so all actual KRW settlement must take place through a domestic foreign-exchange bank. Export proceeds are required to be collected within six months from shipment and may be either converted into KRW or retained in a foreign-currency deposit; no fixed statutory surrender ratio to the Bank of Korea is currently imposed. Profit remittance for a foreign-invested company is permitted after proper inward investment registration, corporate tax payment, and presentation of audited financial statements; the key hurdles are documentary, not convertibility-based. Persistent non-reporting of foreign currency receipts, unlicensed offshore EMI usage, or mismatched transaction purpose codes creates direct legal and banking compliance exposure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Trade Foreign Exchange Regulations, export proceeds must be returned and settled through an authorized domestic foreign-exchange bank within six months from the date of shipment, unless an extension is approved. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0 percent; there is no requirement to surrender export foreign currency to the Bank of Korea. Qualifying proceeds may be held in a foreign-currency deposit with the designated bank, but the underlying export declaration, invoice, and transport documents must be preserved and submitted to the bank for transaction-purpose verification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with the Foreign Exchange Administration of the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the primary foreign exchange policy and enforcement authority, supported by the Financial Services Commission and Korean financial intelligence unit

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI account-holding is not viable for this Korean resident entity. A Korean corporation must process inbound and outbound foreign exchange through a domestic bank registered as a foreign-exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Digital EMIs such as offshore banking-as-a-service platforms are not licensed in Korea to settle KRW, issue Korean IBANs, or receive export proceeds in compliance with Korean transaction-purpose codes. They may be used only as a commercial payment gateway that ultimately sweeps funds to a domestic Korean bank account; using an EMI as the settlement account creates LRS non-compliance, administrative fines, and possible corporate bank account termination. Feasibility rating: Low as a settlement rail, Medium only as a software-based merchant collection layer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must designate a domestic Korean foreign-exchange bank and file a foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before or on the date of remittance. The investor opens a temporary capital deposit account at the bank, transfers funds through SWIFT MT103, and the bank verifies the foreign investor identity, share subscription agreement, and the exact foreign exchange purpose code for acquisition of Korean equity. After the remittance is credited, the company issues shares and the bank issues a certificate of foreign investment enterprise registration.

No statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) under the Commercial Act; however, at least one share must be issued and the total amount of capital subscribed must be fully paid-in, in cash, before the date of incorporation. Capital in kind, if used, must be appraised by a court-appointed examiner and physically contributed prior to registration. Non-resident shareholders may contribute foreign currency, but must repatriate funds through a Korean exchange bank and must comply with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reporting.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal is HomeTax (hometax.go.kr). Customs export clearance and cross-border documentation interface is Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity operating as a Yuhan Hoesa with a Korean place of business, the domestic representative must submit an Application for Business Registration to the local district tax office having jurisdiction over the principal business address. The filing pack must include certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, appointment of a domestic business representative, power of attorney if a tax representative is appointed, proof of the Korean fiscal address, bank account details, and the representative's foreign-registration or alien certificate where applicable. Upon approval NTS issues the Business Registration Certificate and assigns the 10-digit Business Registration Number used as the taxpayer identification number for corporate tax and VAT. For a foreign entity with no Korean establishment that supplies electronic services, including web scraping, proxy API, or other digital services, to non-registered Korean customers, the entity must file simplified foreign VAT registration through HomeTax, appoint a statutory tax representative in Korea for service of process and VAT compliance, and obtain a VAT registration identification number before the first taxable supply. Registration is mandatory from first taxable activity; there is no turnover-based de minimis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the VAT Act, exported goods are zero-rated when they are physically exported under a Korea Customs Service export clearance declaration. Qualifying exported services, including international services and certain offshore digital services, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident customer, supplied from Korea to overseas users, performed or consumed wholly outside Korea, and settled in foreign exchange with documented repatriation. For the ISIC 6201 web scraping and proxy API gateway business, the exported digital service qualifies for 0% VAT if the Korean supplier provides the API or data services to non-resident customers using the service outside Korea, invoices in foreign currency, and receives the consideration through an authorized foreign exchange bank or legitimate cross-border settlement rail. The supplier must retain the service contract, foreign-currency commercial invoice, usage logs or gateway records showing offshore consumption, and bank remittance evidence. Any digital service that is actually used or exploited in Korea remains standard-rated regardless of the residence of the contracting customer.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate) generated through UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or international consignment note
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Earning Certificate issued by an authorized Korean foreign exchange bank confirming receipt of the export proceeds

Korea operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice regime under the VAT Act. All VAT-registered suppliers issuing tax invoices for domestic supplies must transmit e-tax invoices to the National Tax Service through HomeTax or certified e-invoice intermediaries. The supplier must issue and transmit the e-tax invoice within the statutory deadline generally by the 10th day of the month following the supply date; the NTS performs electronic schema validation, documentary serial-number control, and automated matching between supplier output invoices and purchaser input invoices. This is not a real-time pre-clearance model but is a statutory near-real-time e-reporting and anti-fraud matching system with ERP-to-HomeTax API integration for high-volume filers. Paper invoices are permitted only in limited cases involving simplified taxpayers or technical failure contingencies.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly audit export zero-rating by cross-matching UNI-PASS customs declarations, e-tax invoice output, input invoice data, bank foreign-exchange records, and VAT refund claims. If the exporter or service supplier cannot produce the required documentation bundle, or if export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, the zero-rate is disallowed retroactively. NTS will assess unpaid VAT at the standard 10% rate plus binding interest and additional penalties for insufficient returns, unreported returns, or false returns. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims may trigger penalty surcharges of up to 40% of underpaid VAT and criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for intentional tax evasion.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is convertible on the current account; trade-related and ordinary service payments are liberalized and may be remitted through foreign exchange banks with reporting obligations. Capital account transactions, including equity injections, loans, and acquisition of local assets, are generally permissible but require prior declaration, purpose-code reporting, and supporting underlying documentation. A foreign enterprise may convert USD or other foreign currency into KRW at prevailing market rates, but the won is not an internationally deliverable currency outside specified markets; offshore trading is concentrated in NDFs because KRW settlement is restricted outside licensed domestic banks. Profit and dividend remittance is permitted after local payment of corporate and withholding taxes; the paying bank requires audited financial statements, board resolution, tax payment certificate and, if applicable, a tax treaty residence certificate to reduce the statutory 22% withholding rate. The regime therefore offers current-account freedom but imposes compliance gates on capital repatriation rather than absolute bans. Treasury structuring should rely on a licensed Korean foreign-exchange bank for all conversions and avoid offshore KRW liquidity pools.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds are collected through a Korean authorized foreign-exchange bank; for goods, the customary statutory settlement timeline is 90 days from export declaration unless an extension for deferred payment terms is approved, while services/API proceeds follow the invoiced due date. No mandatory surrender percentage exists; exporters may hold 100% of proceeds in a foreign currency account or convert into KRW freely. Each receipt must be filed by the bank in the Electronic Foreign Exchange Reporting System within 30 days.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Overseas fintech EMIs without a Korean license cannot hold KRW deposits or clear payments under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. They may operate only as an interface over a licensed Korean bank account; direct handling of the entity's funds is legally unavailable. Stripe can aggregate foreign card receipts but must settle to a local bank account in KRW or USD via an acquiring bank partnership.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 100 billion annual revenue - earning companies with related-party cross-border transactions exceeding KRW 50 billion must prepare and submit Local File and Master File; Refresh documentation threshold for a standard small-cap Yuhan Hoesa is KRW 5 billion in intercompany transactions (same country relation) and KRW 10 billion for cross-border controlled transactions (if annual revenue is under KRW 100 billion, simplified / real-time audit documentation applies).

Economic substance scope: Although Korea does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, the National Tax Basic Act Article 14 applies a substance-over-form doctrine. For a cross-border API gateway company, the Korean entity must prove that its management decisions, server operations, customer contracting, billing systems, and data-processing employees are located in Korea. If the entity is a shell with its actual income-generating activities in a no-tax jurisdiction, the NTS may recharacterize the arrangements and impose tax on the true passing-through of profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Underreported Revenue

Statutory sanction: Article 9 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act - imprisonment up to 10 years, plus a fine of up to 5 times the evaded tax amount; monetary penalty may not be less than the tax avoided if intentional tax evasion exceeds KRW 100 million.

Director personal liability: A corporate veil will be pierced where the director ignored internal controls or used tax saving structures to enrich himself individually. Directors become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax plus penalty accruals (National Tax Basic Act Article 39). The NTS may enjoin departure (travel ban), freeze personal bank accounts, and sale of personal assets. Imprisonment is available for the maximum statutory term; a criminal fine is also executed together with civil restitution.
Thin Capitalization and Disguised Dividend Abuse

Statutory sanction: Article 54 of the Korean Tax Procedure Act (Thin Capitalization Rules) - interest recharacterized as a non-deductible distribution, corporate income tax recomputed, and 22% withholding tax imposed on the recharacterized interest; additional 20% penalty for unfiled withholding; administrative and criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act if deceit is involved.

Director personal liability: A director who served as the signor of the loan agreement or who structured the funding arrangement is personally liable for the recharacterized dividend tax and any criminal fine. The NTS may issue an oarsman (travel ban) and impose a seizure over real estate, automobiles, and securities held by the director. If the director is convicted under Article 9 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, imprisonment between 1 and 10 years applies, plus a fine of up to 5 times the intentionally evaded amount.
AML / Unlicensed Virtual Asset Receipt

Statutory sanction: Article 17 of the Act on Reporting and Using Specific Financial Information (Korea Anti-Laundering Act) - imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to KRW 50 million for unlicensed VASP operation; income tax evasion penalty up to 40% of the unreported amount under the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Director personal liability: Because the director owns and directs the Korean Yuhan Hoesa, the NTS and Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) treat the entity as a transparent conduit for the non-reporting financial conduct. The director faces personal criminal liability for money laundering, punitive fines, and confiscation of the illicit gains. A corporate veil can be pierced under the Basic Act if the director used the entity as a shield to conceal his own crypto arbitrage; the director may be blacklisted by the Korean Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU) for cross-border remittance restrictions, and travel abroad is prohibited while under criminal investigation. Korean courts have upheld director imprisonment of up to 5 years and KRW 50 million fine for funneling crypto proceeds through shell companies without KYC/AML procedures.

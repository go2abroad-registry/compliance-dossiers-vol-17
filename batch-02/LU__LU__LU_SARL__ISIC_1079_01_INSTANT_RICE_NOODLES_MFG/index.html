Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) for direct taxes and VAT; Luxembourg Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA, AEDT) for VAT registration; single citizen/business portal: MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or registering in Luxembourg must first obtain a Luxembourg business identification number (matricule d'identification) via the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) eRegistration platform or through MyGuichet.lu. For VAT purposes, the foreign taxable person must apply for a Luxembourg VAT number (numéro de TVA) by submitting Form 104 or the applicable eForm to the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu, appointing a VAT representative where required for non-EU established persons. The application must state the intended effective date of taxable activity, expected turnover, and the nature of supplies. Upon approval, the AEDT issues the VAT identification number. Foreign entities established in another EU Member State may instead register under the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) regime for distance sales or non-established business-to-consumer services; a fixed establishment or direct taxable B2B supplies may require a local Luxembourg VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The Luxembourg SARL is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune), with no check-the-box transparency election available. Statutory elections include: (i) group taxation/fiscal unity for corporate income tax and municipal business tax where a resident parent holds at least 95% of a resident subsidiary for at least one full tax year; (ii) VAT group registration (assujetti unique) for vertically integrated or economically linked Luxembourg entities; (iii) optional 50% net wealth tax deduction for entities owned by tax transparent persons; and (iv) hybrid mismatch election options limited to aligning treatment under ATAD / DAC6 for EU cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual taxable turnover for the small business exemption; mandatory VAT registration is required for any taxable person making taxable supplies in Luxembourg that exceed this threshold or for non-established foreign suppliers providing distance sales to Luxembourg consumers exceeding EUR 10,000 per calendar year, and for businesses engaged in supplies subject to reverse charge or with a Luxembourg fixed establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) the supply of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union by a Luxembourg taxable person or on its behalf; (ii) supplies of goods covered by the customs warehousing or free-zone procedures; and (iii) supplies of services directly connected with the export or to recipients established outside the EU, provided the recipient is a taxable person or the service falls under specific territoriality rules. For instant rice noodle manufacturing, typical export zero-rating is available when the seller retains valid export proof showing the goods physically left EU territory and entered a third country; the goods must be exported within 3 months of supply, and the manufacturer must hold appropriate documentation to evidence both the export and the receipt of payment from the foreign buyer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ENS/SAD) - Declaration of Export (Déclaration d'exportation) with assigned MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical dispatch from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT payment evidence from the foreign buyer proving repatriation of export proceeds

Electronic invoicing is mandatory in Luxembourg exclusively for business-to-government (B2G) transactions, requiring structured e-invoices in PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format transmitted through the PEPPOL network; submission is via the MyGuichet.lu portal or direct access points. For business-to-business (B2B) and business-to-consumer (B2C) invoices, there is no real-time fiscal clearance requirement, no mandatory continuous transaction controls (CTCs), and no digital stamping regime. Luxembourg does not currently operate an API-based real-time transaction reporting system like those in Italy or France; however, taxpayers must retain full audit trails and issue invoices electronically or on paper in compliance with EU VAT Directive invoicing rules.

Luxembourg tax authorities may deny zero-rating if the exporter fails to provide conclusive proof of physical export within the statutory period or lacks valid payment evidence. In audit, the AEDT may retrospectively assess VAT at the standard 17% rate on invoiced amounts, plus late-payment interest (intérêts de retard) and penalties for tax fraud or negligence. For fraudulent export zero-rating claims (e.g., fictitious exports, forged shipping documents, or unrepatriated proceeds), penalties may reach up to 100% of the tax evaded, and criminal prosecution is possible under Luxembourg tax law. The statute of limitations for assessments is generally 5 years, extended to 10 years for fraud, and joint liability may be imposed on directors and VAT representatives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as a euro-area member state, maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the EU Capital Movement Directive (Dir. 88/361/EEC). No exchange-control approval, conversion restriction, or prior authorization is required for ordinary business transactions, capital contributions, dividends, or profit repatriation. The practical remittance hurdles are corporate-law based: certified annual accounts, a timely shareholder meeting, statutory auditor certification where required, and compliance with dividend distribution tests under Luxembourg company law; non-resident shareholders may face Luxembourg withholding tax of 15% on dividends unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no holding-period restriction on export receipts. Export invoices may be denominated in EUR or any freely convertible currency, and receivables may be retained in foreign currency accounts; only standard accounting, controllers, and VAT invoice requirements apply.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking conduct and prudential AML supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for auxiliary cross-border cash movements but Low for primary corporate treasury. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Luxembourg under the Payment Services Directive (PSD2) may legally hold transactional funds and issue IBANs; however, Tier-1 Luxembourg lenders generally will not extend credit, issue commercial documentary LCs, or provide committed FX lines at an EMI. CSSF-regulated payment institutions and EMIs are also subject to client-money safeguarding rules and may impose balance or turnover thresholds less suited to a manufacturing company's high-value trade payments. Legal and operational risks include unavailability of MT103 correspondent messaging, potentially narrower UBO/KYC evidence requirements, and lack of insolvency protection under a banking license.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant methodology: inward remittance from the ultimate shareholder or financing entity to the SARL's dedicated capital escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution using SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer where both accounts are within SEPA. The message must specify the beneficiary SARL with complete IBAN/BIC, the payment reference 'Souscription au capital social' or 'Share capital contribution', and an ISO 20022 external purpose code indicating capital/investment. The remitting bank should attach source-of-funds documentation, including audited accounts, dividend resolution, or loan agreement, and the Luxembourg bank will perform a sanctions and MLRO check. For share capital in excess of the minimum, a Luxembourg notary certifies the notarized deed and bank certificate; the RCS filing follows. No Central Bank approval or exchange control license is required; only the standard CSSF/AML compliance gates and a debt-to-equity compatibility test apply where a shareholder loan is used.Primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct T/T settlement to suppliers/distributors; (ii) commercial letters of credit issued under ICC UCP 600 via SWIFT MT700 to overseas beneficiary banks; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer/Instant payment instructions for intra-European EUR payments; and (iv) TARGET2 gross settlement for high-value euro payments. Documentary trade payments require compliant commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading/CMRs, certificates of origin and, where applicable, phytosanitary/food-safety certificates. The SARL's account mandate must clearly evidence dual signatory authorization for all outbound flows above the agreed threshold, with recurring counterparty screening.Outright FX spot and forward contracts with CSSF-authorized credit institutions, principally EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, and EUR/SEK/CHF tenors aligned to the cash-flow cycle, under an ISDA/CSA or local master agreement with collateral posting.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts for illiquid emerging-market currencies, concluded only with a regulated Luxembourg bank or EEA investment firm and backed by a full netting and credit-support annex.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps for working-capital or capital-injection conversion, entered under a negotiated ISDA 2002 master agreement, with valuation, breakage clauses, and internal treasury delegation.Foreign exchange options and zero-cost collars for manufacturing-input or export-revenue currencies, structured only as hedging instruments and not speculative, with board-approved VaR/notional limits and trade confirmation under EMIR clearing obligations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate effective headline rate for commercial companies in Luxembourg City (FY 2024/2025), composed of: (i) resident corporate income tax (CIT) of 17.00% on taxable profits exceeding EUR 175,000 (15.00% on the first EUR 175,000 bracket); (ii) unemployment fund surcharge of 7.00% calculated on the CIT amount; (iii) municipal business tax (IFC) of 6.75% applied on taxable commercial profit after statutory abatement.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends distributed by an SARL to non-resident shareholders. The rate is reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the beneficial owner is an EU/EEA company holding at least 10% of the SARL's capital for an uninterrupted 12-month period; under comprehensive DTA treaties the rate is typically 5% for substantial holdings (e.g. 10% under LU-US; 10% under LU-CN unless the Chinese corporate holder owns 25% and meeting treaty tests, then 5%); domestic and treaty relief require proof of beneficial ownership.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the Model 362 return is statutorily due on 30 April of the following calendar year; administratively, the Luxembourg direct tax administration (ACD) customarily grants extensions to 30 September upon request filed by a registered tax advisor; subsequent ex officio assessments follow after the deadline is missed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Luxembourg Local File and Master File documentation apply where the SARL belongs to a multinational group with consolidated net turnover of at least EUR 50,000,000, or where the aggregate value of controlled transactions in the fiscal year exceeds EUR 100,000; documentation must be furnished to the ACD within 15 days of a formal demand (Law of 24 February 2023 codifying LIR Art. 56 and ACD Transfer Pricing Circular 164/2023).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but substance requirements flow from (i) LIR Art. 6, requiring the effective place of management to be situated in Luxembourg; (ii) LIR Art. 56, requiring arm's length remuneration for all functions, assets, and risks; (iii) OECD BEPS and EU ATAD anti-abuse rules, including the GAAR and the controlled foreign company rules; a rice-noodle manufacturing SARL with physical production lines, quality-control laboratories, raw-material inventory, local payroll, and managerial decision-making in Luxembourg fully satisfies the substance doctrine, whereas a letterbox entity routing 100% margins without local activity will be recharacterised or disregarded; the ACD will pierce the arrangement and reassess at the full 24.94% with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Invoice Manufacturing

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under LIR Art. 395 and the General Tax Procedure Act: fines from EUR 10,000 to EUR 250,000, imprisonment up to 5 years; civil reassessment with a 25% penalty surcharge and denial of all deductions linked to the falsified documentation; publication of the sanction by the tax administration.

Director personal liability: The gérant or managing director who signed false vouchers or knowingly allowed off-book revenue is personally liable for the full additional tax, interest, and penalties; the corporate veil is pierced where the SARL is unable to pay, and courts have ordered direct civil restitution from the director's personal estate, issued exit travel bans, and imposed custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation or disregard of the SARL's legal form under the OECD CAAR and ATAD GAAR; reassessment of the full 24.94% CIT on the deemed Luxembourg profit; denial of DTA and EU directive benefits; retroactive interest and penalties of up to 25%; potential mutual agreement and secondary adjustments.

Director personal liability: Sole or shadow directors are jointly liable for the SARL's liabilities under para. 435 of the Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies where inadequate capitalisation or artificial restructuring caused damage; sanctions include disqualification from holding directorships for up to 10 years, personal assessment for the unpaid CIT, and criminal fines.
AML / Cash Transactions and Suspicious Reporting

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004, as amended by the Parity Law of 10 July 2020: administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 imposed by the Ministry of Justice/Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier; freezing of the funds involved; publication of the sanction and suspension or revocation of the food-manufacturing licence.

Director personal liability: Directors who consciously or negligently omitted the CTR/SAR filing are criminally liable with imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 500,000; in bankruptcy, the directors are civilly liable to creditors and the tax authority where the AML failure is the proximate cause of the SARL's insolvency.
Persistent Late Filing, Non-Payment, and Clandestine Manufacturing

Statutory sanction: Ex officio assessment on the ACD's unfavourable estimate; late-filing penalties escalating to 25%; late-payment interest at 0.6% per month; reassessment within a ten-year limitation period where a taxpayer acted with intent to conceal; dissolution action initiated by the ACD; revocation of the RCS registration for solvent companies.

Director personal liability: The directors are personally liable for the increased tax burden arising from gross negligence (faute lourde) or bad faith under general Luxembourg civil law; travel bans and exit restrictions have been imposed in aggravated cases, and the directors may be sentenced to imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years, with a prohibition on managing any commercial company for up to 10 years.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. South Korea imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, the total capital must be fully subscribed and paid in at incorporation, and the amount must be adequate for the intended business scope. There is no blocked escrow account requirement; capital may be deposited into a corporate bank account, and a certificate of deposit is required for registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate (So-bang-an-jeon-gi-hoe) from the National Fire Agency (NFA) for the registered office and any storage facilities, including installation of fire extinguishers, sprinklers, and emergency exits. Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) compliance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including workplace safety training, personal protective equipment, and accident reporting.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic filing and entity registration are processed through the Hometax portal (www.hometax.go.kr), while export declarations are processed through Korea Customs Service via the UNI-PASS system.

E-government filing portal

For a Yuhan Hoesa (Korean LLC) with non-resident shareholders, the LLC itself is a Korean domestic taxable entity; the non-resident shareholders do not need a separate Korean tax ID solely for the corporate vehicle. The procedure is: (1) incorporate the Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act at the competent District Court Registry, with non-resident shareholders permitted to hold 100% of equity; (2) appoint a Korean resident statutory representative or domestic tax agent for service of process and NTS correspondence; (3) after commencement of business, file the business registration application with the competent local district tax office, either by visitation or electronically through Hometax using an authorized digital certificate; (4) attach the certificate of incorporation, court registration certificate, shareholder register, passport or ID of the statutory representative, Korean place-of-business lease or ownership evidence, and bank account confirmation; (5) the NTS assigns a ten-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which also functions as the VAT registration number. If the taxpayer is instead a foreign non-resident enterprise with no Korean incorporation but making taxable supplies in Korea, the foreign enterprise must appoint a tax representative and file a VAT registration application through the tax representative at the representative's local district tax office before the first taxable supply; no minimum turnover threshold applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No KRW turnover threshold applies to a Yuhan Hoesa LLC; VAT registration is mandatory upon commencement of taxable business activity. A foreign non-resident enterprise making taxable supplies in Korea must also register through a tax representative without any minimum turnover exemption. The simplified taxpayer regime is reserved for small individual business operators and is not available to a Korean LLC.

All corporations in Korea are required to issue electronic tax invoices through the Hometax electronic tax invoice system or through NTS-certified EDI/ERP interfaces. The electronic tax invoice details must be transmitted to NTS by or before the day following issuance to support real-time matching against VAT returns and customs export data. There is no physical fiscal clearance device or digital stamp requirement in the Korean VAT system; anti-fraud control is achieved through mandatory electronic transmission, automated NTS data matching, and a statutory prohibition on issuing paper-only or fabricated tax invoices. A zero-rated export transaction must also be evidenced through the issuance of an electronic tax invoice referencing the export declaration or qualifying offshore service.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalised but tightly monitored current-account convertibility regime. The Korean won is freely convertible for bona fide trade transactions, but all cross-border movements are subject to reporting and validation through authorised foreign exchange banks and the Foreign Exchange Information System. Capital-account transactions, including the contribution of equity into the Yuhan Hoesa LLC, require an advance foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Once the investment capital is credited to a domestic foreign exchange bank, conversion from USD to KRW is permitted for the LLC's operational treasury. Profit and dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted after payment of corporate income tax and withholding tax, with evidence of tax filing and audited financial statements. South Korea does not impose a mandatory export surrender ratio, but heavy-haul logistics providers must collect export proceeds within the statutory period and either deposit them into a domestic foreign-currency account or exchange them through a designated bank. All hedging and currency swap arrangements must have a demonstrated underlying commercial exposure; speculative forward trading by non-financial corporates is restricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from out-of-gauge project cargo logistics must be received within 18 months from the date of export declaration, unless a prior extension is approved by the competent authorities. The full amount must be paid to a Korean designated foreign exchange bank or deposited into a domestic foreign currency account. There is no mandatory percentage conversion into KRW; however, every collection must be reported under the Foreign Exchange Information System and reconciled to the related export declaration and bill of lading.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in conjunction with the Ministry of Strategy and Finance (MOSF) and the Foreign Exchange Information System under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI-only platforms are not a compliant substitute for a Korean corporate bank account. An unlicensed fintech EMI is legally barred from holding Korean won settlement accounts, executing FEIS duties, or performing foreign exchange bank functions. At most, such providers may offer front-end payment orchestration through a licensed Korean banking partner, but the underlying treasury rails and KYC responsibility must remain with the Tier-1 bank. The project cargo business should therefore direct all liquidity clearing, FX conversion, and trade settlements through domestic licensed banks.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% over KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion; 21% over KRW 20 billion up to KRW 300 billion; 24% over KRW 300 billion; a 10% local income tax surcharge is separately added to the national corporate income tax liability.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate non-resident dividend withholding composed of 20% national corporate income tax and 2% local income tax surcharge.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and payment must be submitted within 3 months after the end of the fiscal year; for a December 31 year-end the filing deadline is March 31 of the following calendar year.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

Box 1 - Corporate entity classification and head office Identifies whether the taxpayer is a domestic corporation or foreign corporation, the head office address, business registration number and the fiscal year covered. Enter 'domestic corporation' for the Korean LLC; attach a schedule identifying every non-resident shareholder's country of residence, ownership ratio, tax reference number and DTA entitlement; failure to identify ultimate beneficial owners may trigger GAAR and treaty-benefit audits.

Segregate revenue from international carriage outside Korean waters and clearly source Korean domestic haulage and stevedoring activities; mis-sourcing cross-border project cargo revenue can understate Korean-source income and attract 40% fraud penalties.
Box 3 - Non-deductible expenses and related-party adjustments Reports book income adjustments for statutory disallowances, entertainment expense limits, excessive compensation and non-arm's-length international payments. Management fees, charter hire, intergroup insurance, equipment rental and interest paid to foreign related parties require arm's-length substantiation; excessive payments to a non-resident parent may be recharacterized as a deemed dividend subject to 22% withholding.
Box 4 - Transfer pricing adjustments Records the taxpayer's self-adjustment to the arm's length price on cross-border controlled transactions. Attach a transfer pricing discloser covering all international related-party transactions in OOG logistics; apply CUP or TNMM with a tested-party profit level indicator; NTS accepts a comparability range and may impose an adjustment plus penalties if the Korean LLC's margin falls outside the interquartile range.
Box 5 - Foreign tax credit reconciliation Claims credit for foreign income taxes suffered on overseas project revenues and branch income. A Korean LLC is taxed on worldwide income; claim the credit only for legally imposed foreign taxes, attaching country-by-country tax credit schedules and foreign tax assessment documents; uncredited residual foreign taxes are not deductible in Korea except where statute expressly allows.
Box 6 - Withholding tax on payments to non-residents Declares dividends, interest, royalties, commissions and other Korean-source income paid to foreign persons during the tax year. List each foreign payee and treaty base, certify the Tax Residency Certificate and beneficial-ownership statement on file, and confirm the exact DTA withholding rate applied; if treaty documentation is incomplete, the rate must be stepped back to 22%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are required when annual aggregate international related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion; Country-by-Country reporting notification applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substance is strictly tested through the Korean GAAR, DTA Limitation-on-Benefits clauses, OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test and controlled foreign company rules; the OOG logistics company must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea by keeping operational control, key personnel, heavy-haul contract execution, vessel/truck deployment decisions and asset management in Korea; a thin shell deriving logistics income for a foreign parent risks deemed-dividend treatment and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Article 47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes applies a 40% fraudulent under-reporting penalty; Article 3 and Article 10 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act authorize criminal prosecution with imprisonment for up to 10 years and fines for tax fraud exceeding KRW 300 million.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced if the LLC is operated as a sham; directors are personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, penalties and daily surcharges; fraudulent asset transfers are recoverable in civil proceedings; the NTS can impose departure-restriction/travel-ban orders, and conviction leads to imprisonment, disqualification from serving as directors and personal asset seizure.
Treaty Shopping and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Principal Purpose Test and LOB articles in Korean tax treaties permit full denial of treaty benefits; the Korean GAAR allows reassessment at the full 22% withholding rate plus a 40% fraud penalty; false treaty-benefit certifications are independently punishable under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Act on Regulation of Tax Evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign treaty-certification documents knowing the Korean entity lacks beneficial ownership are personally liable for the withheld taxes, expose themselves to criminal prosecution for false certification, personal asset restitution, travel restrictions and cross-border extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Misdeclared Intercompany Pricing

Statutory sanction: The Adjustment of International Taxes Act authorizes reassessment using the NTS's own benchmark, disallowance of the taxpayer's self-selected method, a 20% under-reporting penalty under Article 47-3 of the Framework Act and daily late-payment surcharges; persistent non-production of documents can lead to criminal prosecution for obstruction of tax audits.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false intercompany pricing or withhold required documentation can be assessed personally where the corporate structure is used to avoid collection; the NTS may seize personal assets, impose departure restrictions, and courts may impose imprisonment for willful intent to evade international tax obligations.

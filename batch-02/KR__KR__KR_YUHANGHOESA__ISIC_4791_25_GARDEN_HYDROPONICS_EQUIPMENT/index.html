Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. The paid-in capital must be the amount stated in the Articles of Incorporation and fully subscribed and deposited into a temporary capital subscription bank account before court registration. There is no legal requirement for a notarial escrow or blocked account; after incorporation registration, the deposited capital becomes available for the Company's operating account, subject only to bank-level know-your-customer and risk-based due diligence thresholds for non-resident founding members.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic portal (www.hometax.go.kr) for all tax registrations, filings, and payments.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must first appoint a domestic tax representative (with a registered place of business in Korea) if it has no permanent establishment. The representative must file an application for business registration (사업자등록신청서) with the competent district tax office (세무서) having jurisdiction over the representative's address, using the HomeTax portal or in-person submission. The application requires: (1) a certificate of incorporation or equivalent legal existence document from the home jurisdiction, apostilled or consular-legalized; (2) a copy of the foreign entity's statutory ID; (3) a power of attorney appointing the tax representative; (4) evidence of the Korean business address (e.g., lease agreement or registered office confirmation). Upon approval, the NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (사업자등록번호), which serves as both the TIN and VAT ID. For entities without a physical presence but engaging in e-commerce, a simplified registration for foreign e-commerce operators is available under the revised VAT Act, requiring the same documentation but allowing a virtual address provided by the tax representative.

None. South Korea does not offer check-the-box elections for foreign entities. Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for domestic entities; all business entities must register for VAT upon business commencement. For foreign non-resident e-commerce suppliers, a mandatory registration threshold of KRW 100 million (approx. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to the export of goods (including hydroponics equipment) when the goods are physically delivered outside Korea under a customs export declaration. For services, zero-rating applies to services supplied to non-residents for use outside Korea, including digital services, provided the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not effectively used in Korea. The seller must retain evidence of the export transaction, including the customs declaration, shipping documents, and proof of payment from the foreign buyer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (수출신고필증) issued by Korea Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing physical movement of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank receipt confirming receipt of foreign currency payment from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of foreign buyer's non-resident status (e.g., business registration certificate or passport copy)

All VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices (전자세금계산서) through the NTS HomeTax system for all B2B supplies. Real-time clearance is not mandatory for standard invoices, but the NTS operates a mandatory electronic invoice issuance system with a 5-day submission window after the supply date. For B2C e-commerce, no invoice is required, but the seller must maintain transaction records. There is no API-based fiscal clearance or digital stamping requirement; however, the NTS uses data analytics and cross-referencing with customs and payment data to detect non-compliance. For cross-border digital services, the simplified registration regime requires quarterly filing and payment via the HomeTax portal.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service conduct joint audits on export zero-rating claims. If the required documentation is incomplete or the foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days (unless extended by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act), the zero-rating is revoked and the VAT becomes payable with interest. Penalties include: (1) an additional 10% of the unpaid VAT as a negligence penalty; (2) a 20% penalty for false or fraudulent claims; (3) criminal prosecution for tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with imprisonment up to 5 years or fines up to KRW 50 million. Retrospective clawback can extend up to 5 years from the filing date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a substantially liberalised current account where trade-related foreign currency receipts and payments are generally convertible without prior central bank licensing, provided they are routed through a designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment and foreign currency borrowings, are largely open but subject to a declaration, notification, or reporting regime rather than blanket prohibitions. Korean won is legally convertible domestically, but non-resident won accounts and cross-border won settlement are tightly regulated to protect financial stability. Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory conversion to Korean won, but they must be collected through a designated foreign exchange bank and reported within prescribed statutory timelines; the exporter may hold the proceeds in a foreign currency account or convert them freely in the spot market. Profit remittances by a non-resident shareholder are permitted once corporate tax, withholding tax, and foreign investment registration obligations are satisfied; the remittance must be supported by audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, dividend resolution, and a designated foreign exchange bank submission. The main operational hurdle is evidentiary, not necessarily prohibitive: the bank must verify underlying documents for each remittance, and capital exports may trigger additional scrutiny if the declared purpose is inconsistent with the entity's registered business scope. In practice, a carefully documented cross-border treasury structure using a primary domestic Korean bank and standard SWIFT rails will provide full convertibility for dividends, royalties, and trade disbursements, but speculative or unexplained won flows will be blocked.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be collected within 18 months from the shipment date, unless an extension is granted by the designated foreign exchange bank for valid commercial reasons. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio to Korean won; exporters may retain the receipts in foreign currency, deposit them in a designated foreign exchange bank account, or convert to Korean won at prevailing spot rates. The surrender requirement is satisfied by presenting the export declaration, shipping documents, and payment evidence to the foreign exchange bank, which also records the transaction for quarterly regulatory reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the foreign exchange policy authority; enforcement is delegated to designated foreign exchange banks and the Korea Customs Service under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Foreign Investment Promotion Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, and Airwallex generally do not hold a domestic banking license under the Korean Banking Act and cannot provide resident Korean corporate entities with compliant KRW settlement accounts or local clearing membership. They may be used merely as customer-facing payment collection gateways for foreign retail customers, but they are legally barred from being the primary treasury or clearing rail for inbound FDI capital, export proceeds collection, or operational disbursements. A Korean entity must maintain its primary transactional accounts with a designated foreign exchange bank that is a licensed domestic commercial bank. EMIs may be considered as ancillary utility providers for certain non-KRW payouts only if they operate through licensed third-party banks and do not expose the Korean entity to unlicensed foreign currency exchange business.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI capital must be remitted by the foreign investor through an international bank using SWIFT MT103 or an equivalent wire to the designated foreign exchange bank holding the Korean entity's capital escrow account. The remittance must reference the Foreign Investment Report Certificate number and clearly identify the purpose as paid-in capital contribution under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The foreign exchange bank will perform FX purpose-code classification and verify the remittance against the capital verification certificate; once cleared, the funds are converted to KRW or retained in the foreign currency capital account as declared. The entity must then complete capital verification through a certified domestic accountant and register the received amount with the Korea Foreign Investment KOSIS/KDX reporting platform. All subsequent capital withdrawals or refunds are restricted and require documentary proof of the investment purpose.Settlements for hydroponics equipment purchases and trade-related operating expenses should be processed through the designated foreign exchange bank using SWIFT MT103 for direct foreign supplier payments, Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit where the supplier requires bank undertaking, and Documentary Collections where invoice and shipping document control is preferred under D/P or D/A terms. Each outbound payment must be supported by a purchase order, pro-forma invoice, commercial invoice, bill of lading or air waybill, insurance certificate where applicable, and the relevant FX declaration or trade report filed with the foreign exchange bank. They may be used merely as customer-facing payment collection gateways for foreign retail customers, but they are legally barred from being the primary treasury or clearing rail for inbound FDI capital, export proceeds collection, or operational disbursements. A Korean entity must maintain its primary transactional accounts with a designated foreign exchange bank that is a licensed domestic commercial bank. EMIs may be considered as ancillary utility providers for certain non-KRW payouts only if they operate through licensed third-party banks and do not expose the Korean entity to unlicensed foreign currency exchange business.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File mandatory when intercompany transaction amounts with foreign related parties exceed KRW 10 billion in a fiscal year; Simplified Local File required where individual intercompany transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; companies with gross revenue below KRW 10 billion and cross-border related-party transactions below KRW 5 billion may be exempt. Contemporaneous documentation must be submitted within 6 months after the fiscal year-end when the threshold is met.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not maintain a formal ESR/CIGA statute, but the substance doctrine is enforced through domestic tax law and OECD BEPS principles. A Korean entity must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (purchasing, logistics, payment processing, and e-commerce platform operations) are performed in Korea. A shell Yuhan Hoesa whose board decisions and key functions are executed abroad may be disregarded or recast as a foreign permanent establishment, triggering branch profit remittance taxes of 22% on repatriated profits. The OECD principal-purpose test under applicable DTAs prevents treaty abuse where substance is lacking.

Tax Evasion / False Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Article 12 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment up to 10 years and fine up to 3 times the tax evaded. Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced: directors, shadow directors and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties and surcharges under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 4.

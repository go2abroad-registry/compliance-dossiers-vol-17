Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa. Each member must, however, fully pay the agreed contribution before or concurrently with the incorporation registration, and no part of the paid contribution may be withdrawn, lent, or set off before registration is completed. In-kind contributions require a court-appointed appraiser unless all members are solvent and the Commercial Act exemption applies. For non-resident members, capital must be transferred into a Korean foreign-exchange bank account and reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; the company obtains a Foreign Investment Report Certificate. A blocked escrow account is not statutorily mandated for this service-only entity.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea; online portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Korean LLC (Yuhan Hoesa), the entity must register with the National Tax Service (NTS) within 10 days of commencing business. The procedure involves: (1) preparing the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, and proof of business address; (2) submitting the Business Registration Application (사업자등록신청서) to the local district tax office; (3) providing the corporate seal registration certificate and identification of the representative; (4) receiving the Business Registration Number (사업자등록번호) which serves as both the TIN and VAT ID. For foreign non-resident entities, a tax representative must be appointed in Korea to handle filings and communications with the NTS. The application can be filed online via HomeTax or in person at the district tax office.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Services supplied to a non-resident and used outside Korea qualify for 0% VAT. For engineering and design services (including BIM and structural drafting), the place of supply is deemed to be where the recipient is located if the recipient is a business (B2B). The service must be performed for a foreign client and the benefit must be received outside Korea.

  • Export contract
  • Commercial invoice
  • Bank remittance advice
  • Foreign exchange repatriation certificate (FIRC)

Mandatory electronic tax invoice issuance and submission via the HomeTax system for all B2B transactions. Electronic tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS within 10 days after the end of the month in which the supply occurs. The system employs digital signatures and validation to prevent fraud. For B2C transactions, a cash receipt system is mandatory for retail businesses.

Penalties for fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating include additional tax of up to 40% of the evaded VAT, interest on unpaid tax, and potential criminal prosecution for tax evasion. The NTS may retrospectively claw back the zero-rated VAT and impose penalties if documentation is incomplete or false.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a free-floating exchange rate regime for the KRW, with current-account transactions largely open under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital-account flows require advance registration or ex-post reporting through a domestic foreign exchange bank. For a Yuhan Hoesa LLC receiving equity investment and paying overseas engineering costs, neither a minimum capital requirement nor a blanket FX license is imposed, but every inbound/outbound transfer must be associated with a valid foreign exchange purpose code. KRW remains a partially restricted offshore currency; non-resident bank accounts and settlement between non-residents in KRW are limited, which pushes most treasury operations to be structured in USD or KRW through licensed domestic institutions. Profit remittance is legal after corporate taxes are filed; dividends are paid in KRW and converted to foreign currency through the entity's settlement bank, subject to submission of audited financial statements, dividend minutes, and tax payment certificates. Export proceeds from BIM and CAD services are treated as service export earnings, must be collected within six months, and can be held in a foreign currency account without forced surrender, though a bank receipt and service contract are mandatory for VAT and corporate tax purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, service export proceeds (including CAD/BIM deliverables) must be received in Korea within six months from the invoice due date or performance completion; there is no mandatory conversion ratio, but all receipts must be credited to a foreign exchange account and reported to the foreign exchange bank. Any delay exceeding six months requires BOK approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the overarching foreign exchange policy authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury rail. Wise is a licensed e-money institution abroad but not a licensed deposit-taking bank in South Korea; it cannot open a corporate KRW operating account or provide a legal domestic settlement account for tax registration or FDI capital verification. It may be used only as a secondary payout channel for non-regulated low-value invoices where the recipient's bank accepts foreign fintech transfers; all statutory capital flows must move through domestic Tier-1 banks.

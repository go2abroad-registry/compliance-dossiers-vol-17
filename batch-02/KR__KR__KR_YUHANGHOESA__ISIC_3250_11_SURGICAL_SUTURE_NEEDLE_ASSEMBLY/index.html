Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Commercial Act for a Yuhan Hoesa; a nominal capital amount such as KRW 1 may be adopted. The full subscribed capital must, however, be deposited into a temporary bank account bearing the intended corporate name before the registration application is filed. There is no formal escrow or blocked-account statutory requirement, but banking practice requires the deposited amount to remain immobilized until the District Court issues the certified registration record of incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the National Fire Agency, regular annual fire inspections, evacuation and emergency lighting installation, and fire suppression system inspection; local government structural load and floor-load approval for the cleanroom and assembly area; KOSHA workplace safety and health clearance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including machine guarding, ergonomic workstations for micro-assembly, biological safety cabinet certification, chemical storage cabinets, hazardous-substance disclosure, and emergency medical response planning.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of South Korea (NTS); electronic tax portal: Home Tax system accessible via hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that makes or intends to make taxable supplies in South Korea must complete Business Registration under Article 8 of the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes. The procedure requires the entity to (1) establish whether it has a domestic place of business; (2) if no domestic fixed place of business exists, appoint a statutory tax representative domiciled in Korea; (3) file the Business Registration application with the competent district tax office via the Home Tax portal or in person; (4) attach the certificate of foreign corporate existence (equivalent of a certificate of incorporation), articles of association, board resolution authorizing the local representation, power of attorney for the tax representative, passport or corporate ID, and evidence of the local sales office address; (5) receive the 10-digit business registration number, which serves as the VAT ID and national tax ID; and (6) for customs purposes, additionally register with the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS portal to obtain a customs user code for export declarations.

The procedure requires the entity to (1) establish whether it has a domestic place of business; (2) if no domestic fixed place of business exists, appoint a statutory tax representative domiciled in Korea; (3) file the Business Registration application with the competent district tax office via the Home Tax portal or in person; (4) attach the certificate of foreign corporate existence (equivalent of a certificate of incorporation), articles of association, board resolution authorizing the local representation, power of attorney for the tax representative, passport or corporate ID, and evidence of the local sales office address; (5) receive the 10-digit business registration number, which serves as the VAT ID and national tax ID; and (6) for customs purposes, additionally register with the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS portal to obtain a customs user code for export declarations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None (zero KRW threshold); all persons - including foreign non-resident entities - that make taxable supplies of goods or services in South Korea are required to register for VAT through Business Registration before commencing business. No minimum turnover waiver exists for foreign-entity export traders; even an entity with solely zero-rated export turnover must register if it has a domestic place of business or otherwise meets Korean VAT nexus criteria.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act applies to the export of goods, including surgical suture needles and related surgical consumables, from South Korea to a foreign country. Eligibility conditions: (1) proven physical export of the goods through Korean customs procedures; (2) export proceeds received in foreign currency from the overseas purchaser via recognized banking channels; and (3) receipt of the export proceeds within the statutory repatriation period (180 days for this dossier, subject to NTS extension guidelines). The zero rate also applies to certain offshore intangible and B2B service exports meeting the strict criteria of the VAT Act; for goods, customs export declaration is the core evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) submitted through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic clearance system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing departure of the surgical suture needle cargo from a Korean port or airport
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the goods, quantity, export price, HS code 9018 or 3006 as applicable, and consignee information
  • Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds

South Korea mandates electronic tax invoice issuance and real-time digital transmission for all VAT-registered businesses. Every VAT-inclusive supply must be documented in an e-tax invoice (electronic tax invoice) issued through the NTS Home Tax system; the invoice must be transmitted to the NTS within one day after the supply date. Businesses with high turnover or high-risk industries are further required to use API-based machine-to-machine reporting, and NTS issues a digitally signed certificate for each cleared invoice. Anti-fraud digital stamping is built into the Home Tax e-invoice platform; no separate physical fiscal stamp is permitted. Non-compliant issuance is subject to denial of input VAT credit and penalty surcharges under the VAT Act.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly audit export zero-rating claims. If the required customs or foreign-exchange documentation is not produced, the claimed zero rating is reversed, the VAT at 10% is assessed as unpaid tax via a notice of correction, and a penalty surcharge of up to 30% of the unpaid VAT is imposed under the Basic Act on National Taxes Collection and the VAT Act. Additionally, if false documents or forged export declarations are detected, the Punishment Tax Evasion Act imposes criminal liability: imprisonment up to five years or a fine up to 50 million Korean won. For corrupt e-tax invoices, the statute of limitations for reassessment is ten years; the NTS may claw back the input VAT credit taken by the exporter and imposes retrospective adjustment interest per month of delinquency.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is fully convertible for current account purposes under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; trade-related payments, dividends, and royalties may be made through bank transactions without prior approval, subject to submission of supporting trade documentation. Capital account transactions such as inbound direct investment, foreign borrowings, and portfolio investment are liberalized but require filing with a designated foreign exchange bank or the Bank of Korea through the Foreign Exchange Information Network. There is no general obligation to surrender export proceeds to the central bank; receipts may be credited to foreign currency deposit accounts or converted to won at prevailing market rates. Profit remittance by a foreign-invested entity is allowed once the capital contribution has been fully verified and foreign-investment reporting is completed; the commercial bank will require an audited financial statement, a board resolution on dividends, and a certificate of foreign investment. Temporary withholding constraints or enhanced reporting can apply when excess volatility triggers emergency measures, but no blanket exchange controls are in force. The practical hurdles are anti-money laundering documentation, tax clearance certificates, and the requirement that all settlements pass through licensed foreign exchange banks rather than surrender quotas. Non-resident financed capital injections must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and once the reporting certificate is issued, the company can freely purchase foreign currency for cross-border payments. There is no fixed percentage conversion of export collections, but banks are obliged to file suspicious transaction reports for round-number or same-day pattern remittances, making complete underlying trade evidence essential. Overall, the regime is investment-friendly with prudential supervision, requiring careful KYC paperwork instead of punitive capital controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected within six months from the export clearance date, and any extension must be approved by the transaction bank. All incoming export settlements must be receipted into the exporter's designated foreign exchange bank account; no minimum percentage of collection currency must be converted to KRW, but the bank is required to convert when foreign exchange balance limits are exceeded and all foreign currency deposits remain subject to the foreign exchange position reporting framework.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The central bank and monetary authority of the Republic of Korea; foreign exchange administration is also exercised through the Ministry of Economy and Finance and designated foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. A foreign EMI without a Korean banking license cannot open won-based accounts or provide payment services for a local entity's money transmission. The Specialized Credit Financial Business Act and Electronic Financial Transactions Act require either a local financial holding license or a partnership with a licensed bank; in practice banks view EMIs as third-party agents rather than counterparties, and the foreign exchange information network does not accept foreign EMI licenses as sponsoring institutions. The only viable route is for the Korean company to open at a licensed Tier-1 bank and use an EMI as a subordinate FX execution channel, which is generally barred by the banks' own onboarding agreements and by the strict Korean correspondent banking compliance requirements. Therefore, fintech EMIs are not a substitute for Tier-1 commercial bank onboarding for this manufacturing entity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Progressive Korean national CIT under Article 56 of the Corporate Income Tax Act: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 19% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 21% on KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion, and 24% on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; after adding the 10% local income tax surcharge, the top aggregate rate is 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For a non-resident corporate shareholder, Korean domestic law imposes 20% national CIT withholding on gross dividends plus a local income tax surcharge of 10% on the national tax, producing an aggregate statutory rate of 22% before treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File are mandatory when the aggregate amount of international related-party transactions exceeds KRW 10 billion in a fiscal year; country-by-country reporting is required for Korean constituent entities of multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of KRW 100 billion or more. Simplified TP disclosure schedules must be attached to the annual corporate tax return even where the KRW 10 billion threshold is not met.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not administer a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but Korean tax law gives effect to the economic substance doctrine through Article 14 of the Corporate Income Tax Act and the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) in the Framework Act on National Taxes. A legal entity with no real office, employees, decision-making functions, business risks, or substantive contracts may be disregarded as a conduit or shell; treaty benefits can be denied under the principal purpose test, and income will be re-attributed to the person or entity that economically performs the relevant functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Deliberate Tax Evasion and False Corporate Return

Statutory sanction: A 40% unlawful understatement surcharge on the unpaid tax, daily late-payment interest of approximately 0.025% per day, and criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act carrying imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine of up to KRW 150 million for tax evasion of KRW 300 million or more; repeat and aggravated evasion may result in higher imprisonment terms and fines.

Director personal liability: The representative director and de facto controlling officer are personally exposed to criminal imprisonment, fines, departure bans, and secondary tax liability under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act when corporate assets have been siphoned off or dissipated to prevent tax collection.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may disregard the shell entity under the substance-over-form doctrine and the GAAR, reallocate the income to the real economic owner, impose a 40% fraud penalty, recapture unpaid withholding tax at the 22% domestic gross rate, and impose late-payment interest and related surcharges on the Korean payer.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false substance declarations or authorize circular transactions may face criminal liability for tax evasion facilitation, civil restitution for corporate losses under the Commercial Act, and personal secondary liability for unpaid corporate taxes if the entity is left without assets.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may make an upward transfer pricing adjustment, recharacterize the transaction under the substance-over-form rule, impose understatement penalties of up to 40% of the resulting tax deficiency, apply daily late-payment interest, and use estimated income methods where documentation is absent or unreliable.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve undocumented or non-arm's-length related-party arrangements can be named as secondary taxpayers under the National Tax Collection Act if the company's assets are insufficient, and may be civilly liable to the company and third parties under Article 401 of the Commercial Act.
AML and Unreported Cross-Border Financial Flows

Statutory sanction: Violations attract administrative fines, forfeiture of unlawful proceeds, reporting penalties, and criminal liability under the Act on Reporting and Using of Certain Financial Transaction Information and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, including imprisonment of up to 5 years for serious laundering-related offenses; the National Tax Service may also impose tax on undeclared offshore income and assess concealment penalties.

Director personal liability: Individual directors can be prosecuted personally for failing to establish anti-money laundering controls, face travel bans and asset seizure, be ordered to pay civil restitution, and have the corporate veil pierced when the company is used as an instrument for fraud or laundering.

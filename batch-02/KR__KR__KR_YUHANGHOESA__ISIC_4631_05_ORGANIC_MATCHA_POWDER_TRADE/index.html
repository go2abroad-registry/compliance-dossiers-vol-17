Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. The incorporator must pay all subscribed contribution units in full into a domestic bank account opened in the name of the incorporator before court registration is filed. The funds remain blocked as a deposit and cannot be withdrawn until the incorporation registration certificate is issued and a corporate operating account is opened. There is no separate government escrow regime; the designated bank acts as depositary and performs source-of-funds verification under Korean anti-money laundering regulations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; official electronic portal: Home Tax Service (HTS) at hometax.go.kr; export/customs electronic single window: Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

For the domestic Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company), after incorporation under the Commercial Act, the statutory representative files a business registration application with the competent district tax office of the principal place of business, or submits the same online through the Home Tax Service portal using an authorized digital certificate, within 20 calendar days of business commencement. The application must attach the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, lease agreement or ownership certificate evidencing the Korean business address, passport and proof of authority of the statutory representative, and, if the representative maintains no domestic address, a tax representative appointment notice under the Framework Act on National Taxes. For a foreign non-resident company without a Korean permanent establishment that nevertheless incurs VAT obligations, the procedure is identical except that the non-resident must appoint an approved domestic tax representative, submit the foreign certificate of incorporation, board resolution, power of attorney, certificate of foreign tax residence, and the representative's appointment notice, and the NTS will issue a foreign-entity VAT registration number. The resulting Business Registration Certificate contains the statutory 10-digit Taxpayer Identification Number, which serves concurrently as the corporate income tax TIN and the VAT registration ID.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based registration threshold exists; any entrepreneur supplying taxable goods or services in Korea must file for VAT registration within 20 calendar days of business commencement regardless of expected revenue. The simplified taxpayer regime for small vendors is a calculation and compliance concession, not a registration exemption, and a corporate wholesaler is in all cases required to maintain full general VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods qualify for 0% VAT when a VAT-registered exporter (i) holds a written export sales contract/purchase order, (ii) files and secures an Export Declaration through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS, (iii) ships the goods to a foreign consignee, evidenced by a Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, and (iv) receives the sale proceeds from abroad through a licensed Korean foreign-exchange bank within the statutory repatriation period. The supply must be identified as an export on the e-tax invoice, with the zero rate applied. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated but the subject trade is physical matcha powder, so the physical export clearance documentary chain is controlling.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Clearance Confirmation Notification issued electronically by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS; equivalent to the Bill of Entry)
  • Original or certified electronic Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for airfreight) showing shipment from a Korean port to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by the Korean settlement bank, evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial Invoice and complete Packing List, with quantities and unit values agreeing with the export declaration
  • Export Sales Contract / Purchase Order and the e-tax invoice stating '0% zero-rated export'

The NTS mandates issuance of an electronic tax invoice (E-Tax Invoice) for all taxable supplies by corporations. E-tax invoice data is transmitted through the Home Tax Service electronic tax invoice channel to the NTS at the time of issue, where it is validated and cross-matched against VAT returns, payment history, cash receipts, and customs export data. There is no separate private-sector pre-clearance stamping requirement; anti-fraud is enforced centrally by NTS in near-real time. Licensed e-invoicing service providers or direct Hometax web submission may be used as the transmission channel, subject to NTS-approved data schema requirements. Paper tax invoices are not permitted as the primary issuance mode for corporate suppliers, and late or non-transmitted e-tax invoice records attract additional tax penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act. For exports, the e-tax invoice must specify the zero rate and cite the Korea Customs Service export declaration reference.

NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-verify export declarations, e-tax invoices, steamship manifests, bill of lading details, and bank foreign-exchange settlement records. If an export zero-rating claim is unsubstantiated, physically unverified, or fraudulent, the supply is retrospectively reclassified as taxable domestic supply at 10% VAT, input VAT previously credited is clawed back with statutory additional tax, the exporter loses refund entitlement, and the NTS imposes understated tax penalties plus daily overdue interest. Deliberate fabrication or alteration of export documents is additionally punishable under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Customs Act, carrying criminal fines, imprisonment, and forfeiture of the goods or proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a liberalized current-account convertibility regime but applies a comprehensive notification- and approval-based framework for capital-account movements. KRW is not fully convertible offshore; onshore current-account receipts and payments, including trade in goods, can generally be settled without prior BOK license if the transaction is reported through a local foreign exchange bank. Profits and dividends remittance to non-resident shareholders are permitted after tax clearance and submission of audited financial statements, provided the original foreign investment was reported. Capital remittances are subject to documentation and may require a certificate of foreign investment. The surrender requirement for export proceeds is effectively zero as Korea abandoned mandatory conversion; however, exporters must collect proceeds within 180 days and report through FEIS. The main operating hurdle lies in anti-money-laundering documentation for cross-border payments, particularly when the Korean Yuhan Hoesa is owned by a non-resident and funds originate from non-KRW currencies. In summary, Korea is a convertible current-account governed capital-account jurisdiction with intensive Bank of Korea surveillance and no material convertibility restriction for bona fide trade transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds for goods must be received within 180 calendar days from the date of export declaration or shipment. Extensions may be granted by the managing foreign exchange bank for realistic delays such as inspection or extended payment terms, but must be registered in FEIS. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% - South Korea removed the legal surrender requirement; exporters may retain foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Account or convert to KRW at market rates. If the proceeds are not collected within the statutory period, the exporting entity must file an uncollected export proceeds extension or otherwise report the delay to the responsible bank, which triggers heightened trade compliance scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) is administered by the Bank of Korea.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore EMI account for a Korean Yuhan Hoesa is legally unviable. The Bank of Korea and Financial Supervisory Service require any entity providing payment or remittance services in Korea to hold an Electronic Financial Business Operator license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Non-Korean digital EMIs are not recognised as equivalent licensed banks; local Korean banks reject the use of offshore EMI virtual accounts for trade settlement, and FEIS reporting cannot be performed through an EMI. Feasibility rating: Low - use only as an ancillary vendor pay-out mechanism, never as a primary clearing rail for matcha procurement or capital transactions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national marginal rate on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion, plus 10% local income tax surtax (aggregate top effective incremental rate 26.4%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax plus 10% local income tax surtax, producing an aggregate statutory dividend withholding rate of 22%; applicable to dividends paid by the Korean entity to non-resident shareholders. Tax treaties may reduce the rate to 5%–15% depending on the residence jurisdiction and shareholding level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation is triggered when annual revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion. Annual disclosure of international related-party transactions is required on the corporate tax return when the aggregate transaction amount with overseas related parties is KRW 5 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not impose a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Return, but substance is legally required under the substance-over-form principle, Controlled Foreign Company rules, the International Tax Adjustment Act, and the OECD Principal Purpose Test. The Korean matcha distributor must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea, including procurement negotiation, inventory risk, quality control, local sales contracts, invoicing, logistics, and distribution decision-making. A passive Korean entity with no employees, no office, and no business risk used to invoice related-party matcha transactions will be pierced, treated as a PE of the foreign principal, or denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Intentional Corporate Tax Evasion / False Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Articles 3(1) and 3(2): imprisonment with labor for up to 2 years or a fine not exceeding twice the evaded tax; where the evaded tax exceeds KRW 500 million, imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to three times the evaded tax. The NTS also imposes unfaithful-return additional tax up to 40% under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-2 and disallows the false deductions.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil where the Yuhan Hoesa is a mere alter ego or abused as a sham. Directors are jointly and severally liable to the tax authority and injured parties under Commercial Act Article 401 for intentional or gross-negligent acts; the NTS may impose secondary liability on the director’s personal assets under Framework Act Article 23, issue an overseas departure restriction/blacklist, and seek criminal imprisonment personally against the director, which is not dischargeable by corporate liquidation.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Default / Shell Margin Strip

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 4 and 5 authorize the NTS to re-compute Korean taxable income at the arm’s length price; Article 13 and Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-2 apply understatement penalties up to 20%–40% of the adjusted tax; failure to submit TP documentation may be fined up to KRW 30 million.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve a fictive transfer price or false TP policy may be treated as joint tortfeasors; Commercial Act Article 401 imposes direct personal liability for damages caused to the Korean company and the National Tax Service. Aggravated TP fraud accompanied by false invoices is prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and directors face asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment for intentional evasion.
Withholding / DTA Relief Abuse and Shell Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: The NTS will deny treaty benefits under the OECD Principal Purpose Test reflected in Korean case law and the International Tax Adjustment Act, assess the full 22% statutory withholding, and impose withholding penalty additional tax under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-4 (up to 50% of the unwithheld amount) with statutory interest. A false TRC or fraudulent withholding omission triggers criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Articles 3(1) and 3(2).

Director personal liability: The director acting as withholding agent is personally liable for the uncollected withholding tax and surcharges if he knowingly caused the omission; a Korean court may pierce the corporate veil and hold the director jointly and severally liable. Measures include NTS blacklisting with an overseas departure ban, seizure of personal bank accounts and real estate, joint and several civil restitution to the tax authority, and criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for aggravated tax evasion through sham shareholders and treaty abuse.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of the digital X-ray flat panel detectors qualifies for 0% VAT under the Korean VAT Act when: (a) the goods are sold by the Korean Yuhan Hoesa to a foreign buyer; (b) the goods are declared to Korea Customs Service as an export and physically depart Korea on or before the invoice date; (c) the exporter issues a zero-rated electronic tax invoice; and (d) the export proceeds are received in foreign currency by a Korean foreign exchange bank within the statutory settlement period. Offshore digital services would require separate proof that the service is B2B and consumed outside Korea, but the controlled project scope is physical goods OEM export, so goods-export evidence is the controlling substantiation.

Mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) transmission through NTS HomeTax. A general VAT taxpayer must issue an electronic tax invoice for every taxable supply and transmit it to NTS no later than the day after the invoice date. South Korea does not use per-invoice digital stamping; instead, NTS applies centralized anti-fraud matching of e-tax invoices with KCS export declarations and foreign exchange bank reports. Direct HomeTax filing or NTS-approved outsourced electronic tax invoice service providers may be used for machine-to-machine submission. Export invoices must be marked as zero-rated supplies and linked to the KCS UNI-PASS export declaration.

NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly verify actual export through UNI-PASS records, match the zero-rated e-tax invoice with the export declaration, and confirm that export proceeds were repatriated to Korea in foreign currency. If the zero-rating is disallowed due to absence of valid customs clearance, unrepatriated foreign exchange beyond the 180-day deadline without an approved extension, false e-tax invoice coding, or fabricated export documentation, NTS will retroactively assess 10% VAT on the export value, add statutory late-payment interest and additional tax for false or non-issued tax invoices, and claw back input VAT previously refunded. Fraudulent schemes are also referred under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and may result in criminal fines, imprisonment, and personal exposure for the responsible officers of the Yuhan Hoesa.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates under a managed floating exchange rate system but statutory foreign exchange controls under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account transactions, such as trade in goods and services, are generally liberalized and can be settled through authorized foreign exchange banks; payment for exports must be received within three months of exportation, unless extended by bank approval. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, portfolio flows, and loans, are subject to a notification or approval regime with specific reporting through foreign exchange banks or the Ministry of Economy and Finance. There is no mandatory surrender of export foreign currency proceeds into Korean won, provided the exporter maintains a foreign currency account at a domestic foreign exchange bank; however, all receipts must be repatriated to South Korea within the statutory timeline. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is permitted after corporate tax settlement, but requires proof of tax payment, audited financial statements, and board resolution confirming distributable reserves. Foreign exchange banks verify the underlying trade documents and assign a foreign exchange classification code. For Korean LLCs with non-resident shareholders, dividend remittance is generally possible via SWIFT MT103 once the bank compliance team clears the beneficial ownership profile. The capital account remains selectively controlled, so all inward direct investments must be notified to the designated foreign exchange bank and reported to the Ministry of Economy and Finance through the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before any capital inflow can be converted. Convertibility of the Korean won is limited for speculative purposes but fully supported for genuine trade, dividend, and repatriation flows, provided documentary evidence and tax compliance are demonstrated. The practical hurdle is not the absence of convertibility but the heavy documentary burden imposed by the foreign exchange bank in aligning the transaction with the statutory foreign exchange classification code and beneficial ownership verification standards.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export receipts must be received and repatriated within three months from the date of exportation (customs clearance); the Korean exporter may retain 100% in a designated foreign currency account and no statutory conversion ratio to Korean won is imposed, but the bank must be notified and the receipt must be deposited to the foreign currency account with matching export documentation such as the invoice, packing list, and customs declaration.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of the Republic of Korea) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the Foreign Exchange Administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low to nil for offshore fintech EMIs. South Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires cross-border payment and deposit-taking services to be executed by domestic foreign exchange banks. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and global virtual account providers are not licensed as foreign exchange banks in Korea and cannot legally provide payable accounts or settlement rails for this Korean LLC. A limited passporting possibility exists under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act only for registered domestic e-money institutions, but those companies still cannot provide foreign exchange clearing rails without a sponsorship arrangement with a Tier-1 chartered commercial bank. Attempting to route inbound capital or export proceeds through an EMI would create a reportable unapproved foreign exchange transaction and trigger immediate regulatory scrutiny. The KYC underwriting team should therefore disregard EMI proposals and require the entity to open all corporate accounts directly with a domestic foreign exchange bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For non-resident equity investment in a Korean LLC, the investor must report the foreign direct investment to the designated Foreign Exchange Bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The capital must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to a foreign currency account at a domestic foreign exchange bank using the Foreign Exchange Classification Code for direct investment; the bank will issue a certificate of capital verification. The funds may be converted to Korean won at the closing spot rate and deposited into the corporate won account. All inbound remittances must be tagged with the FD purpose code, and any loan or equity injection exceeding KRW regulatory thresholds must be reported to the Ministry of Economy and Finance within the specified statutory timeline. The source of funds must be evidenced through audited financial statements or documented retained earnings to pass the bank's enhanced due diligence.Trade disbursements for radiology components should be settled via SWIFT MT103 for open account transactions, documentary collections (DP/DA) through the foreign exchange bank for confirmed purchase orders, and commercial letters of credit for non-established counterparties. All settlements require submission of the commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, packing list, and customs declarations to the foreign exchange bank. For supply of vapor deposition equipment and imported cesium iodide crystals from Japan or China, a back-to-back letter of credit structure may be used to mitigate counterparty risk. The bank will perform a transaction-level foreign exchange review and assign the appropriate trade classification code before outward remittance is released. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Progressive national Corporate Income Tax: 9% up to KRW 200 million; 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion, plus 10% local corporate income tax surcharge on the national tax (combined top statutory effective rate of 26.4%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory withholding on cash dividends paid to a non-resident is 20% national income tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge (22% aggregate), absent a Korea DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation is triggered when aggregate foreign-related transaction value with Korean overseas related parties exceeds KRW 50 billion; Master File is required when such transactions exceed KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country Report applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue above KRW 1 trillion. Even below thresholds, a contemporaneous Local File is prudent whenever the digital X-ray flat-panel manufacturing company has intercompany export sales, royalty payments, or management fees.

Economic substance scope: Korea follows the substance-over-form principle and applies Controlled Foreign Company rules under the International Tax Adjustment Act. A Korean entity cannot be treated as a mere invoice operator or limited-risk shell unless its assets, functions, and risks are contractually and factually allocated in accordance with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. The Core Income-Generating Activities for a medical-device OEM include design control, regulatory licensing, manufacturing, quality assurance, and logistics decision-making; these must be carried out in Korea with real personnel and assets. Excessive profit diversion to low-tax beach-head entities will be recharacterized and penalized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Non-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, aggravated tax evasion exceeding KRW 300 million is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to five times the evaded tax. Under the Framework Act on National Taxes, fraudulent under-reporting triggers a penalty tax of 40% of the underpaid amount plus monthly arrears charges.

Director personal liability: The representative director who signed the fraudulent return may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act; personal bank accounts and assets can be seized; where unpaid tax exceeds KRW 50 million, the tax authority may request a departure restriction (travel ban) preventing the director from leaving Korea; criminal conviction can bar the director from serving as an officer.
Transfer Pricing Abuse and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service will apply the arm's-length method under Articles 4 and 5 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, adjust taxable income, disallow excess deductions, and impose penalty tax for undisclosed or incorrectly reported foreign-related transactions, generally 30% of the amount adjusted where intentional non-disclosure is found. A criminal referral is possible if the arrangement is characterized as fraudulent avoidance of tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the transfer pricing structure can be personally assessed as promoters under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act; they are jointly and severally liable for the company's resulting tax if they acted as de facto principals; under Korean Commercial Act Article 401 they may owe civil damages for injury to creditors; the tax authority may freeze assets and impose an overseas travel suspension on the director.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Disguised Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The Korean paying company, as withholding agent, is liable for the un-withheld tax and penalty tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes: 20% of the un-withheld tax for willful failure, 10% for negligent failure, plus monthly late payment surcharges. If treaty relief is denied, the entire national/local withholding tax becomes immediately collectable, with interest, from the Korean entity.

Director personal liability: The representative director is personally liable for withheld taxes collected but not remitted to the National Tax Service under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act; the amount can be recovered directly from the director's personal property. Willful suppression of cross-border payments can be prosecuted as embezzlement of national tax under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with imprisonment up to 5 years; departure blacklisting and asset attachment are standard collection remedies against the director.

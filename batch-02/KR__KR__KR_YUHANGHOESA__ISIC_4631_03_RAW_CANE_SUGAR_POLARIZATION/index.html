Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. The total capital must be stated in the Articles of Incorporation, fully subscribed by the participation unit holders, and deposited in a domestic Korean bank account opened in the company's name before registration. No blocked escrow account is required by law. If any non-resident investor subscribes KRW 100,000,000 or more, a prior or simultaneous Foreign Investment Report must be filed with the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) or the processing foreign exchange bank, and the inward remittance must be routed through a foreign investment special account.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a predominantly liberalized but prudently managed foreign-exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is freely convertible for most current-account transactions, including the importation of raw cane sugar, payment for freight and insurance, and incidental trading costs, and such transactions do not require a license from the Bank of Korea. Residents and non-residents may settle trade through licensed foreign exchange banks at market-determined rates, and the bank has no obligation to obtain a prior foreign-exchange purchase permit for ordinary trade. Capital-account transactions, including the subscription of shares or capital contributions in a Yuhan Hoesa by a non-resident, are subject to prior foreign-investment reporting and capital-account monitoring; the initial injection must be reported to KOTRA or the designated foreign exchange bank before remittance, and the bank issues a foreign-investment registration that evidences the capital has been properly introduced. Dividend and profit remittances are permitted as an investment income transfer after the company has filed its corporate income tax and the dividend-paying accounts are verified, but the remittance must be supported by audited financial statements, a board resolution, a dividend withholding tax payment certificate, and the correct foreign-exchange report code. There is no surrender requirement for trade proceeds into KRW, but exporters and importers are bound by legal settlement periods and must maintain complete documentary trails. Therefore, KRW is convertible in substance but not in an unregulated sense: a real underlying transaction and correct reporting codes are required for each capital movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Korean foreign-exchange practice, exporters must receive their export proceeds within six months from the export date and report that receipt to a designated foreign exchange bank. There is no statutory mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds; a Korean exporter may lawfully hold the foreign currency in a foreign-currency deposit account at a licensed bank or convert it to Korean won at the bank's quoted market rate. The practical obligation is repatriation into a domestic foreign-currency account, transaction reporting under the Foreign Exchange Statistics Reporting System, and reconciliation of the trade contract, customs export declaration, invoice, and the actual payment tenor. Any extension of the six-month settlement window must be requested by the exporter through the foreign exchange bank and accepted for a valid commercial reason.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK); also administratively cooperating with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Financial Supervisory Service for the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability Rating: Low. Offshore fintech electronic-money institutions that do not hold a Korean foreign-exchange bank license cannot legally provide the primary settlement rail for this entity. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Korean banking regulations, only banks licensed as foreign exchange banks can capture foreign-exchange purpose codes, perform capital-verification checks, issue foreign-investment registration certificates, and open KRW demand deposit accounts that can be used freely for payroll, trade settlements, and tax payments. A foreign EMI can at most act as a payment front-end connected to the Korean account mandate, but it would not be permitted to accept corporate deposits directly and cannot replace the regulated domestic bank. The best design is a Tier 1 bank relationship for all money movement; any EMI should be limited to notifying the treasury about approvals and cannot clear KRW.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Layered foreign ownership and an unidentifiable ultimate-beneficial-owner chain, particularly when the foreign member is a holding company in a zero-tax jurisdiction or uses nominee shareholders.

Root cause: Layered foreign ownership and an unidentifiable ultimate-beneficial-owner chain, particularly when the foreign member is a holding company in a zero-tax jurisdiction or uses nominee shareholders.

Defense evidence: An unbroken ownership waterfall from the Yuhan Hoesa to each natural-person ultimate beneficial owner; certified copies of the articles of incorporation and shareholder registers of every intermediate company; notarized UBO declarations for every entity in the chain; audited financial statements or equivalent evidence of the funding entity for the last two years; and valid passport copies of all UBOs. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

21% top marginal corporate income tax rate applicable to taxable income exceeding KRW 20 billion, plus a 10% local income tax surtax, producing a combined aggregate marginal rate of 23.1%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, comprised of 20% corporate withholding tax plus a 2% local income tax surtax on the withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation are mandatory where aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; a Master File and group-level documentation are required where worldwide consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 50 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate an ESR-style registration regime, but Korean tax administration and treaty relief firmly apply the OECD substance-over-form doctrine: the Korean LLC must perform its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA), including price setting, shipping negotiation, LC discounting, FX risk management, contract execution and commodity risk assumption, through real Korean office, staff, books and bank accounts. A sugar arbitrage vehicle functioning solely as a letter-box conduit is disregarded and any directed payments are recharacterized. Korean CFC rules also attribute income of low-tax foreign affiliates back to the Korean controlling entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and fraudulent underreporting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47-3 of the Basic Act for National Taxes, fraudulent underreporting carries a 40% penalty on the evaded tax, in addition to daily late-payment interest of 0.022% per day. Amounts exceeding KRW 300 million are referred to criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with corporate fines up to five times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: The director may be jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax, penalties and interest under the Basic Act for National Taxes. The corporate veil is pierced when the LLC is an alter ego or sham; criminal consequences include asset seizure, travel ban and blacklisting, and imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years where evaded tax exceeds statutory thresholds under the Aggravated Punishment for Specific Economic Crimes Act.
Transfer pricing manipulation and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: The NTS may disregard the purported pricing and re-determine Korean taxable income under the International Tax Adjustment Act arm's length article. The adjustment triggers an unfaithful-return penalty of 20% to 40% plus daily late-payment interest; fraudulent transfer pricing documentation is a criminal predicate leading to prosecution and fines up to twice the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fabricated contracts, approve artificial invoices or direct the economic substance stripping are liable personally for the adjusted tax and penalties through civil veil-piercing. Criminal liability includes imprisonment up to 7 years for fraud, travel ban/blacklisting, and court-ordered restitution of the diverted margin plus interest.
AML and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act violations involving shell-company settlement routing

Statutory sanction: The Specific Financial Information Act requires suspicious transaction reporting; violation may expose the entity to administrative fines, account freezes, revocation of foreign-exchange banking designation and FIU criminal referral. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act imposes penalties on unreported international transactions, including fines up to twice the transaction amount and imprisonment for willful violation.

Director personal liability: A controlling director is treated as a beneficial owner and may be ordered to personally restore the full amount routed through the Korean account; the corporate veil is pierced where the entity has no separate board, no operational substance and no independent books. Criminal outcomes include money-laundering imprisonment up to 5 years, an international travel ban/blacklisting, and disqualification from serving as a director of any Korean company.

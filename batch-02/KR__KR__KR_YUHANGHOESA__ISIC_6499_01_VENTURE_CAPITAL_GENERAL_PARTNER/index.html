Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital and no mandatory blocked escrow account are imposed for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. The stated capital must nevertheless be specified in the Articles of Incorporation and all capital contributions must be fully paid or transferred to the company before the filing of the incorporation registration. For a venture capital general partner, the Financial Services Commission or the Ministry of SMEs and Startups may impose a prudential or equity-capacity requirement under the applicable fund registration rules if the entity is authorized to perform discretionary investment management, but no legal minimum capital escrow is required at the initial incorporation stage.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; official online portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr) for business registration, VAT returns, electronic tax invoicing, tax clearance, and national tax payments.

E-government filing portal

A domestic Yuhan Hoesa must file a Business Registration Application with the competent District Tax Office (or via HomeTax using the representative's digital certificate and the entity's incorporation certificate). The NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (local TIN) and a Business Registration Certificate within approximately one week. A foreign non-resident entity that has a Korean VAT obligation without a domestic place of business must appoint a tax representative where required, submit the foreign entity's registration documents, and file the Business Registration Application with the District Tax Office or HomeTax using foreign registration authentication; the NTS issues a foreign business operator registration number for VAT, enabling e-invoice issuance, VAT refund claims, and zero-rate export substantiation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A domestic Yuhan Hoesa must file a Business Registration Application with the competent District Tax Office (or via HomeTax using the representative's digital certificate and the entity's incorporation certificate). The NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (local TIN) and a Business Registration Certificate within approximately one week. A foreign non-resident entity that has a Korean VAT obligation without a domestic place of business must appoint a tax representative where required, submit the foreign entity's registration documents, and file the Business Registration Application with the District Tax Office or HomeTax using foreign registration authentication; the NTS issues a foreign business operator registration number for VAT, enabling e-invoice issuance, VAT refund claims, and zero-rate export substantiation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Korean tax law; the Yuhan Hoesa is unconditionally taxed as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act. A qualifying 100% owned domestic corporate group may elect consolidated corporation tax filing with the NTS under the Corporate Tax Act, and the venture capital fund may elect specialized Venture Capital Tax Incentive regimes only where separately qualified; no other entity-level pass-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration from commencement of business without a revenue threshold; no corporate VAT registration exemption. Individual entrepreneurs with prior-year annual taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80 million may qualify for simplified taxable person status, but registration is still required. Foreign non-resident providers of electronic/digital services must register with NTS from the first Korean taxable supply, with no volume exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is allowed under the Value Added Tax Act for exported goods delivered outside Korea, services rendered in connection with exported goods, international transportation services, and qualifying offshore services supplied to and consumed by non-resident recipients. For the venture capital general partner's management and advisory fees, 0% VAT can be claimed only if the service recipient is a non-resident fund, the service is not consumed at a Korean establishment, the fee is settled in foreign currency or as contractually agreed, and the GP holds contemporaneous evidence of offshore use, the underlying advisory contract, and an invoice to the non-resident fund.

  • UNI-PASS export declaration / export customs clearance certificate issued by Korea Customs Service
  • Commercial invoice and, for goods, packing list
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or other transport document evidencing physical export (for goods exports only)
  • Written management/advisory or services agreement with the offshore fund (for service exports)
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation (Inward Remittance Certificate/FIRC) issued by a foreign exchange bank or SWIFT credit advice showing receipt of the export/service proceeds
  • Service performance report, offshore fund incorporation and control evidence, and contemporaneous board-minute evidence that the service is rendered for and consumed by the non-resident recipient

All VAT-registered general taxable persons must issue electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) via the NTS HomeTax e-Tax Invoice Service; no paper tax invoices are allowed except for statutory exceptions. The invoice data must be transmitted through the HomeTax authenticated portal or the NTS system-to-system e-Tax Invoice API at issuance or within the statutory filing cycle. HomeTax assigns each e-tax invoice a unique registration number and integrity seal, and NTS risk-analytics monitors issuance, supplier-purchaser matching, and refund patterns. Erroneous or non-transmitted invoices are not eligible for input VAT credit and expose the issuer to additional tax and penalties.

The NTS will disallow zero-rating if export/service proceeds are not collected, the foreign receipt is not traced to the declared transaction, or the offshore-consumption evidence is inadequate. The disputed amount is reclassified as domestic taxable supply, attracting 10% VAT, understatement penalties of up to 40% of the unpaid tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes, monthly delay interest, and the loss of any input VAT deduction. False or fabricated export documentation may additionally trigger criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evasion and Tax Offenses Act, with imprisonment and fines, and the original refunded VAT is clawed back with penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized foreign-exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance and supervised by The Bank of Korea and the Financial Supervisory Service. Current-account transactions are freely convertible and require only bank-level notification; capital-account transactions are open but subject to mandatory reporting, registration, and, in certain investment categories, prior approval. The Korean won is not a fully deliverable offshore currency; non-residents cannot freely clear KRW outside authorized domestic banks. Profit remittance is permitted once lawful dividends or capital gains are proven, but the bank must obtain a Foreign Exchange Transaction Report and the remitting corporation must evidence realized retained earnings or an exit event. Withholding taxes, if applicable, must be paid before remittance. There is no general administrative surrender requirement for export earnings, but exporters must repatriate proceeds into an authorized bank account within the statutory timeframe. Overall risk is moderate; the regime is best described as information-intensive rather than restrictive, requiring precise purpose codes, robust source-of-funds documentation, and full reconciliation between inward capital calls, operational cashflow, and outward treasury movements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must ensure that export proceeds are received within one year from the date of the export declaration, with extension up to two years subject to approval by the foreign exchange bank. Once received, export foreign-currency proceeds must be credited to a foreign-currency deposit account at an authorized Korean foreign-exchange bank and reported through that bank. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent; export proceeds are not required to be converted into Korean won, but any voluntary conversion is executed at the prevailing market rate and must be reflected in the bank's foreign-exchange transaction records.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea, the central bank of South Korea, acting in concert with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Financial Supervisory Service under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low for the primary treasury relationship. South Korean law forbids non-bank offshore fintech EMIs from opening Korean-won current accounts, issuing domestic IBAN-equivalent settlement infrastructure, or acting as authorized foreign-exchange banks for resident corporate entities. A venture capital general partner domiciled in Korea must maintain its regulatory reporting, capital verification, and corporate treasury through a licensed Korean bank. Offshore EMIs such as global business payment platforms may be used only as auxiliary tools for non-KRW vendor payments outside Korea, and even then the entity must disclose the intermediate settlement route to the Korean bank and obtain approval where foreign-exchange reporting is triggered. EMIs cannot legally support inbound capital calls or outbound investment disbursements that require Korean foreign-exchange purpose codes, and any attempt to route KRW through an offshore EMI would violate the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Real Name Financial Transactions Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions must be wired to the GP's dedicated foreign-currency or Korean-won account at an authorized Korean bank using SWIFT MT103 with a clear purpose code describing the capital contribution or equity investment. If the contribution qualifies as foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the remitter and the Korean entity must complete the applicable registration and notification with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and obtain an inward investment certificate from the receiving bank. The receiving bank issues an inward remittance certificate and files the transaction under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. A Korean certified public accountant must later perform capital verification against the paid-in capital and issue a capital verification report for the registry and the bank compliance file. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top national corporate income tax rate for the 2024 tax year, plus a 10% local income tax surtax on the assessed national corporate tax, producing a combined top marginal effective rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% on outbound dividends to non-resident corporate shareholders, composed of 20% national tax and 2% local income tax surtax, unless a DTA provides a lower rate subject to NTS approval.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Act for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, mandatory Master File and Local File preparation is triggered when annual international intercompany transactions exceed KRW 10 billion. CbC Report filing is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion. NTS may request documentation below these thresholds where cross-border management fees, carried interest, or intra-group financing present material risk.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies the substance-over-form principle and the OECD Principle Purpose Test. A VC GP must evidence personnel, premises, investment committee authority, risk-taking, portfolio management CIGA, and bank accounts in Korea. Without such substance, the NTS will characterize the GP as a conduit, recharacterize transactions, deny treaty benefits, and impose CIT and withholding on deemed profit repatriation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Artificial Management Fee and Carried Interest Shifting

Statutory sanction: The NTS re-adds the fee and carried interest to Korean taxable income under the substance-over-form rule, imposes a 40% unfaithful-return penalty, adds local income tax surtax, and accrues daily delinquency charges.

Director personal liability: Korean courts pierce the corporate veil under the Commercial Act; the managing director becomes jointly and severally liable for CIT, penalties, local tax, and interest. The NTS may freeze personal assets, impose an overseas travel ban, and refer the director for criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to five years and fines up to five times the evaded tax.
Shell Company and PE Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: The NTS applies Corporate Tax Act Articles 4 and 6, disallows the no-PE declaration, assesses tax on all Korean-source income, and imposes the 20% negligence penalty under the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Director personal liability: A director who signs a false no-PE return can be personally liable for the corporate tax once the company is stripped of assets. Criminal consequences include imprisonment for up to two years, fines, personal asset attachment, and an NTS international exit control order.
Treaty Shopping and Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: The NTS denies treaty benefits under the Limitation on Benefits and Principal Purpose Test, recovers the additional withholding tax, imposes the 40% fraudulent understatement penalty, and recharacterizes the Hong Kong entity as a Korean PE if investment management occurs in Korea.

Director personal liability: A director who orchestrates the conduit arrangement is personally liable for unpaid withholding tax after corporate dissolution; travel permits may be suspended, and criminal charges under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act carry imprisonment of up to two years and a fine exceeding the evaded amount.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None; the Korean Commercial Act imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa LLC. All subscribed capital must, however, be fully contributed to a Korean bank account in the name of the proposed company before the incorporation registration is filed, and the bank must issue a certificate of paid-in capital. There is no statutory blocked escrow account requirement. For foreign-invested LLCs, the inbound remittance must be reported to a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; that report is procedural and does not create an escrow condition.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS) through the HomeTax electronic portal (hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr) for export declarations and customs verification.

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Yuhan Hoesa (LLC): (1) complete Korean incorporation and commercial registration at the local court, obtaining the Certificate of Incorporation and corporate seal registration; (2) within 20 days of business commencement, submit an Application for Business Registration under Article 8 of the Value-Added Tax Act to the jurisdictional district tax office, physically or through HomeTax electronic authentication; (3) NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number which serves as the corporate TIN and VAT ID, and the Business Registration Certificate (Saupja Deungnokjeung) becomes the sole tax identification credential; (4) if the shareholder is a foreign non-resident entity, file the foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act with KOTRA or a licensed foreign exchange bank, and enter the foreign shareholder in the corporate share register - this is not a separate tax registration but may require exchange-control reporting. For a foreign non-resident entity operating through a Korean branch or permanent establishment without forming an incorporated Korean subsidiary, the additional path is: (5) register the domestic place of business with NTS using the Foreign Corporation Domestic Place of Business Registration procedure, appoint a resident tax representative, and obtain the same Business Registration Number; this registration automatically establishes corporate income tax, VAT, and withholding registration obligations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for a corporation. A Yuhan Hoesa must register for VAT before business starts and in all cases no later than 20 days after business commencement. An individual business with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80 million may qualify as a simplified taxable person, but a corporate legal entity is always a general taxable person and cannot rely on that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and qualifying international services are zero-rated under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act and its Enforcement Decree. For this franchise business, zero-rating applies to: (a) physical exports of bubble tea syrups, ingredients, packaging, and equipment supplied from Korea to foreign franchisees, evidenced by a Korea Customs Service export declaration through UNI-PASS; and (b) franchise licensing and royalty services supplied to foreign franchisees where the service is effectively consumed or used outside Korea, evidenced by the franchise agreement, foreign country trademark or store operations records, and foreign currency remittance. Domestic supplies to Korean franchisees, including local syrup sales and domestic royalty fees, are subject to 10% VAT and are not zero-rated.

  • Export Declaration Receipt lodged in and approved by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS for physical export of goods
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international consignment note evidencing movement of goods from Korea to the foreign franchisee destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the export declaration value, quantity, and consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency from the foreign franchisee
  • For franchise service zero-rating: executed franchise agreement, evidence of offshore use or consumption of the licensed rights, service completion records, and corresponding inbound foreign-currency payment records

Electronic tax invoicing is mandatory for all corporations under Article 32 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act. A Yuhan Hoesa must issue an Electronic Tax Invoice (e-Tax Invoice) and transmit it to NTS HomeTax no later than the day following the supply. NTS validates the e-Tax Invoice series, matches it to the taxable person's VAT return and to the counterparty's purchase records, and uses this cross-matching as the anti-fraud clearance mechanism. There is no separate physical fiscal stamp or third-party real-time clearance API; the HomeTax e-Tax Invoice system is the statutory fiscal clearance channel.

NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly verify export zero-rating by matching UNI-PASS export declarations, foreign exchange remittance confirmations from Korean banks, and e-Tax Invoice records. If the required customs, logistics, or FX repatriation documentation is missing, incomplete, or inconsistent, NTS will reclassify the export as a taxable domestic supply, assess 10% VAT on the full unreported value, disallow related input VAT credits, and reassess corporate income tax accordingly. Statutory additional taxes under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes apply, and serious or fictitious export claims are referred to the public prosecutor under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the Customs Act, and the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Economic Crimes. Retrospective reassessment and clawback are permitted under the National Tax Basic Act limitation periods: 5 years for ordinary omissions and 10 years for fraudulent or deceptive omissions, with interest and penalties accruing from the original return due date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but supervised convertibility regime. Current-account transactions for franchise royalties, management fees, and goods import/export settlement are generally free from prior approval, yet they require the underlying transaction to be reported through authorized domestic banks using standardized foreign exchange declaration forms. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity injection and foreign-direct-investment (FDI) funded working capital, are not freely convertible without registration; the foreign investor must file an FDI declaration with KOTRA or a registered receiving bank, open a foreign-currency account, and document the exact business purpose before converting to Korean won. There is no mandatory forex surrender quota for exporters, but all inward remittance must pass through a licensed foreign exchange bank, and the bank is obliged to verify the remittance route, purpose code, and tax status. Profit remittance is permitted after corporate income tax and dividend withholding tax settlement, provided the entity produces audited financial statements, an external audit report where required, a certificate of tax clearance, and a valid foreign-exchange transaction report. The practical bottleneck is not legal prohibition but documentation density; the Bank of Korea and KoFIU expect an A-Z evidentiary trail covering UBOs, source of funds, business substance, and tax compliance before each large outward payout is cleared.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected by the Korean exporting entity within 6 months from the date of the export declaration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; extensions can be granted by the Ministry of Economy and Finance or the receiving bank for a documented period if the exporter demonstrates justifiable delay such as non-payment by the overseas franchise. There is no mandatory publicly fixed percentage of export receipts that must be surrendered into Korean won; exporters may retain collections in their corporate foreign-currency account or convert to KRW at a licensed bank's market rate. However, the bank must be given a copy of the export customs declaration, the commercial invoice, the bill of lading, and the foreign exchange transaction report showing the expected payment date; any collection beyond the six-month window triggers an overdue export-report filing and may expose the entity to administrative penalties or AML escalation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) with regulatory execution by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Financial Services Commission (FSC) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as global neo-banking treasury tools do not qualify as deposit-taking institutions under the Korean Banking Act; they cannot issue a corporate KRW account, cannot link to the Korean tax administration's electronic cash-receipt system, and cannot process the statutory Foreign Exchange Transactions Report on behalf of a Korean resident entity. Fintech EMIs may be used only as an auxiliary payment collector for non-Korean franchisees if those merchants prefer card/MCAD payments, but the primary operating treasury, FDI escrowing, and trade settlement must remain with a commercial bank licensed by the FSC. Using a foreign EMI as the main treasury rail would create a significant KYC gap because the Korean depository bank cannot verify the source of KRW funding and KoFIU would flag the structure as an unlicensed money-service operation. Therefore, treasury architects should not propose any cross-border EMI as the settlement spine for the Yuhan Hoesa franchise; a licensed domestic bank is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity must be preceded by a Foreign Investment (FDI) declaration through KOTRA or the receiving tier-1 bank in Korea. The remitting overseas franchise operator should wire USD or KRW using SWIFT MT103 and clearly reference the FIPA declaration number, the target company name, and the capital purpose in field 70; a reference to personal expense, salary, or unspecified loan will be rejected by the Korean clearing bank. The receiving bank then validates the foreign exchange purpose code, screens the counterparty, and transfers the funds into a temporarily held Foreign Currency Deposit Account before converting to KRW at the market rate. Within one month, the Yuhan Hoesa must retain an external certified accountant to produce a paid-in capital verification report and secure the Foreign Investor Registration Certificate. This certificate must be delivered to the depository bank, the ADB, and the tax office before the working capital can be treated as free corporate funds.For franchise royalty and management service payments to the overseas brand owner, the clearing rail should be a SWIFT MT103 wire backed by the signed franchise contract, a Korean tax invoice with VAT, and evidence of withholding tax payment to the National Tax Service. For physical syrup, tapioca pearls, packaging, and equipment, the rail should be a commercial letter of credit under SWIFT MT700 or a documentary collection route, with required documents being commercial invoice, packing list, insurance certificate, bill of lading or cargo receipt, K-Unipass export declaration, and the applicable health/food import certificate. Domestic franchise stores collecting from consumers in Korea use local POS rails cleared through Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute (KFTC) with KRW settlement; no international SWIFT charge is required. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as global neo-banking treasury tools do not qualify as deposit-taking institutions under the Korean Banking Act; they cannot issue a corporate KRW account, cannot link to the Korean tax administration's electronic cash-receipt system, and cannot process the statutory Foreign Exchange Transactions Report on behalf of a Korean resident entity. Fintech EMIs may be used only as an auxiliary payment collector for non-Korean franchisees if those merchants prefer card/MCAD payments, but the primary operating treasury, FDI escrowing, and trade settlement must remain with a commercial bank licensed by the FSC. Using a foreign EMI as the main treasury rail would create a significant KYC gap because the Korean depository bank cannot verify the source of KRW funding and KoFIU would flag the structure as an unlicensed money-service operation. Therefore, treasury architects should not propose any cross-border EMI as the settlement spine for the Yuhan Hoesa franchise; a licensed domestic bank is mandatory.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (applicable to taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; lower progressive brackets: 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% up to KRW 20 billion, 21% up to KRW 300 billion; plus 10% local income tax surtax on the national corporate tax, yielding a combined top effective rate of 27.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national withholding tax + 2% local income tax surtax) on dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders unless reduced under an applicable tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion (total intercompany transactions with foreign related parties in the fiscal year) for Master File and Local File preparation; KRW 5 billion for local file only; lower thresholds apply for cross-border intercompany service transactions (KRW 1 billion) and indirect contracts (KRW 5 billion). Contemporaneous documentation must be prepared by the tax return due date and submitted within 60 days of an NTS request.

Economic substance scope: Under the Corporate Income Tax Act and the Korea Tax Treaty Act, the NTS applies a substance-over-form doctrine. For a Yuhan Hoesa acting as a franchise master or supply chain hub, it must demonstrate that its core income-generating activities (CIGA) – e.g., contract negotiation, logistics coordination, quality control, regional marketing, and IP management – are economically occurred in Korea. If the entity is merely a shell that receives royalty or resale income without substantive employees, premises, or functions, the NTS may disregard the entity's legal form, reallocate income to the beneficial owner, and deny treaty benefits under the Limitation on Benefits (LOB) or principal purpose test (PPT).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 9 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, intentional tax evasion (underreporting taxable income or claiming fraudulent deductions) is punishable with imprisonment for up to 5 years or fines up to 3 times the evaded tax; if the evaded tax exceeds KRW 500 million, imprisonment of up to 10 years; additional 40% of the evaded tax as an administrative surcharge under the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the concealment or who approve false filings are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties (corporate veil pierced); criminal conviction leads to imprisonment; the NTS may blacklist the director, impose travel bans, and attach personal assets for restitution.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 6 of the Act on Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the NTS may re-determine the arm's length price and assess additional corporate income tax, plus an underreporting penalty of up to 20% of the adjusted profit; if documents are not provided, a penalty of up to KRW 100 million may apply per document (Article 11(3)).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the transfer pricing controls or knowingly cause the non-arm's length documentation to be suppressed are liable for the resulting tax delinquency; the NTS can seek civil damages from the directors for failure to exercise due diligence; in egregious cases, the director's personal assets (bank accounts, properties) can be frozen and attached.
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Abuse (Anti-Money Laundering & Treaty Shopping)

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Treaty Act (Article 30-2) and the Financial Transaction Reports Act, treaty shopping and sham transactions are denied treaty benefits; the NTS imposes ordinary corporate tax at 22% (27.5% effective) plus a 20% penalty for unjustified tax benefits; the Financial Services Commission may impose administrative fines up to KRW 50 million; repeated violations may lead to business registration cancellation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly form or maintain a sham entity are personally liable for the disgorgement of all tax benefits; they may face criminal charges for fraud or breach of fiduciary duty under the Commercial Act, leading to imprisonment up to 7 years and a fine up to KRW 100 million; directors may also be subject to an international travel ban and Interpol red notice if assets are secreted abroad.

Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS); electronic administration portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr).

        
E-government filing portal
For a foreign non-resident entity such as Yuhan Hoesa (KR_YUHANGHOESA) that will make taxable supplies in Korea, the entity must first determine whether it has a domestic place of business. If no domestic fixed place exists, it must appoint a Korean tax representative and file a non-resident business registration application with the National Tax Service, either in person at the competent district tax office or online through the HomeTax portal using the non-resident electronic registration menu. The filing must include the entity's certificate of incorporation or equivalent constitutional document, corporate register extract or articles of association, evidence of its foreign tax identification number or official business registration in the home jurisdiction, a power of attorney appointing the Korean tax representative, and proof of the representative's Korean address. NTS will process the application and issue a 10-digit Korean Tax Identification Number (Business Registration Number, which also serves as the VAT registration number) and a Business Registration Certificate. Registration must be completed within 20 days of the commencement of taxable business activities in Korea. Foreign suppliers of electronic services may use the simplified non-resident registration facility in HomeTax but a Korean tax representative is still required for service of documents and compliance.

10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No monetary threshold: every person making taxable supplies in Korea is required to register for VAT irrespective of turnover, under the Value-Added Tax Act. Foreign non-resident suppliers of electronic services are required to complete simplified VAT registration from the first taxable supply made to Korean customers.         Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating is available for exported goods as well as foreign-currency-earning services under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act. For the online sale of musical instruments and studio audio gear, zero-rating applies to physical goods supplied to a foreign buyer where the goods are actually exported from Korea under a customs export declaration, ownership passes to the foreign buyer, payment is received in foreign currency or an approved settlement method, and the export proceeds are repatriated within the statutory deadline. For digital or electronic services, zero-rating is restricted to services performed and consumed outside Korea; digital services supplied to Korean consumers are taxable at the standard 10% rate under the place-of-supply rules. The exporter must maintain an auditable file containing export contracts, shipping documents, customs export declarations, invoices, and bank settlement records.

All VAT invoices issued between registered businesses must be issued as electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoice) through the National Tax Service HomeTax system or a certified e-invoice service provider. The e-invoice must be transmitted to NTS no later than the day following its issuance. There is no separate real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping; Korea instead relies on mandatory e-invoice API transmission and the NTS automatic cross-checking system for transaction-level matching. For retail sales not covered by a tax invoice, a Cash Receipt must be issued and reported to NTS. Export sales must be recorded with zero-rated e-invoices or export electronic tax invoices.

      
If the National Tax Service determines that a zero-rated export claim lacks actual export, a valid customs declaration, or timely foreign exchange repatriation, it will deny the zero-rating and assess VAT at the standard 10% rate on the full supply value. The assessment is subject to statutory interest and penalty surcharges under the Framework Act on National Taxes, including a 10% penalty for erroneous underreporting and up to 40% for fraudulent understatement. Any input VAT deducted in relation to the failed export is retrospectively clawed back, and additional penalties apply for false e-Tax Invoices or non-issuance of tax invoices. Korea Customs Service may also impose customs penalties or refer the matter for criminal prosecution for export fraud. The NTS may open retrospective audits within the statutory assessment periods, which are extended for cross-border or fraudulent cases.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea maintains a liberalised but administratively monitored foreign exchange system. Current-account transactions are freely convertible and South Korea has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, meaning trade-related receipts and payments are generally unrestricted. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment into a Yuhan Hoesa, are also encouraged, but they must be reported through a registered foreign-exchange bank and, where applicable, registered under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. There is no general restriction on holding foreign currency, and a Korean limited-liability entity may maintain foreign-currency deposit accounts alongside KRW accounts. Profit remittance hurdles are largely procedural rather than impeditive: a foreign shareholder can remit dividends provided the company has completed external taxation, filed the appropriate withholding report, and the bank verifies the board dividend resolution and audited financial statements. Surrender requirements have been significantly liberalised; export receipts are not forcibly converted into KRW, but the receiving bank must be informed, and any conversion is executed at the market rate. Overall, Korea's regime is best described as free convertibility with reporting-based controls rather than approval-based controls for ordinary trade, while certain capital flows above reporting thresholds require a foreign-exchange transaction form and supporting documentation.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and related regulations, South Korea does not impose mandatory conversion ratios or an obligatory surrender of export foreign-currency receipts. Exporters are required to settle export proceeds through domestic foreign-exchange banks within the agreed contractual maturity, which for open-account or deferred-payment exports is generally no more than one year from the date of shipment unless an extension is approved by the relevant bank. Receipts may be retained 100% in foreign-currency deposit accounts or freely converted into Korean won at market rates. If retained, subsequent cross-border outbound use must comply with transaction reporting thresholds.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound foreign direct investment must be reported at the receiving foreign-exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before issuance of a Foreign Investment Report Certificate. The remitter must wire funds via SWIFT MT103 using the purpose code for foreign direct investment in a Korean corporation to the Capital Escrow Account held in the Korean LLC's name at a registered foreign-exchange bank. The bank performs capital verification by matching the certificate value, the share subscription agreement, and the actual credit advice. Once verified, funds may be converted into KRW at market rate and transferred to the company's Basic Operational Currency Account. All inward remittances must be properly reported through the receiving bank, and the standard foreign-exchange transaction form must reflect the true nature of the equity capital.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000 for cross-border related-party transactions (Local File); Global Master File required for multinational group with consolidated revenue exceeding KRW 100,000,000,000; CbCR when group revenue exceeds KRW 1,000,000,000,000.

      
Economic substance scope: Korea applies a 'substance over form' doctrine and GAAR under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the International Tax Adjustment Act. Non-resident structures must demonstrate genuine business purpose and economic substance; artificial arrangements will be re-characterized and treaty benefits denied.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine up to 3 times the amount of evaded tax, plus additional tax of up to 20% of the unpaid amount and daily interest.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in evasion can be personally criminally liable, subjected to civil restitution, and have exit/travel bans imposed by the court.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Sham Transaction
        
Statutory sanction: Under GAAR, NTS may disregard the entity/transaction, deny treaty relief, retroactively reassess tax, add surcharges and interest, and impose penalty tax of up to 40% of the avoided tax.

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced; directors can be held jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency, and may face criminal prosecution for fraud.

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violation
        
Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to KRW 50 million or imprisonment up to 5 years, or both; corporate entities subject to penalty payment orders and possible revocation of licenses.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under AML law for failure to implement KYC/STR procedures; potential imprisonment, travel bans, and forfeiture of proceeds.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Mispricing
        
Statutory sanction: NTS adjustment to arm's length, additional corporate tax on the imputed income, 10% underreporting penalty, 0.022% daily interest, and penalty for failure to produce contemporaneous TP documentation.

        
Director personal liability: In cases of gross neglect or intent, directors may be held personally liable for penalties and interest; serious violations may trigger criminal prosecution.

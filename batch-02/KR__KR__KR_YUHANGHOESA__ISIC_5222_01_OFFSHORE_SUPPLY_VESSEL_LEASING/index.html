Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The total capital of a Yuhan Hoesa must be at least KRW 10,000,000 and must be fully paid in before the incorporation registration is filed. All subscription amounts must be deposited into a blocked capital escrow account at a Korean commercial bank in the name of the Company, and the bank certificate of capital payment must be submitted to the District Court Registry. Withdrawal of the subscribed capital for corporate purposes is permitted only after the certificate of incorporation is issued; any repayment, refund, or disguised return of capital to members before legal incorporation is void under the Commercial Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold triggers VAT registration; all persons, including non-resident suppliers, that make taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea must register as VAT business operators within 20 days of commencement. Small businesses with annual revenue below the legislated simplified-taxation threshold may qualify as simplified taxpayers (gan-i-gwa-se-ja), but this is not an exemption from registration and does not remove VAT collection obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the chartering and operation of offshore platform support ships, zero-rated VAT treatment applies when the charter services are distinctively supplied for direct export earning, performed in the course of exporting goods, or rendered to a foreign non-resident user with actual use and enjoyment occurring wholly outside Korean customs territory. Offshore vessel chartering or transport from a Korean port to a foreign offshore installation qualifies as export or foreign-service zero-rating where the chartered vessel physically departs Korean customs waters and the service is provided beyond Korean territorial jurisdiction. The taxpayer must maintain documentary evidence proving the foreign nature of the transaction, including the vessel's departure, international itinerary, and receipt of payment in foreign currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Cargo Declaration / export report confirmation issued by Korea Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal/sea waybill
  • Charter party agreement and vessel service performance certificate
  • Final VAT invoice expressly marked 'Export / Zero-rated' or 'Foreign Service / Zero-rated'
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) and SWIFT/Foreign Exchange Bank remittance proof
  • Vessel position log, AIS/GPS tracking report, or captain's log evidencing performance outside Korean customs waters
  • Offshore installation service acceptance report confirming receipt of vessel services by the foreign charterer

All VAT-registered businesses are required to issue electronic tax invoices (jeon-ja-se-geum-gye-san-seo) for B2B supplies, and to transmit those invoices to the NTS through the Home Tax Service real-time fiscal clearance API by the day after supply issuance; the e-invoice system authenticates the electronic document and cross-checks declarations with VAT returns. B2C card payments and cash payments above the threshold must be reported through the NTS Cash Receipt system. There is no physical fiscal stamping requirement; anti-fraud integrity is enforced by mandatory e-invoice issuance, real-time transmission, NTS encryption/digital signature protocols, and report-clearing reconciliation between e-invoices, cash receipts, card settlement data, and customs declarations.

The NTS, Korea Customs Service, and the Bank of Korea cross-match e-invoices, VAT returns, export declarations, vessel movement data, and foreign exchange repatriation records. If the required export documentation is not retained or produced, if the vessel charter and movement evidence does not confirm foreign use, or if exported goods or foreign service payments are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period without an approved extension, the zero-rating is rejected. The originally omitted VAT is then assessed at 10% plus statutory interest, and penalties of up to 40% of the short-paid or unreported tax apply under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the VAT Act. Refunded zero-rated output VAT plus interest and penalties are subject to immediate retrospective clawback; intentional submission of false export documentation or inflated export refund claims may constitute tax evasion and carries criminal penalties, delinquency charges, and publication of the taxpayer's identity by the NTS.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won is freely convertible for current account transactions, and the Republic of Korea operates a liberalized negative-list capital account system. Foreign direct investment into a Yuhan Hoesa requires a foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, which is filed through a designated foreign exchange bank or KOTRA. Inbound capital contributions may be converted into Korean won at the prevailing market rate through a licensed foreign exchange bank, and there is no statutory forced conversion once the funds are properly reported. Profit remittance is permitted for current account purposes, including dividends and lease income, provided the entity submits audited financial statements, tax payment certificates for dividend withholding, corporate tax filings, and the foreign investment registration record. The Bank of Korea maintains monitoring and reporting obligations under the Foreign Exchange Information System, and residents must use authorized foreign exchange banks for cross-border settlements. Non-resident shareholders may hold foreign currency accounts in Korea, and the LLC may operate both KRW and foreign currency accounts. The convertibility regime is mature and consistent with OECD standards, but capital repatriations are subject to documentary scrutiny and require that all underlying transactions are traceable to bona fide commercial activities such as vessel chartering, maintenance, crew costs, and related marine services. A significant compliance burden is the need to document each foreign exchange transaction with supporting contracts, invoices, and customs or maritime declarations to satisfy the bank's internal trade-based anti-money-laundering controls and the BOK's post-event verification processes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and BOK regulations, export and charter-hire proceeds from offshore supply vessel leasing must be received through a designated foreign exchange bank within one year from the date of the underlying export or service performance. There is no mandatory surrender ratio requiring conversion to KRW; the entity may retain the foreign currency in a permitted foreign currency deposit account. If the proceeds are used to settle KRW obligations, conversion is performed at the licensed bank's prevailing customer rate. Extensions beyond the one-year receipt deadline require a written application to the managing foreign exchange bank or the Ministry of Economy and Finance with evidence of the commercial reason for delay, such as disputed charter hire, late voyage adjustments, or protracted insurance claims. All export proceeds must be reported in the Foreign Exchange Information System, and supporting documents such as the charter party agreement, commercial invoices, bill of lading or port call records, and proof of settlement must be archived for at least five years to satisfy audit and AML obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Financial Services Commission under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as a primary treasury rail in South Korea. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and cannot lawfully open Korean won accounts or execute foreign exchange transactions on behalf of a resident Korean entity. They are also not authorized to issue local account identifiers for KRW clearing nor to file the mandatory Foreign Exchange Information System reports with the Bank of Korea. The Korean financial regulator requires any entity conducting money transmission and payment services to be registered with the Financial Services Commission under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and most offshore EMIs have no such license. An offshore EMI may at best serve as a secondary non-KRW collection vehicle if the Yuhan Hoesa separately maintains all required reporting records, but this is strongly discouraged because it complicates capital verification, foreign investment registration, and trade-based AML audit trails. The only compliant and scalable route is a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank account combined with SWIFT rails for all inbound and outbound treasury activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable as a primary treasury rail in South Korea. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and cannot lawfully open Korean won accounts or execute foreign exchange transactions on behalf of a resident Korean entity. They are also not authorized to issue local account identifiers for KRW clearing nor to file the mandatory Foreign Exchange Information System reports with the Bank of Korea. The Korean financial regulator requires any entity conducting money transmission and payment services to be registered with the Financial Services Commission under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and most offshore EMIs have no such license. An offshore EMI may at best serve as a secondary non-KRW collection vehicle if the Yuhan Hoesa separately maintains all required reporting records, but this is strongly discouraged because it complicates capital verification, foreign investment registration, and trade-based AML audit trails. The only compliant and scalable route is a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank account combined with SWIFT rails for all inbound and outbound treasury activity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000 (approximately USD 3.5 million) in annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation and the LCITA international transaction report; groups with consolidated revenue of KRW 100 billion or group-wide intercompany transactions of KRW 50 billion must additionally prepare a Master File.

Economic substance scope: The Republic of Korea does not operate a Gulf-style formal Economic Substance Regulation filing, but treaty entitlement requires beneficial ownership and real economic substance. For Yuhan Hoesa chartering offshore platform support vessels, the entity must exercise strategic decision-making, crew employment, vessel dispatch, risk management, and technical maintenance in Korea; a shell booking company will be denied treaty benefits under substance-over-form, general anti-avoidance rules, CFC legislation, and OECD Pillar Two income inclusion rules, and may be recharacterized as a conduit lacking Core Income-Generating Activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 imposes imprisonment up to three years or a fine up to three times the evaded tax; the National Tax Basic Act Article 47-3 imposes a 40% surcharge on fraudulent underreporting; transfer pricing adjustment adds delinquency interest and accelerates the assessment window.

Director personal liability: A director who signs or causes the false return may be personally liable under Korean Commercial Act Article 401 for intentional or negligent damage to the company and creditors. Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil where the LLC is a mere alter ego, exposing the director's personal assets, bank accounts, and real estate to seizure; serious tax delinquency triggers overseas travel ban, public blacklisting by the National Tax Service, and criminal imprisonment up to three years.
Withholding Tax Evasion / DTA Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: National Tax Basic Act Article 47-5 imposes a 10% surcharge on uncollected withholding tax plus daily delinquency interest; Income Tax Act Article 156 and Corporate Income Tax Act Article 156-3 impose direct liability on the payer; Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 6, concerning false statements in withholding declarations, carries imprisonment up to five years or fines; the DTA conduit claim is void and full WHT becomes immediately assessable.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute the false treaty documentation are personally liable as withholding agents and may face criminal imprisonment and fines. The corporate veil may be pierced if the dividend or royalty payment stripped the company of assets while leaving liabilities unpaid; personal civil restitution, blacklisting, travel restrictions, and seizure of dual-citizenship assets can follow.
Shell Company / AML and Maritime Licensing Violations

Statutory sanction: Specific Financial Transactions Information Act Article 5-2 penalizes willful concealment and reporting violations with imprisonment up to five years and fines up to KRW 50 million; Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Article 32 imposes administrative penalties up to 50% of the unreported transaction value; Korean Maritime Safety Act licensing false declarations trigger license revocation, suspension, and monetary fines.

Director personal liability: The de facto director risks criminal asset forfeiture under the Act on Regulation and Punishment of Concealment of Criminal Proceeds, imprisonment up to seven years for laundering escrowed charter payments, and personal civil liability for maritime torts.

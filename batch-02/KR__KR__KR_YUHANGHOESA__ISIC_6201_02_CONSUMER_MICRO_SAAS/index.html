None. The Korean Commercial Act imposes no minimum paid-in capital for a yuhan hoesa and does not require blocked escrow, bank guarantee, or capital deposit with a public authority. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS) - official central tax administration; electronic registration, VAT filing, and e-invoice portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

Under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes, a foreign non-resident entity or a newly formed domestic Yuhan Hoesa LLC must obtain a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which is used as both the statutory TIN and the VAT ID. For a foreign non-resident supplier of digital services with no Korean place of business, registration is executed exclusively through the NTS HomeTax portal using the foreign/overseas business operator electronic-services VAT registration workflow. The procedure is as follows: (1) prepare the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation or equivalent public registry extract, the foreign tax identification certificate, a board resolution designating a Korean licensed tax agent if in-Korea procedural representation is required, the executed micro-SaaS subscription/merchant-of-record agreement with Paddle or Lemon Squeezy, and bank/payment-processor evidence showing no Korean fixed establishment; (2) complete the electronic Business Registration Application via HomeTax and state the date Korean taxable activity commenced; (3) upload apostilled or notarized corporate documents, foreign TIN evidence, ownership details, and the identification of the ultimate beneficial owner; (4) await NTS risk review and issuance of the Business Registration Certificate; a physical visit is normally not required for non-resident electronic service suppliers, but a Korean bank account or a licensed tax agent may be necessary for effective VAT refund settlement; (5) use the issued 10-digit registration number for all subsequent VAT returns, e-tax invoice issuance, withholding compliance, and HomeTax filings. For a Yuhan Hoesa LLC formed domestically, the same registration is made at the district tax office having jurisdiction over its Korean registered address, with the incorporation certificate and business premise lease evidence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Yuhan Hoesa LLC is a domestic legal entity under the Korean Commercial Act and is not fiscally transparent. It is taxed at the entity level on its worldwide income under the Corporate Tax Act, with progressive corporate income tax rates of 9%-24% plus a local income tax surcharge of 4% of the corporate income tax. For VAT purposes it is mandatorily classified as a general taxpayer because corporations are not eligible for simplified-taxpayer status. No pass-through or partnership classification is available to the Korean LLC shareholders.

Available election forms

None with respect to entity tax classification. The Republic of Korea does not operate an elective check-the-box regime, and a Yuhan Hoesa LLC cannot elect to be treated as transparent for income tax or VAT. There are no hybrid mismatch elections available for a domestic consumer micro-SaaS company. The only relevant elections are administrative: monthly or semi-annual VAT filing timing as prescribed by NTS for the relevant VAT period, and the choice of e-invoice issuance method through HomeTax; these do not alter the default entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 (zero) for a Yuhan Hoesa LLC and for foreign non-resident electronic service providers. A Korean corporate entity is required to register as a VAT general taxpayer from its first taxable supply; there is no economic turnover exemption or SME registration threshold for corporations. Individual/simplified taxpayers may enjoy certain turnover-based simplifications, but the corporate micro-SaaS entity cannot benefit from such treatment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for export of goods applies when goods are physically delivered outside Korea and the export declaration is accepted by the Korea Customs Service. For digital services such as consumer micro-SaaS subscriptions, zero-rating applies to supplies that are exported services i.e., services actually used or consumed wholly outside Korea by a non-resident customer, as established under the Value-Added Tax Act export-services rules and place-of-supply rules. To qualify, the Korean entity must demonstrate: (i) the customer is a non-resident with a foreign billing address and, if a business, a foreign corporate registration or VAT ID; (ii) the SaaS is not used or consumed within Korea, evidenced by IP/geolocation logs and account onboarding records; (iii) consideration is received in a foreign currency from a foreign source; and (iv) the commercial contract clearly identifies the foreign customer and the tax treatment as a zero-rated export of services. Korean-resident end customers are always subject to Korean VAT at 10%. When Paddle or Lemon Squeezy serves as merchant of record, the Korean exporter must retain the underlying merchant-of-record agreements, settlement reports, refund ledgers, and corresponding export evidence to substantiate any Korean zero-rating claim.

  • For physical exports: KCS Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry/SAD), the Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, commercial invoice, packing list, and KCS validation records. For digital SaaS exports, no physical customs declaration is generated; the NTS accepts a zero-rated electronic tax invoice plus the service contract and platform settlement records as the export substantiation bundle.
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence: Korean foreign-exchange bank Foreign Exchange Purchase Confirmation / Certificate of Acquisition of Foreign Exchange, overseas customer or payment-processor remittance advice, and Paddle/Lemon Squeezy settlement ledger entries showing receipt of foreign currency within the repatriation window.
  • Non-residence and use evidence: signed subscription order or SaaS terms acceptance, foreign billing address, non-Korean IP/geolocation logs, customer tax identification or VAT registration certificate for B2B customers, and any server/usage records demonstrating consumption outside Korea.
  • Commercial contract and merchant-of-record file: master SaaS agreement, order form, applicable platform merchant-of-record agreement, refund/chargeback ledger, and payment processor business reports used to trace each consumer subscription to the zero-rated export claim.

All VAT invoices issued by a registered Yuhan Hoesa LLC must be electronic tax invoices unless a specific statutory exception applies. The NTS operates the HomeTax electronic tax invoice system (e-Sero platform) and requires transmission of an electronic tax invoice to the NTS no later than the day following the date of issuance. For recurring micro-SaaS subscriptions, an aggregated electronic tax invoice may be issued per customer for the applicable tax period under NTS guidance. Large-volume issuers may integrate through NTS-open API connections using XML or JSON payloads for direct e-invoice clearance. There is no separate state-level sales-and-use tax regime in Korea, and no blockchain fiscal stamping is imposed on digital SaaS supplies, but NTS anti-fraud review is supported by real-time e-invoice submission, matching of e-invoices to VAT returns and bank/payment-processor settlement data, and mandatory reporting of zero-rated export invoices.

Zero-rated export claims are high-risk audit items for the NTS. The NTS may compel production of the zero-rated e-tax invoice, customer contracts, foreign exchange purchase confirmations, bank statements, payment processor settlement reports, and IP/usage logs. If the zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply is reclassified as taxable at 10% and the NTS will assess the unpaid VAT together with interest and additional tax penalties. General unfaithful underreporting may trigger additional tax of approximately 10%-20% of the underpaid VAT, while fraudulent or collusive misrepresentation can trigger a 40% additional tax penalty. Failure to issue or transmit an electronic tax invoice may trigger an additional tax equal to 10% of the supply value, and issuance of a false tax invoice may trigger up to 20% of the supply value. Any input VAT refund previously paid to the zero-rated exporter may be clawed back retroactively with statutory interest, and the statute of limitations for fraudulent evasion is extended up to 10 years. Criminal referral and prosecution for tax evasion are possible where false export documents, fabricated foreign-use evidence, or sham merchant-of-record arrangements are used to obtain improper zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a liberalised but monitored foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account transactions, including subscription revenues from the Micro SaaS business and ordinary vendor payments, are freely convertible without advance licence approval. Capital account transactions, such as inbound equity investment, foreign currency borrowing, and certain cross-border transfers of money belonging to non-residents, are reportable to the designated foreign exchange bank and recorded by the Bank of Korea. In practice, an incorporation capital remittance from an overseas shareholder is remitted in foreign currency, converted in the domestic market, and verified with a Foreign Investment Report Certificate before the entity can move the funds from the capital escrow account to an operating account. Profit remittance is allowed once distributable reserves are evidenced by financial statements and tax payment certificates; the bank will then capture a foreign exchange receipt and issue a foreign exchange settlement certificate. The main hurdles are documentary rather than administrative: the bank expects board minutes, annual financial statements, shareholder resolution, tax clearance, and a CRS evidence pack. There is no direct central bank licensing step for routine dividend remittance, but any failure to submit the foreign exchange transaction report carries sanctions, so the company must create a full audit trail.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must normally be collected within 90 days from the shipment date, extendable to 180 days with the foreign exchange bank's approval when the underlying trade allows deferred settlement. The exporter must direct the counterparty to remit into a domestic foreign exchange bank account, either as a foreign currency deposit or a KRW settlement. Korea does not apply a fixed percentage surrender ratio; the bank treats the full 100% of proceeds as subject to the export collection declaration, and any portion not retained in the permitted foreign currency account is converted into won at the interbank exchange rate. For the Micro SaaS entity, subscription fees collected through global payment rails are treated as software exports or service revenue, so the underlying merchant net settlements must be supported by invoices, licence agreements, customer logs, and payout statements to satisfy both the tax authority and the foreign exchange bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (the central bank) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs are not viable as the primary treasury bank for the Korean entity. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Korean Banking Act, only licensed domestic foreign exchange banks may receive export proceeds, open corporate won accounts, and perform statutory foreign exchange reporting. Global EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Airwallex do not hold a Korean foreign exchange bank licence and cannot provide a regulatory-capable KRW corporate IBAN or issue capital verification documentation to KOTRA and the tax office. The target subscription rails Paddle and Lemon Squeezy may collect consumer subscription revenues, but those processors must remit net proceeds through the domestic Korean bank account nominated by the entity, or through a settlement bank that supports the necessary reporting code. The fintech products may be used only as auxiliary merchant information dashboards, never as an account holding treasury of foreign-invested Korean capital.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File and Master File are mandatorily required when the aggregate amount of covered intercompany transactions with related parties during the fiscal year reaches or exceeds KRW 50 billion, or when the related-party transaction volume with an individual foreign related party is at or above the statutory sub-threshold of approximately KRW 5 billion; the CbCR requirement is triggered for a multinational group with annual consolidated revenue of KRW 1 trillion or more. Every taxpayer engaging in cross-border related-party transactions must nevertheless complete the annual report of international transactions and be prepared to produce TP documentation on request.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not have a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the substance-over-form doctrine in Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, the GAAR in Article 17, and the Controlled Foreign Corporation rules in Articles 61 through 65 of the International Tax Coordination Act require that the Korean Yuhan Hoesa perform real Core Income-Generating Activities, including software development, subscription pricing, contract execution, customer support, payment reconciliation, and risk management. The mind and management of the Korean entity must not be displaced to an offshore shell; if a non-resident parent or related party has no office, employees, or business decision-making relevant to the income, NTS may recharacterize the arrangement, attribute the profits to the Korean entity, and impose interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion and Intentional Revenue Omission

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Articles 3 and 3-2: imprisonment for up to two years or a fine up to twice the amount of tax evaded, in addition to crediting of unpaid national tax, local income tax surtax, unfaithful return penalties, and statutory delinquency interest under the Corporate Tax Act and Framework Act on National Taxes.

Director personal liability: The representative director and the directors who signed or caused the false return are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax; the NTS may seize personal bank accounts and assets under tax collection procedures, impose an overseas departure or travel ban for substantial arrears, publicly blacklist the delinquent taxpayer, and seek criminal imprisonment personally against the responsible director.
Transfer Pricing Abuse and Excessive Offshore Royalty or Fee Extraction

Statutory sanction: Article 4 of the International Tax Coordination Act permits the NTS to reassess the Korean taxpayer on an arm's length price; Article 12 imposes penalty surcharges for non-compliance with offshore transaction reporting and documentation; the deduction may be disallowed under the Corporate Tax Act, additional WHT may be assessed on deemed royalties, and the amended tax plus interest and penalties is collected.

Director personal liability: Directors are required to prove the functional and risk profile of the Korean entity; where false intercompany documentation has been executed, the veil can be pierced for fraud, the director's personal assets can be pursued through secondary liability rules, and the responsible directors face civil restitution, travel restrictions, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent tax evasion.
Shell Company, CFC Avoidance, and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Article 17 of the Framework Act on National Taxes permits recharacterization of the abusive transaction; the CFC rules in Articles 61 through 65 of the International Tax Coordination Act attribute the undistributed income of the controlled foreign company to the Korean controlling shareholder; the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may also apply, and the AML legislation, including the Reporting and Use of Specified Financial Transaction Information Act, may impose fines on financial intermediaries that process payments while concealing the beneficial owner.

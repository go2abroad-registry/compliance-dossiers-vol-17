Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) under the Korean Commercial Act after the 2012 amendment. The only capital requirement is that the full amount of each quota (지분) as stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be paid in cash into the company's temporarily established depository bank account before or at the time of incorporation registration. The bank must issue a certificate of capital deposit, which is filed with the registry. There is no governmental blocked escrow account requirement beyond this ordinary deposit; capital becomes immediately usable after registration, subject to the Capital Regulation Rules of the Commercial Act prohibiting payment-in-kind unless certain statutory asset valuation reports are obtained.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa is treated as a per se corporation under the Korean Commercial Act and is taxed under the Corporation Tax Act at entity level on its worldwide income subject to foreign tax credit relief. Korea does not provide a US-style check-the-box pass-through election for domestic limited liability companies. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under the International Tax Adjustment Act, controlled foreign company rules, and thin capitalization rules may apply to cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary VAT registration threshold applies in Korea. Any business person with a domestic business establishment must apply for VAT registration before making taxable supplies. A simplified taxable person regime exists for businesses with annual taxable supplies below KRW 80,000,000, but registration itself remains mandatory; this turnover threshold affects VAT calculation and reporting only.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, 0% VAT applies to goods that are physically exported from Korea and cleared by Korea Customs Service under an export declaration. For services, 0% VAT applies to supplies made from a Korean place of business to a non-resident or foreign enterprise where the consideration is received in foreign currency and the service is not consumed in Korea; this includes qualifying export of gaming anti-cheat middleware, software licenses, updates, and integrity service subscriptions supplied under a cross-border contract to an overseas game developer. The Korean entity must substantiate that the recipient is a non-resident, the payment is received in convertible foreign currency, the contractual risk and benefit sit outside Korea, and the digital service is actually delivered to and used overseas. If the service is deemed consumed in Korea or connected to the foreign entity's Korean business establishment, standard 10% VAT applies instead.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration submitted and cleared through UNI-PASS, evidencing physical movement of goods out of Korea; for electronically delivered anti-cheat engine services, the retained file must contain the system export service evidence because no customs entry is issued.
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing removal of exported goods from Korea to the overseas destination.
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract identifying the non-resident purchaser, the ISIC 6201 anti-cheat middleware license, service period, quantity, unit prices, and stated consideration in foreign currency.
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank, functionally equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), showing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency in the Korean entity's foreign exchange account.
  • Zero-rated VAT electronic tax invoice and electronic service delivery or license usage logs demonstrating that the gaming security middleware was effectively delivered and consumed outside Korean territory.

All VAT taxpayers in Korea, including foreign-owned domestic entities, must issue electronic tax invoices through National Tax Service HomeTax or through an NTS-certified electronic tax invoice issuance service provider. Electronic tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS by the statutory deadline, ordinarily on the day following the invoice issue date or within the aggregation period permitted under the Value-Added Tax Act. The NTS receives e-tax invoice data through secure API, EDI, and web-service channels in HomeTax and cross-matches such data with VAT returns, corporate tax filings, foreign exchange settlement records, and Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declarations. Korea does not operate a Latin American style real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory anti-fraud digital stamping system, but the NTS applies automated matching, risk scoring, and audit selection to detect false invoices and mismatched export zero-rating claims. Taxpayers may use the NTS public API or certified commercial issuance platforms and must retain the original electronic records for the statutory retention period.

Korea Customs Service performs post-clearance export audits while the NTS conducts VAT audits by matching electronic tax invoices, UNI-PASS export declarations, corporate income records, and foreign exchange settlement data. If export proceeds are not repatriated within the required foreign exchange settlement period or the zero-rating documentation is missing, contradictory, or forged, the NTS will reclassify the supply as domestic taxable, reverse any input VAT deducted or refunded, and re-assess VAT at the standard 10% headline rate on the export value. Additional tax and unjustified refund penalties are imposed under the Framework Act on National Taxes; where the KCS export declaration or tax invoice is deliberately falsified, the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act and Customs Act penalties apply, including criminal prosecution, imprisonment, fines, and publication of the tax offense. Exporters should also expect clawback of refunded VAT together with interest and surcharges if the supporting customs and foreign exchange documents are not retained for the statutory audit period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a formally liberalised but supervisory-driven FX regime. Current-account transactions are fully convertible and do not require individual central bank permission; payments for software licensing, middleware subscriptions, royalties and cross-border business services qualify as current-account invisibles and may be remitted freely subject to Supporting Document Rules. Capital-account transactions, however, are subject to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act's quarantine principle: inbound equity injections, loans, acquisitions of foreign securities, and foreign-currency borrowings must be filed with a designated foreign exchange bank or, in large or exceptional cases, reported to the Bank of Korea or MOEF. The won's onshore exchange rate is market-determined in the Seoul Foreign Exchange Market, but the Korean Financial Supervisory Service and BOK impose macroprudential levies on short-term external debt and may exercise moral suasion during episodic volatility. For a Korean LLC owned by non-residents, profit remittance and liquidation proceeds are treated as current-account outbound transfers if backed by audited financial statements and tax payment certificates; however, any dividend distribution exceeding the bank's ordinary de minimis threshold triggers enhanced beneficiary-title verification. There is no forced surrender of export proceeds, but all foreign-currency receipts must be received through a designated foreign exchange bank account and supported by a customs or service export with an assigned transaction classification code. Repatriation of capital and exit proceeds may be blocked temporally if the outflow involves unresolved tax clearance, undisclosed bearer instruments, or mismatches between the capital contribution records and the Foreign Currency Acquisition Certificate. In practice, stringent identification of the ultimate economic beneficiary is the dominant friction, not exchange control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds arising from goods or services must be collected within one year from the export declaration date or the service-rendering date, unless an extension is approved by the designated foreign exchange bank. No mandatory conversion ratio applies: the Korean regime permits 100% foreign-currency retention in an onshore foreign-currency account, so the LLC may hold KRW or USD at its discretion. The exporter must submit a received-export-proceeds report to the designated bank within 10 business days of deposit when the proceeds are not in the precise invoiced currency or when certain high-risk settlement patterns are triggered, and the bank is obliged to transmit data to the Bank of Korea for surveillance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable as a primary banking infrastructure. South Korea's Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act require any entity holding customer funds or executing cross-border payments to hold a domestic banking licence or a licensed e-money/prepaid issuer licence from the Financial Services Commission (FSC). Offshore digital EMI operators such as Airwallex, Wise Business, Revolut Business or Payoneer are not recognised as designated foreign exchange banks and cannot issue Korean IBFs, KRW settlement accounts, or foreign-currency accounts for a Korean-domiciled legal entity. They may only be used as a complementary collection rail from overseas customers, but any KRW settlement into the Korean entity's tax registration must flow through a Tier-1 onshore bank. Attempting to route export revenues through an unlicensed EMI would create a foreign-currency acquisition violation, a VAT filing mismatch, and a criminal exposure under foreign exchange regulation. Therefore, treasury operations must be anchored in a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank with branch-level foreign exchange dealing capabilities.

Offshore digital EMI operators such as Airwallex, Wise Business, Revolut Business or Payoneer are not recognised as designated foreign exchange banks and cannot issue Korean IBFs, KRW settlement accounts, or foreign-currency accounts for a Korean-domiciled legal entity. They may only be used as a complementary collection rail from overseas customers, but any KRW settlement into the Korean entity's tax registration must flow through a Tier-1 onshore bank. Attempting to route export revenues through an unlicensed EMI would create a foreign-currency acquisition violation, a VAT filing mismatch, and a criminal exposure under foreign exchange regulation. Therefore, treasury operations must be anchored in a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank with branch-level foreign exchange dealing capabilities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Criminal Fraud

Statutory sanction: Violation of Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act; imprisonment for up to 2 years or a fine of up to 3 times the tax evaded, in addition to the 40% unfaithful-return civil penalty and statutory daily interest on all unpaid national and local corporate income taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or knowingly permit fraudulent evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax through piercing of the corporate veil, may have personal bank and real estate assets seized by the NTS, may suffer a travel ban and tax-delinquent blacklisting, and may receive a criminal prison sentence of up to two years or more for aggravated evasion.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income under Article 12 of the International Tax Adjustment Act; additional tax of 10% of the adjusted understated income (up to 20% for fraudulent or grossly negligent noncompliance), plus penalties for failure to file the international related-party transaction statement and documentation (which can be assessed separately under the Act and are nondeductible for corporate income tax).

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically personally liable for transfer pricing adjustments, but the tax authority may pierce the veil when intercompany transactions are transacted fraudulently or have no commercial substance. In such cases directors may be liable for civil damages and jointly liable for the tax and surcharges; persistent deliberate noncompliance can lead to criminal indictment, travel bans, and blacklisting as a serious tax delinquent.
Shell Company, Treaty Abuse, and Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Application of the Korean general anti-abuse rule (Framework Act for National Taxes) and the beneficial ownership / limitation-on-benefits provisions of the relevant tax treaty to deny treaty reduction or exemption; re-characterization of transactions, disallowance of deductions, and an additional 30% or more of the tax avoided may be imposed. If the shell is used to move illegal proceeds, the AML regulatory fine can reach KRW 100 million and tax fraud penalties may be added.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the shell is a mere conduit; directors may be personally ordered to restore assets and pay avoided tax, subject to asset attachment and departure bans.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital; the articles may validly fix capital as low as KRW 1. No blocked escrow account is required by Korean company law. For a non-resident foreign investor, the subscribed contribution must be remitted through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank, and the bank's deposit confirmation or balance certificate naming the proposed company or the incorporator's dedicated subscription account must be preserved as evidence of full payment-in before court registration.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable to the digital SaaS company formation, but general office compliance is mandatory: the leased office must have valid fire-fighting and evacuation certification under the Fire Services Act, the building owner must maintain a multiple-occupancy building fire safety certificate, and the company must comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act for office employees. No civil defense permit, chemical safety clearance, or structural load inspection is required because the company occupies standard office space and does not operate warehouses or industrial premises.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: Hometax (hometax.go.kr). Customs export filings: Korea Customs Service (KCS) via UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity with no domestic place of business that supplies taxable electronic/digital services in Korea, file a simplified VAT registration application through Hometax or with the competent district tax office through a designated Korean tax representative. Attach certificate of incorporation or foreign registration certificate, articles of association, board resolution appointing the tax representative, power of attorney, identity documents, proof of foreign address, and bank account details; authenticate foreign documents by apostille/consular legalization and Korean translation. Upon approval NTS issues a simplified VAT registration number with a leading 0 prefix. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law has no check-the-box election; a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) is mandatorily classified as a domestic corporation subject to entity-level corporate tax under the Corporate Tax Act. No hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary exemption threshold for LLCs; registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply. An optional simplified taxpayer regime applies only to individual small businesses with annual turnover below KRW 80,000,000. For non-resident suppliers of electronic/digital services to Korean consumers, simplified VAT registration is mandatory without de minimis turnover exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to goods physically exported from Korea after acceptance of an export declaration by Korea Customs Service, with title/risk transferred offshore and foreign exchange proceeds collected within the statutory settlement period. Qualifying services such as fleet telematics SaaS are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident customer for use wholly outside Korea; the supplier must demonstrate through contracts, technical logs, IP/user evidence, and billing files that the services were not consumed in Korea.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (UNI-PASS export clearance certificate)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Korea
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching export declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency

Mandatory electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) must be issued through Hometax or an NTS-approved ASP within one day after each supply, with real-time or near-real-time transmission to NTS using official API or web service. Export invoices must be reported electronically as zero-rated supplies with customs declaration numbers linked to UNI-PASS data. No physical anti-fraud stamp is required; NTS uses e-invoice database matching, cash receipt reporting for consumer sales above KRW 10,000, and trade data reconciliation to detect fraud.

NTS and KCS jointly scrutinize zero-rated export records. If export evidence is false or insufficient, zero-rating is denied; NTS assesses 10% VAT on the supply, adds 40% penalty tax for fraudulent underreporting or 20% for negligent underreporting plus daily interest. Criminal liability may be pursued under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, and false customs declarations are separately sanctioned by Korea Customs Service under the Customs Act, including imprisonment and fines.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea applies a liberalised current-account convertibility regime for residents, meaning that trade-related receipts and payments in foreign currency can generally move through authorised foreign exchange banks after routine documentary verification. The Korean won is convertible for genuine export earnings, service fees, and operational remittances, but capital and financial account transactions, such as foreign direct investment, offshore borrowings, equity subscriptions, and acquisition of foreign financial assets, are not fully frei without prior registration or post-transaction reporting. Inbound capital under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act is routed through Invest KOREA and the designated foreign exchange bank, where the foreign investor obtains a foreign investment registration certificate and the domestic entity receives a capital verification report. Profit repatriation in the form of dividends or liquidation distributions is legally permissible once the foreign capital has been fully registered and the outgoing remittance is supported by an audit-confirmed distributable profit computation, sufficient proof of paid-in capital, and the bank's foreign exchange transaction declaration. Practical hurdles still exist because each conversion and outward remittance must be linked to an original source document, the company's frequently used foreign exchange bank must certify the transaction, and incomplete customer due diligence dossiers, missing source-of-wealth narratives, or inconsistent UBO structures will slow approval and may trigger deferral of the remittance until the bank's anti-money laundering committee is satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its enforcement decree, export proceeds from goods or cross-border services, including logistics software subscriptions and telematics licensing fees, must be collected within one year from the date of the export declaration or the date of service completion, unless the authorised foreign exchange bank approves an extension for long-term contracts or staged deliveries. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio requiring the surrender of export proceeds into Korean won; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a dwelled foreign exchange account or convert it to KRW at the agreed bank rate. However, 100% of the proceeds must flow through an institution licensed in Korea to handle foreign exchange, and any single transaction exceeding the equivalent of USD 50,000 must be reported by the bank to the Bank of Korea through the electronic reporting system within three business days of settlement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting as the central bank and delegated foreign exchange administrator, with supervisory powers exercised jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance, the Financial Services Commission, and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion aggregate intercompany transaction value triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; a consolidated group revenue of KRW 100 billion or more also requires Master File; Country-by-Country Report threshold is KRW 400 billion consolidated revenue. Reportable international transaction Schedule 30 disclosure applies when intercompany transactions exceed KRW 500 million.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulation like common law free zone jurisdictions. Instead, the Corporate Tax Act applies the 'head office/place of effective management' test to determine domestic corporate status. For the Korean LLC to be treated as fully taxable in Korea, strategic management, the board of directors' meetings, treasury decisions, and core income-generating activities (CIGA) for software development, fleet data analytics, contract negotiation, and risk management must actually be performed in Korea. A pass-through Yuhan Hoesa with no locally maintained employees, no premises, or no operative substance will be challenged on substance-over-form doctrine, its payments to offshore affiliates will be disallowed, and CFC rules may attribute profits to Korean controlling shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Abusive Royalty/Gross Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act Articles 15, 26, 35 and Punishment of Tax Evasion Act Article 3: unlawful tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to 30 times the evaded tax; tax adjustment assessment with a 40% fraudulent understatement penalty under CIT Act Article 66, plus daily delinquency interest.

Director personal liability: The Korean Commercial Act Article 401(3) permits creditors and tax authorities to pierce the corporate veil and demand personal compensation from directors who knowingly authorized false expenses. The director may be named a 'representative taxpayer' under the National Tax Collection Act and subject to asset seizure, travel restriction (overseas departure ban or blacklisting), and criminal indictment for joint principal tax evasion with exposure to imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Omission of Cross-Border Disclosure

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Article 12-2 and Article 12-13 authorize the NTS to reject the reported price and estimate a commensurate arm's-length income; a 30% additional tax and up to KRW 100 million penalty may be imposed for deliberate non-submission. If the intercompany transaction is a financing arrangement, thin-capitalization penalties of 20% of deemed under-distribution apply.

Director personal liability: Director or representative who knowingly fails to disclose cross-border transactions is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate income tax plus surcharges; NTS may revoke the company's business registration, enforce a departure ban on the director, and pursue criminal penalties under the Act on Aggravated Punishment for Specific Economic Offences if the scale of tax under-reporting exceeds KRW 100 million, with imprisonment of up to 10 years.
Shell Company and AML/Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Article 17 and Financial Information Act (AML) impose administrative fines and criminal punishment of up to 3 years' imprisonment for unregistered beneficial ownership and false reporting. The withholding agent is liable for the 22% WHT not withheld, plus an additional tax of up to 50% under the Corporate Tax Act Article 67-2 for failure to withhold tax.

None. The Commercial Act imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a yuhan hoesa. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea; online portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity, the procedure to obtain a statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is to apply for a Business Registration Number (BRN) with the NTS. The application can be submitted online via the HomeTax portal or in person at the local district tax office. Required documents include: certificate of incorporation, articles of association, passport of the legal representative, and a power of attorney if applicable. The BRN serves as the TIN for both corporate income tax and VAT purposes. For foreign suppliers of electronic services (including SaaS), a simplified registration process is available via HomeTax, requiring submission of the entity's details and appointment of a local tax representative if no permanent establishment exists in Korea.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated if the goods are physically exported from Korea and evidenced by a customs export declaration. Services (including digital services) are zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident and the benefit is enjoyed outside Korea. For SEO analytics SaaS, the supplier must retain evidence that the customer is a non-resident and that the service is not used in Korea. The zero-rating applies to B2B and B2C supplies to non-residents, provided the service is not effectively connected with a permanent establishment in Korea.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or Foreign Exchange Bank Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Contract or Purchase Order

All B2B transactions require electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoice) issued and transmitted to the NTS via the HomeTax system within the prescribed deadline (by the 10th of the following month). For B2C transactions, cash receipts or electronic sales information must be reported. Foreign suppliers must also issue e-Tax Invoices for B2B sales to Korean customers. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, but the NTS performs automated cross-checking of invoices. Anti-fraud measures include digital stamping and encryption of e-Tax Invoices.

If zero-rating is claimed without proper documentation or if foreign exchange is not repatriated within 180 days, the NTS may impose additional VAT, interest, and penalties up to 40% of the tax amount. Fraudulent claims may result in criminal prosecution and fines up to KRW 100 million.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won (KRW) is classified as a fully convertible currency for current account transactions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with the overwhelming majority of trade-related settlements free from quantitative restrictions. Capital account transactions, however, remain subject to a monitored liberalization regime: foreign direct investment, loans, and issuance of securities must be reported through designated foreign exchange banks; prior filing or approval is required for certain categories. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds into KRW; exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic foreign currency accounts without conversion obligation. Profit remittance, including dividends and royalties, is generally permissible provided that the underlying transaction is evidenced and tax compliance verified (with certificate of withholding taxes). The MOEF and BOK impose ex-post reporting and statistical collection requirements rather than exchange controls, but deviations from the declared business purpose or trade documentation can trigger immediate on-site inspections and asset freeze action under the criminal provisions of FETA. For a foreign-invested LLC engaged in SEO content analytics, the most material hurdles are not convertibility limits but documentary strictness in capital verification, the five-year foreign exchange reporting history required of the designated bank, and mandatory electronic submission through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Given the firm's MarTech SaaS nature and expected outbound licensing revenue, the classification of platform-based service fees as either goods or services can materially alter the surrender timeline and the procurement of an export certificate from the Korea Customs Service.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 12 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds for general merchandise and digital services must be received within 6 months from the export declaration date; the deadline may be extended by the designated foreign exchange bank if the exporter demonstrates justifiable grounds (e.g., deferred payment terms) for up to an additional 6 months. There is no mandatory statutory conversion ratio to KRW; all export receipts may be held in foreign currency accounts at domestic banks without surrender, but the exporter must file a transaction statement with the designated bank and report the inward remittance within 30 days. For digital content analytics subscriptions treated as cross-border provision of services, exporters are expected to maintain an export invoice with a Korea Origin Certificate or a digital service contract to prove the underlying real transaction and avoid misclassification penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the monetary authority, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) which exercises statutory administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Overseas fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex are legally barred from acting as full-service deposit-taking or settlement banks in Korea because they do not hold a banking license under the Banking Act nor a crypto- or electronic-money license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. These EMIs cannot provide a domestic KRW IBAN that is cleared through Korea's financial system (KOFI or J-Inet) nor can they accept inbound wire transfers intended for tax compliance under FIPA. At best, an offshore EMI can be used as a non-Korean, segregated collection vehicle outside Korea for foreign customers; however, remittance of those proceeds into the Korean LLC will still trigger the same FX reporting requirements and designated-bank routing. Therefore, the entity must maintain its primary settlement and treasury accounts at a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. The strategic recommendation is to use Stripe and Paddle as upstream billing portals, but to auto-route daily settlements via API into a Woori or Shinhan KRW operational account, thereby preserving the valid bank channel for tax and FX compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions for a foreign-invested Yuhan Hoesa must be routed through a designated foreign exchange bank (DFEB) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and reported to the MOEF (practically delegated to applicable ministries). The procedure is: (a) the foreign investor reports the proposed foreign direct investment to the MOEF before fund transfer via the FEIS system, specifying the FIPA purpose code 'CAPITAL_INVEST_IN_KOREA'; (b) the investor transfers the capital from its offshore bank account to the DFEB's foreign currency account opened in the name of the Korean LLC, denominated in USD or KRW, using SWIFT MT103/202. The remittance must include a precise narrative with the taxpayer identification number (KR LLC business registration number) and the reference to the FDI report registration number. Upon receipt, the DFEB issues a Foreign Currency Inward Remittance Certificate and converts the funds to KRW at the inter-bank spot rate as per instruction. The capital verification certificate is generated by the DFEB and must be registered with the court registry of the LLC within two months; after verification, the funds are freed from escrow and can be transferred to the operational KRW account. Inbound payment for working capital other than FDI (e.g., shareholder loans) requires separate notification to the MOEF under the 'borrowing' section; the total outstanding loan is subject to a 50 million KRW minimum threshold before approval. For the SEO analytics tool, dividend repatriation will follow the same rail with the route 'SECURITIES_CAPITAL_GAINS_DIVIDEND_DOMESTIC_REMITTANCE' and must be accompanied by a certified statement of distributable profits from the external auditor.For outbound disbursements to global SaaS vendors, subcontractors, and advertising partners, the preferred clearing rails in Korea are (1) SWIFT MT103 for straightforward vendor payments and subscription fees, settled through the DFEB with the remittance purpose code 'PAYMENT_FOR_SERVICES' (click-stream data procurement, cloud computing costs) and 10% domestic VAT withholding consideration where applicable; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance) for any hardware or dedicated server purchases from overseas dealers, valid under the UCP 600 rules issued by the Korean bank and typically requiring a pro forma invoice, transport document, and compliance certificate; (3) Documentary Collections (CAD / D/P) for pragmatic settlements with emerging-market partners in the content analytics ecosystem where contracts are established but L/C costs are unjustifiable; (4) Cross-border payroll and contractor payments via standard bank transfers with the purpose code 'LABOR_COST_ABROAD_PAYMENT', supported by employment contracts and evidence of tax residence (W-8BEN / CNI / NRIC). All outward payments must be recorded in the FEIS and the bank's periodic reporting obligation to the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit will trigger additional scrutiny if the beneficiary is in a financially lenient or sanctioned jurisdiction. It is imperative to maintain a full reconciliation subsystem inside the enterprise resource planning software that maps each outgoing transfer to the corresponding inbound revenue stream, thereby proving that the movement of funds is aligned with the firm's declared business plan and is not a disguised currency swap.Onshore KRW/USD Forward FX Contracts with a licensed designated foreign exchange bank, restricted to hedging 120% of the underlying trade receivables evidenced by a valid purchase order or contract.FX Options (European style, call/put) on KRW/USD, permissible for hedging anticipated subscription revenue cash flows, with the notional amount capped at the firm's average four-quarter projected revenue reported to the tax office.Cross-Currency Swaps only for larger capital financing / loan rollovers; permissible where the swap tenor matches the underlying FX loan and all payments are run through the firm's DFEB.Offshore Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are not permitted for onshore Korean companies under the FETA except when the transaction has an underlying real capital-account exposure approved by the MOEF; therefore, the entity should enter only onshore deliverable forwards and avoid accessing the NDF market, as unauthorized NDF use may constitute a criminal act and invalidate the hedging protection.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Overseas fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex are legally barred from acting as full-service deposit-taking or settlement banks in Korea because they do not hold a banking license under the Banking Act nor a crypto- or electronic-money license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. These EMIs cannot provide a domestic KRW IBAN that is cleared through Korea's financial system (KOFI or J-Inet) nor can they accept inbound wire transfers intended for tax compliance under FIPA. At best, an offshore EMI can be used as a non-Korean, segregated collection vehicle outside Korea for foreign customers; however, remittance of those proceeds into the Korean LLC will still trigger the same FX reporting requirements and designated-bank routing. Therefore, the entity must maintain its primary settlement and treasury accounts at a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. The strategic recommendation is to use Stripe and Paddle as upstream billing portals, but to auto-route daily settlements via API into a Woori or Shinhan KRW operational account, thereby preserving the valid bank channel for tax and FX compliance.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Progressive CIT: 9% up to KRW 200 million; 19% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% on KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% on cash or deemed dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, composed of 20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in cross-border related-party transactions for the same fiscal year, combined with global consolidated revenue of at least KRW 100 billion, triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation. Smaller intercompany transactions still require annual international transaction schedules and contemporaneous documentation when the amount exceeds approximately KRW 100 million per related counterparty.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the National Tax Service examines substance through the OECD general anti-avoidance rule, beneficial ownership analysis, and the place-of-effective-management test. A Yuhan Hoesa that is managed from abroad, lacks a domestic board seat, or performs no Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea may be reclassified as a foreign tax-resident or lose treaty benefits. Such a shell-corporation structure may also trigger controlled-foreign-company rules and director liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Unreported SaaS Revenue

Statutory sanction: Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding twice the evaded tax; additionally, an unfaithful tax return surcharge of 40% of the underreported amount plus 0.6% monthly delay interest.

Director personal liability: The director who participated in concealment or failed to exercise supervisory duty is jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties. Criminal conviction may result in disqualification from serving as a director, seizure of personal assets, and a travel ban imposed by the National Tax Service during investigation and collection.
Transfer Pricing and Shell Company Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment under Article 4 of the International Tax Coordination Act, with a penalty surcharge of up to 20% of the adjusted tax amount. Failure to submit a Local File or Master File is penalized per unfiled document, and a wrongfully claimed treaty benefit may be denied in full.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil when the company is a mere alter ego, making the foreign parent, shareholder, and local director personally liable for adjusted tax and penalties. Fraudulent intercompany documentation can result in criminal investigation for obstruction of tax collection, with possible imprisonment up to 5 years and a travel ban until liabilities are settled.
AML and Foreign Financial Account Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Article 16 of the Act on Reporting and Use of Specific Financial Information authorizes a penalty of up to 20% of the unreported balance and a criminal fine of up to KRW 50 million. Repeat violations can trigger aggravated imprisonment under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act when linked to tax evasion.

Director personal liability: The director is individually criminally liable if the foreign account belongs to the corporation but is controlled personally. Sanctions include confiscation of hidden funds, imprisonment up to 5 years, personal asset seizure for restitution, and placement on the immigration blacklist preventing overseas business travel until disclosure and tax liabilities are resolved.

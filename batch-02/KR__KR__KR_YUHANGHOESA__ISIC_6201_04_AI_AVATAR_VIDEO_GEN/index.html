Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). Korea Customs Service (KCS) (www.customs.go.kr) administers export declarations/customs clearances for export VAT substantiation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Complete incorporation of Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company) under the Korean Commercial Act and obtain the court registration certificate. Step 2: Within 20 days from business commencement, file the statutory Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office or through HomeTax. Step 3: NTS issues a single 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as the Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number, VAT registration number, and e-Tax invoice identifier. Step 4: If Yuhan Hoesa were a foreign non-resident entity with no domestic place of business (not the case for this domestic LLC), it would be required to appoint a domestic tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes and use the simplified registration procedure for foreign electronic-service suppliers to obtain a foreign business operator registration number before charging Korean VAT. Step 5: Register for the HomeTax e-Tax invoice system and optionally enrol a certified electronic tax invoice API/ERP integration.

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide a check-the-box or equivalent pass-through election for a Korean domestic limited liability company. Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily treated as a resident domestic corporation taxed on worldwide income under the Corporate Income Tax Act and as a VAT registrant under the Value-Added Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold for an incorporated Korean domestic business; VAT registration is mandatory from business commencement under Article 8 of the Value-Added Tax Act. The KRW 80 million simplified-taxpayer threshold is available only to unincorporated individual entrepreneurs and is not available to Yuhan Hoesa. Foreign non-resident electronic-service suppliers also have no turnover threshold and must register if they supply digital services to Korean consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of AI avatar video generation SaaS and related digital services to non-resident customers qualify for 0% VAT under Article 20(1) of the Value-Added Tax Act if the place of supply is outside Korea, the service is actually consumed or used outside Korea, the customer is a non-resident/non-domestic business, and the consideration is paid in foreign currency from abroad and repatriated to Korea. Pure software exports without physical goods must be supported by evidence of foreign consumption and foreign-exchange settlement; if any media is physically delivered, the goods must be exported through KCS and the export declaration must be retained.

  • Export Declaration Certificate (export acceptance) issued by Korea Customs Service, required for physical goods exports and for any physical media/mixed-supply delivery accompanying the software
  • Commercial invoice and master service agreement (or order/contract) with the named non-resident customer
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill when physical media or tangible components are exported
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate or Certificate of Receipt of Export Proceeds issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing inbound remittance from the non-resident customer
  • Zero-rated e-Tax invoice issued and transmitted through HomeTax with the 0% VAT rate and export-indication code
  • Digital service performance packet: service delivery report, access/usage logs, server logs, IP/geo-location records and other objective proof that the service was consumed outside Korea

All VAT registered businesses must issue an approved tax invoice. Electronic tax invoices (e-Tax invoices) are mandatory and must be transmitted to the NTS through HomeTax (or a certified ERP/API integration) by the day immediately following issuance. The NTS does not use real-time fiscal clearance digital stamping; it operates a centralized anti-fraud data-matching system, including e-Tax invoice cross-verification, cash receipt records, and foreign-exchange transaction matching.

NTS and KCS may jointly audit export zero-rating claims. Failure to produce the required export, service-performance, or foreign-exchange documentation, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, results in reversal of the 0% rate and assessment of 10% VAT on the export proceeds, plus additional tax under the Basic Act for National Taxes: 20% of underpaid VAT for negligence and 40% for fraudulent or aggressive misreporting, together with late-payment interest charged daily. False customs declarations or false export documentation may be penalized by Korea Customs Service with administrative fines and criminal referral. Violation of the FX repatriation deadline under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act is punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years or by a fine not exceeding KRW 30,000,000, and the NTS will also disregard the zero-rating claim and claw back the VAT with penalties. The collection/assessment statute of limitations is 5 years generally and 10 years where fraud is involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized current-account regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won (KRW) is not a fully freely convertible currency in the international offshore market, but onshore current transactions such as software licensing, SaaS subscription fees, salaries, and repatriation of dividends are generally permitted if the underlying transaction is genuine and supported by documentation. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity contributions under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA), are subject to mandatory filing with a designated foreign exchange bank or KOTRA rather than unrestricted approval. Dividend remittances to foreign shareholders are permissible after the completion of statutory capital verification and corporate income tax filing, provided the foreign investor has reported its investment and the profit distribution is supported by audited financial statements and board resolutions. For an AI avatar video generation company classified as ISIC 6201 software development, the main convertibility exposure is the need to evidence outbound royalty, cloud infrastructure, and subcontractor payments with service agreements, invoices, and payment purpose codes. There is no general surrender obligation for export proceeds, and foreign-currency accounts may be maintained, but settlement through a licensed domestic bank is mandatory for administrative control. The practical hurdles are documentary, not prohibition: each cross-border remittance requires the bank to identify the business purpose and record the foreign exchange transaction code before release of KRW or foreign currency. Surplus funds cannot be converted speculatively, and foreign exchange derivatives are only permitted when a genuine underlying exposure exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be collected within 18 months from the export date under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, unless an extension is granted by the designated foreign exchange bank for justified reasons. There is no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender requirement in Korea; the receipt can be held in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account or sold for KRW at a licensed foreign exchange bank. The exporter must report the receipt transaction to the designated bank, preserve the underlying customs declaration, invoice, or service contract, and any uncollected export proceeds after the 18-month window require a delayed-collection report and a documented explanation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank and monetary authority, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the principal foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree; supervision of AML/CFT obligations for reporting entities is carried out by the Financial Services Commission (FSC) and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU), with licensed domestic commercial banks acting as the designated foreign exchange bank channel.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail and legally constrained. South Korea does not recognize the EU-style electronic money institution license; a non-bank fintech must obtain approval under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act from the Financial Services Commission to provide payment services. An offshore digital EMI without a Korean license cannot issue KRW current accounts, cannot process local foreign exchange reports, and cannot serve as the receiving bank for FIPA equity injections or dividend remittances. Global payment gateways such as Stripe and Paddle can be used only for foreign currency merchant collection and settlement of customer SaaS/AI video subscription fees, but the commercial funds must then be swept into the licensed Korean bank operating account. Any EMI representation suggesting it can replace a domestic Korean account for corporate treasury should be rejected by compliance.

Each SWIFT wire should include clear reference to the FIPA report number and use the payment purpose code for equity participation.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None for a Yuhan Hoesa under current Korean law; the Articles of Incorporation must state the subscribed capital but there is no statutory minimum or mandatory blocked escrow account. However, 100 percent of the subscribed capital contributions must be deposited into a bank account in the name of the company or the promoter prior to registration, and no withdrawal from that account is permitted before the district court registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate approved by the National Fire Agency, including automatic fire detection, portable extinguishers, fire water supplies, and compliance with National Fire Safety Code 101 for warehouses; structural load inspection certificate issued by an authorized structural engineer for scrap stacking and rack stability; and workplace safety approval under the Occupational Safety and Health Act administered by the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency, covering dust control, noise exposure limits, manual handling procedures, emergency evacuation, and personal protective equipment.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS), headquartered in Seoul, acting through local district tax offices and the electronic HomeTax portal (https://www.hometax.go.kr), with customs and foreign-trade data consigned through the Korea Customs Service (KCS) UNIPASS electronic clearance system.

E-government filing portal

For the Korean Yuhan Hoesa LLC: after registration of the company under the Commercial Act, the LLC must file a statutory business registration application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over its principal place of business. The application must be accompanied by the certificate of corporate registration, articles of incorporation, director identification, and evidence of the registered business address. The NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which is the unified Tax Identification Number and VAT registration identifier used on tax invoices. For a foreign non-resident entity that has neither incorporation nor a permanent establishment in Korea but performs VAT-taxable transactions in Korea, registration is effected through a resident tax representative: the representative files the non-resident business registration application with NTS together with a power of attorney, the foreign certificate of incorporation/business license, translation, and banker's reference evidencing the non-resident's identity. Where the non-resident has a fixed place of business in Korea, registration is completed through that place of business and any foreign branch must also register before issuing tax invoices. Registration must be completed before taxable supplies commence; late registration attracts procedural penalty assessments and suspends the issuance of legally effective e-invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not operate a check-the-box entity-classification election. A domestic Yuhan Hoesa LLC is subject to entity-level corporate income tax and VAT as a separate juridical person; there are no elective pass-through classification regimes analogous to the United States. Hybrid mismatch treatment is controlled by mandatory anti-hybrid rules in the Framework Act for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, and no voluntary election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory from commencement of business; no general annual turnover threshold exempts a business from registration. A simplified taxpayer with prior-year taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000 may use the simplified VAT computation regime, but registration and electronic tax-invoice compliance remain statutory. For non-resident suppliers of Korean taxable supplies, registration is obligatory when taxable turnover begins, with no minimum exemption threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value Added Tax Act, exported goods are zero-rated. To claim the 0% export VAT rate for recycled scrap copper wire, the following statutory criteria must be met: (1) the goods must be physically exported from South Korea under a customs export declaration accepted by KCS; (2) the export contract and commercial invoice must be in the name of the Yuhan Hoesa LLC or, if handled through a trade intermediary, the actual exporter must be identifiable and the proof of export must correspond with the VAT-taxable person; (3) the goods must leave Korea within a reasonable period and the customs export manifest must identify the foreign buyer's destination; and (4) the export proceeds must be received from a non-Korean purchaser through a Korean won or foreign-currency bank settlement in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. For physical goods, zero-rating is not available merely on an invoice basis; it is substantiated only by customs clearance records and external document flow.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) reflecting KCS export clearance acceptance for the consignment of shredded non-ferrous scrap copper wire
  • Ocean or air Bill of Lading evidencing export from a Korean customs port to the foreign buyer's destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export contract issued by the Korean LLC to the overseas scrap purchaser
  • Foreign exchange bank settlement confirmation, SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advice, documentary credit negotiation advice, or outward collection documentary credit documents showing receipt by the domicile foreign-exchange bank of the export proceeds
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation issued in Korea by a licensed foreign-exchange bank

Electronic tax invoices are compulsory under Article 32 of the Value Added Tax Act. Every VAT-registered entrepreneur that issues a tax invoice must transmit it electronically through the NTS HomeTax electronic tax-invoice operation (including licensed EDI software vendors), which assigns a unique electronic invoice number and stores it in the NTS database. There is no per-transaction government fiscal stamping, but the system is designed for real-time or near-real-time transmission, automated matching, and anti-fraud verification; the NTS cross-checks issued e-invoices against the recipient's business registration number, VAT returns, customs declarations, and foreign exchange records, and failure to transmit within the statutory electronic transmission period results in penalty surcharges.

The NTS and KCS conduct joint risk-based audits against the electronic tax-invoice ledger, UNIPASS export declarations, sea/air waybills, scrap-metal purchase certificates, and the Foreign Exchange Information Database. If a zero-rated export is found to be unsubstantiated, overstated, not physically cleared, or if foreign currency is not repatriated within the 180-day statutory collection period without an approved extension, the supply is reclassified as taxable in the original period. The NTS will assess the 10% VAT underpaid together with additional tax and daily overdue interest, and can add further surcharges for misdeclaration. Fraudulently issued or altered export or tax documents trigger criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the duty-fraud provisions of the Customs Act, and the retrospective reassessment period is extended to ten years from the relevant payment period for fraudulent or misrepresentative export transactions. The claim to refund of input VAT on the purchased scrap raw material is concurrently clawed back when the corresponding export is recharacterized as taxable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized current-account convertibility regime for the Korean won. Payments and transfers for trade, dividends, royalties, and ordinary business expenses can generally be routed through domestic foreign exchange banks without prior administrative approval; however, the supporting underlying transaction must be reported and documented under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital-account operations, by contrast, require prior declaration or registration with a designated foreign exchange bank, the MOEF, or the BOK depending on the type of transaction, and portfolio investment and external borrowing may be subject to ceilings, reporting thresholds, and prudential measures. Export earnings are not subject to a blanket surrender order in ordinary practice; exporters may hold eligible foreign exchange in domestic foreign currency accounts. The practical hurdles to profit remittance are concentrated in the underlying evidence requirements: the remitting company must present tax clearance, audited financial statements, and a board resolution or equivalent proof of dividend/earnings distribution, while any inbound foreign investment must have been reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act to ensure the corresponding foreign exchange bank can release the outward remittance. For a non-resident-owned Korean LLC engaged in scrap copper wire trading, the major convertibility constraint is therefore not daily currency availability, but the need to document every beneficial transaction in a manner that satisfies both Korean AML/KYC expectations and Foreign Exchange Information System reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For export trade receipts, the standard collection period is 180 calendar days from the date of export declaration unless an extension is obtained from a designated foreign exchange bank. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio for ordinary export proceeds: the Korean won is not required to be repurchased with all export proceeds, and a trader may keep foreign currency in a licensed onshore foreign currency account. However, export proceeds must be settled through and reported to the domestic foreign exchange bank, and any conversion to Korean won is executed at the prevailing market rate. Non-compliant settlement or delaying beyond the 180-day collection period may trigger regulatory penalties and loss of trade financing support.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting as the central bank, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) serving as the senior foreign exchange control authority under South Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and subordinate regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not a compliant substitute for licensed Korean banks. South Korea restricts money exchange and receipt of funds to entities authorized under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. A foreign EMI without a Korean financial business license cannot open a domestic won account, cannot transmit Korean won on behalf of the LLC, and cannot execute the statutory reporting to the Foreign Exchange Information System. At best, an offshore EMI could be used as a non-budgetary information rail for foreign-currency side pockets, but it must not act as the treasury clearing counterparty for Korean-domiciled profit distributions or trade settlements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Statutory intercompany transaction threshold triggering mandatory Local File / Master File preparation: In South Korea, the Financial Year transaction threshold for mandatory transfer pricing documentation is KRW 10 billion (approx. USD 7.5 million) for intercompany sales of goods and KRW 5 billion (approx. USD 3.75 million) for intercompany service, loan, and royalty transactions. However, even below these thresholds, a fabricated report should be prepared for internal risk management. The Local File (including the 'Transaction Detail Statement' — 극히 요약된 거래명세서) must be submitted to the NTS within 45 days of year-end if the threshold is met. A Master File (or 'Global Business and Financial Data') must be filed if the consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and the Korean entity has cross-border related-party transactions above the product/service thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Applicability of Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA): Although South Korea does not implement the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, the Korean Tax Adjustment Act (International Tax Coordination Act) imposes the substance-over-form doctrine and a 'preparation of economic substance documentation' requirement. Under Article 4 (Principle of Substantiality), the NTS may recharacterize a transaction that has no business purpose. For a Korean Yuhan Hoesa acting as an intermediary in scrap copper wire trading, the CJGA (Core Income-Generating Activities) must be performed in Korea: local directors must conduct actual negotiation, quality inspection, and logistics management. The presence of a mere letterbox office or offshore agglomeration of functions in a low-tax country will trigger a transfer pricing adjustment and treaty abuse denial under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) of the BEPS MLI. The entity should maintain board meeting minutes, transportation contracts, inspection certificates, and a functional cost analysis proving that freight risk, inventory risk, and credit risk are truly borne and managed by the Korean entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Illicit Commercial Trade / Export Misdeclaration (shipping or customs fraud)

Statutory sanction: Under the Korean Customs Act (관세법) Article 270 and the Foreign Trade Act (대외무역법) Article 3-5, false declaration triggers a penalty of up to 10 times the evaded customs duty (minimum KRW 100 million), plus an administrative fine for customs entry. Additionally, the VAT Act Article 67 imposes a 2% invoice penalty and an additional evasion penalty of up to 40% of the evaded VAT. The exporter may be suspended from trading privileges for up to three years.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: The deputy director and statutory representative may be deemed jointly and severally liable for the unpaid export duties and penalties under the Customs Act Article 277-2 if they materially participated in the misdeclaration. Civil asset restitution of all undeclared profits, a travel ban (blacklisting) is automatically issued for unpaid customs dues exceeding KRW 5 million, and criminal imprisonment up to 5 years under Article 270 can be imposed. Additionally, the director's personal tax information will be published by the NTS Custom Office in annual defaulters' lists.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance (anti-abuse and tax evasion)

Statutory sanction: The International Tax Coordination Act (국제조세조정에관한법률) Article 5 permits the NTS to recharacterize the income of a no-substance Korean entity as business income attributable to a permanent establishment of the foreign parent, with a 20% corporate income tax plus 20.4% withholding on 'deemed dividend' remittance. Article 6 of the same law disallows excessive interest deduction for thin capitalization if the debt-to-equity ratio exceeds 3:1 (6:1 for bank loans). The total penalty can reach up to 60% of the understated corporate liability, and the entity may be deregistered from the corporate register under the Commercial Act.

Director personal liability: The Korean courts frequently apply the 'actual decision-maker' doctrine under Article 401 of the Commercial Act: a director who knowingly authorized or facilitated the shell arrangement is personally liable to the entity and to the NTS for the amount of tax evaded plus interest. A criminal verdict under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 (willful evasion exceeding KRW 100 million) carries imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to triple the evaded tax. The director is also subject to blacklisting by the National Tax Service, which blocks overseas travel for up to one year, and revocation of the Foreign Investment Registration Status, thereby eliminating future visa permissions.

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Transactions Reports Act (특정금융거래정보의보고및이용에관한법률) and the Crime Proceeds Concealment Regulation, culpable parties may be fined up to three times the laundered amount (minimum KRW 100 million); the Korea Customs Service imposes a penalty of KRW 50 million up to KRW 300 million for unregulated cross-border currency and trade-based laundering; the export license for recycled scrap copper is revocable permanently, and the company may be subject to a mandatory freezing order and asset seizure.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act of Korea. The incorporators may fix the initial capital at any amount; however, a customary initial paid-in capital of at least KRW 10,000,000 (approx. USD 7,450) is recommended for bank account opening, medical device licensing, and supplier credibility. The subscribed capital must be paid by cash deposit into a domestic commercial bank account in the name of the company before registration, and a certificate of deposit must be filed with the registry. No government-mandated blocked escrow account rule applies after registration; the funds are freely available for lawful business purposes.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS), Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr) for corporate income tax, VAT, and business registration; customs export clearance and export declarations are processed through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system (https://unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity not yet registered in Korea, the entity must appoint a statutory Korean tax representative and file a foreign business operator registration application under the Value-Added Tax Act with the District Tax Office having jurisdiction over the tax representative or the place of establishment. Required submissions include the home-country certificate of incorporation, evidence of existence, power of attorney to the Korean tax representative, and a registration form detailing the business activity, expected taxable supplies, and no fixed place of business in Korea. Upon approval, the NTS issues a 10-digit Korean business registration number (TIN) and certifies the entity as a foreign business operator for VAT purposes. If the entity establishes a domestic Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company), the entity completes commercial registration under the Commercial Act, then applies for a business registration certificate through HomeTax or the competent District Tax Office within 20 days from the commencement of business, obtaining the standard 10-digit business registration number used for corporate tax, VAT, and e-tax invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification; Korea has no check-the-box election and does not permit pass-through taxation for a limited liability company. A wholly-owned domestic subsidiary group may elect consolidated corporate income tax filing under the Corporate Tax Act if the statutory shareholding and other consolidated-filing qualification conditions are satisfied. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to a Korean corporate entity; VAT registration is mandatory from commencement of business or from the first taxable supply in Korea. For individual sole proprietors only, a simplified taxable person regime may apply when annual taxable turnover is not more than KRW 80,000,000; this simplification is not available to a Yuhan Hoesa corporation. A foreign non-resident supplier with no Korean establishment becomes subject to VAT registration from the first taxable supply, including electronically supplied services to Korean consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate under the Value-Added Tax Act when the goods physically depart Korea as evidenced by an export customs declaration accepted in the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system, title and risk pass to a non-resident buyer, and the export proceeds are received in foreign currency and repatriated through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. For intraocular acrylic lenses, the zero-rating also depends on the underlying medical-device export being made by the Korean taxable business operator and not by a reseller without export processing. Qualifying offshore services supplied to non-residents and related to exported goods may also be zero-rated where the consideration is settled in cross-border foreign exchange transactions.

  • Customs Export Declaration approved by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS electronic clearance (the Korean export clearance certificate equivalent to an export bill of entry)
  • Original Bill of Lading or Export Air Waybill consigned to the non-resident buyer and showing exit from a Korean seaport or airport
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing the inward remittance of export proceeds, being the Korean functional equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and signed export sales contract or purchase order
  • Copy of the Korean automated customs export loading confirmation where required by NTS/KCS audit protocols

All Korean VAT current taxpayers must issue electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) transmitted to the NTS through HomeTax or an approved authorized service provider using the NTS tax-invoice API and prescribed XML schema. Electronic tax invoices must be transmitted no later than the day following the taxable supply. The NTS assigns a unique electronic tax invoice control number and maintains an anti-fraud transaction database, functioning as real-time fiscal clearance and digital stamping for each invoice. Failure to issue or transmit an e-tax invoice triggers statutory addition to tax under the Value-Added Tax Act. Foreign business operators use the HomeTax foreign business operator electronic tax invoice portal.

NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly verify the UNI-PASS export declaration, physical export documentation, and the foreign exchange receipt ledger. If export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day basic deadline without an approved extension, or if documentation is incomplete, false, circular, or inconsistent with the declared export supply, the 0% rate is retrospectively denied and the supply is re-assessed at 10% VAT together with statutory addition to tax, interest for late payment, and possible denial or recoupment of any input VAT refund previously paid. Cases involving fabricated export declarations, forged e-tax invoices, or fraudulent offshore payment chains are referred for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with penalties including fines, imprisonment, and revocation of tax registrations. Export and VAT supporting records must be retained for at least five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalised market economy under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act: current account payments are generally unrestricted once routed through a designated foreign exchange bank, while capital account operations such as direct investment, overseas remittances and external borrowings require declaration, notification or ex-post reporting rather than prior licensing. KRW is convertible for underlying trade and business purposes, but international settlement must be executed in USD or EUR via SWIFT because offshore KRW clearing is limited and tightly monitored. Export proceeds no longer require mandatory surrender to the Bank of Korea; however, they must be collected within the statutory trade settlement period and repatriated to a domestic foreign currency account or converted at market rate. Dividend and profit remittance to a non-resident is lawful after final tax clearance, but the paying bank needs FIU-compliant corporate documents, audited financial statements and the FDI notification certificate to verify that profit is real and not disguised capital flight. Korean regulators aggressively review round-tripping, pre-payment imports and non-resident intercompany lending; all foreign capital injections must be reported to the designated bank before arrival and cannot be commingled with trade proceeds. Balance of payments reporting obligations declare quarterly external assets and liabilities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Trade Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the exporter must collect export proceeds within 3 months of the date of exportation unless a written extension is granted by the Director of the relevant Customs Office; there is no mandatory percentage conversion to KRW; the exporter may retain 100% of the proceeds in a foreign currency account with an authorised foreign exchange bank, provided the bank is notified and the receipt is recorded under the corresponding export declaration. Overdue returns, write-offs, or settlements via unlicensed payment providers are treated as settlement violations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with delegated foreign exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act co-administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and Korea Customs Service

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability - legally barred. No overseas fintech EMI is licensed as a foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, so it cannot open a corporate deposit account, clear KRW, or execute external payments on behalf of the Korean entity. A licensed EU or UK EMI can only act as a supporting agent or auxiliary service provider; all settlement and custody must pass through a Korean domestic deposit-taking bank. Operation through an unlicensed EMI would trigger bank-side suspicious transaction reporting, transaction freezing, regulatory sanctions, and a fatal AML defence weakness.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign equity capital must be notified to the designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before remittance; the inbound wire should be executed via SWIFT MT103 with the remitter identified as the foreign shareholder, the beneficiary as KR_YUHANGHOESA, and the purpose code referencing foreign direct investment rather than trade settlement. Upon receipt the bank issues a foreign exchange purchase/conversion certificate and an FDI registration confirmation, which is the capital verification evidence for the Korean corporate registry. The funds must remain in a dedicated capital escrow account until the paid-in capital entry is completed and the corporate bank account is fully activated; no commingling with operating revenue or trade proceeds is permitted.Outbound trade disbursements should run through the settled clearing rails that match the counterparty risk and instrument cycle: SWIFT MT103 for low-value resin and packaging suppliers, Commercial Letter of Credit (sight or usance) for high-value CNC lathe and hydrophobic acrylic material imports, and Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) only where a complete original bill of lading and independent inspection certificate are attached. Every outward payment must be matched to a Korean customs export/import declaration in UNI-PASS, supported by purchase orders and invoices, and netted through the authorised foreign exchange bank accounts; KRW clearing is reserved solely for domestic purchases, with no use of offshore digital wallets or unlicensed payment channels.Deliverable forward contracts: permitted only when supported by real underlying export/import exposure evidenced by invoices, LCs, or purchase orders, executed with a domestic FX-licensed bank and capped to the projected cash flow tenor.FX swaps: permitted to hedge principal repatriation and timing mismatches between FDI injection, operational revenue conversion, and dividend remittance, provided all legs correspond to actual cross-border flows and no leverage is embedded.Non-deliverable forwards: not permissible as a speculative instrument for this onshore Korean entity; any NDF access would require a documented genuine hedge and prior BOK/MOEF counterparty eligibility, and should otherwise be treated as a compliance red flag.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% on first KRW 200 million of taxable income; 19% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% on KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate withholding on dividends paid to a non-resident corporate shareholder, composed of 20% national corporate income tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surtax

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Final corporate income tax return must be filed within 3 months after the fiscal year-end. A December 31 fiscal year-end therefore requires filing by March 31 of the following year. Interim tax prepayment returns are due within 2 months after the first half of the fiscal year, generally by August 31.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File: annual revenue of KRW 100 billion or more. Local File: annual revenue of KRW 100 billion or more and cross-border related-party transaction amounts of KRW 50 billion or more in the preceding fiscal year. Country-by-Country Report: consolidated group annual revenue of KRW 1 trillion or more. All KRW thresholds apply per Korean fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply a narrow Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but Korean tax law applies the substance-over-form doctrine, general anti-abuse rules, controlled foreign corporation rules, beneficial ownership tests and real economic activity standards to prevent treaty shopping and thin capitalization. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa distributing intraocular acrylic lens profits must demonstrate actual staff, offices, decision-making, inventory risk and operational substance; passive shell or conduit arrangements will be disregarded or recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False or Non-Filed Corporate Returns

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 3 years and a fine of up to 3 times the evaded tax under Korean tax punishment statutes; aggravated criminal liability for evasion amounts exceeding KRW 500 million; civil additional tax of 40% plus daily surcharges of 0.025% per day.

Director personal liability: Representative directors who instruct, approve or knowingly benefit from evasion are subject to criminal fines, imprisonment, exit and travel bans, and civil restitution of the evaded tax with interest. Korean courts routinely pierce the corporate veil where funds are diverted to shareholder personal accounts.
Shell Company or Conduit Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions under the substance-over-form doctrine, transfer pricing adjustment, penalties of 10% to 30% of the adjusted tax amount, and denial of refund applications. Korean Customs and tax authorities may also impose additional import valuation adjustments on lens components supplied by foreign affiliates.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for unpaid corporate taxes when corporate formalities are ignored, assets are stripped from the company, or income is attributed to a paper foreign subsidiary. Directors can face asset freeze orders, civil damages to creditors, and criminal prosecution for aiding fraudulent tax structures.
AML and Cross-Border Payment Irregularities in High-Risk Medical Device Trade

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Transactions Reports and Speculative Transactions Act, failure to submit a suspicious transaction report is punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years or a fine of up to KRW 50 million. Money laundering offenses carry forfeiture of proceeds, monetary penalties, and license suspension or revocation of the statutory medical device manufacturing license. Regulators may also impose administrative sanctions against the licensed manufacturer.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors with supervisory authority face criminal liability where AML reporting was wilfully bypassed. Directors may be banned from re-entering South Korea or from serving as a corporate officer, their assets subject to civil forfeiture, and principal offenders are imprisoned.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Incorrect International Transaction Reporting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing assessment of the profit margin using the most reliable comparable data, additional tax equal to the difference computed at the statutory corporate tax rates, penalty of up to 20% on the underreported income for absence of documentation, up to 30% penalty for grossly inaccurate international transaction reports, and daily additional tax on unpaid assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the omission of international transaction reporting bear personal criminal exposure and can be fined or imprisoned. The Korean tax authority may hold the director personally responsible for penalties where the company is dissolved, insolvent or used as a conduit for punitive tax positions.

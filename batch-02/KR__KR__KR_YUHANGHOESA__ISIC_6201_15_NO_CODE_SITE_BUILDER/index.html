Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korean law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. The Articles may provide for zero or nominal capital. Capital may be subscribed as cash or in-kind, and paid-in receipts must be obtained and retained. No blocked escrow account is mandated; however, funds must be actually paid or contributed before the District Court Registry will accept the incorporation registration. Registration tax and court filing fee are assessed in KRW based on the registered capital, with an approximate USD-equivalent state fee of 120 for a low-capital entity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS; Korean central tax authority). The statutory electronic portal is HomeTax (hometax.go.kr), which is the integrated system for business registration, VAT e-invoice submission, filing, and payment. Customs-side clearance is managed by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS trade facilitation system.

E-government filing portal

For a Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company) formed in Korea: [1] complete corporate registration under the Korean Commercial Act with the competent registry court and obtain the corporate registration certificate. [2] Submit the statutory business registration application to the district tax office that has jurisdiction over the registered headquarters, via HomeTax or in writing, within the period required by Korean tax procedure law. The application must identify the corporation's legal name, registered address, actual principal office, representative director, main business activity (classified under ISIC 6201 / software and IT services), ownership structure, and banking details. [3] The NTS issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which is simultaneously the VAT registration number, the corporate income tax return reference number, and the mandatory identifier to be printed on tax invoices. [4] If the foreign shareholder is a non-resident entity that has not established a domestic Korean operation, the foreign entity must appoint and file a domestic tax representative with the NTS and obtain a non-resident business tax identifier through the HomeTax foreign business registration function. [5] All changes to registered particulars, including changes of address, representative, bank account, or business scope, must be amended through the same HomeTax application to keep the tax-ID record valid and to avoid administrative penalties. [6] Digital businesses selling to overseas customers should also request exporter identification registration with the Korea Customs Service and must retain the UNI-PASS exporter code, because the BRN remains the primary tax reference while the customs exporter code is required for export declarations in the UNI-PASS system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean corporate tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. A Yuhan Hoesa is always a domestic corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax on its worldwide income. There is no pass-through entitlement. The only elective regimes that may be relevant are group-level consolidated tax filing for resident wholly-owned or substantially-held affiliates (subject to NTS approval) and the small/medium-sized enterprise or export-related tax reduction incentives that require a formal application; neither alters the default entity-level corporate tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW; no general registration threshold. Every person carrying on a business that supplies taxable goods or services in Korea must register with the NTS. A simplified taxable person status is available for a small business whose annual taxable turnover does not exceed KRW 80,000,000, but that status is not a registration exemption and the business still requires a VAT/tax identification number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate where the goods are exported to a foreign country and the export declaration is accepted by the Korea Customs Service; the goods must physically depart Korea and the export transaction must be supported by standard export documents. Cross-border services, including digital and cloud products such as the no-code website-builder subscriptions described in this dossier, qualify for 0% VAT where the service is supplied to a non-resident purchaser and the place of supply is treated as outside Korea under the Korean VAT Act. For B2B digital services, the place of supply is normally determined by the customer's place of business; for B2C digital services, the service is zero-rated when the foreign individual is resident outside Korea, payment is received in foreign currency, and the evidence demonstrates offshore access or consumption. Services physically performed in Korea, or services supplied to a Korean-resident customer, are taxable at the standard 10% rate.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration accepted through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system (the definitive export evidence for physical goods; functionally the export bill of entry for Korean export law).
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or an international courier export manifest / delivery confirmation in parcel exports) evidencing that the goods left the Republic of Korea.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and the zero-rated VAT e-tax invoice showing the 0% VAT rate.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a licensed Korean foreign-exchange bank, or a functionally identical foreign-currency receipt confirmation, evidencing that the foreign currency export proceeds were received in Korea from the non-resident purchaser; in practice this is the Korean equivalent of a Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate required for 0% export-vat substantiation.
  • For digital service exports: the signed contract or online subscription agreement with the non-resident customer, the service-delivery log or IP/location data showing offshore use, the foreign-currency payment remittance record, and, where the customer is a foreign corporation, a foreign corporate registry extract or a self-certificate establishing its non-resident status.
  • Where export proceeds are paid after shipment, the foreign-exchange bank's export-settlement authorization or approved extension schedule must be retained to prove that the exporter received the funds within the statutory period.

Korea operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) system through the NTS HomeTax platform. Every VAT-registered business must issue a tax invoice containing the supplier BRN, purchaser BRN (for B2B supplies), supply date, supply value, VAT amount, and a unique e-invoice serial number, and must transmit the e-invoice to the HomeTax e-invoice system no later than the day after the invoice is issued. Electronic tax invoice transmission became mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses regardless of turnover from January 2022. There is no separate physical or cryptographic fiscal stamp applied before invoice issuance, but the NTS validates and stores every e-invoice centrally, and suspicious suppliers are flagged in the NTS anti-fraud data-analysis engine. For B2C card and cash sales, payment gateways and approved value-added-network operators transmit settlement data to the NTS Cash Receipt system on a real-time or near-real-time basis. The combination of e-tax invoices, cash receipts, and bank settlement data constitutes the anti-fraud clearance framework.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service can retrospectively audit export zero-rating claims. If an export is found not to satisfy the zero-rating conditions, the NTS reclassifies the supply as a domestic taxable supply, assesses VAT at 10%, and adds statutory additional tax and overdue interest charges under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the VAT Act. The default assessment period is generally 5 years, extended to 10 years for deliberate concealment or tax evasion. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory Korean foreign-exchange settlement period (15 months from the export declaration, extendable by a licensed bank in limited cases) can lead to reversal of zero-rated treatment, recovery of unpaid VAT plus penalties, and administrative fines for violations of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, which may be up to 250% of the foreign currency not repatriated. In cases of intentionally false documentation, round-tripped payments, fake non-resident customers, or manipulated service-use data, the responsible entity and representative director may also face criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment and fines calculated at multiples of the evaded tax. Accordingly, the compliance archive must preserve all export declarations, transport/receipt evidence, contracts, service-usage logs, payment remittance records, and the foreign-exchange bank confirmation for the full statutory retention period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized but tightly monitored foreign exchange system. Current account transactions, including digital service exports and ordinary business operating expenses, are broadly free and convertible at market rates through designated foreign exchange banks, with post-trade reporting rather than prior licensing. Capital account transactions are more restricted and require either advance declaration, registration, or approval before execution. A Yuhan Hoesa can receive foreign equity investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, but the capital funds must first arrive in a domestic foreign currency account at a designated bank and be reported before conversion into Korean won. Profit remittance is permitted once distributable reserves are certified by audited financial statements, but requires foreign exchange transaction reporting, payment of applicable withholding tax on dividends, a board resolution, corporate tax payment certificates, and proof that the investment was originally registered with the Korean authorities. Although there is no fixed statutory surrender ratio for export proceeds, the full amount of export earnings must be collected through a designated foreign exchange bank and repatriated into the Korean banking system within the statutory collection period or an approved extension. The practical hurdles are not convertibility itself but documentation: every material inbound and outbound flow must be traceable on the bank record, linked to an approved foreign exchange purpose code, and supported by contracts, invoices, tax certificates, and corporate resolutions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected into a bank account at a designated foreign exchange bank within 18 months of the export date, unless an extension is approved by the foreign exchange authority. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, but the statutory export collection obligation requires 100% of the export invoice value to be received in Korea; the funds may be retained in a foreign currency account or converted to Korean won through the designated bank, and every receipt must be reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low in Korea. A pure offshore fintech EMI is not legally equivalent to a designated foreign exchange bank and cannot serve as the principal treasury account for a Korean resident Yuhan Hoesa. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires corporate FX transactions, export collection, FDI capital receipt, and tax-related remittances to be routed through a bank registered in Korea. Offshore digital EMI platforms may be used only as ancillary SaaS billing rails, invoice presentment, or card acquisition networks, but all settlement must ultimately land in a domestic bank account. They also cannot perform capital verification, statutory export collection reporting, or dividend withholding documentation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Korean entity is required to prepare and submit a transfer pricing Local File and Master File when the annual gross turnover exceeds KRW 100 billion and the combined volume of international related-party transactions exceeds KRW 50 billion; a simplified TP disclosure schedule is required where cross-border related-party controlled transactions exceed the lower KRW 5 billion threshold. Even below the mandatory documentation threshold, the Yuhan Hoesa must maintain contemporaneous evidence of the arm's length price for any cross-border intercompany royalties, service fees, cloud infrastructure charges, or software distribution arrangements.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but it applies the anti-abuse doctrine of substance over form under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Act for Coordination of International Tax Affairs. The Yuhan Hoesa must actually perform Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Korea: a real office, real employees, Thai board and management decisions, ownership and control over the intellectual property of the no-code website templates, active customer contracts, and day-to-day product development. A Korean registered company that does not have CIGA will be treated as a shell, its Korean tax residence may be challenged, and treaty benefits, permanent establishment protection, and withholding reductions can be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Suppressing Gross Revenue and Using Offshore Payment Routes

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, a fraudulent return or fraudulent evasion of Korean corporate income tax is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years and a fine of up to five times the evaded tax. The National Tax Service additionally applies the 20% unregistered or 40% fraudulent understatement negligence tax under Articles 47-3 to 47-5 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, together with a daily overdue charge until full payment, and may blacklist the company as a habitual high-risk tax debtor.

Director personal liability: The representative director and any de facto director who signed the false return or instructed the use of the offshore route is personally liable under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act for the unpaid corporate tax plus penalties to the extent of the personal assets of the director. Criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment, a travel ban, seizure of personal real estate, bank accounts, and shares, and disqualification from serving as a director. The civil asset restitution obligation extends to corporate property that was dissipated through the foreign shell.
Artificial Transfer Pricing and Defective Intercompany Documentation

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may adjust the intercompany consideration to arm's length under Articles 4 and 5 of the Act for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, disallow the excessive intercompany fees, impose the unfaithful-report negligence tax on the resulting underpaid corporate tax, assess a transfer pricing penalty for failure to supply contemporaneous documentation when requested, and add late-payment surcharges. The NTS may also characterize the transaction as tax fraud if the documents are fabricated.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the sham intercompany agreement are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax under the National Tax Collection Act Article 39. In an intentional transfer pricing arrangement, the director can be criminally prosecuted for tax evasion by way of artificial profit shifting, with a travel ban, an asset attachment order, and imprisonment risk; the corporate veil is pierced to recover hidden profits and unpaid penalties.
Treaty Shopping, Shell Beneficial Owner, and Improper Dividend/Royalty WHT Reduction

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service will deny the treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership rule and under Korea's anti-treaty-abuse rules in Article 98-6 of the Income Tax Act and Article 9 of the Act for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, assess the unpaid 22% withholding tax plus the local surcharge, impose the 20%/40% unregistered return penalty, and demand late-payment surcharges. The payer company is jointly liable for failing to withhold the statutory amount even though the DTA claim was presented.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed a false treaty withholding declaration without a Tax Residency Certificate or without verifying beneficial ownership are personally responsible for the uncollected withholding tax under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act. Criminal prosecution is available where the treaty evasion is intentional and large; the director can face imprisonment, a travel ban, personal asset seizure, and permanent blacklisting, while the foreign shell remains vulnerable to disqualification and threatened from public filing.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Commercial Act. The Company has no statutory minimum paid-up capital; the founders must nevertheless declare a capital amount in the Articles of Incorporation. All cash contributions must be deposited into a subscription bank account opened in the name of the prospective company or held with an escrow/blocked account instruction before registration. Such funds are legally blocked and may not be withdrawn or disbursed for corporate use until the District Court completes incorporation registration, except for refund to subscribers if incorporation fails. Contributions in kind must be fully transferred to the Company and verified by a court-appointed appraiser. Registration tax is separately assessed by the local government on the declared capital; because capital is not fixed in this payload, no single official USD fee amount is stated.

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold. Registration is mandatory from business commencement for every taxable person carrying on a trade or business in Korea. The KRW 80,000,000 annual turnover figure is relevant only to the simplified taxable person status for small individual merchants; a Yuhan Hoesa corporation is always a general taxable person and must register regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act zero-rates the export of goods. For this wholesale supply of drummed and bagged penetration grade bitumen, the Yuhan Hoesa will qualify for 0% VAT if it is the exporter of record, the goods are physically exported from the Republic of Korea to an overseas destination under the Customs Act, legal title and risk pass to the foreign buyer under the contractual Incoterms, and the export consideration is received in foreign currency from the overseas buyer. The foreign exchange must be settled within the statutory period, ordinarily 180 days from export clearance. Qualifying international transport and logistics services connected to the export are also zero-rated, but the physical export of the goods is the primary basis for the claim.

  • UNI-PASS Customs Export Declaration and the Korea Customs Service Export Declaration Confirmation, evidencing the customs clearance number and exporter-of-record status.
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list describing the containerized drummed and bagged bitumen/asphalt, quantity, unit price, and overseas consignee.
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal transport document proving physical shipment from a Korean port to the foreign destination.
  • Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation and foreign currency receipt statement issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing inward remittance of the export proceeds from the overseas buyer.
  • Export contract or purchase order, and where requested by the NTS, the Korea Customs Service export clearance status report or cargo manifest evidence.

Korea mandates electronic tax invoicing under the Value-Added Tax Act. Tax invoices must be issued in electronic form and transmitted to the NTS through the HomeTax electronic tax invoice service, or through an authorized EDI/API solution, within the prescribed time after each supply. For export sales, the electronic tax invoice must be marked zero-rated and should reference the UNI-PASS customs export declaration number. Korea does not operate a physical fiscal stamping or fiscal printer clearance regime; instead, the NTS and Korea Customs Service automatically cross-match electronic tax invoices, customs export declarations, foreign exchange transaction records, and refund claims. Export transactions may be subjected to pre-refund verification or prior-review requests before any zero-rated VAT refund is released.

The NTS actively cross-matches zero-rated VAT declarations with Korea Customs Service export data and bank foreign-exchange receipt records. If an export zero-rating claim is not substantiated by valid customs documentation, if the goods are not actually exported, or if foreign-currency proceeds are not received within the 180-day settlement period, the NTS may retrospectively disallow the zero-rating and reclassify the transaction as a taxable domestic supply. In that case, 10% VAT is assessed on the export sales price, together with additional tax under the Value-Added Tax Act, late-payment interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and, in cases involving fraudulent or grossly false documentation, criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. The ordinary statute of limitations for tax assessment is five years from the end of the relevant taxable period, extended to ten years where false or fraudulent declarations are established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a highly open capital account with full current account convertibility and a liberalised capital account subject to notification and reporting rather than prior approval. The Korean won is fully freely convertible for trade and most service transactions. Profit remittance (dividends, royalties, and interest) is permitted without central bank approval, provided the remitting entity meets tax clearance obligations under the Corporate Tax Act and submits the appropriate Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) report through a domestic foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections and inbound loans, require a report to the MOEF/BOK under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; however, these are typically administrative notifications with no conversion restrictions. There are no surrender requirements for export proceeds, but exporters must collect export receipts within the statutory period (generally 6 months, extendable to 12 months with approval) and report collection through an authorised foreign exchange bank. The won's exchange rate is freely floating under a market average rate system, and the BOK may intervene to smooth disorderly conditions. For a bitumen wholesale trading LLC, the principal FX hurdle is ensuring accurate documentation of the underlying physical trade (invoice, bill of lading, and payment flow) to satisfy the FEIS reporting and to avoid classification as a disguised capital transaction. Overall, the regime supports efficient treasury operations, but non-resident shareholders and foreign directors must appoint a domestic tax representative for remittance-related tax registrations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio for export proceeds in South Korea. Under FETA and the Customs Act, exporters must ensure that foreign currency receipts are remitted into the domestic foreign exchange bank account within a maximum of six (6) months from the date of exportation, unless an extension up to twelve (12) months is granted by the designated bank based on justifiable commercial reasons. All export proceeds must be reported through the FEIS within one (1) business day of receipt. The exporter may retain the foreign currency in a corporate foreign exchange account or convert it to KRW at the prevailing market rate; no forced conversion applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as co-administrators under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) and offshore digital banks have very limited to negligible viability for serving a Korean-incorporated LLC engaged in bulk commodity trading. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act (EFTA) and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only domestic licensed banks and financial investment companies are authorised to execute FX trades, handle outward/remittance processing, and issue trade-related guarantees for domestic corporations. Offshore EMIs such as TransferWise/Revolut are not licensed deposit-takers in Korea and cannot open a KRW settlement account for a Korean entity; they may only offer ancillary multi-currency receipts in the name of the customer, but these fail the FEIS reporting obligations and bank-grade compliance requirements for trade settlement. Consequently, the recommended treasury infrastructure relies exclusively on domestic Tier-1 commercial banks for statutory capital escrow, trade settlements, and FX hedging. Any fintech solution is limited to invoice reconciliation or expense automation and must never be used for trade payment rails because it would trigger automatic regulatory scrutiny and potential administrative penalties.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) and offshore digital banks have very limited to negligible viability for serving a Korean-incorporated LLC engaged in bulk commodity trading. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act (EFTA) and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only domestic licensed banks and financial investment companies are authorised to execute FX trades, handle outward/remittance processing, and issue trade-related guarantees for domestic corporations. Offshore EMIs such as TransferWise/Revolut are not licensed deposit-takers in Korea and cannot open a KRW settlement account for a Korean entity; they may only offer ancillary multi-currency receipts in the name of the customer, but these fail the FEIS reporting obligations and bank-grade compliance requirements for trade settlement. Consequently, the recommended treasury infrastructure relies exclusively on domestic Tier-1 commercial banks for statutory capital escrow, trade settlements, and FX hedging. Any fintech solution is limited to invoice reconciliation or expense automation and must never be used for trade payment rails because it would trigger automatic regulatory scrutiny and potential administrative penalties.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible for this ISIC 6430 trust-administration entity as a stand-alone arrangement. The National Tax Service, the Financial Services Commission, the Korean FIU, and prospective Korean banks collectively require a physical registered office where the actual management, books, records, and compliance function are maintained. For the high-risk special license category applicable to fiduciary administration and asset protection trusts, a serviced office will be accepted only if it is a dedicated physical suite under an executed lease of at least twelve months, with a visible company plaque, full-time administration staff, a fixed landline, physical vault or file room, and access to original documents. A virtual mailbox alone will expose the entity to business license denial, bank account refusal, and recharacterization as a sham office by the tax authority.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required for forming a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. However, because the company will apply for a Trust Business License to administer irrevocable discretionary asset protection trusts, the Financial Services Commission requires a minimum paid-in capital of Korean Won 10,000,000,000 (approximately US Dollar 7,400,000). This amount must be deposited in a blocked escrow account at a licensed Korean bank and remain impounded until regulatory approval is obtained. A certificate of the escrow deposit must be submitted with the trust business license application.

Funding & unlock path: Occupancy of the leased office requires a basic fire safety inspection certificate from the local fire station, a structural load approval for commercially occupied floor area above 200 square meters, and registration under the Occupational Safety and Health Act for non-manufacturing office premises. The company must install certified fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and a business safety management plan signed by an occupational safety coordinator.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must first appoint a domestic tax representative in Korea, then file a business registration application with the competent district tax office having jurisdiction over its Korean place of business. The application is submitted under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, accompanied by the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, power of attorney for the tax representative, domestic lease agreement for the registered place of business, and identification documents of the director or foreign responsible person. After authentication of identity and submission via HomeTax or via in-person filing, the NTS issues a Business Registration Number (TIN/VAT ID). The procurement procedure may also be performed through the tax representative using a certified certificate (GPKI or accredited digital certificate).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for mandatory VAT registration; any business making taxable supplies in Korea must register before commencing activities. A simplified taxpayer regime is available only for small traders with annual taxable turnover up to KRW 80 million.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and certain services qualify for zero-rated VAT relief. Goods must be physically shipped from Korea to a foreign country and supported by an export customs declaration. Offshore digital content, game-related services, and fiduciary asset protection services supplied to non-resident foreign beneficiaries qualify as zero-rated services when the service is rendered to a non-resident, consumption occurs outside Korea, and the consideration is earned in convertible foreign currency. Zero-rating also applies where services are supplied to a foreign business and paid in foreign currency under the statutory exception for export services in the Value-Added Tax Act and its Enforcement Decree.

  • Export Customs Declaration (Export Cargo Clearance Report) submitted through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical exportation from Korea
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign Currency Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 credit advice issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Cross-border trust administration or offshore asset protection service contract showing the non-resident recipient and foreign-currency settlement terms

VAT e-Tax Invoice issuance is mandatory and must be submitted to the NTS through the HomeTax system in real time. VAT-registered businesses are required to use the electronic tax invoice API or the secure HomeTax web interface; transactions must be authenticated with an accredited digital certificate. The system performs real-time clearance of e-Tax Invoices with NTS. Cash receipts under the Cash Receipts Act must also be issued to resident consumers for transactions exceeding the statutory threshold. No independent anti-fraud digital stamping protocol is used; the NTS database serves as the central fiscal repository.

The NTS may conduct a retrospective audit of zero-rating claims within the statutory five-year prescriptive period. Taxpayers must produce the complete export documentation bundle and foreign exchange receipt evidence; failure to do so triggers a reassessment at the standard 10% VAT rate plus statutory penalties. Negligent misreporting carries penalties of 20% of the understated VAT, while intentional fraud attracts a 40% penalty, additional late-payment interest, and referral to criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. False or fabricated customs documentation may also lead to customs penalties and forfeiture of Zero-Rating relief.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea generally maintains full current-account convertibility for ordinary trade, services, and profits; exporters and foreign investors may move trade-related foreign currency freely through a registered foreign exchange bank. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment into a Yuhan Hoesa and settlement of a discretionary trust, are not completely free: investment subscriptions must be routed through a certified foreign exchange bank, reported under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and ordinary loans or derivative positions may require prior registration or approval. Foreign-invested companies may remit dividends and disposed proceeds after tax clearance using purpose codes 110121 or 110122, but the bank will demand certification of the foreign investment report, annual audited financial statements, and a tax payment confirmation. The Korean won is not convertible outside the country and non-residents cannot freely open won accounts without a licensed bank, so all repatriation must be executed inside Korea. Although there are no mandatory surrender requirements for export proceeds, the capital account remains carefully policed; transfer pricing documentation, CRS/FATCA filings, and FETA reporting are compulsory. Practical hurdles include slow approval for large settlements, evidentiary burdens for beneficial owners, and the risk of punitive customs or foreign-exchange fines if the incorrect purpose code is used.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, exporters must settle proceeds of exports through a designated foreign exchange bank and obtain an export receipt within one year from the date of export declaration; extensions to two years are available with approval. There is no mandatory statutory conversion ratio; the exporter may maintain a foreign-currency deposit, but any conversion to Korean won must be executed by a licensed foreign exchange bank and matched to a specific export declaration through Korea Customs Service data. This one-year rule is a collection limitation rather than a forced surrender duty.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea; foreign exchange administration delegated to the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with Korea Customs Service monitoring trade settlement flows

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low - legally not viable as a stand-alone treasury solution. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign direct investment for Yuhan Hoesa must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to an account held at a domestic foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The remitter must quote FX purpose code 110101 (foreign direct investment) and the beneficiary must deposit funds into a Foreign Investor Deposit Account before any conversion to Korean won. After the inward remittance notification, the company files a foreign investment report through the bank or KOTRA system; an external auditor then performs capital verification and issues a certificate of investment to enable registration of the trust-funded equity or capital contribution.For outbound disbursements, SWIFT MT103 is appropriate for ordinary fee and service payments, while commercial letters of credit are mandatory for tangible goods requiring transport documentation, and documentary collections are acceptable for sales on bill of exchange. All outgoing wires must state the correct FX purpose code such as 210101 for import payments, include invoice and contract identifiers, and clear through a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared when intercompany transactions between the Korean entity and foreign related parties exceed KRW 10 billion in the tax year. Master File is required when aggregate revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion. Country-by-Country Report notification follows separate CbC filing thresholds.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not operate a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations regime like forum jurisdictions, but substance is assessed under the International Tax Adjustment Act using substance-over-form, principal purpose, and controlled foreign corporation rules. Where a discretionary asset protection trust has no real office, employees, core income-generating activities (CIGA), or decision-making functions in Korea, the trust may be disregarded and its income reattributed to the Korean settlor or beneficiaries. All trust administration must retain board minutes, trust deeds, KYC files, AML/CDD records, and client due diligence for at least five years.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Unreported Offshore Trust Income

Statutory sanction: Under Article 8 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, imprisonment of 3 to 5 years plus a fine up to 10 times the tax evaded, and a maximum fine of KRW 100 million. The NTS may additionally levy a 40% negligence penalty under the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: the managing director is jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, surcharges, and penalties. The NTS may impose a travel ban, blacklist the director, attach personal bank accounts, and seize personal assets. Criminal conviction carries 3-5 years' imprisonment, and amounts exceeding KRW 300 million may yield a sentence of 5 years or more.
Sham Trust / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization as a sham trust under the International Tax Adjustment Act results in treaty benefit denial, tax reassessment, and a 38% understatement penalty. Filing a false written declaration may trigger corporate fines of up to KRW 50 million under the Act on Special Cases Concerning Anti-Corruption.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in establishing a sham trust are personally exposed to joint and several liability for all taxes owed by the Korean settlor, personal asset freezes, foreign travel restrictions, and civil restitution of assets moved out of Korea. Criminal prosecution for obstruction of tax levy may lead to imprisonment up to 5 years and judicial confiscation of trust assets.
AML and Trust Licensing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Failure to report suspicious transactions under Article 8-2 of the Act on Proceedings for Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information is punishable by a fine up to KRW 30 million and administrative sanctions. Unlicensed trust business is prohibited and may constitute criminal conduct.

Director personal liability: The chief executive officer and compliance director face willful blindness liability under Korean AML law. KoFIU may refer the matter to prosecution, resulting in imprisonment up to 5 years and corporate fines up to KRW 50 million. The corporate license may be revoked and directors may face a 10-year industry disqualification.

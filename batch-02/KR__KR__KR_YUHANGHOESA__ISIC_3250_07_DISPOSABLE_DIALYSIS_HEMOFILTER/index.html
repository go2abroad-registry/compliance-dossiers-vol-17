Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a Yuhan Hoesa. The initial stated capital is fixed freely in the Articles of Incorporation, but every share subscription must be fully paid in cash or lawful in-kind contribution before registration. There is no blocked escrow account required for domestic founders; however, a foreign investor must remit capital from abroad through a designated foreign exchange bank, file a foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and ensure the remitted funds are credited as capital investment in a Korean bank account to be evidenced by a subscription-price deposit certificate or foreign investment registration documentation before incorporation registration.

Funding & unlock path: The manufacturing facility must obtain a Fire Safety Completion Inspection Certificate under the Fire Service Act from the local fire department, including approved fire alarm and suppression systems, emergency lighting, exit signage, evacuation routes, fire hydrants, and fire extinguishers. A certified fire protection system inspection and annual fire safety maintenance must be documented. Civil Defense emergency evacuation planning under the Framework Act on Civil Defense is required for any factory facility. The facility must also obtain occupational safety clearance from the Ministry of Employment and Labor, including a Process Safety Management plan if hazardous chemicals are stored or used above the statutory thresholds, and a workplace environment measurement report covering chemical agents such as N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone, polyvinylpyrrolidone, isopropyl alcohol, and ethylene oxide. Structural load inspections by a licensed architect or engineer are required for the cleanroom, HVAC systems, pure water generation equipment, compressed air systems, and sterilizer installations to verify that the building can support the physical manufacturing infrastructure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None - no turnover-based VAT registration threshold applies in Korea. Every entity engaged in business, including a dialysis hemofilter manufacturer such as Yuhan Hoesa LLC, must register with the NTS at or near the commencement of operations. The KRW 80,000,000 annual revenue figure merely determines eligibility for simplified taxable person treatment, and that simplified regime is not available to a corporate manufacturer; the Yuhan Hoesa LLC is therefore mandatorily a general taxable person, which enables full input VAT deduction and claims for the 0% export VAT rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 11 of the Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods, including disposable dialysis hemofilters manufactured in Korea, are subject to VAT at the 0% rate when the goods are physically shipped from Korea to a foreign destination, ownership passes to the foreign buyer, and the export declaration has been duly accepted by Korea Customs Service. The exporter must retain evidence of the actual export and of the receipt of the export price in foreign currency through the Korean foreign exchange banking system. Qualifying offshore digital services are also zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident consumer or foreign user and delivered or performed outside Korea as set out in the Enforcement Decree. Incidental services directly connected with the exported goods, such as international freight and export handling, may also qualify under the statutory export service zero-rating rules.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate) filed and accepted through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system
  • Bill of Lading (ocean bill of lading or air waybill) evidencing physical departure, the exporter as shipper, the foreign buyer as consignee, and the dialysis hemofilter goods with corresponding details
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List in original form matching the customs export declaration and shipment records
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation / Export Proceeds Receipt issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank, which is the functional equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), proving receipt of the foreign currency export proceeds from the foreign counterparty

Korean law mandates electronic tax invoice issuance for B2B supplies. A general taxable person must transmit each electronic tax invoice through the NTS HomeTax platform or through an NTS-certified ERP/API connector no later than the day following the invoice issuance date. The NTS performs real-time validation, risk scoring, digital signature verification, and digital stamping of each electronic tax invoice with a unique receipt number. For zero-rated exports, the electronic tax invoice is issued with the zero-rated supply code and is automatically cross-matched against the Korea Customs Service export declaration transmitted through UNI-PASS. There is no physical fiscal stamping requirement; anti-fraud compliance is achieved through mandatory real-time electronic transmission, unique document identification, cryptographic integrity controls, and NTS reconciliation with customs and foreign exchange data.

The National Tax Service conducts post-export verification by cross-matching UNI-PASS export declarations, cargo departure and vessel/aircraft movement data, electronic tax invoices coded as zero-rated supplies, and the exporter's foreign exchange receipt records obtained through the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act banking information. If the exporter fails to substantiate actual physical export or fails to receive the export proceeds in foreign currency within 180 days without an approved NTS or customs extension, the NTS will reassess the supply at the standard 10% VAT, disallow the corresponding input VAT deduction, and claw back any VAT refund previously paid. The taxpayer will also be liable for statutory additional tax, including up to 40% for fraudulent underreporting and 20% for unfaithful underreporting, together with statutory interest for overdue tax. If zero-rating documentation is false or forged, the case may be referred for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, Korea Customs Service may impose penalties and suspend export privileges under the Customs Act, and the foreign exchange bank may report violations under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a largely liberalized capital and current account system, yet all cross-border payments and foreign exchange transactions remain subject to a post-transaction reporting and declaration framework under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account operations, including ordinary trade payments, dividends, and service fees, are convertible without quantitative restriction provided the beneficiary is identified, the underlying transaction is genuine, and the correct foreign exchange purpose code is reported through an authorized foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, however, require pre-notification or ex-post reporting depending on the type: direct foreign investment into an entity, inbound loans, and equity purchases by non-residents must be cleared through the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and reported to KOTRA/MOEF; securities investment above certain thresholds must pass through the registry on the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN). For a non-resident-owned Yuhan Hoesa, the principal hurdle is demonstrating that the initial equity capital or shareholder loan is actually inbound investment, not a domestic deposit or re-credited offshore funds, and that all subsequent profit remittances are settled after full corporate tax and local surtax obligations are discharged. There are no formal mandatory surrender or conversion ratio requirements for export receipts in ordinary circumstances; foreign currency earned in exports may be held in onshore foreign currency bank accounts or converted to KRW at the exporter's discretion, but the underlying claim must be collected within the statutory settlement period and the resulting foreign currency position must be reported. Practical convertibility is high for trade-backed operations, but banks will enforce beneficial-ownership disclosure and exact-wire-purpose integrity to avoid disguised capital flows or tax arbitrage. The Bank of Korea can, in a stress scenario or severe capital flow reversal, reinstate surrender-like measures under emergency decrees, so a compliant treasury must maintain audit-ready trade documentation for every transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and delegated regulations of MOEF, export proceeds must be collected through an authorized foreign exchange bank within 90 calendar days from the date of the export declaration (shipment), unless an extension is approved for designated commodity categories, overdue accounts, or delayed collection from non-Puro jurisdictions. There is no fixed statutory mandatory conversion ratio into KRW; the exporter may maintain the foreign currency exposure in an export proceeds account or convert at prevailing market rates. However, the full amount must be evidenced by the exchange bank's receipt confirmation and matched against the electronic customs document in the Korea Customs Service EDI system. Withholding taxes, transfer pricing adjustments, and discounting of long-term receivables may require prior approval or declaration to the tax authority before the repatriation surplus can be distributed or netted.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with resident inspection power delegated from the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; policy and licensing authority is shared with the Financial Services Commission (FSC) and the Bank of Korea is the statutory manager of South Korea's foreign exchange reserves, clearing settlement systems, and KRW liquidity conditions for designated foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMI providers (e.g., digital-only banks or multi-currency e-money wallets) are not viable as a primary settlement rail for this Korean LLC. Korean law defines electronic money institutions and foreign EMIs cannot provide KRW-denominated deposit accounts or execute local wire transfers under the Banking Act unless licensed by the FSS as a domestic bank or registered broker with a physical local branch under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Tier-1 Korean banks may refuse to clear payments for an unlicensed EMI because the end beneficiary KYC cannot satisfy the Act on Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Information. The only permissible use of a fintech gateway would be as a pre-account message/aggregation layer for invoicing data, provided the actual funds remain inside the local FX bank's correspondent channel. Therefore, a physical banking relationship with a Tier-1 commercial bank is mandatory, and any foreign EMI such as Wise or Revolut Business cannot legally maintain the company's KRW liquidity or satisfy capital verification.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion in aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers the mandatory Master File and Local File preparation; KRW 100 million in related-party transactions requires the annual international transactions disclosure schedule attached to the Korean corporate income tax return; a Country-by-Country Report is required when the consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 200 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Korea applies a substance-over-form principle under Article 17 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and the International Tax Adjustment Act. A Korean manufacturing LLC must perform Core Income-Generating Activities inside Korea: the physical extrusion, potting, sterilization, testing, and dispatch of disposable dialysis hemofilters. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 47-2 and 47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, fraudulent underreporting attracts an additional tax of 40% of the omitted corporate income tax plus 0.025% daily delinquency interest; the International Tax Adjustment Act permits recharacterization of the transaction and disallowance of the deductible payment; criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act carry imprisonment up to 10 years and fines up to KRW 200 million.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the underpaid corporate income tax, penalties, and delinquency interest; the National Tax Service may attach personal bank accounts and real estate under civil asset restitution statutes; the director may be blacklisted and subjected to an overseas travel ban if unpaid tax exceeds KRW 50 million; criminal conviction for deliberate tax evasion results in imprisonment of up to 10 years and disqualification from serving as a corporate director.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may disregard the shell entity under Article 17 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and tax the beneficial owner directly; the Foreign Investment Promotion Act registration can be revoked because the investment lacks substantive operation; the International Tax Adjustment Act will deny the trademark or patent royalty deduction absent CIGA in Korea and levies a 20% understatement penalty plus withholding tax at 22%.

Director personal liability: A director who signs financial statements for a sham entity commits actionable fraud and becomes personally liable for the full amount of corporate tax plus penalties; the director's assets are exposed to civil restitution claims; the Immigration Service enforces a travel ban and passport revocation while the National Tax Service blacklists the director for tax collection; criminal courts impose up to 5 years imprisonment for issuance of false documentation and abetting tax evasion.

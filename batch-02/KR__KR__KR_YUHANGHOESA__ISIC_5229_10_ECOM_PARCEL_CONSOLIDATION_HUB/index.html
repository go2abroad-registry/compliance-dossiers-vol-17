Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Commercial Act for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, if foreign-direct-investment status is desired, FIPA requires the foreign investor to subscribe at least KRW 100,000,000 (approximately USD 75,000) and to remit that amount into a foreign-investment deposit account at a Korean foreign-exchange bank. The deposit certificate must be presented at registration; the funds are not held in a blocked escrow and may be used for corporate purposes after the certificate of registration is issued. If no FDI status is sought, there is no minimum capital and no escrow block.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety and civil-defense clearances are mandatory prior to occupancy: (1) Fire-Fighting System Completion Inspection under the Act on the Installation, Maintenance, and Safety Management of Fire-Fighting Systems; (2) appointment of a certified Fire Safety Manager if the warehouse floor area is at least 10,000 square metres, and firefighting equipment inspection for racks and conveyance equipment; (3) Civil Defense Act shelter-use or exemption filing with the competent municipal government for the floor area of the basement or shelter area; (4) KOSHA site inspection under the Occupational Safety and Health Act for conveyors, forklifts, pallet racks, and loading docks, including structural load approval from the local building authority for mezzanine racks and an emergency evacuation plan.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident investor establishing a Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) in South Korea must complete business registration with the NTS through the local district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered Korean place of business. The application must be filed within 20 days from business commencement using HomeTax or a certified tax accountant. Required submissions include the articles of incorporation, a certificate of incorporation or equivalent registration evidence from the foreign parent entity (apostilled or consular-legalized and translated into Korean), proof of registered address (lease agreement, ownership certificate, or confirmation of free use), evidence of paid-in capital, a resolution appointing the domestic statutory business representative, and a power of attorney granted to the Korean tax representative. Upon verification, the NTS issues a Business Registration Certificate with a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which simultaneously serves as the corporate income tax ID, VAT ID, and withholding tax account number. Changes to business details must be reported within 15 days. For a foreign non-resident entity not creating a Korean corporation, a non-resident business operator registration may be required under the VAT Act for taxable e-commerce supplies, but the present Yuhan Hoesa parcel consolidation hub operates as a resident domestic corporation and is registered as a general business operator.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None - every business operator is required to obtain VAT registration and issue tax invoices from the commencement of taxable supplies regardless of turnover. For reference, a simplified taxpayer regime exists for eligible individual operators with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80 million, but a Yuhan Hoesa corporation is always a general VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supply of goods is zero-rated under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act where the goods depart Korea and the Korea Customs Service accepts the export declaration. Services supplied to and performed for a foreign person may qualify as zero-rated deemed exports where the service is directly related to exported goods or constitutes international freight, transportation, sorting, or linehaul for cross-border parcels. A parcel consolidation hub must document the service as an export-related logistics supply, identify the foreign recipient, separate domestic handling from export handling, and demonstrate that the service is physically performed on goods destined for export and that sales consideration is received in a qualifying foreign currency. Offshore digital services supplied by foreign providers are separately within the scope of Korean VAT surrogate collection rules; however, for the present Korean hub, export zero-rating is claimed only on goods exported and on export service supplies satisfying the documentary evidence requirements.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Acceptance Certificate) issued by the Korea Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing overseas destination and carrier acceptance
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for exported parcels
  • Consolidation and sorting manifest or linehaul shipment register showing foreign destinations and export status
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Purchase Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds within the repatriation deadline
  • Service contract or purchase order with the overseas customer for deemed export service zero-rating

VAT-registered business operators must issue tax invoices for all taxable supplies. Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for businesses required by the Value-Added Tax Act to issue e-invoices, generally including general taxpayers of substantial size, and must be transmitted to the NTS through HomeTax no later than the day following issuance. The NTS operates centralized real-time e-invoice matching and anti-fraud analytics but does not use a Latin American style fiscal clearance API, advance digital stamping, or third-party certification. Late submission, failure to issue, or intentional manipulation of e-invoices triggers electronic invoice penalties, VAT reassessment, and administrative sanctions under the Value-Added Tax Act.

If the NTS determines that export zero-rating was claimed without sufficient customs or foreign-exchange documentation, the unsubstantiated supply is reclassified as taxable and VAT at 10% is assessed, together with understatement penalties ranging from 10% to 40% of the tax evaded depending on negligence or fraud. The tax collection and assessment period is 5 years for ordinary cases and 10 years for fraudulent or deliberate evasion. Fraudulent export refund claims can result in criminal prosecution under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, including imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to three times the evaded tax, plus retrospective clawback of VAT refunds and monthly unpaid-tax additional charges. The NTS may also initiate field audits of the parcel hub's export declarations, transport documents, warehouse logs, and FX receipts to verify that the export proceeds were actually repatriated within 180 days and that the zero-rated supplies were not diverted into domestic consumption.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a broadly liberalised regime for trade in goods and services. Current-account transactions are fully convertible, with settlement generally permitted through any authorised foreign-exchange bank without prior approval; only post-transaction reporting is mandatory for statistical reconciliation. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment (FDI), are effectively open, subject to a post-reporting requirement to the MOEF/BOK if a certain threshold is exceeded; minor equity injections into an existing entity require simply a duplicate report via the receiving bank. Profit remittance is legal and straightforward for foreign-invested enterprises, but the company must submit an investment registration certificate, audited financial statements, a corporate tax clearance certificate, and supporting board minutes, and must withhold 15% corporate income tax (or a lower treaty rate if a tax treaty applies, e.g., with the investor's home jurisdiction). The Bank of Korea manages foreign-exchange liquidity by applying no mandatory surrender requirements for export proceeds, yet exporters are obliged to convert, hold, or dispose of the proceeds exclusively in an authorised bank, and must report the receipt within 30 calendar days. For capital outflows such as dividends or loan repayments, banks may request evidence of the original FDI declaration (e.g., Form B or a notification number from the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency). The principal hurdle is not the conversion stance but documentation: a Korean operational account used for substantial recurring parcel volumes will trigger enhanced scrutiny, and any mismatch between the declared cargo volume, the value of outward payments, and the purchaser of records (B2B consignee) will be treated as an indication of potential trade-based money laundering. In addition, funds inbound from jurisdictions not on the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) white list may be delayed while the bank requests a beneficial-ownership breakdown.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA, export proceeds must be credited to a domestic foreign-exchange bank account within 18 months from the date of export; upon actual receipt, the exporter must report the proceeds to the bank within 30 days. There is no mandatory conversion to Korean won, and the foreign currency may be retained, sold forward, or used for approved payments. For goods valued at over USD 50,000, the bank requires an electronic submission of the export settlement certificate; failure to report is subject to fines and may delay future remittances.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), with operational enforcement through the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only licensed 'foreign-exchange banks' may handle cross-currency remittances on behalf of a Korean entity. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut, Payoneer) are not licensed foreign-exchange banks in Korea and cannot maintain independent local-currency operational accounts for a Korean company. They may partner with a Korean tier-1 bank to provide a virtual local IBAN for collection, but the Korean entity's primary payroll, VAT, and FX settlement must flow through a domestic licensed bank. EMIs are legally barred from offering proprietary cross-border payment accounts unless they obtain a domestic payment institution licence from the Financial Services Commission, which is rarely granted to out-of-state fintechs. Therefore, for the KR parcel consolidation hub, onboarding with a tier-1 Korean commercial bank is mandatory; EMIs can serve only as a complementary collection rail when operated as a white-label wrapper over a Korean partner bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial equity injection must be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the designated capital escrow account at an authorized foreign-exchange bank. The payment reference field must include the 'Foreign Investment Notification Number' and the purpose code 901003 (foreign direct investment). The bank will verify the notification with the MOEF/BOK and Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) database, and will issue a 'Foreign Investment Certificate' upon clearance. For any inbound transfer exceeding USD 100,000, a prior report to the foreign-exchange bank is required; the bank will assign a transaction reference number disclosed to the capital verification auditor. All funds must enter the escrow account before they can be converted to KRW for operating expenditure; the conversion may be executed at the prevailing market rate with a maximum 0.5% upcharge as allowed by Korean bank guidelines.Settlement of international linehaul and courier fees should be made by SWIFT MT103 using purpose code 290010 (freight) or 320040 (international courier charges). Korean banks do not require documentary credits for services; a direct wire with underlying contract and invoice is standard. For recurring intra-country logistics charges, an automated bulk clearing mechanism (e.g., B2B interbank settlement via the Korean Financial Telecommunications & Clearings Institute) is used, and VAT-exempt invoices must be filed through the Hi-Tax portal. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only licensed 'foreign-exchange banks' may handle cross-currency remittances on behalf of a Korean entity. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut, Payoneer) are not licensed foreign-exchange banks in Korea and cannot maintain independent local-currency operational accounts for a Korean company. They may partner with a Korean tier-1 bank to provide a virtual local IBAN for collection, but the Korean entity's primary payroll, VAT, and FX settlement must flow through a domestic licensed bank. EMIs are legally barred from offering proprietary cross-border payment accounts unless they obtain a domestic payment institution licence from the Financial Services Commission, which is rarely granted to out-of-state fintechs. Therefore, for the KR parcel consolidation hub, onboarding with a tier-1 Korean commercial bank is mandatory; EMIs can serve only as a complementary collection rail when operated as a white-label wrapper over a Korean partner bank.

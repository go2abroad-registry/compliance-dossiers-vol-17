Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Korean Commercial Act; the law does not prescribe a minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, the incorporators must demonstrate that the shares subscribed have been fully paid into a blocked escrow account at a licensed Korean commercial bank, evidenced by a bank-issued capital deposit certificate attached to the court registration application. A working capital of at least KRW 10,000,000 is market practice to facilitate corporate account opening and vendor contracts. In-kind contributions must be verified by a court-appointed inspector's report under Article 314.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS), online via HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service (KCS), online via UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr), for export declarations and customs clearance records.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Korean Yuhan Hoesa, the statutory TIN/VAT ID is the 10-digit Korean Business Registration Number issued by the district NTS office through HomeTax. Step 1: determine whether the entity has a Korean domestic place of business for Corporate Tax Act and VAT Act purposes. Step 2: prepare the foreign entity incorporation certificate, articles of incorporation, evidence of the Korean office or permanent establishment, and a written appointment of a local statutory tax representative if the head office is outside Korea. Step 3: file the business registration application in the HomeTax portal (or in person at the competent district NTS office) using the non-resident entity's legal name, domestic place of business address, and representative details. Step 4: after registration, the NTS issues the Business Registration Number, which serves as the Korean TIN and VAT registration number. Step 5: for cross-border trade, register the same Business Registration Number with Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS and obtain a KCS beneficiary/user ID for electronic export declarations. Step 6: if the foreign entity has no domestic place of business but nonetheless makes taxable supplies in Korea, it must still register as a taxable person for VAT before making those supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean domestic tax law does not provide a check-the-box election. A Yuhan Hoesa is automatically taxed at the entity level under the Corporate Tax Act. A foreign corporation may be classified as a domestic or foreign corporation depending on whether its head office or actual place of effective management is located in Korea; no hybrid mismatch or passthrough election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold; mandatory VAT registration from commencement of taxable business in Korea. Simplified taxable person status may lower VAT calculation for qualifying small businesses but does not create a monetary VAT registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act, a 0% VAT rate applies to goods physically exported from Korea to a non-resident buyer through a customs export declaration, and to qualifying services supplied for use outside Korea. For the electronic patient monitor/ECG hardware business, zero-rating requires actual shipment of assembled monitors and calibration components out of Korea, transfer of title and risk to the foreign buyer, a valid export declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service, and contemporaneous evidence of export proceeds being settled in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Input VAT incurred on export-related purchases can be refunded through the periodic VAT return once export documentation is retained and substantiated.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration accepted through UNI-PASS (electronic export cargo declaration and clearance record)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the ECG patient monitors, model numbers, quantities, and export value
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight evidencing actual physical departure from Korea
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice showing 0% VAT, the buyer/seller details, and the export declaration reference
  • Purchase/sales contracts or purchase orders from the non-resident customer
  • Certificate of export proceeds receipt or foreign exchange settlement confirmation issued by an accredited Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing repatriation of the invoiced export value
  • NTS E-tax invoice submission receipt for the zero-rated export invoice
  • KCS export clearance record matched to the goods declaration

Korea operates the NTS electronic tax invoice system (E-tax invoice system) under the VAT Act. All VAT tax invoices, including zero-rated export tax invoices, must be issued electronically and transmitted to NTS through HomeTax or an authorized API by the end of the day following issuance. B2C cash receipts are mandated through the NTS cash receipt system for customer-requested or large over-the-counter purchases. There is no per-invoice pre-clearance digital stamping, but NTS validates electronic tax invoice data and maintains a real-time anti-fraud reconciliation ledger for input VAT matching, export declarations, and foreign exchange settlements.

NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-match export declarations, VAT returns, electronic tax invoices, and bank foreign exchange settlement records. If export zero-rating is claimed without actual shipment, without a valid export declaration, or without receiving export proceeds within the statutory deadline, the NTS reverses the 0% rating, assesses 10% VAT on the supply value, denies related input VAT credits, and may impose additional tax penalties under the VAT Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes, including penalty tax of up to 40% of the underpaid tax. Fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating also exposes the entity and responsible officers to criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with retrospective clawback periods of up to 5 years for ordinary under-reporting and up to 10 years for fraudulent or aggravated tax evasion.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely convertible capital account with a liberalized current account regime. The KRW is fully convertible for current account transactions such as trade settlements, service payments, and dividend remittances, subject to documentary verification and bank reporting. Capital account transactions, including inbound foreign direct investment, acquisition of domestic securities, and cross-border loans, are freely allowed but require prior or post-event reporting to the designated foreign exchange bank or the Ministry of Economy and Finance. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic foreign currency accounts or exchange freely. Profit remittances by foreign-invested companies are permissible subject to presentation of audited retained earnings, tax clearance certificates, and proof of foreign investment registration with the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The absence of multiple exchange rates and the existence of an interbank market make the KRW effectively convertible, but banks apply anti-money laundering verification to every cross-border payment. Temporary structural measures may apply during financial stress, however current regulations do not restrict conversion or transfer for legitimate business. Corporate treasurers must still obtain Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation forms, purpose codes, and maintain underlying trade or investment documentation to avoid regulatory sanctions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected within one year from the export date under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and any extension requires prior approval from the designated foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender requirement; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a domestic foreign currency account, convert to KRW at prevailing market rates, or use it for approved external payments. Receipts must be declared to the collecting bank, which verifies the underlying export declaration and issues a foreign currency receipt confirmation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (as central bank and monetary authority), operating under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance through the Foreign Exchange Market Intervention and Capital Flow Management framework

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions and offshore digital EMI platforms are not viable as primary corporate bank accounts for a South Korean legal entity. Under the Korean Banking Act and Electronic Financial Transactions Act, only licensed domestic banks and authorized foreign exchange banks can provide won and foreign currency accounts to domestic corporations, receive export proceeds, and execute capital account transactions. Offshore EMIs lack a domestic branch license in South Korea and cannot legally issue local IBANs or participate in the Korean Foreign Exchange Information System. They may be used only as supplementary payment wallets for low-value disbursements, but they cannot satisfy statutory capital verification, dividend remittance, or corporate tax payment requirements. Therefore the treasury structure must rely exclusively on domestic Tier-1 banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign direct investments must be registered under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act by filing an Investment Notification with the designated foreign exchange bank before remittance. The funds should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the investing company's bank to the Korean entity's capital escrow account held at a Tier-1 Korean bank. The remittance message must include the foreign investment purpose code, typically code 210 for acquisition of shares, and a clear reference to the registered Foreign Investment Report. After the funds arrive, the Korean bank issues a Foreign Currency Receipt Certificate, which is used by a licensed Korean accountant to prepare the statutory capital verification report. No sweep to operating accounts is permitted until the capital verification is completed and the foreign investment registration is updated. Dividends and subsequent loans require separate reporting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.Outbound trade settlements for ECG monitor components and finished devices should use SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers on ordinary open account terms, or SWIFT MT700 issued and advised letters of credit for higher-value transactions. Documentary collections using SWIFT MT400 and MT202 messages are feasible where trust exists between the Korean entity and overseas buyers. All disbursements must be matched to an export declaration filed through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system, and the remitting bank must verify the foreign exchange transaction code, invoice value, shipping date, and import country documentary evidence. Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in aggregate annual international related-party transactions for the mandatory preparation and filing of Master File and Local File; KRW 100 billion for Country-by-Country Reporting obligation for ultimate parent groups with a Korean constituent entity; smaller thresholds (KRW 5 billion) apply for related-party financial services transactions

Economic substance scope: The Korean taxing authorities apply the Economic Substance Regulation through the 'restriction of Imputed/Inordinate Transactions' rule under Article 66(2) of the Corporate Tax Act, OECD BEPS Actions 3 and 8-10, and the Korean CFC rules under Article 44 of the International Tax Coordination Act. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa claiming the benefit of a tax treaty or free-zone regime must prove sustainable core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Korea, including actual physical premises, qualified employment, and executive decisions to implement, hold, and manage the ECG telemetry patents in Korea; otherwise the NTS will disregard the legal entity and recharacterize profits to the ultimate non-resident principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Fraud / Mispriced Intercompany Sale

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may issue a transfer-pricing adjustment under Article 4 of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs; the added tax income is recharacterized as a constructive dividend to the parent, with a 20% underreporting surcharge and daily delinquency surcharge applied to the underpaid corporate tax and local income tax.

Director personal liability: The NTS will pierce the corporate veil if the director knowingly signs false intercompany contracts and invoices; the director becomes jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and surcharge; criminal proceedings under the Special Act for Punishment of Tax Evasion can impose up to six years’ imprisonment, a fine of up to 30 times the evaded tax, and seizure of personal assets located in Korea and abroad.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The NTS denies the beneficial ownership claim under Article 7 of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs and under the OECD’s limitation-on-benefits principle, treating the entity as a shell; it re-routes the income ($500,000 of ECG monitor export commission) to the ultimate non-resident shareholder and reclassifies the payments as a constructive dividend, compulsory recomputing profits at 22% withholding plus the ordinary corporate tax penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell company personally pay restitution of the understated corporate tax and withheld dividend tax; the Korean court may impose a criminal fine up to 5 billion KRW, prohibit the director from re-entering Korea, and blacklist the director’s personal transit visa; such touch court judgment also permits a Foreign Tax Credit denial and asset seizure of the director’s personal equity in the Korean legal entity.

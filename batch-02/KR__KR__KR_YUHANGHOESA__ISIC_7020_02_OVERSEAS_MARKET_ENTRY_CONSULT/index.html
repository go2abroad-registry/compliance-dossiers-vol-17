Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is imposed on a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act; a member may subscribe for even 1 Korean won if the capital is fully paid. Nevertheless, a pre-incorporation bank account must be opened and the full amount must be deposited in cash and remain blocked (escrowed) until the registration is accepted. The filing fee shown is a representative estimate for KRW 100,000,000 of subscribed capital, computed by statute as 0.4% registration tax, a 20% education surtax, and the court registration fee of 0.04%, totaling roughly USD 250; the exact fee follows the amount of subscribed capital in the individually filed Articles. A separate escrow account is not required unless the bank's money-laundering policies or Foreign Investment Promotion Act reporting rules impose an interim blocking procedure for funds remitted from abroad.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr). For export evidence and customs clearance: Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a Korean domestic Yuhan Hoesa: within 20 days of business commencement, the company files a Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office through HomeTax; NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the unified corporate tax identification number and VAT registration identifier. For a foreign non-resident entity with no domestic place of business: the foreign entity must appoint a Korean tax representative, submit a Business Registration Application under the non-resident business registration procedure, and attach certified proof of foreign incorporation, constitutional documents, evidence of foreign tax residence, a power of attorney for the tax representative, and details of the intended Korean taxable activities. Registration is mandatory before the taxable supply occurs, and NTS issues the same 10-digit Business Registration Number for all subsequent VAT filing, e-tax invoice issuance, and withholding obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Korean Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and is taxed on worldwide income at the entity level; no check-the-box or pass-through classification is available for Korean domestic corporations.

Available election forms

None. Korea does not maintain check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections. A foreign corporation whose place of effective management is in Korea may be statutorily reclassified as a domestic corporation, but this is a facts-and-circumstances determination rather than an elective regime. The simplified taxable person election under the Value-Added Tax Act is a VAT compliance option, not an entity income tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold triggers VAT registration. All business operators making taxable supplies in Korea must register with NTS within 20 days of business commencement. A small business with prior-year taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000 may qualify as a simplified taxable person, but still must be registered. Non-resident entities are required to register when they begin taxable supplies in Korea, including cross-border digital or consulting services subject to Korean VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods when the export declaration has been accepted by the Korea Customs Service, the goods are physically removed from Korea, and the foreign sale is evidenced by standard international trade documents. For services, including offshore market-entry consultancy under ISIC 7020, zero-rating applies when the service is supplied to a non-resident or a foreign place of consumption, is actually rendered or consumed outside Korea, and the consideration is received in foreign exchange through an authorized Korean foreign exchange bank. The taxpayer must retain contracts, work deliverables, engagement correspondence, and bank-certified exchange receipts to prove the foreign consumption. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated export supplies is refundable, and export-oriented businesses may apply for early VAT refunds through NTS when input tax exceeds output VAT.

  • Export Declaration Certificate (Export Clearance Confirmation) issued by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical export of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation issued by an authorized Korean foreign exchange bank
  • For services: signed consulting and market-entry engagement contract, delivered advisory reports, documentation of offshore use by the foreign client, and foreign exchange receipt evidence

Electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) are mandatory for all B2B supplies under the Korean VAT regime. E-tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS through HomeTax, either via the web portal or certified ERP/API integration, no later than the day following issuance. NTS validates e-invoices against Business Registration Numbers in real time and maintains a central audit database. There is no physical fiscal stamping or continuous customs-style clearance for domestic invoices; the mandatory next-day electronic transmission serves as the anti-fraud control. Failure to issue or transmit an e-tax invoice in the prescribed manner triggers additional tax penalties and may deny input VAT deduction to the recipient.

NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-match export declarations, e-tax invoice data, customs exit records, and bank foreign-exchange receipts to verify zero-rating claims. If the export or foreign-exchange documentation is deficient, false, or inconsistent, NTS reclassifies the supply as domestic, assesses VAT at 10% on the full supply value, and adds statutory withholding-adjusted additional taxes and penalties, including late-payment interest and e-tax invoice penalties. In cases involving fraudulent export invoices, fabricated FIRCs, or sham offshore service arrangements, the taxpayer faces retrospective clawback of all VAT refunds and criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with potential fines and imprisonment for the responsible directors and tax representatives.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a market-convertible regime for the current account, while the capital account is managed rather than fully liberalized. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and its Enforcement Decree, ordinary service-related payments, including market feasibility studies and regulatory licensing agency fees payable by this Yuhan Hoesa, may be converted and remitted freely through authorized foreign exchange banks, subject to documentation and reporting. Korean won is convertible on current-account transactions without general surrender requirements: corporates may retain foreign currency receipts in onshore foreign currency accounts or convert into Korean won at market rates. The capital account, however, imposes ex ante report/license obligations for certain inbound and outbound capital transactions, debt, equity, derivatives and financial guarantees. Profit remittances from a Korean LLC are generally allowed if the company has audited distributable profits, payment of corporate tax, and remittance through a bank with supporting board resolutions, balance sheet, tax payment certificate and investors' source documents; the bank may not reserve outright discretionary approval but will file the transaction through Korea's Foreign Exchange Information System. Although Korea avoids surrender quotas, banks remain mandatory checkpoints under FETA for verifying supporting documents and reporting material movements. This creates gatekeeping rather than conversion prohibitions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio. Export proceeds for goods or services must be collected within five years from the date of export declaration or service completion, unless an authorized foreign exchange bank or the Minister of Economy and Finance grants an extension. Once collected, the Korean company may hold the foreign currency in an onshore foreign currency transaction account or sell it to the bank at the market rate; no compulsory conversion into Korean won applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK); foreign exchange regulation and reporting are jointly administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) pursuant to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Korean financial regulation treats banking, deposit-taking and cross-border FX payment services as licensed activities under the Banking Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise or neo-banks cannot lawfully open regulated KRW transaction accounts or hold settlement balances on behalf of this Yuhan Hoesa unless they are separately registered/licensed in South Korea as electronic financial business operators or foreign exchange dealers, maintain a Korean physical presence, and satisfy local CDD/AML requirements. In practice the fintech cannot replace a tier-1 domestic commercial bank as the legal clearing participant for inbound capital or outbound service payments; at best it may be used as a reporting/payment-initiation layer on top of the bank account but is not a permissible primary treasury or FX settlement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection should be routed by direct SWIFT MT103 from the overseas corporate treasury to Yuhan Hoesa's settlement account at a designated Korean tier-1 bank. The remitter must use the deposit/payment purpose category for non-resident equity participation and provide a capital-contribution declaration; the Korean bank reports the receipt via Korea's Foreign Exchange Information System under FETA. If the non-resident owner wishes to qualify as a foreign-invested enterprise, the investment must be reported to the Ministry of Economy and Finance/KOTRA through the designated bank before wiring; after remittance the bank issues a certificate of foreign exchange purchase and credit advice, and capital verification is evidenced by the foreign investment registration certificate, receipt statement, bank confirmation and corporate registry filings. Transfer should not be fragmented to avoid reporting thresholds.The default clearing rail for consultancy-related outbound payments is SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer executed through the Korean bank's correspondent network, accompanied by the service contract, tax invoice, corporate signature mandate and board approval. Commercial documentary letters of credit are inappropriate for intangible consulting services; Documentary Collections may be considered only when the overseas subcontractor requires cash-against-documents, but the underlying transfers still settle as MT103/202 messages after documents have been checked. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Korean financial regulation treats banking, deposit-taking and cross-border FX payment services as licensed activities under the Banking Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise or neo-banks cannot lawfully open regulated KRW transaction accounts or hold settlement balances on behalf of this Yuhan Hoesa unless they are separately registered/licensed in South Korea as electronic financial business operators or foreign exchange dealers, maintain a Korean physical presence, and satisfy local CDD/AML requirements. In practice the fintech cannot replace a tier-1 domestic commercial bank as the legal clearing participant for inbound capital or outbound service payments; at best it may be used as a reporting/payment-initiation layer on top of the bank account but is not a permissible primary treasury or FX settlement rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; a Master File may also be required when the consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to Korean ultimate parent entities with consolidated group revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies a codified substance-over-form rule under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 14. For a Korean LLC providing overseas market-entry consulting, economic substance is tested by the presence of a real office, locally engaged employees who manage clients, actual negotiation and contracting activities, own banking, invoicing, board decisions, and ongoing risk management. The Core Income-Generating Activities of the consultancy must occur in Korea; if the foreign parent alone exercises strategic management and the Korean LLC is merely a booking vehicle, the National Tax Service will disregard the LLC or attribute income to the foreign parent under anti-abuse doctrines.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Hidden Offshore Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Punishment of Tax Offenses Act Article 3, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine of between 3 times and 5 times the evaded tax. Under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-2, a fraudulent understatement of tax liabilities additionally triggers a 40% penalty on the underpaid corporate tax and daily interest of 0.022% until paid.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 39; the director who exercises effective control is secondarily liable for the unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and late-interest. Criminal conviction leads to personal imprisonment, confiscation of undisclosed offshore assets, and immigration blacklisting by the National Tax Service including a departure ban where delinquent tax exceeds KRW 50 million.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may make an arm's-length adjustment under the International Tax Adjustment Act, impose an additional tax of up to 30% of the corrected taxable income, impose a KRW 10 million fine for incomplete documentation, and assess late-payment interest on the resulting deficiency. If the scheme is accompanied by false invoices, Punishment of Tax Offenses Act penalties apply as well.

Director personal liability: The director who knowingly approves fabricated intercompany contracts or misstates transfer pricing schedules is personally jointly and severally liable under the veil-piercing doctrine when corporate assets are stripped to the foreign parent. Criminal referral for fraudulent transfer pricing records carries imprisonment up to 5 years; the director may be placed on the National Tax Service foreign-departure blacklist and denied clearance certificates.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping Conduit

Statutory sanction: Framework Act on National Taxes Articles 14 and 34 authorize the National Tax Service to deny the artificial corporate form and treaty benefit, reclassify the distributions as Korean-source taxable dividends, impose the 22% unconditional withholding rate, and apply a 40% unjustified understatement penalty. If financial transactions require reporting under the Act on Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information, failure to file a suspicious transaction report can lead to fines and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: A director who engineered the shell arrangement loses the protection of limited liability; courts may pierce the veil and recover unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and interest from the director's personal estate. In serious cases the director faces criminal imprisonment for tax fraud or money laundering, asset forfeiture orders, public disclosure as a major delinquent taxpayer, and a travel ban issued during criminal investigation and tax collection proceedings.

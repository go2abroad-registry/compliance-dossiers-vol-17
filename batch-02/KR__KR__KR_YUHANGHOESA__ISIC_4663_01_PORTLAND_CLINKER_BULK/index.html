Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Korean Commercial Act imposes no statutory minimum stated capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. The only capital rule is that each founding member must pay the full amount of his or her subscribed contribution in cash or in kind before the incorporation registration is filed. No blocked escrow account is mandated by law. For cash contributions, the Court Registry requires a bank-issued capital deposit certificate. For in-kind contributions, an independent appraiser valuation report approved by the Court Registry is required. For the intended Portland clinker wholesale and ocean-borne trade business, the stated capital should nevertheless be sufficient to evidence creditworthiness for letters of credit, vessel charter payments, customs duties, and port terminal deposits.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory turnover threshold for corporations; VAT registration is mandatory upon first taxable supply of goods or services in Korea. The simplified taxpayer regime for very small entrepreneurs applies only to qualifying individual traders, not to a Yuhan Hoesa LLC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act, the export of goods is zero-rated where the goods are actually exported from Korea, the Korean VAT payer is the exporter named in the customs export declaration, the sale is made to a foreign purchaser, and the consideration is paid in foreign currency (or under a specific foreign-exchange arrangement) and repatriated to Korea. For the current scope of portland clinker in bulk for ocean vessel freight and wholesale distribution, zero-rating is available only on documented export sales with customs exit confirmation and foreign exchange earnings substantiation. Offshore digital services likewise qualify for zero-rating when supplied exclusively to non-residents and paid for in foreign currency, but goods export is the primary applicable category under this task payload.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronically filed through Korean UNI-PASS export clearance system)
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean Bill of Lading evidencing export vessel shipment)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent proof of foreign currency repatriation issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank

All registered VAT taxpayers must issue electronic tax invoices (ETIs) for business-to-business supplies and transmit them through the NTS HomeTax System (ETS). ETIs must be issued and sent to the NTS no later than the day following the supply date. The NTS maintains near-real-time automated cross-checking of electronic tax invoices and cash-receipt data to detect invoice fraud; there is no separate physical fiscal stamping regime but electronic invoice data submission is a mandatory statutory compliance duty. Failure to issue or transmit an ETI subjects the supplier to VAT penalties and assesses the purchaser's input tax disallowance.

The NTS will retrospectively claw back zero-rated VAT and assess 10% VAT on the export invoice amount if the required customs export declaration, ocean bill of lading, or foreign exchange repatriation evidence is missing, falsified, or inconsistent. Additional penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value Added Tax Act range from 10% (negligent underpayment) to 40% (dishonest fraud) of the assessed tax, plus daily overdue interest of 0.025% per day. Deliberate export-evidence fraud is a criminal offense carrying potential imprisonment up to three years and fines up to KRW 30,000,000.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised but supervised convertibility regime. Current-account and most capital-account transactions are permitted under a negative-list system and may proceed after a declaration or ex-post report through a designated foreign exchange bank. The Korean won is not freely convertible outside the onshore system; all corporate KRW funding, dividend remittances, and trade payments must be routed through domestic banks with proper Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS/EFDS) reporting. For a non-resident-owned Yuhan Hoesa in the wholesale cement and vessel freight business, equity contributions are treated as inbound direct investment and are freely convertible after the statutory report and bank verification. Export proceeds from clinker/cement sales may be received in foreign currency and retained onshore, but they must be collected within the statutory export-collection window tied to each export declaration. Profit remittance requires a bank withholding on the dividend, plus confirmation that the domestic entity has filed corporate tax and that the distribution is not classified as a disguised outflow. Capital-account items such as shareholder loans, guarantees, and derivative positions require underlying trade or investment evidence and are subject to bona-fide exposure reviews.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion ratio is applied under current Korean foreign-exchange policy; export collections may be held in a designated foreign-currency account. The statutory collection timeline for general export proceeds is 18 months from the date of export declaration; if vessel freight or bulk clinker delivery timing causes later receipt, the exporter must apply for an extension before the deadline. The full invoice amount must be collected or supported by approved discounts, weight/quality tolerances, or short-delivery/insurance adjustment documentation; any unjustified shortfall will require a formal FEIS explanation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting as the monetary and foreign exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), the Financial Services Commission, and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit jointly overseeing foreign-exchange information and AML enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not a viable primary rail for Yuhan Hoesa. Korean law reserves the operation of foreign-exchange business and KRW account services to domestic banks and licensed foreign-exchange companies; a foreign EMI with no Korean FETA licence cannot legally open a KRW settlement account, issue corporate cards, or perform FEIS declaration for trade payments. Such EMIs may only support ancillary multi-currency payment products through a domestic partner, but the resulting funds flow would still settle via a domestic bank. Therefore the treasury blueprint excludes fintech EMIs and designates tier-1 domestic banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity is remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the Yuhan Hoesa capital account at the selected domestic bank. The remitter purpose message must state Equity contribution to Yuhan Hoesa, Republic of Korea; the receiving bank will register the transaction in the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) and, where the contribution exceeds the Foreign Investment Promotion Act threshold, require an FDI declaration reference from MOEF/KOTRA before crediting. The bank then verifies the paid-in amount against the corporate registration and issues a capital receipt/capital verification certificate for the Yuhan Hoesa filing. No third-party collection account, unlicensed EMI, or cash deposit can be used for a compliant capital injection.Disbursements use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 covers, irrevocable documentary letters of credit under UCP600, or documentary collections under URC 522, depending on the supplier terms. For bulk clinker and cement, an irrevocable sight LC opened by the Korean bank is recommended to protect both seller and buyer. The bank will settle after checking the documents against the LC terms, including B/L, invoice, insurance, inspection certificate, and vessel notification. Trade payments for ocean freight are cleared as service payments through MT103 with the freight invoice and vessel charter party retained in the FEIS file.Onshore deliverable USD/KRW forward contracts, available to the entity only when the amount and tenor do not exceed the identified export invoice, import trade payable, or ocean-freight exposure, with the bank reviewed at each rollover.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts, permitted only as a hedge against committed foreign currency flows and only where the domestic bank accepts an NDF structure for a client without the ability to deliver the KRW leg; the notional must be validated by the underlying LC or trade contract.Cross-currency swaps and interest-rate currency swaps, allowed only where a confirmed corporate loan or structured trade-finance obligation exists in matching currency; speculative or unrelated FX positions will not be approved by the Korean bank.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company) is legally required to appoint a certified public accountant and undergo an independent statutory audit under the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies if total assets exceed KRW 12,000,000,000 (approximately USD 9,300,000) at the end of the preceding fiscal year. In addition, an audit is mandatory where total assets exceed the lower Presidential Decree threshold of KRW 8,000,000,000 (approximately USD 6,200,000) and annual gross turnover exceeds KRW 30,000,000,000 (approximately USD 23,100,000). Listed entities, public-interest companies, and subsidiaries of audit-designated business groups are also subject to statutory audit regardless of individual entity size. Employment headcount alone does not trigger the audit mandate for a Yuhan Hoesa, but a company with 300 or more employees that is part of a designated audit-target business group is captured through group-level designation.

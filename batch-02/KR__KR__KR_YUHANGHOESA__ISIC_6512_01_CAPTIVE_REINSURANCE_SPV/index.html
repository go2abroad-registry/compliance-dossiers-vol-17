A virtual office, shared commercial mailbox, or correspondence-only address is not permissible for a Yuhan Hoesa with a captive reinsurance business scope under ISIC 6512. The Financial Services Commission and the Financial Supervisory Service require licensed financial entities to maintain a fixed, independently identifiable physical place of business in the Republic of Korea, with a visible nameplate, physical records, telephone line, and at least one director or responsible employee present during business hours. None. Korea does not provide a check-the-box or pass-through election for a Yuhan Hoesa. It is per se a taxable corporation. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 / no monetary threshold. A domestic corporation is compulsorily required to register for VAT before business commencement under Article 8 of the Value-Added Tax Act regardless of expected turnover. The captive reinsurance SPV must remain VAT-registered and file periodic VAT returns, reporting zero-rated and exempt supplies with corresponding restrictions on input VAT recovery for exempt financial and insurance services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods and exported services supplied to non-residents are eligible for a 0% VAT rate if the transaction is substantiated by the statutory export documentation and foreign exchange repatriation evidence. For the captive reinsurance SPV, premiums received from a foreign ceding insurance group company can qualify as zero-rated exported services when the reinsurance contract is with a non-resident, the risk is located outside Korea, the premium is paid in a convertible foreign currency from an overseas account, and the service is economically rendered from Korea. If the zero-rating criteria are not met, the reinsurance service is ordinarily an exempt financial/insurance service under Article 26 of the Value-Added Tax Act, making the SPV unable to recover related input VAT. For qualifying offshore digital services, the same foreign-currency payment and non-resident recipient conditions apply, plus contemporaneous evidence that the digital service is not effectively used or consumed in Korea.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration Confirmation (export bill of entry filed through UNI-PASS), including the HS commodity description and declared value in Korean won or foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by KR_YUHANGHOESA to the non-resident buyer
  • Original Bill of Lading or, for air cargo, Air Waybill evidencing removal of goods from Korea; for captive reinsurance service exports, the executed reinsurance contract, placement slip, and board underwriting minutes evidence the offshore supply
  • Bank-issued Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate (FIRC-equivalent foreign exchange settlement receipt issued by an authorized Korean foreign exchange bank) proving that the premium or export price was wired from the non-resident counterparty in convertible foreign currency within the statutory export settlement period

All VAT invoices in Korea must be issued and transmitted as Electronic Tax Invoices (ETIs) through the NTS HomeTax portal or a certified e-tax invoice service provider. ETI data must be transmitted to the NTS by the end of the day following the date of issuance, creating a real-time self-declared clearance and audit trail. There is no government fiscal stamp or pre-clearance authorization before invoice issuance; anti-fraud controls are achieved through real-time reporting, sequential invoice numbers, NTS certification of issuers, matching of purchaser submissions, and automatic exception analysis of missing or inconsistent ETIs. Taxpayers integrate accounting systems with the HomeTax OpenAPI or Internet-EDI channels for automatic invoice transmission. Failure to issue or transmit an ETI triggers automatic statutory additional tax, and deliberate omission is treated as a tax evasion indicator. For cross-border reinsurance services, the Korean yuhan hoesa must ensure that any domestic reverse-charge obligation for imported exempt or taxable services is correctly self-assessed, while retained evidence of zero-rating is kept contemporaneously.

The NTS may reopen an export zero-rating claim within the extended statutory reassessment period, which can reach 10 years for fraudulent or untruthful returns under the Framework Act on National Taxes. If the customs declaration, FX wiring, or reinsurance contract is not substantiated, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at the standard 10% VAT rate, with assessment of VAT, statutory additional tax for unfaithful returns or non-issuance of electronic tax invoices, and daily delayed-payment interest on the unpaid amount. False invoices, sham wiring, circular foreign-currency flows, or artificial offshore contractual structures are referred for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, subjecting the statutory representative, directors, and materially contributing tax advisers to penalties including imprisonment and fines. For captive reinsurance, the NTS also scrutinizes economic substance, premium pricing against arm's-length standards, capital reserve adequacy, and compliance with the Insurance Business Act, and any clawback is retrospective from the date the original zero-rated supply was claimed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won (KRW) operates under a partially convertible foreign exchange regime. Current account transactions, including reinsurance premium receipts for services rendered, are largely liberalized but remain subject to declaration and electronic filing through EMTRAC. Capital account transactions, including equity injections, foreign currency debt, and certain hedging products, require a preemptive declaration with a foreign exchange bank unless an explicit exception applies. The BOK and the MOEF maintain strict monitoring over cross-border funds flows to detect sudden reversal or speculative activity. For profit remittance, a captive reinsurer must first complete annual financial audits, settle all corporate and withholding taxes, and obtain a tax clearance certificate from the National Tax Service before receiving approval to remit dividends. Surrender requirements are operational, not conversion-like: exporters and service receivers must bring proceeds within a statutory deadline and may maintain the proceeds in foreign currency accounts without mandatory conversion. However, every receipt must be reported to EMTRAC, and conversion transactions must be executed through authorized foreign exchange banks at market rates. The convertibility is therefore full for operational settlements but contains procedural frictions and verification stages for capital flows, which must be baked into the treasury timeline.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export and service proceeds, including premium income under a captive reinsurance SPV, must be collected within one year from the date of the relevant trade declaration, extendable up to an additional six months upon MOEF approval. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; the entity may hold or utilise the foreign currency in a designated foreign currency account. All receipts must be individually reported to EMTRAC within one business day of collection.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Information System (EMTRAC) administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) cannot serve as the primary banking layer for a Korean-domiciled captive reinsurer. The Electronic Financial Transactions Act requires any entity holding customer funds or issuing multi-currency accounts to hold a Korean banking or electronic financial business license. Foreign EMIs such as Revolut or Wise Business do not hold such license and cannot maintain local settlement rails into EMTRAC, nor can they execute BOK/USD FX deliveries. Their utility is limited to payment card initiation and does not satisfy the statutory repatriation and tax reporting requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the designated Capital Deposit Escrow Account under the Korean bank's Foreign Currency Trading Code 201 (capital payment receipt). The remitter shall complete the EMTRAC report within one business day using Purpose Code 601 (Investment in Korean branch/subsidiary). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top corporate income tax bracket; with 10% local surtax, aggregate effective top rate 26.4%. Lower taxable-income brackets: 9% (for taxable income up to KRW 200 million) and 19% (for taxable income over KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate (20% national dividend WHT + 2% local surtax) when a Korean corporation distributes dividends to non-residents; reduced by DTA to 0-15% (e.g., 10% for direct shareholdings of at least 10% under the US-Korea treaty; 0-5% under certain treaties, subject to domestic law).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000 for filing the International Transaction Report; Master File and Local File are required when aggregate foreign-related-party transactions exceed KRW 10,000,000,000 in a fiscal year, unless the SPV qualifies as a small business (documentation excused).

Economic substance scope: Korea has no statutory Economic Substance Regulation akin to UAE ESR, but under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the National Tax Service will apply the 'real business purpose and substance over form' doctrine in audits. A captive reinsurance SPV must prove its own premises, full-time and qualified personnel (actuary, risk manager, legal counsel), board meetings physically held in Korea, actual management of underwriting and investment decisions, and assumption of material risk. Failure to do so may result in disregarding the SPV for CIT purposes, reallocating income to its foreign parent, denying DTA benefits, and treating undistributed profits as deemed dividends for CFC purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Sham Reinsurance Transactions

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Punishment Tax Act, tax fraud is punishable with imprisonment up to 3 years, plus a fine up to 3 times the tax evaded; additionally, the corporate tax underreporting surcharge is 40% of the underpaid tax under Article 47-4 of the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or negligently failed to prevent the sham arrangements are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties as transferors under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act. A criminal conviction includes imprisonment (up to 3 years), confiscation of proceeds, asset seizure, travel ban (blacklist), and disqualification from future directorship.

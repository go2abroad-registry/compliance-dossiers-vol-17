Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. South Korean law does not impose a minimum paid-up capital requirement for a Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company). However, the total issued shares must be fully paid in at the time of incorporation, and the paid-in amount must be deposited into a temporary bank account in the name of the company or its incorporator, with a certificate of deposit issued by the bank. The funds are released only upon completion of incorporation registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic tax administration portal; export-clearance matching is performed with Korea Customs Service under the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Yuhan Hoesa, the foreign non-resident entity first forms the Korean limited company at the competent district court registry. The company must then file a business registration application with the National Tax Service district tax office that has jurisdiction over its registered place of business no later than 20 days from incorporation or commencement of business; the standard filing requests a 10-digit business registration number that simultaneously functions as the corporate enterprise taxpayer ID, VAT registration ID, and withholding tax account number. Required evidentiary package includes the corporate registration certificate issued by the court registry, articles of incorporation, proof of registered address, identity and passport documents of the legal representative, and, where applicable, the foreign investment certificate issued under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. If the foreign non-resident has no local place of business and no Korean subsidiary, it may use a tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes to obtain a business registration number for VAT withholding, e-invoicing, and refund purposes from the tax office with jurisdiction over that tax representative, depending on National Tax Service practice. The issued registration certificate confirms the legal entity classification as a domestic corporation for corporate tax and as a VAT-registered enterprise for indirect tax purposes.

Korean tax law does not provide a check-the-box pass-through election or a hybrid-mismatch election for a Yuhan Hoesa. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0; no monetary VAT registration exemption applies to a domestic incorporated Yuhan Hoesa. Business registration must be submitted within 20 days of business commencement, and the resulting business registration certificate includes the mandatory VAT registration identifier. A pure equity holding SPV holding passive shares and receiving corporate dividends is registered for VAT administration purposes but has no VAT output tax liability on dividend receipts because dividends are outside the scope of VAT. Small-business simplified VAT filing rules do not create an exemption threshold for corporations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from South Korea are zero-rated under the VAT Act only where an electronic export declaration has been filed with Korea Customs Service, the goods actually leave the Korean customs territory within the statutory period, and the export proceeds are repatriated in a foreign-exchange settlement transaction to Korea in the name of the exporting enterprise. Offshore services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated where the place of supply is outside Korea; for digital services, the place of supply rules apply and the supplier must retain evidence of consumer residence and foreign payment. For the ISIC 6420 pure equity holding SPV, dividend receipts from subsidiary shares are not supplies at all for VAT, so they are neither taxable nor zero-rated; if the SPV later licenses intangibles or sells services abroad, the same export zero-rating evidence set is required.

  • Korea Customs Service electronic export declaration (customs clearance certificate, commonly evidencing the exporter, shipper, commodity code, quantity, and FOB value)
  • Commercial invoice issued by the exporting entity
  • Packing list and transport documents such as the bill of lading or air waybill
  • Foreign exchange settlement confirmation / inward remittance certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank
  • Zero-rated export electronic tax invoice with the export declaration number inserted
  • Foreign buyer purchase order or contract if requested in a refund audit

VAT-registered entities must issue electronic tax invoices through the National Tax Service HomeTax electronic tax invoice system for every taxable supply to another VAT-registered business. Transaction details must be transmitted to tax administration in near real time through the mandated electronic tax invoice clearance channel; the National Tax Service validates and assigns a digital invoice number and digital signature preserving a fiscal anti-fraud stamp. Businesses subject to the mandate must integrate their ERP, accounting, or billing systems with the National Tax Service application programming interface or use the HomeTax portal, and every electronic tax invoice must contain the zero-rate or standard-rate code, supplier and customer business registration numbers, supply value, VAT amount, and export declaration identifier where applicable. Paper invoices are reserved for prescribed exceptional cases; intentionally issuing forged, duplicated, or untransmitted tax invoices triggers the electronic invoice fraud penalty provisions. The mandatory cash receipt system supplements VAT control for B2C payments where a customer requests a cash receipt.

The National Tax Service automatically cross-matches zero-rating claims against Korea Customs Service export declarations, electronic tax invoices, and foreign exchange bank settlement data. If a zero-rated supply is not supported by the required customs or foreign-exchange documentation, or if export proceeds are not repatriated within the mandatory 180-day foreign-exchange settlement window and no lawful extension has been approved, the 0% rate is retroactively overturned. The Korean exporter must repay the input VAT previously attributed or offset, pay 10% output VAT on the export price, and is liable for statutory penalty surcharges under the Framework Act on National Taxes, including understatement or unfaithful return penalties and the statutory daily late-payment penalty. Intentional fabrication of export declarations, bills of lading, or FIRCs is punishable under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act with imprisonment and fines, and the retrospective clawback follows the ordinary VAT correction period, extended for fraudulent omission. Any VAT refund already issued is recovered with statutory interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized but closely supervised convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current-account transactions, such as dividends, royalties, trade payments, and operational disbursements, are fully convertible and require only standard bank verification of underlying supporting documents. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity subscriptions, cross-border borrowing, and investment from the holding SPV, remain subject to a risk-based reporting and approval system administered by the Bank of Korea through designated foreign exchange banks. Foreign capital can generally be wired into a domestic KRW or foreign-currency account without a shutdown period, but the corresponding FX trade reporting code must be accurately filed and the bank must validate the underlying transaction. There is no statutory surrender of export proceeds; Korean resident exporters may retain foreign exchange in domestic foreign-currency deposit accounts. For a non-resident shareholder injecting equity into a Yuhan Hoesa, the practical hurdles are mostly documentary and compliance-driven rather than discretionary: capital verification requires an actual receipt of funds, a bank-issued foreign currency trading certificate, and corporate registration amendments filed within the statutory deadline. Dividend repatriation requires evidence of distributable reserves, corporate tax filing confirmation, applicable withholding tax remittance, and the mandatory reporting of outward remittance. Bank compliance committees scrutinize the economic substance behind a passive holding SPV. Therefore, day-to-day convertibility is high for established treasury flows, but AML and beneficial-ownership documentation gaps can delay both capital contributions and profit remittances if the holding SPV lacks a transparent corporate structure and clean source-of-funds proof.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its enforcement decree, export proceeds must be collected through a designated foreign exchange bank within 18 months from the date the export declaration is accepted, unless the Bank of Korea grants a justified extension. There is currently no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender requirement; the exporter may choose to maintain foreign currency receipts in a foreign-currency deposit account. If the SPV or its operating export subsidiaries require KRW liquidity, conversion is executed at prevailing market rates through a licensed bank and must be recorded under the appropriate macroeconomic foreign-exchange transaction code.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Ministry of Economy and Finance retains broad foreign-exchange policy oversight and issuing authority for key public notices.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not viable for this Korean corporate treasury mandate. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and prudential banking rules, only financial institutions licensed in South Korea and registered with the Financial Supervisory Service may conduct corporate foreign-exchange business, open KRW settlement accounts, access domestic interbank clearing rails, or file the mandatory FX transaction codes with the Bank of Korea. Offshore fintech EMIs cannot provide a legally valid Korean corporate account, cannot execute statutory capital verification, and cannot report equity or trade-related foreign-exchange transactions on behalf of a resident Yuhan Hoesa. Therefore, the client must use a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank for all KRW-denominated and cross-border settlement activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a compliant inbound capital injection into the Yuhan Hoesa, the wiring bank must debit the overseas parent account and route funds via SWIFT MT103 to the SPV's designated domestic account at a licensed Korean bank. The remitter must be identical to the subscribing shareholder and the FX purpose code must identify an inward equity investment or foreign direct investment. The Korean bank will require the corporate registration certificate, board resolution, share subscription documentation, and source-of-funds evidence before posting value. After receipt, the bank issues a foreign currency trading certificate for capital verification purposes, the relevant capital increase is registered with the Korean corporate registry, and the foreign-exchange report is filed with the Bank of Korea through the bank within the regulatory timeline. Funds should not be commingled with trade receivable proceeds or operating income in the capital escrow account.Outbound treasury disbursements should be cleared using SWIFT MT103 for operating expenses, intercompany service fees, dividends, and other non-trade payments. Physical trade settlements should be structured through commercial letters of credit governed by UCP600 or documentary collections, supported by invoices, bills of lading, packing lists, customs declarations, and the relevant FX purpose codes. The Korean bank executes cross-border legs through its correspondent banking network, while domestic KRW clearing between local accounts operates through BOK-Wire and the banking settlement infrastructure. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; Master File is mandatory when group consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and foreign related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; the NTS may request contemporaneous evidence for any lower-value transaction where the price is not arm's length

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations reporting regime, but Article 4 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, the CFC rules, and treaty beneficial ownership doctrines require genuine commercial substance; for a pure equity holding SPV, the Core Income-Generating Activities are acquisition, holding, financing, management, and disposal decisions over the subsidiary shares, and those functions must be performed by the Korean board with offices, records, bank accounts, personnel, and board minutes in Korea

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act penalizes fraud or other unlawful acts to evade corporate tax by imprisonment not exceeding two years or a fine not exceeding twice the evaded tax; the understated amount is also subject to a 40% unlawful understatement penalty under the Framework Act on Taxes plus statutory late-payment interest

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the false return can be treated as secondary tax obligors under Article 12 of the National Tax Collection Act, exposing their personal assets to civil execution, a travel ban (Blacklisting), and individual criminal imprisonment for tax evasion
DTA Abuse / Beneficial Ownership Conduit

Statutory sanction: Article 4 of the International Tax Adjustment Act allows the NTS to disregard the conduit and recharacterize the income; Corporate Tax Act Article 98(2) then requires the full 22% statutory withholding, additional tax penalties up to 40% for fraudulent understatement, and criminal prosecution under Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act if the DTA claim is intentional and fraudulent

Director personal liability: Directors and officers acting as withholding agents are personally liable for taxes withheld or under-withheld; personal bank accounts and property may be seized, travel officials may impose a travel ban (Blacklisting), and intentional participation in the conduit scheme carries criminal imprisonment
Shell Company / AML and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Incomplete or false beneficial ownership and suspicious transaction reporting violates the Act on Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Transaction Information, carrying criminal fines and imprisonment under the AML and proceeds-of-crime laws; the shell arrangement is also attacked under Article 4 of the International Tax Adjustment Act and Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act as an unlawful scheme to conceal taxable income

Director personal liability: Nominee and de facto directors cannot use corporate separateness to avoid liability; courts can order civil restitution of diverted assets, confiscate criminal proceeds, impose departure restrictions and a travel ban (Blacklisting), and impose personal imprisonment for AML and tax fraud offenses

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is strongly disfavored and generally not acceptable for a Korean Yuhan Hoesa in the EdTech subscription sector. The Commercial Act requires a registered office at a specific, findable location for service of process, and the National Tax Service requires a real place of business for corporate tax registration, local VAT handling, business invoice issuance, and payroll. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Yuhan Hoesa. The Articles of Incorporation must state the capital amount, and all subscribed capital must be fully paid before the incorporation registration is filed. The paid-in capital must be deposited into a bank account opened in the name of the company and evidenced by a certificate of capital deposit. No blocked escrow account is required beyond the pre-registration restricted deposit period.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with the official electronic fiscal portal known as the HomeTax System (Hometax)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company) must obtain a business registration number from the NTS district tax office with jurisdiction over its Korean principal place of business, or if there is no Korean domestic place of business, from the Seoul Regional Tax Office or by appointing a tax representative in Korea. The foreign entity must file an application for business registration through the NTS HomeTax System or in person at the competent tax office, attaching its articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation or foreign company registration evidence, ownership and capital structure documentation, proof of the registered place of business or authorization of a domestic tax representative, and a power of attorney granting the tax representative the authority to accept Korean tax documents and filings. The NTS will then assign a 10-digit business registration number that functions as the Korean taxpayer identification number for corporate income tax, value-added tax, and withholding tax purposes. For a foreign non-resident entity with no fixed establishment, VAT registration obligations commence with the first provision of taxable supplies in Korea, and the appointed tax representative is jointly obliged to file and pay VAT on behalf of the foreign principal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None in the sense of a US-style check-the-box election. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa is subject to entity-level corporation tax under the Corporation Tax Act and cannot elect pass-through or partnership treatment. A qualifying domestic holding company with wholly owned Korean subsidiaries may elect consolidated corporate income tax filing under the Corporation Tax Act. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold; any person making taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea is required to register for VAT regardless of turnover. Foreign non-resident suppliers of digital services to Korean customers are required to register for VAT through a tax representative beginning 1 July 2015, with no monetary threshold exemption. Domestic small businesses with annual gross receipts below KRW 80 million may be classified as simplified taxable persons, but this classification does not remove the obligation to register for VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment is available for goods that are exported from Korea under a valid export declaration filed with the Korea Customs Service. For digital and software-based language education services, including EdTech subscription services sold through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing, zero rating may be claimed where the services are supplied to non-resident end customers, the digital service is treated as consumed outside Korea, payment is settled in foreign currency, and the service provider retains evidence of the foreign place of supply, foreign subscriber status, IP address or digital delivery logs, and app store or foreign exchange records. The Korean tax authority will deny zero rating if the taxpayer cannot substantiate that the goods physically left Korea or that the services were effectively supplied to a non-resident and consumed abroad.

  • Commercial invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Packing list
  • Foreign exchange receipt confirmation issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

All VAT-registered businesses in Korea must issue an electronic tax invoice through the NTS HomeTax System, or through an NTS-approved EDI or ERP application integrated with the HomeTax interface, for every B2B supply of taxable goods or services. The electronic tax invoice data must be transmitted to the NTS by the day following the date of issuance, and the NTS performs centralized real-time validation and matching of invoice data against VAT returns. There is no separate physical anti-fraud stamp requirement for ordinary digital or goods businesses; the mandatory electronic tax invoice transmission is the statutory anti-fraud control mechanism. Late or non-transmission of electronic tax invoices triggers additional taxes and penalties under the VAT Act.

If export zero rating or VAT refund is claimed without the full statutory documentation, the NTS will disallow the zero rate, assess 10% output VAT on the underlying export supply, and impose additional VAT penalties and interest. Where the false export or false foreign exchange settlement is found to be fraudulent, the taxpayer is exposed to criminal liability and penalty taxes up to 40% of the underpaid tax, plus retroactive clawback of refunds paid. The statute of limitations for assessment is 5 years in ordinary cases and 10 years for fraudulent evasion. Failure to repatriate export proceeds in foreign currency within 180 days without a lawful extension granted by a Foreign Exchange Bank can also invalidate the zero-rating claim.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized foreign exchange regime for current account transactions, but capital account operations remain subject to ex-ante and ex-post reporting through designated foreign exchange banks. The Korean won is not a fully offshore-deliverable currency; non-residents generally must execute conversion within Korea or through authorized offshore won market participants. For an EdTech subscription business, selling digital language-learning software into global markets gives rise to export proceeds that are considered invisible trade earnings and may be received in foreign currency. These receipts must be collected within 18 months from the date the export declaration is accepted, deposited into a corporate foreign currency account, and may be converted into KRW freely at market rates. Profit remittance as dividends is permissible after tax audits and foreign investment registration, but banks may require evidence that earnings were generated from licensed activity and that no outstanding transfer-pricing issues exist. Restrictions on capital repatriation are normally only procedural; however, any same-day round-tripping, over-invoicing, or use of unregistered overseas accounts will trigger BOK/FSS scrutiny. Overall convertibility is high for current receipts and low for speculative capital, with all FX transactions requiring traceability.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds including digital services receipts must be collected within 18 months from the export declaration date. The full amount is subject to mandatory conversion into won or deposit into a foreign exchange bank account; maintaining foreign currency beyond the deadline requires prior approval from BOK or the delegated bank. For export settlement, the exporter must submit a settlement confirmation and invoice matching within 5 business days after receipt.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Financial Supervisory Service under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Overseas digital EMIs are not recognized as authorized deposit-taking institutions for Korean corporate entities. All cross-border settlement must be conducted through a licensed foreign exchange bank under Article 9 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Regulations prohibit unlicensed EMI accounts from functioning as corporate operating accounts for a Yuhan Hoesa, and fintech challengers without a Korean banking license cannot provide legally valid escrow or foreign currency collection services for this entity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

21% national corporate income tax on taxable income above KRW 20 billion plus 10% local income tax surtax, yielding a 23.1% top effective headline rate; lower brackets are 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million and 19% on taxable income between KRW 200 million and KRW 20 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% for outbound dividends paid to a non-resident corporate shareholder, composed of 20% national corporate income tax withholding plus 10% local income tax surtax on the national withholding amount; treaty may reduce the rate if the foreign shareholder is the beneficial owner of the dividend.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is statutorily triggered when cross-border intercompany transactions exceed KRW 10 billion in a fiscal year, and the Master File is required when the Korean entity belongs to an MNE group with annual consolidated revenue of at least KRW 100 billion; separate transfer pricing disclosure forms must be filed through the annual corporate income tax return for foreign related-party transactions exceeding KRW 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations but enforces substance-over-form principles, beneficial ownership tests, and CFC rules. The Korean Yuhan Hoesa must demonstrate real employees, board decisions, contracts, financial accounts, and Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea, covering EdTech content maintenance, user onboarding, subscription pricing, Apple/Google settlement, technical support, and commercial risk. A shell Korean LLC funding a low-tax foreign affiliate and claiming treaty benefits or CFC exemptions without local operating substance will be recharacterized by the Korean tax authority.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Underreporting of Digital Subscription Revenue

Statutory sanction: Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act prescribes imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines up to ten times the evaded corporate income tax, together with a 40% fraudulent understatement penalty and monthly delinquency surcharges up to 36 months on the unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who commingle corporate revenue with personal or foreign affiliate accounts or sign false settlement certificates can be personally assessed for the evaded tax and penalties under piercing-of-the-corporate-veil principles; personal assets may be seized, the directors may be blacklisted as chronic delinquents, and departure restrictions may be imposed while the criminal tax investigation is pending.
Transfer Pricing Abuse / Artificial Profit Siphoning Through Low-Substance Foreign Affiliates

Statutory sanction: Article 5 of the International Tax Coordination Act authorizes the National Tax Service to adjust the intercompany price to arm's length; the excess royalty or fee is nondeductible, additional transfer pricing penalties of 10% may apply, and the disallowed amount can be recharacterized as a constructive dividend or royalty subject to 22% withholding under the Corporate Income Tax Act and the Local Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute and approve artificial intercompany agreements may be jointly and severally liable for the tax and penalties if the Korean corporation is insolvent; deliberate false documentation subjects them to criminal liability, asset attachment, travel bans, and recovery actions against personal property in the hands of the foreign affiliate.
Wilful Withholding Tax Evasion and Fraudulent DTA Treaty Relief

Statutory sanction: Withholding agents are liable under Article 73 of the Corporate Income Tax Act and Article 103-12 of the Local Tax Act for the unpaid 22% tax and penalties; fraudulent treaty claims are penalized as tax evasion under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and the assessment period is extended to 10 years for dishonest or intentional concealment under the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize an un-withheld dividend or knowingly submit a false Tax Residency Certificate may be criminally prosecuted, deprived of departure rights, subjected to court-authorized asset freezing, and held personally liable for the withholding liability when the corporate entity is only an emptying shell used to avoid Korean tax.

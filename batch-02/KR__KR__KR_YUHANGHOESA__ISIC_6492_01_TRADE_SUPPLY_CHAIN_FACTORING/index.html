Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists under the Korean Commercial Act for a Yuhan Hoesa; however, the amount of capital stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid by each member before the registration application is filed, and the paid-in contribution must be deposited into a dedicated capital contribution bank account opened in the proposed corporate name. For non-resident investors, inward remittance must be made through a Korean foreign exchange bank and the bank certificate of deposit or certificate of contribution must be attached to the registration dossier. There is no statutory escrow release arrangement under Korean company law, but the contribution account is treated as blocked for company formation purposes and none of the funds may be withdrawn until the Court Registry issues the Certificate of Company Registration. If the entity will operate factoring under the Specialized Credit Finance Business Act, the Financial Services Commission requires the registered company to maintain real paid-in capital and own capital not lower than the thresholds prescribed by the Enforcement Decree of that Act, and those capital tests must be satisfied before factoring operations lawfully commence.

STEP 2 - If the entity is foreign-invested, complete the Foreign Investment Report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act through KOTRA or a designated foreign exchange bank and obtain the Foreign Investment Report Certificate. A Yuhan Hoesa is per se a taxable corporation under Article 1-2 of the Corporate Tax Act, subject to entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide income when resident; no pass-through election is available. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold applies for VAT registration in South Korea: every entrepreneur commencing a VAT-taxable business, including foreign non-resident vendors selling qualifying electronic services or using a Korean place of business, must register immediately. A simplified small-business facility exists on the computation side for sole proprietors whose annual taxable supplies do not exceed KRW 80,000,000, but it does not remove the registration duty. For factoring and supply-chain financing, credit and financing transactions are generally VAT-exempt financial services while separately itemised management/factoring commission fees may be taxable, and once any taxable supply begins registration is mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to exported tangible goods on the condition that the goods actually depart from Korea after being shipped under the Korean Customs Service export declaration and the exporter holds the evidence of export. Qualifying offshore digital and intangible services are zero-rated under Article 21 of the VAT Act when the service is supplied to a non-resident client and the use/benefit is deemed to take place outside Korea; the invoice must identify the foreign recipient, the foreign place of supply or offshore use, and the service nature. For the trade-finance/factoring scope, pure financing proceeds (interest, discount fees) are VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated, and any separately charged factoring or administration service rendered to a foreign counterparty for use outside Korea may qualify for zero-rating only with documentary proof of the foreign recipient and actual offshore benefit.

  • Korean Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Declaration of Goods / Export Shipment Report) endorsed for export in the exporter's name
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), Air Waybill (air), or equivalent carrier-issued transport document evidencing the physical departure of the goods from Korea
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by the Korean foreign-exchange bank evidencing receipt of the foreign settlement proceeds and matching the invoice, custom export declaration and shipping document

Electronic tax invoices are the legal default for all VAT-registered businesses. Under Article 32 of the VAT Act, the seller must issue the e-tax invoice through the National Tax Service HTS system and transmit it to NTS in near-real-time, no later than one business day after issuance. Corporate ERP systems must integrate with the HTS e-tax invoice API using an accredited digital certificate and the 10-digit BRN; no proprietary API gateway is imposed, but the NTS Public Integrity Certification must be used. The HTS assigns a digitally signed serial/invoice identification number at issuance, and this HTS-certified transmission index is Korea's anti-fraud 'clearance stamp'. The buyer must also confirm the received e-tax invoice in HTS, allowing NTS to perform automated matching, detect missing or false invoices, and apply statutory surcharges retroactively.

NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly verify export zero-rating by cross-matching export declarations, e-tax invoices, shipping manifests, and banking records. If the export declaration, transport document, or FIRC is absent, incorrect, or inconsistent; if the 180-day inward remittance deadline is not met; or if the purported export is found to be a fake/round-tripping transaction, NTS will claw back any VAT refund already paid, assess the 10% VAT on the supply, impose an underreporting/reckless declaration surcharge (generally up to 40% of the unreported tax plus per-day statutory interest), and may refer the taxpayer to prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act for fraudulent zero-rating. The taxpayer bears the full burden of proof and must retain the complete export documentation package for at least five years from the reporting year.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized current account and capital account regime, yet requires strict reporting and verification through licensed foreign exchange banks. The Korean won is convertible for most genuine trading and investment purposes, but every cross-border receipt and payment must be routed through a domestic foreign exchange bank and must carry an accurate foreign exchange purpose classification. Capital account transactions such as equity infusions in the Yuhan Hoesa factoring entity, shareholder loans, and cross-border reverse factoring facilities are generally permitted but require ex post facto or prior reporting via the Foreign Exchange Information System, with threshold-based approvals enforced by the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Profit remittances and dividend outflows are accepted once corporate tax filings, audited financial statements, and capital verification reports are submitted, although banks may demand additional evidence of the underlying trade receivable portfolio and outstanding factoring obligations. Current account transactions, including factoring collections and settlement of assigned trade receivables, face minimal convertibility friction but must be traceable to bona fide cross-border trade contracts, tax invoices, and customs declarations. There is no overnight restriction that blocks lawful repatriation of profits, but Foreign Investment Promotion Act registration is required for foreign direct investment into the operational Korean entity, and all capital inflows must be received in a designated foreign exchange bank account before conversion into Korean won. The central bank and financial supervisory agencies monitor for round-tripping, phantom trade finance, and disguised cross-border lending; therefore, the factoring company must maintain a complete audit trail of underlying invoices, debtor acknowledgments, assignment notices, and proof of re-payment to foreign credit lines. Overall, convertibility is high for licensed commercial transactions, and the treasury structure can use standard SWIFT rails, KRW settlement through BOK-Wire+, and onshore deliverable forwards, provided that all required reports are filed on time.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters operating under the Korean foreign exchange regime are required to repatriate all export proceeds derived from Korean trade into South Korea, with a standard statutory collection window generally not exceeding the due date stated in the export contract or 90 days from the date of export clearance unless an extension is approved by the licensed foreign exchange bank. The mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign exchange receipts is essentially 100% in respect of repatriation: the proceeds must either be sold to a domestic foreign exchange bank or be credited into a proper foreign currency deposit account at such bank within the permitted period. Retention of export proceeds offshore without a licensed bank arrangement is not permitted for a resident Korean legal entity. The Yuhan Hoesa factoring operation, when financing Korean export receivables, must therefore ensure that each factored export invoice is supported by customs declarations, e-tax invoices, bills of lading, and a remedial duty to surrender all related collections through the designated Korean foreign exchange bank.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea, operating in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the Foreign Exchange Administration authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Information System.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions are not viable as primary banking or treasury rails for a licensed South Korean factoring entity. South Korea requires any entity handling domestic KRW settlement, escrow, or payment products to obtain authorization under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and related Financial Services Commission supervision. Most foreign EMIs and neobanks do not hold a Korean EFT license, cannot establish direct participation in Korea's interbank clearing systems, and are not formally recognized as foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Consequently, relying on a cross-border EMI for aggregation of export factoring proceeds, capital injection verification, or outbound trade disbursements would create severe KYC and reconciliation exposure. The EMI route may be used only as an auxiliary, non-escrow channel for small foreign-currency receivables from remote digital content markets, but all operational liquidity, capital verification, and statutory FX reporting must remain with a regulated Korean foreign exchange bank.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. However, the capital quota subscribed in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid before the incorporation registration application. Cash contributions must be deposited into a pre-formation blocked account at a licensed bank, and the bank must issue a paid-in capital deposit certificate. The funds are legally blocked and cannot be withdrawn by the incorporators until registration is completed and the corporate account is activated. In-kind contributions must be fully delivered and independently appraised. If a subscription amount is unpaid or the valuation of in-kind property is lower than the stated value, the members must make a supplementary cash contribution before filing.

Funding & unlock path: No Civil Defense fire permit is required for a non-industrial office used only for company secretarial and registered agent services, provided that the leased premises contain standard fire extinguishers, exit signs, and evacuation routes in accordance with the Fire Services Act. If the office occupies more than 3,000 square meters or is located in a multi-tenant commercial building requiring a fire prevention manager, the building manager must submit a fire safety plan to the local 119 fire safety authority. For occupational clearance, if the company employs workers, it must subscribe to the Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Scheme and file a customary completion of the workplace health and safety registration with the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency, along with basic occupational safety induction records.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online taxpayer portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr). For goods export and customs-related substantiation, Korea Customs Service (KCS) via UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation or foreign non-resident entity seeking to maintain a Korean tax presence must first determine whether it has a domestic place of business in Korea (a fixed place of business). If it has a domestic place of business, the representative of that place must file the NTS standard business registration application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered office. If the foreign corporation has no domestic place of business but has Korean-source taxable activity or Korean tax filing obligations, it must appoint a Korean tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and the tax representative files the business registration on behalf of the foreign corporation. The application is submitted through the HomeTax portal using an NTS-accredited digital certificate, accompanied by: (i) a certified copy of the foreign company registration or equivalent corporate registration document, apostilled or consular-legalized and translated into Korean; (ii) articles of incorporation and a corporate resolution appointing the Korean legal representative or tax representative; (iii) a notarized power of attorney in favor of the appointed Korean tax representative; (iv) evidence of the domestic registered office, such as a lease agreement or title certificate; and (v) identification documents for the representative and responsible officers. After approval, NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which constitutes the Korean TIN for corporate income tax, the VAT registration number, and the withholding tax identifier. The entity must also register as an electronic tax invoice issuer in the HomeTax system and obtain and accredit its HomeTax digital certificate for e-Tax Invoice issuance and transmission. A foreign supplier of remote electronic services to Korean consumers may be required to complete the NTS VAT registration process for businesses without a domestic place of business before commencing Korean-source supplies; such registration does not itself establish a Korean domestic place of business for corporate income tax purposes.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic corporation under the Corporate Income Tax Act and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold. VAT registration in Korea is compulsory from commencement of any taxable business activity, irrespective of annual revenue. A Yuhan Hoesa, being a corporation, is always a general taxable person and cannot use the simplified taxable person regime, which is limited to small individual operators under the Korean VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% export VAT rate under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act applies to: (1) goods that are declared to Korea Customs Service for export and physically leave Korea under the Customs Act; (2) services supplied to non-resident persons or foreign corporations when the services are performed outside Korea or are otherwise consumed outside Korea, the consideration is paid in foreign currency, and the Korean supplier retains the required export evidence; and (3) other prescribed foreign-currency-earning supplies. For the Yuhan Hoesa registered-agent and commercial secretarial services, zero-rating is available only where the client is a non-resident person or foreign corporation, the service is supplied from Korea but used or performed overseas, and the consideration is received in foreign currency. Supplies to Korean-resident clients are standard-rated at 10%. Zero-rated exports carry full input VAT credit or refund entitlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration Certificate (Export Declaration/Permit) issued by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS, evidencing export clearance of the goods from Korea.
  • Commercial invoice with detailed description of goods and services, including HS code, quantity, unit value, and incoterms/terms of delivery.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, as applicable, showing departure from Korea and arrival outside Korea.
  • Foreign exchange settlement evidence, generally an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), Foreign Exchange Bank Foreign Currency Receipt Certificate, or foreign currency settlement record from a bank wire or credit-card merchant account, proving receipt of the export consideration in foreign currency.
  • Executed sale contract, purchase order, or service agreement identifying the foreign buyer or foreign recipient of services.
  • For offshore services, additional performance evidence such as dated service reports, deliverables, task completion records, and the foreign recipient's business registration details.

Korean VAT law mandates the issuance and NTS transmission of an electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) for taxable supplies between registered business operators. The e-Tax Invoice is issued through the HomeTax electronic tax invoice system using an NTS-accredited digital certificate, with data transmitted to NTS at or promptly after issuance via the HomeTax portal, EDI, or application-to-application integration. Korea does not operate a pre-supply government clearance stamp or real-time fiscal approval mechanism; anti-fraud integrity is achieved by NTS near-real-time matching and validation of transmitted e-Tax Invoice data against the buyer's VAT return, automatic discrepancy detection, pre-population of VAT returns, mandatory cash receipt reporting for B2C sales, and automatic capture of credit/debit card sale information from payment networks. Issuance of a paper tax invoice in a circumstance where an e-Tax Invoice is statutorily required, or failure to transmit the e-Tax Invoice data to NTS, can lead to disallowance of input VAT credits and penalty surcharges.

NTS cross-matches zero-rated VAT claims against Korea Customs Service export declarations, e-Tax Invoice data, and foreign-exchange settlement records. The exporter must retain all supporting customs, transport, contract, deliverable, and foreign-currency receipt documentation for at least five years, or ten years where fraud is alleged. If the NTS determines that the export documentation is incomplete or contradictory, the foreign exchange is not collected within the statutory deadline (generally 180 days from export clearance unless a prior extension is approved by the designated foreign exchange bank), or the claimed offshore service was not actually exported or consumed abroad, the NTS will re-assess VAT at the standard 10% rate on the supply value, disallow the corresponding input VAT credit or refund, impose penalty surcharges for underreporting and underpaid tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and may apply criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment, fines up to a multiple of the evaded tax, and full retrospective clawback of refunded tax within the extended statutory limitation period for fraudulent claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a fundamentally market-determined exchange rate regime. The Korean won is fully convertible on current-account transactions, consistent with Korea's Article VIII obligations under the IMF Articles of Agreement. Exporters and service providers may freely remit trade-related proceeds, subject to a statutory collection window and the requirement that settlement be routed through a licensed foreign exchange bank. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment in a Yuhan Hoesa, are largely liberalized under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA). Non-resident investors can inject capital and hold 100% equity without prior approval, but the transaction must be notified to the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) via a designated bank. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is freely permitted once corporate tax obligations are discharged and the required tax payment completion certificate or dividend distribution board resolution is submitted to the remitting bank. Practical hurdles arise not from surrender requirements (no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio applies) but from banks' defensive KYC practices toward corporate secretarial entities. A foreign-currency account can be retained, but all inward receipts must be attributed to a matching export invoice or service contract; lapses trigger foreign exchange violation reports and tax audit exposure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, proceeds from service exports must be collected through a licensed foreign exchange bank within 18 months from the completion of the service or the scheduled settlement date stated in the underlying contract. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the depositing company may retain foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Account at a domestic bank and convert it to KRW at the prevailing market rate when needed. Any extension beyond the statutory window requires an application for a permissible delayed collection permit, and any unrepatriated balance is subject to a foreign exchange settlement violation penalty.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability as a primary banking rail. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, offshore digital banks and EMI-type wallets are not licensed to maintain Korean Won-denominated deposit accounts or to provide BOK-Wire / CGS clearing access. A Korean corporate entity may only execute foreign-exchange business through a licensed foreign exchange bank. Certain cross-border fintech platforms (e.g., Airwallex or Payoneer) may be used for supplementary receivables collection outside Korea if the ultimate KRW settlement is made through a licensed local bank, but such structures raise KYC redemption risk and may be rejected by Korean tax authorities as inadequately supported for export verification. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must be anchored on a Tier-1 domestic licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign direct capital injection into the Yuhan Hoesa must follow FIPA notification and verification. The investor transfers foreign currency from an overseas bank to the dedicated account opened with a designated foreign exchange bank in Korea. Incoming SWIFT MT103 messages must include the remitter's name and address, the Korean bank account (Foreign Investment Capital Deposit Account), and a clear FX purpose code. After crediting, the bank verifies the remittance against the foreign investment notification and issues a Foreign Investment Registration Certificate. The capital can then be converted into KRW at the market rate, and after the capital verification report is filed with the Ministry of Strategy and Finance (or delegated bank), it is released to the operational account. The Korea Tax Service is furnished with the paid-in capital schedule via the annual corporate tax return.For operational disbursements and dividend remittances, the treasury rail is a direct SWIFT MT103 payment through the settlement bank's foreign exchange network. The company will use Reuters/IFX pricing from its bank to execute FX conversions before making payments, ensuring all trade-related remittances are captured under FETA approved purpose codes. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as a primary banking rail. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, offshore digital banks and EMI-type wallets are not licensed to maintain Korean Won-denominated deposit accounts or to provide BOK-Wire / CGS clearing access. A Korean corporate entity may only execute foreign-exchange business through a licensed foreign exchange bank. Certain cross-border fintech platforms (e.g., Airwallex or Payoneer) may be used for supplementary receivables collection outside Korea if the ultimate KRW settlement is made through a licensed local bank, but such structures raise KYC redemption risk and may be rejected by Korean tax authorities as inadequately supported for export verification. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must be anchored on a Tier-1 domestic licensed bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory for international related-party transactions exceeding KRW 10 billion during the fiscal year; Master File is mandatory when such transactions exceed KRW 100 billion; a Country-by-Country Report is required for multinational groups whose consolidated revenue is at least KRW 100 billion.

Economic substance scope: Korea has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the National Tax Service applies the substance-over-form doctrine, the Business Purpose Test, and the International Tax Adjustment Act to disregard offshore entities lacking Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA). For an ISIC 8299 registered-agent/offshore secretarial office, CIGA means the Korean entity must employ personnel, take management decisions, maintain board minutes, and execute contracts in Korea; a mere mailbox that books a secretarial fee has zero economic substance and will be treated as a sham conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Disguised Dividend and Outbound Withholding Evasion

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act Articles 93 and 98 require 22% withholding; the National Tax Service will reassess the payments as dividends, deny cost deductions, add a 20% under-declaration penalty for the corporate payer, and impose a 10% or 40% penalty depending on fraud; unpaid withholding tax plus daily late-payment interest is collected from the withholding agent.

Director personal liability: A director who controls the false service agreements is jointly liable under Korean Commercial Act Article 401 for third-party losses; courts may ignore the corporate shell under Article 321 where the entity was incorporated for fraud. The director will face personal restitution of the evaded withholding tax, restriction of departure (Korean tax-travel ban), and criminal imprisonment under Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Articles 3 and 12.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Artificial Offshore Interposition

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 4-6, a transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length price triggers additional corporate tax, local income tax, and an under-report penalty of 20% of the under-reported income; missing documentation causes document penalties under Article 11 and can extend the assessment period to 10 years.

Director personal liability: The representative director who signs the false benchmarking study or organization chart is liable for the consequential tax shortfall to creditors and the revenue authority under Commercial Act Article 401; civil seizure of personal assets occurs together with blacklisting as a delinquent taxpayer and travel restriction; if the authority proves an intention to evade, the director may be tried under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act with imprisonment up to 10 years.
Unlicensed Registered-Agent and AML/CFT Breach (Shell Factory)

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Unit and National Tax Service may revoke the registered-agent license, impose administrative fines of up to KRW 100 million, and refer criminal charges under the Act on Reporting and Using of Certain Financial Transaction Information Article 14; nominee shareholders can be fined up to KRW 50 million and prohibited from serving as directors of any Korean entity.

Director personal liability: Because the company's entire purpose is to shield the foreign owner, Korean courts pierce the veil and treat the director as the real wrongdoer; directors face civil restitution to the defrauded tax authority or third parties, a formal blacklisting by the National Tax Service, restriction of departure, disqualification under the Commercial Act, and criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for AML facilitation; any tax fraud completed through the shell can add separate imprisonment under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

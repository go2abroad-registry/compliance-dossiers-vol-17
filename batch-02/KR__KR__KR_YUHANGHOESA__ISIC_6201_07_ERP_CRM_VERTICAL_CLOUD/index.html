None. South Korea has no U.S.-style check-the-box regime and no domestic-law pass-through election for a Yuhan Hoesa. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold: every business operator in Korea must complete business registration before starting taxable business activities. A corporate Yuhan Hoesa is always a general VAT taxpayer. The simplified taxpayer status available to individual enterprises with annual taxable supplies below KRW 80,000,000 is not available to a corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when exported from Korea under an accepted Korea Customs Service export declaration. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident and used or consumed outside Korea. For the vertical ERP or CRM cloud business, the Korean supplier must prove that the customer is a non-resident, the cloud service is contractually delivered for offshore use, the service is not consumed by the customer's Korean domestic establishment, the invoice states the service is for foreign consumption, and the foreign currency proceeds are received from abroad. Any cloud service consumed by a Korean branch or permanent establishment of the foreign customer is a domestic taxable supply at 10% VAT. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated export supplies is creditable and can be refunded in the periodic VAT return.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS Export Declaration with acceptance number and export date for goods; not applicable to pure cloud services
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the foreign consignee and Korean exporter for goods
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order, and signed export contract
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange receipt certificate or inward remittance confirmation evidencing collection in foreign currency
  • For cloud or ERP SaaS: signed offshore service agreement, statement of work, service consumption logs, IP or geolocation records showing end-user access outside Korea, and a service acceptance certificate from the non-resident customer

Mandatory electronic tax invoicing applies to all B2B taxable supplies. The VAT-registered supplier must issue a standard NTS e-tax invoice through the HomeTax e-invoicing system on or before the day following the supply date. The NTS provides an e-tax invoice API used by certified accounting and ERP systems for data transmission. Korea does not operate a real-time pre-clearance fiscal stamping regime; anti-fraud integrity is enforced through the mandatory HomeTax e-tax invoice pipeline, buyer and seller cross-matching, and the cash receipt system for end consumers.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly verify export declarations, e-tax invoices, service agreements, and foreign exchange settlement records. If export zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply is recharacterized as domestic taxable, VAT is assessed at 10% on the full supply value, input VAT credits attributable to the supply are clawed back, penalty surcharges for false or unreported e-tax invoices are imposed under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, and underpayment interest accrues. Deliberate false export claims can lead to criminal proceedings under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act with fines and imprisonment, and the assessment may be reopened within the statutory limitation period for fraudulent or undisclosed matters.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bank of Korea administers the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, which divides all cross-border flows into current account and capital account items. Current account transactions, including trade receivables, software royalties, and service fees, are freely convertible; Korean resident companies may purchase KRW from or deposit KRW to their foreign exchange banks without prior approval, provided the transaction is supported by an invoice, contract, or customs declaration and a valid purpose code. The Korean won is also redeemable through foreign exchange banks for travel, education, and ordinary consumption within annual remittance quotas. Capital account flows, such as equity injections, intercompany loans, and M&A consideration, operate on a negative-list system: permitted transactions must be reported to the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) through the designated foreign exchange bank, either before or after the transaction, based on the amount and nature. For profit remittance, shareholders must prove the actual receipt of paid-in capital, present audited accounts, show retained earnings calculation, and pay withholding tax; remittance is only allowed after such evidence is accepted. Surrender requirements still apply: all export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to Korea within six months of receipt and are subject to a conversion-to-KRW obligation, unless an approved offshore account is used. Overall, South Korea is generally current-account convertible but maintains a comprehensive notification and monitoring architecture for capital-account entries and exits, giving banks a central role in applying MOEF foreign-exchange regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and MOEF Implementing Rules, the statutory timeline for export proceeds is six (6) months from the date of receipt (or, in the case of usance L/C remittances, from the date of shipment). 100% of the foreign currency export receipts must be repatriated to an authorised foreign exchange bank in Korea and then sold for KRW or credited to a domestic foreign currency account within the same deadline, unless the exporter obtains an MOEF exemption (e.g., offshore retention account, intercompany netting, or reinvestment abroad). Failure to comply triggers administrative penalties and delays in future FX clearances.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (central bank) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% CIT headline marginal rate applicable to taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion, plus a 2.4% Local Income Tax surcharge (10% of the corporate income tax), producing a combined top effective rate of 26.4%. The progressive rate schedule under Article 55 of the Korean Corporate Income Tax Law (CITL) is: 9% for taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% for KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% for KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; and 24% above KRW 300 billion, in each case exclusive of the 10% local surcharge.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% on outbound dividends (20% corporate income tax withholding as final tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge), as provided under CITL Article 98(5) and the Local Tax Act. The 22% rate applies to dividend distributions made by the Yuhan Hoesa to its foreign corporate shareholders unless a bilateral double tax treaty (DTA) reduces the rate; typical Korean DTA rates for portfolio dividends range from 10% to 15%, and for substantial shareholdings up to 5% to 10%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 500 million (KRW 5억, approximately USD 400,000) in annual cross-border related-party transactions aggregated across all contract types; if this threshold is exceeded, the Yuhan Hoesa must prepare the Local File under Article 11 of the International Tax Adjustment Act (ITAA). In addition, the Master File must be prepared where the multinational group has consolidated revenue of KRW 1 trillion or more, and a Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) is mandatory for a group with consolidated revenue of KRW 1 trillion or more. Routine intercompany transactions below the threshold remain subject to the arm-length principle but do not require the statutory documentation file unless specifically demanded by the NTS.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) statute; instead, economic substance is enforced through the 'actual control test' under Article 8 of the ITAA (the so-called abuse-of-rights doctrine), under which a corporate vehicle lacking genuine business purpose, sufficient employees, operating premises, and actual decision-making functions in Korea can be disregarded or recharacterized. For a KH Yuhan Hoesa operating a vertical ERP/CRM cloud business, the NTS applies the substance-over-form rule to determine whether the Korean entity is the true recipient of the cloud service revenue or merely a service-based pass-through shell. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis requires that the strategic product development, software development, and cloud infrastructure deployment be performed by personnel based in Korea, that the board of directors for the Korean entity convene in Korea, and that the financial accounts reflect the actual commercial activity in Korea; a pure invoice-raising entity with a foreign decision center would be treated as a sham for CITL purposes and as a controlled foreign company of its foreign parent where the passive income is concerned. The NTS also applies the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and Articles 4-6 of the ITAA to reallocate income to the jurisdiction where the economically significant functions, assets, and risks are located, with particular scrutiny on the attribution of software-licensing intangibles between the Korean entity and the foreign group IP holder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-3, the NTS will impose an additional tax of 40% of the tax evaded for fraudulent filing; under Article 47-4, a 20% penalty applies for un-reported tax; under CITL Article 109, a representative who deliberately evades corporate income tax is subject to imprisonment for not more than 5 years or a fine of not less than the amount evaded and not more than 5 times the amount evaded; the evasion penalty does not preclude the tax authority from collecting the principal tax plus daily overdue interest of 0.022% per day.

Director personal liability: The NTS will pierce the corporate veil where fraud or gross negligence is proved: directors who participated in the concealment of revenue are jointly and severally liable with the corporation for the unpaid corporate income tax, penalties, and interest under Korean commercial law principles and tax law doctrine developed under Article 42 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. In addition, the NTS may blacklist the director and issue a travel ban preventing departure from Korea until the outstanding tax is secured; where the evasion exceeds statutory thresholds, the director faces criminal imprisonment for up to 5 years, and the court may additionally order forfeiture of benefits and a fine of 5 times the evaded tax. Civil liability extends to the restoration of corporate property misappropriated through the shell arrangement, and the director is banned from serving as a director of any newly incorporated Korean company for up to 5 years.
Treaty Abuse, Shell Entity, and Failure to Meet Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Article 9 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, the NTS may deny the benefit of the tax treaty where the main purpose of the arrangement is to obtain treaty benefits; the withholding rate will be restored to the full statutory 22% (20% CIT plus 2% local surcharge), and additionally a 30% penalty tax on under-withholding can be imposed under Article 47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. Where the Korean entity is a fake shell, the NTS may tax the beneficial owner as the deemed Korean domestic enterprise, thereby converting the gross-basis withholding tax into net-basis corporate income tax at the 24% headline rate plus the 2.4% local surcharge, creating a substantially larger tax demand.

Director personal liability: Korean courts have applied the substance-over-form principle to disregard the separate legal personality of a conduit corporation when it is used as a vehicle for tax evasion; in such cases, the foreign parent and the Korean directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid taxes. Directors face personal assessment for the tax amount under the comprehensive liability clause, asset seizure orders, provisional attachment of personal bank accounts, travel restrictions, and criminal indictment for aiding and abetting tax evasion, with a maximum term of 5 years in prison. Additionally, the Financial Supervisory Service may impose a concurrent administrative fine for failing to report the beneficial owner under the Financial Transaction Reports Act, and the Korean customs and foreign exchange authorities may revoke the corporate foreign exchange business registration.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Misuse of Intercompany Payments

Statutory sanction: Under Article 11(1) of the ITAA, failure to submit the Local File or Master File gives rise to a civil penalty of up to KRW 30 million, and the NTS may concurrently adjust the entity's taxable income under Article 4, 5, and 6 of the ITAA by applying the arm-length principle; the tax adjustment carries an additional tax equal to 40% of the under-reported tax amount for improper transfer pricing declarations under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and if the adjustment exceeds KRW 500 million, the case is automatically transferred to the National Tax Investigation Bureau for criminal review. Filing false TP documentation also triggers the criminal provisions of Article 109 (falsification) and Article 110 (fraud) under the CITL, with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to 5 times the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign the false transfer pricing documentation are personally liable for the additional tax assessment to the extent that the corporate assets are insufficient, on the theory that the director has breached the duty of care owed to the company and its creditors. The NTS may impose a departure ban, place the director on the black-list for large delinquent taxpayers, and apply a separate penalty of percent from 10% to 40% on the director's personal remuneration. In a criminal proceeding, the director, as the person who actually executes the false declaration, is the primary defendant and may receive a sentence of up to 5 years in prison; in egregious cases involving the creation of sham contracts and false invoices, the indictment will include charges of document forgery and fraud under the Korean Criminal Act, under which cumulative sentences can exceed 10 years. Additionally, the director must make full civil restitution of the unpaid tax plus interest, and is prohibited from leaving Korea until the tax lien is satisfied or a payment guarantee is posted.

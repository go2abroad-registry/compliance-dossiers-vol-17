Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital exists under the Korean Commercial Act. However, the full subscribed capital must be contributed before the registration application is filed, and the founders must obtain a bank capital deposit certificate or certified savings passbook evidencing that the capital is held in a designated corporate bank account in the name of the company. No separate escrow account is required, but released funds are restricted to incorporation expenses and must remain unwithdrawn until the registration is formally completed. In-kind contributions must be appraised and stated in the Articles of Incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: The facility must obtain fire safety approval under the Fire Safety Installation and Maintenance Act, including inspection of fire extinguishers, sprinklers, emergency exits, automatic fire detection systems, and fire-resistant compartmentation; after installation, a completion inspection is conducted by the Korea Fire Safety Authority or the local fire station. Civil Defense registration is required for emergency planning, including an evacuation route, shelter designation, and business continuity plan. The occupational licensing side requires compliance with the Occupational Safety and Health Act, appointment of a workplace safety and health manager where applicable, risk assessment for hot-oil cooking hazards, electrical safety, ventilation system maintenance, and delivery-service worker safety protocols, all of which must be documented and kept available during government inspection.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic filing and registration through the HomeTax electronic portal at www.hometax.go.kr; customs export clearance is administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance portal at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity engaging in Korean domestic business, the entity must first confirm whether it has a domestic place of business or Korean-source taxable income. If there is no fixed domestic place of business, it must appoint a domestic tax representative under Korean tax law who will receive tax documents, issue and receive tax invoices, and represent the entity before the NTS. The non-resident entity must prepare corporate formation documentation, including certified or apostilled articles of incorporation, a certificate of foreign business registration or equivalent corporate existence certificate, a board resolution appointing the domestic tax representative, a power of attorney, and identity documents for the representative. The application for business registration must be filed through HomeTax or in person at the district tax office having jurisdiction over the Korean place of business, using the standard non-resident foreign corporation business registration procedure. Upon validation of the foreign corporate existence, the domestic place of business, and the tax representative appointment, the NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number which serves as both the statutory Tax Identification Number for corporate income tax purposes and the Value-Added Tax registration number. After obtaining the Business Registration Number, the entity must activate the electronic tax invoice system through HomeTax or a certified value-added network service provider, open a Korean won foreign exchange bank account for settlement of export proceeds, and file quarterly VAT returns and annual corporate tax returns in accordance with Korean statutory deadlines. A foreign entity that has no Korean place of business but is eligible to claim a VAT refund may be registered through its Korean tax representative under the foreign non-resident VAT refund registration regime, but ordinary business registration applies when the entity actually operates a Korean trade or business.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not operate a US-style check-the-box election nor a general pass-through election for Yuhan Hoesa. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa is per se a domestic corporation subject to entity-level taxation under the Korean Corporate Tax Act, and its income is not treated as pass-through to shareholders. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None. Korean VAT law requires registration by every person engaged in a business activity as defined under the VAT Act, without a general turnover-based exemption threshold for corporations. Registration must be completed within 20 days after business commencement. The simplified taxable person status is available only to eligible small individual businesses and does not remove the obligation to register; it is not available to a limited liability company.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported from Korea by filing an export declaration with Korea Customs Service and to supplies of services rendered to foreign recipients where the place of supply is deemed outside Korea under the Korean VAT Act, including qualifying international services and digital services supplied to non-resident recipients. The taxpayer must prove that the goods were physically exported or that the services were actually supplied to a foreign counterparty, that the consideration in foreign currency was received from the foreign buyer, and that the applicable statutory export documentation has been preserved. For the ghost-kitchen and virtual-brand commercial model, local Korean delivery sales through platform payouts are standard-rated at 10%; zero-rating is available only for the physical export of packaged food products, or the cross-border licensing of virtual brand intellectual property or digital content to foreign non-resident buyers, provided those supplies satisfy the Korean VAT place-of-supply and export evidence requirements.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration and export clearance certificate issued through the UNI-PASS system
  • Bill of Lading for ocean freight or Air Waybill for airfreight evidencing physical export from Korea
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank proving receipt of export proceeds
  • Customs broker authorization or power of attorney if export clearance is undertaken through an agent

South Korea does not employ a real-time fiscal pre-clearance stamping model. All business-to-business supplies must be documented through the mandatory electronic tax invoice (ETI) system, and invoices must be submitted in XML form to the NTS through HomeTax or a certified VAN provider using NTS-integrated interfaces. The electronic tax invoice must be issued no later than the last day of the month following the supply for ordinary quarterly filing, and transmission to the NTS must occur within the statutory filing deadlines. There is no per-transaction anti-fraud digital seal, but the NTS uses the ETI data, cash receipt reporting for business-to-consumer supplies, customs export declarations, and foreign exchange settlement records for automated cross-verification and audit targeting.

The National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service jointly cross-match export declarations, electronic tax invoices, bills of lading, and foreign exchange settlement records. If a zero-rated export is found to be unsupported, fraudulent, or invalid because the goods never left Korea, the services were not supplied to a foreign recipient, or export proceeds were not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period without an approved extension, the NTS will disallow the zero-rating and assess VAT at 10% on the full supply, together with interest for underpaid tax. Additional tax penalties apply under Korean VAT law, including up to 20% for negligent non-filing or understatement and up to 40% for fraudulent evasion. Deliberate use of false export declarations, fabricated transport documents, or fictitious foreign recipients triggers criminal liability under the Act on the Punishment of Tax Evaders, with possible imprisonment and fines, in addition to retrospective clawback of any VAT refunds previously issued. The general tax assessment statute of limitations is 5 years, extended to 10 years for fraud or deliberate concealment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a dual-account framework governed by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The won is fully convertible on the current account, including trade-related payments, invoicing for exported services, and profit remittances from a Yuhan Hoesa, provided the underlying transaction is documented and reported through a designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, however, are subject to a prior report or approval regime. A non-resident shareholder injecting equity into a Korean LLC must file a capital transaction report through the domestic bank or via the Korea Exchange Information System. Once the equity is registered, dividend remittances are treated as current account invisibles and can be wired abroad with a standard tax withholding certificate. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds; Korean entities are permitted to hold foreign currency in domestic bank foreign currency accounts indefinitely, but proceeds from exports must be collected within six months from the date of export unless an extension is granted. Practical hurdles for profit remittance include the need to prove the absence of outstanding tax arrears, submission of audited financial statements if paid-in capital exceeds statutory thresholds, and the 'substance over form' review by the bank to confirm the distribution does not violate capital preservation rules. The Bank of Korea periodically monitors net foreign currency positions, and banks enforce transaction-level health checks to deter money laundering. Overall, the won is highly convertible for bona fide trade and service flows, but all crossings must be accompanied by purpose codes and supporting contracts to satisfy the reporting engine.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 32 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the related Enforcement Decree, Korean exporters must collect export proceeds within 6 months from the exportation date (Korean customs clearance or service completion date, whichever is applicable). Extension is allowed for up to 12 months with bank approval and up to 24 months with a BOK report. There is no mandatory conversion into Korean won; proceeds may be credited to a foreign currency account with a registered bank, but the full amount must be repatriated, not left offshore, and the receiving bank must be notified with supporting invoices and bills of lading (or delivery confirmations for cloud kitchen supply contracts) to generate the outward/ inward foreign exchange report. Failure to repatriate within the timeline results in administrative penalties and possible criminal sanctions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting through the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Foreign Exchange Operations Team under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with daily administration delegated to authorized foreign exchange banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low - Not legally viable for domestic Korean settlement. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-authorized e-money institutions, EU EMI licenses, or US fintech BaaS platforms) are not authorized to open virtual IBANs or provide KRW clearing on behalf of a Korean-domiciled LLC. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires all local currency settlements and foreign exchange conversions to flow through a foreign exchange bank licensed in Korea. Foreign EMIs cannot report capital transactions, cannot issue Korean purpose codes, and cannot interface with the Bank of Korea's reporting systems. They may only serve as an auxiliary foreign-currency collection tool for non-Korean sales, but even that requires entering into a cross-border payment arrangement with a domestic bank, which defeats the purpose. Therefore, a Yuhan Hoesa must maintain a primary relationship with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. No grain of legal workaround exists for ghost kitchen payouts from UberEats or door-to-door aggregators; those payouts must be received in KRW through domestic acquiring and settlement rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial capital contribution from a non-resident shareholder must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 using the explicit purpose code 'A100 - Equity investment (non-financial)' under the Korean Foreign Exchange Information System. The receiving domestic bank will verify the remitter's name against the shareholder register, issue a Certificate of Foreign Exchange Transaction, and report the transaction to the Bank of Korea through the Foreign Exchange Information System. Within 30 days of the remittance, the Korean entity must file a capital investment report with the Ministry of Economy and Finance or complete an automated report through the receiving bank. The capital verification process requires the external auditor or certified public accountant to reconcile the SWIFT receipt, the bank's credit advice, and the board resolution approving the share issuance. For a Yuhan Hoesa with no minimum capital requirement, the injected funds may be used immediately for kitchen deposits, equipment purchases, and payroll once the bank confirms the purpose code and the 'alien investment' free-conversion route is approved. If the shareholder uses a non-Korean intermediary, the bank will apply enhanced due diligence and request a full chain of custody to ensure no third-party settlement risk.For settlement of raw ingredient imports, packaging supplies, and franchise technology royalties, the Korean LLC should use a SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers under purpose code 'B210 - Import payments' or 'B310 - Royalties and license fees'. For high-value or recurring trade flows, a Documentary Collection (DP/DA) or Irrevocable Confirmed Letter of Credit issued by a Korean Tier-1 bank is preferred, especially when importing specialized kitchen machinery from Japan or the United States. Local supplier payments (rent, utility, third-party delivery fleet fees) are cleared through the domestic Banking Payment Network (BOK-Wire+ and CD/ATM switch), using standard KFTC account transfers with settlement messages. DoorDash and UberEats payouts are received in KRW through the platform's Korean acquiring arm, typically via a daily electronic settlement message directly into the operating account; the bank reconciles those against the Delivery Aggregator Settlement Report. For cross-border royalty outbound remittances, the bank will require the corresponding trademark/patent registration certificate, the license agreement stamped with a negotiation date, and withholding tax clearance from the National Tax Service. All outbound wires over USD 10,000 require mandatory foreign exchange reporting and are checked against the UN sanctions list and FATF watchlists in real time.Forward FX contracts with a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank up to 12 months tenor, covering declared USD/ KRW or EUR/ KRW exposure from projected ingredient import invoices and aggregator net settlements; physical delivery is mandatory and unused forward portions must be reversed by maturity.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are strictly limited to non-resident financial counterparties under Korean regulations; a domestic LLC is generally barred from entering NDFs unless it proves to the Bank of Korea that it maintains a genuine underlying foreign-currency asset or liability not eligible for physical delivery.Cross-currency swaps are permitted for synthetic KRW funding when the shareholder lends USD to the LLC and swaps the principal/interest into KRW, provided the swap is executed through a domestic foreign exchange bank and reported as a capital transaction; no exotic option products are allowed for treasury-only speculation.Plain vanilla FX options with zero-premium collars are allowed for hedging against a sudden KRW appreciation on the USD-denominated future payouts from UberEats Global and DoorDash US, but the bank requires a written treasury policy approved by the LLC's board and a mitigation analysis demonstrating that the collars reduce, not enlarge, the firm's FX VaR.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low - Not legally viable for domestic Korean settlement. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-authorized e-money institutions, EU EMI licenses, or US fintech BaaS platforms) are not authorized to open virtual IBANs or provide KRW clearing on behalf of a Korean-domiciled LLC. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires all local currency settlements and foreign exchange conversions to flow through a foreign exchange bank licensed in Korea. Foreign EMIs cannot report capital transactions, cannot issue Korean purpose codes, and cannot interface with the Bank of Korea's reporting systems. They may only serve as an auxiliary foreign-currency collection tool for non-Korean sales, but even that requires entering into a cross-border payment arrangement with a domestic bank, which defeats the purpose. Therefore, a Yuhan Hoesa must maintain a primary relationship with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. No grain of legal workaround exists for ghost kitchen payouts from UberEats or door-to-door aggregators; those payouts must be received in KRW through domestic acquiring and settlement rails.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: Home Tax Service (hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested Korean Yuhan Hoesa, the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number is the 10-digit Business Registration Number issued under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes. The foreign non-resident shareholder must first complete the Korean corporate registration at the local court registry office, and where applicable file a foreign investment report with a Korean foreign exchange bank or Invest Korea. After incorporation, the company must submit a Business Registration Application to the district tax office that has jurisdiction over the principal place of business, or use the NTS Home Tax Service online registration channel. Supporting documents include the certificate of corporate registration, articles of incorporation, tenant contract or ownership proof of the registered office, identity documents and power of attorney of the representative director, and, for a foreign-owned company, copies of the shareholder's identity documents and foreign entity formation certificates. For a branch of a foreign company, the parent company's certificate of incorporation, evidence of corporate existence from the home-country registrar, and the Korean branch registration certificate are also required. The NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number that functions as the VAT identification number, corporate tax identifier, and employer withholding account number. If the entity will export physical goods, it must also obtain an exporter code from the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic customs clearance portal; for pure digital AI API exports, no separate customs exporter registration is required, but the NTS registration remains mandatory before taxable supplies commence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory turnover threshold. Any business supplying taxable goods or services in Korea is required to register for VAT upon commencement of taxable activity; a foreign non-resident supplier of digital services to Korean consumers may also have separate simplified registration obligations under the Korean VAT digital services rules, but for a resident Korean entity registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply with no KRW threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, export of goods is zero-rated when the goods are actually exported from the Republic of Korea under a Korea Customs Service export declaration. Services are zero-rated where they constitute foreign exchange earning services under the Enforcement Decree of the Value-Added Tax Act: the service is supplied to a nonresident or a foreign enterprise, the service is actually used or enjoyed outside Korea, and the consideration is received in foreign currency or in Korean won under lawful foreign-exchange settlement. For an AI/LLM API platform, qualifying zero-rating requires a written service contract with a nonresident customer, evidence that the API consumer and inference processing are supplied for use outside Korea, nonresident customer verification through foreign tax registration, incorporation documents, habitual residence, foreign IP address or foreign payment method, and actual inbound remittance from the overseas customer. A zero-rated export invoice must be reported in the VAT return; input VAT attributable to the zero-rated export may be recovered through the standard NTS VAT refund procedure.

  • Korea Customs Service electronic Export Declaration (UNI-PASS clearance receipt for goods)
  • Commercial invoice and detailed export description of the goods or services
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or transport documentation proving actual removal of goods from Korea
  • For digital services: signed end-user service agreement, API usage and delivery logs, and nonresident customer tax-residence evidence
  • Foreign exchange inward remittance certificate or receipt issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign customer
  • Foreign exchange settlement confirmation or extended settlement approval as required under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

All VAT-registered taxpayers of general-taxpayer status must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS-governed Home Tax System e-invoice service or through a certified ERP/accounting system connected to the NTS e-invoice interface. Electronic tax invoice data is transmitted to the NTS on or before the statutory VAT tax invoice issuance deadline, generally the 10th day of the month following the date of supply, and the NTS stores and validates the data against business registration records, sequential invoice numbers, and transaction counterparties. South Korea does not operate a pre-clearance digital stamping or fiscal seal system; anti-fraud enforcement uses centralized NTS e-invoice database matching, mandatory electronic transmission of tax invoice data, automatic denial of input VAT deduction where the e-tax invoice is not properly transmitted, mandatory cash-receipt devices for certain B2C businesses, and cross-matching of VAT returns with corporate tax, customs and foreign-exchange data.

The NTS cross-checks export zero-rating claims against Korea Customs Service export clearance records, foreign exchange bank reports, electronic tax invoices, service usage logs, and nonresident identification data. If a zero-rating claim is rejected because the required documentation bundle is incomplete, the export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days without an approved foreign-exchange extension, the nonresident recipient is not substantiated, or the service is determined to be consumed in Korea, the NTS will retrospectively assess output VAT at the standard 10% rate on the full supply value, plus penalty surcharges, false-tax-invoice penalties where applicable, and statutory late-payment interest running from the original VAT return due date. A fraudulent claim involving a false tax invoice, false export declaration, or intentionally fabricated export documentation is subject to criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Value-Added Tax Act, including imprisonment, fines, and extended assessment periods of up to 10 years for intentional tax evasion or false export claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is classified as a freely usable currency for current-account transactions under a notification-based system rather than an approval-licensing system for routine trade and service payments. Inward capital injections such as equity contributions to a domestic LLC must be declared through a designated foreign exchange bank using the capital transaction reporting module; after declaration, funds are credited to an escrow/settlement account and can be converted at the onshore won-dollar market rate. Outbound profit remittances are permitted once the entity proves the source of the dividend, pays the applicable 15.4% corporate withholding tax or secures a tax treaty certificate, and files a payment report with the bank. Korea does not maintain a cycling surrender system for export receipts, but all export proceeds must be repatriated and collected within the statutory period, and receipts below certain thresholds may be covered by simplified procedures. The actual convertibility bottleneck is not legal but documentary: exchange banks require source-of-funds evidence, UBO clarity, tax completion certificates, and proper BOK reporting before processing dividends, loan repayments, capital repatriation, or large intercompany transfers. Non-resident shareholders cannot simply wire funds; they need disclosure forms, beneficial-owner declarations, proof of lawful foreign exchange, and alignment with the declared business purpose of the LLC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds must be collected within 6 months from the contract date or shipping date, extendable up to 18 months for legitimate trade credit terms after approval by a designated foreign exchange bank. There is no blanket mandatory conversion of export receipts into KRW at a fixed surrendered ratio; the operational requirement is that all export receipts must be routed through a designated foreign exchange bank in Korea, reported into the Foreign Exchange Information System, and, only when conversion to KRW is required for domestic payments, sold at the onshore market exchange rate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the paramount monetary and central banking authority, jointly administered with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Korea Customs Service also supervises physical cross-border currency movements, and the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) enforces AML/CFT supervision over designated foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a standalone primary banking relationship. Foreign fintech EMIs are not licensed by Korea to open KRW settlement accounts, issue corporate accounts, or provide formal foreign exchange declaration services under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. A digital EMI may act only as a supplementary collection conduit if it partners with a licensed Korean designated foreign exchange bank, but the legal treasury rail and capital verification must remain with a domestic bank. Korean internet-only banks such as KakaoBank and Toss Bank are domestic banks, yet their corporate onboarding remains limited for the scale and cross-border AI API business of this entity; therefore, they are unsuitable as the primary treasury counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The LLC must open a capital escrow KRW account with a designated foreign exchange bank before receiving any equity funding. The non-resident shareholder should file a capital transaction declaration through that designated bank, using the Foreign Exchange Information System purpose code for inbound foreign direct investment and equity participation. The actual wire must arrive via SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary bank, LLC name, and a clear remittance reference matching the declaration; upon receipt, the designated bank shall verify the foreign currency amount, issue a certificate of receipt of foreign capital or capital verification confirmation, convert the funds to KRW at the market rate, and deposit them into the escrow capital account. For subsequent injections, each transfer must be covered by a fresh declaration or amendments, and the cumulative capital register must trace every contribution to the official share allocation records.For trade settlements and operational disbursements, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 through the designated Korean bank, with cross-border KRW or USD payments processed over the global SWIFT infrastructure. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are only appropriate when the counterparty transacts in physical goods or requires a transmissive finance instrument; for AI API licensing and SaaS services, clean MT103 telegraphic transfers under the correct service-import purpose code are faster and more compliant. Domestic disbursements should be executed through Korea's BOK-Wire plus system, while payments to Stripe or other payment facilitators must be structured as settlement transfers to a licensed acquiring bank, not as hidden overseas banking transactions. USDC and other virtual asset outflows are permissible only when routed through a properly licensed Korean virtual asset exchange and must remain distinct from bank clearing rails.Onshore forward foreign exchange contracts for documented current-account and trade-related exposures between the LLC and its designated bankNon-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts for non-resident counterparties hedging KRW exposure when no deliverable onshore KRW account existsCurrency swaps and long-term foreign-exchange swaps permitted for non-speculative balance-sheet hedging of foreign currency debt or intercompany liabilitiesPlain-vanilla KRW/USD options where the premium and settlement remain fully compliant with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the bank's KYC documentation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a standalone primary banking relationship. Foreign fintech EMIs are not licensed by Korea to open KRW settlement accounts, issue corporate accounts, or provide formal foreign exchange declaration services under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. A digital EMI may act only as a supplementary collection conduit if it partners with a licensed Korean designated foreign exchange bank, but the legal treasury rail and capital verification must remain with a domestic bank. Korean internet-only banks such as KakaoBank and Toss Bank are domestic banks, yet their corporate onboarding remains limited for the scale and cross-border AI API business of this entity; therefore, they are unsuitable as the primary treasury counterparty.

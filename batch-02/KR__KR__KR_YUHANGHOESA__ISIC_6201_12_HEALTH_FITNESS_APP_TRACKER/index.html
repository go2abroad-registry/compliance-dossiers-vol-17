Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal HomeTax (Hometax) at https://hometax.go.kr; local jurisdictional district tax office responsible for the principal place of business.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested or non-resident-owned Yuhan Hoesa, the entity obtains a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which simultaneously serves as the corporate Tax Identification Number and the VAT registration number. The procedure requires appointment of a domestic tax and accounting representative, notarisation or apostillisation of foreign corporate constitutive documents (certificate of incorporation, articles of association, board resolution and power of attorney), submission of a Business Registration Application through the HomeTax portal or at the district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered head office, registration of the head office premises, opening of a Korean foreign-exchange bank account for export proceeds, and issuance by the NTS of the Business Registration Certificate. For foreign non-resident entities without a Korean place of business, a statutory tax representative must be appointed in Korea and a simplified foreign seller VAT registration may be obtained through HomeTax before commencing taxable supplies to Korean customers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general monetary threshold for VAT registration; any business operator making taxable supplies in Korea must register. Simplified taxpayer treatment is available to qualifying small businesses with annual taxable supplies not exceeding KRW 104,000,000, above which the general VAT regime is mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Korean VAT law for exports of goods actually leaving Korea and for offshore export services supplied to non-resident customers where the services are genuinely consumed or used outside Korea. For a fitness biometric tracking and wellness telemetry app sold through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing to end users located outside Korea, the Korean Yuhan Hoesa must demonstrate that the digital services are supplied to foreign subscribers, the place of consumption is overseas, and the consideration is received in and repatriated as foreign-currency proceeds to a Korean bank account. Sales made to Korean consumers through app stores remain subject to 10% Korean VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Korea Customs Service Export Declaration Receipt) evidencing lawful outbound clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing foreign consignee and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Foreign Exchange Certificate issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank proving receipt and repatriation of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and export contract or purchase order
  • Apple IAP and Google Play Billing payout reports with subscriber country data for digital export zero-rating

B2B suppliers must issue and transmit electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) through the HomeTax system rather than paper invoices. Each e-tax invoice is assigned a unique digital audit approval number by the NTS, enabling real-time reconciliation with VAT returns. Issuance may be performed through HomeTax web portals or NTS APIs. Export sales require an e-tax invoice linked to the customs export declaration. The VAT return is filed quarterly via HomeTax, and for the health fitness app business model, transaction records from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing must be reconciled with e-tax invoices, cash receipts, and foreign-currency settlement records.

NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-check export declarations, e-tax invoices, bill of lading data, and bank foreign-currency settlement records. If the claimed export zero-rating is not substantiated, or foreign proceeds are not repatriated within the applicable 180-day deadline, the NTS may revoke zero-rating, reassess VAT at 10% on the unsubstantiated supplies, and impose statutory penalty taxes for underreporting, non-reporting, and false invoicing. Fraudulent zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective audit and criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment, fines, forfeiture of unjust enrichment, and reassessment within the statutory tolling periods for tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized and free-floating Korean won regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and BOK supervisory regulations. Current account transactions, including trade settlements, service fees, software royalties, and dividend remittances, are freely convertible and only require reporting through a licensed foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, such as foreign direct investment, equity injections, cross-border loans, and portfolio inflows, are subject to mandatory prior notification or post-reporting via the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). There is no blanket surrender requirement on export proceeds; however, exporters must ensure timely repatriation of receivables and file proper settlement statements with their designated foreign exchange bank. Profit and dividend remittances are permitted once corporate tax obligations are settled and audited financial statements are filed, but banks may require evidence of underlying real business activity, particularly for digital health subscription platforms, to prevent disguised capital flight. Overall, the won is highly convertible for legitimate treasury purposes, while strict granular documentation governs every inbound and outbound fund movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under BOK Foreign Exchange Transactions Act amended notices, export proceeds from digital services and intangible goods must be received and settled through a licensed foreign exchange bank within 180 days from the shipment or service completion date. Extensions up to 3 years may be granted for long-term receivables with prior approval. Exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in foreign currency denominated operational accounts and have no statutory obligation to convert into KRW; however, all receipts must be reported through FEIS and matched against the original export declaration.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) - Foreign Exchange Administration Department, in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border foreign digital EMI platforms are legally barred from offering corporate banking or treasury settlement services to Korean-incorporated entities under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Banking Act. Feasibility rating: Low. Offshore EMIs such as Wise Business, Payoneer, or Airwallex cannot act as a primary clearing rail or hold fiduciary KRW accounts for Yuhan Hoesa. Their permissible role is limited to downstream consumer payout aggregation when white-labeled through a licensed Korean bank, and even that must be structured as a payment facilitation agreement rather than a corporate settlement account. Treasury policy must therefore restrict all inbound capital and outbound operational cash flows through tier-1 domestic banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and contemporaneous TP documentation are mandatory under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs when the aggregate volume of domestic Korean controlled transactions with foreign related parties reaches KRW 50 billion; Master File documentation is mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated worldwide revenue reaches KRW 1 trillion and the Korean entity has annual revenue of KRW 100 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies the OECD BEPS principle-purpose test, the domestic substantial-taxation doctrine (Framework Act on National Taxes), and the arm's-length principle through the International Tax Adjustment Act. In a non-resident controlled health/fitness app structure, economic substance is verified by where Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are actually exercised: software development, app optimisation, algorithm updates, user-data handling, and pricing/risk control. If these CIGA are performed by employees of the Korean entity but the related offshore parent books the profit, the Yuhan Hoesa may be treated as a de facto PE of the foreign company, and the foreign parent will have Korean corporate tax exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Unreported Digital App Revenue

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 9 - imprisonment with labour up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding KRW 200 million, plus the administrative 40% fraudulent-omission surcharge added to the unpaid corporate tax; unpaid tax is collected by the National Tax Service with asset attachment.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced where the Yuhan Hoesa is an instrumentality or alter ego for the foreign investor; the director who signed false returns is personally liable to the State for the unpaid tax and surcharges, is blacklisted and named in the NTS delinquent-taxpayer public notice, is prohibited from leaving Korea under the National Tax Collection Act departure-ban, and can receive a 5-year criminal sentence.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting Through Intangible Migration

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Article 5 gives the NTS the power to re-determine taxable income at the arm's-length price; Article 35 imposes documentation penalties and denies rebuttal rights; a resulting evasion surcharge of up to 40%, together with accumulated daily overdue interest, is assessed on the adjusted tax base.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the underpriced intangible sale or sham royalty owe fiduciary compensation to the Korean company under Commercial Act Article 399, making their personal assets reachable; the Korean court may disregard the corporate entity under the veil-piercing doctrine; the NTS imposes a departure ban, public blacklisting, and criminal prosecution for complicity in tax evasion.
Abusive Treaty Shopping for Dividend WHT Relief

Statutory sanction: Under the OECD MLI and Korea's substantial-taxation rule, the treaty benefit is denied; the short-paid 17 percentage point difference (22% less 5%) is collected with 40% fraudulent understatement surcharge, and the Singapore/Hong Kong borrower may be deemed to be a shell/conduit; separate false-certificate penalties apply under the Framework Act on National Taxes, including fines up to KRW 30 million for a false oath.

Director personal liability: The directors who knowingly signed the conduit arrangement and submitted false beneficial ownership paperwork lose limited liability as the company was a mere shell; they are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid WHT and penalties, face criminal fines and up to 2 years of imprisonment for false declarations; the NTS blacklist and departure ban are triggered since the amount in contention exceeds the serious-delinquency threshold.

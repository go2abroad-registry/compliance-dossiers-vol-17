Virtual office or commercial mailbox addresses are not acceptable as the sole registered office for a Yuhan Hoesa engaged in bulk grain brokerage. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea imposes no minimum paid-in capital for a Yuhan Hoesa; the Articles of Incorporation may fix the contributed capital at any amount, including as low as KRW 1. The full subscribed capital must be deposited, before incorporation registration, into a restricted capital-subscription deposit account at a domestic Korean bank in the name of the company to be formed. The bank issues a certificate of capital payment. The funds are effectively blocked until the court registry completes the incorporation registration; no separate escrow agent is required. The official state filing fee is entered as USD 0 because there is no fixed minimum-capital filing fee; the actual registry tax is computed as a percentage of the contributed capital under the Local Tax Act at filing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For services, zero-rating applies if the service is provided to a non-resident and the benefit is received outside Korea. For goods, zero-rating applies upon export with proof of customs clearance. For brokerage services, the service must be performed for a foreign client, consideration must be received in foreign currency, and the service must not be connected to domestic real estate or goods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized current-account regime that permits trade-related payments without prior approval when supported by underlying shipping, invoice, and customs documents. The Korean won is not fully reserved-currency convertible; offshore KRW settlement is limited and the won typically clears only through domestic banking channels. Capital-account transactions including paid-in capital injections, shareholder loans, acquisition of overseas securities, and foreign direct investment remain subject to reporting, declaration, or approval under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Exporters must repatriate proceeds within a legally required timeline, but no strict surrender ratio forces conversion; the earned foreign currency may be retained in designated foreign-currency accounts at a domestic bank. Profit remittance hurdles therefore arise not from current-account limits but from corporate governance, evidence of foreign investment registration, tax withholding and distribution legality; the bank must verify audited financial statements, board resolutions, and tax clearance before converting remittable dividends. In practice, banks apply stringent beneficial-owner due diligence, documentary consistency checks, and real-time sanctions screening, making the ease of convertibility dependent on the completeness and legitimacy of the treasury dossier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be collected within 90 days from the date of export declaration. There is no mandatory percentage conversion to KRW; exporters may hold foreign currency in a domestic foreign currency account or choose voluntary conversion. A designated foreign exchange bank will require evidence of the export transaction and will report any overdue proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the principal foreign exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Banking Act require foreign exchange business to be carried out by registered domestic banks or licensed FX dealers; an offshore EMI license from another jurisdiction does not confer Korean regulatory permission. An EMI cannot operate a foreign currency account in KR with a Korean corporate, clear KRW, process export settlements, or issue import LCs. The only sustainable use is as a supplementary data or reconciliation layer, not as a clearing or treasury rail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Progressive corporate income tax: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% on taxable income exceeding KRW 200 million but not exceeding KRW 20 billion; 21% on taxable income exceeding KRW 20 billion but not exceeding KRW 300 billion; 24% on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; an additional 10% local income tax surcharge is imposed on the corporate income tax payable, and a further 10% surtax applies to corporate income tax payable attributable to taxable income exceeding KRW 30 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Under Korean domestic tax law, outbound cash or property dividends paid by a Korean domestic corporation to a non-resident corporate or individual shareholder are subject to dividend withholding tax at the rate of 20%, plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the withholding tax, resulting in an aggregate effective statutory withholding rate of 22%, unless a bilateral double tax treaty provides a reduced rate (commonly 5% or 10% for portfolio and substantial shareholdings).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Framework Act on International Taxes and the OECD transfer pricing documentation rules implemented in Korea, the prevailing contemporaneous documentation thresholds are: KRW 10 billion of total cross-border related-party transactions for Korean Local File preparation; KRW 100 billion of group-related international transactions for Korean Master File preparation; and KRW 1 trillion consolidated group revenue for Country-by-Country Reporting. The Korean Revenue Service may nonetheless request transfer pricing documents below these thresholds where facts suggest manipulation, and the failure to produce documentation shifts the burden of proving arm's length pricing to the taxpayer.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies a statutory substance-over-form doctrine under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, and the National Tax Service disregards interposed entities that lack genuine business purpose and actual management. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa engaged in bulk grain and soybeans trading must demonstrate real Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea, including employment of grain traders, maintenance of chartering and freight contracts, grain quality inspection, storage management, risk-management decisions, bank accounts, and board meeting records. Foreign shell intermediaries introduced into the distribution chain for the sole purpose of capturing treaty benefits or shifting profits toward low-tax jurisdictions are vulnerable to anti-abuse adjustments, Beneficial Ownership challenges, Limitation-on-Benefits provisions in Korean tax treaties, and controlled foreign company rules at the shareholder level.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent International Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, intentional evasion of corporate income tax is punishable by imprisonment of up to 2 years or a criminal fine not exceeding twice the amount of evaded tax; aggravated cases carry imprisonment of up to 5 years. In addition, the National Tax Service imposes a fraudulent unfaithful return penalty of 40% of the underreported corporate tax, a negligence penalty of 20% for undocumented deviations, and a daily delay surcharge of 0.03% of unpaid tax for each day of late payment.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly participates in the tax evasion or grossly fails to supervise the preparation of the tax return may be personally liable to the company and its creditors under Korean Company Act Article 401; the National Tax Service can pierce the corporate veil and collect unpaid corporate taxes, penalties, and additional charges from the responsible director's personal assets. The director's name may be added to the National Tax Service's bad taxpayer blacklist, a departure restriction or travel ban may be issued prohibiting international travel, and personal criminal conviction for tax fraud can lead to imprisonment, civil restitution, and disqualification from serving as a director of any Korean company.
Failure to File Transfer Pricing Documentation and International Transaction Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act on International Taxes, noncompliant taxpayers are subject to transfer-pricing adjustments by estimation, and the National Tax Service may impose a negligent unfaithful return penalty of 20% and an underpayment surcharge of 0.03% per day. The failure to submit required international transaction information constitutes a separate violation carrying a criminal fine of up to KRW 10 million under the Korean tax procedure statute; repeated or wilful non-cooperation during an examination can result in imprisonment of up to 5 years as an obstruction or evasion offense.

Director personal liability: The director responsible for the statutory reporting obligation bears primary operational responsibility; if the failure arises from intentional concealment or gross negligence, the director is jointly and severally liable for the resulting corporate tax penalties and transfer-pricing adjustments. The National Tax Service may issue a travel ban against the director, place the director on the national list of serious tax delinquents, and file collection proceedings against the director's personal bank accounts, real estate, and offshore assets; criminal conviction can result in imprisonment and personal fines, and the damage to the director's fitness causes removal from board positions.
Artificial Shell Company and Treaty Shopping Structure without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Korean anti-abuse administrative rules and the substance-over-form doctrine allow the National Tax Service to disregard the shell corporate entity, reattribute the income to the ultimate parent, and assess corporate income tax at the normal statutory rates, together with a 40% fraudulent underreport penalty or 20% negligent reporting penalty, daily delay surcharges, and disallowance of treaty withholding benefits under the beneficial ownership and treaty-shopping articles of applicable income tax treaties. The foreign intermediary may be treated as a permanent establishment of the ultimate owner, leading to additional assessments and criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act if the arrangement was fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly formed or operated the Korean shell for treaty shopping and tax evasion are subject to piercing of the corporate veil, making them personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes, interest, and penalties up to the full amount of their personal assets. The National Tax Service may issue a seizure of Korean real estate and bank accounts, impose a travel ban and international blacklisting, request mutual collection assistance under tax treaties, and pursue criminal prosecution; a malicious shell-company scheme may result in imprisonment of up to 5 years, a fine equal to two or more times the evaded tax, and mandatory civil restitution to the Korean revenue authority.

None. Korean law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal HomeTax (hometax.go.kr) for national tax and VAT registration, and Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr) for customs trader registration and export/import declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the Yuhan Hoesa has a Korean domestic place of business (fixed place, branch, or dependent agent) or is a non-resident entity without one. Step 2: If a domestic place of business exists, file the Foreign Corporation Domestic Place of Business Registration under the Corporate Tax Act and the VAT Business Registration under the Value-Added Tax Act with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the business location via HomeTax before commencing taxable activities. Attach certified true copies of the foreign certificate of incorporation, articles of association with Korean translation, board resolution opening the Korea place of business, proof of business address, power of attorney appointing a Korean tax representative if no resident officer is present, and identification documents. Step 3: NTS will issue the unified 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the statutory TIN used for VAT, corporate tax, withholding tax, and customs-related verification. Step 4: If the foreign entity has no domestic place of business but supplies goods or services subject to Korean VAT, the non-resident entity must file a simplified VAT registration as a foreign business operator with NTS through a tax representative and obtain a BRN for VAT. Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None (South Korea has no check-the-box pass-through election; a Yuhan Hoesa is taxed at entity level as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act). VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 (no general monetary threshold; VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply). A special simplified non-resident electronic/digital service supplier threshold of KRW 100,000,000 in annual Korean turnover applies, but physical reverse logistics operations performed in Korea are not within that special threshold and require registration from commencement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to exports of goods and to qualifying foreign-currency-earning services supplied to non-residents under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act and Article 26 of its Enforcement Decree. For cross-border reverse logistics and returned-goods quality grading, zero-rating is available when the Yuhan Hoesa provides the service under a contract with a non-resident principal, the service is or is directly ancillary to international transport, processing, handling, or re-export of goods physically moved out of Korea, the goods are brought into Korea under a customs importation, temporary importation, bonded warehousing, or transshipment declaration and are subsequently re-exported, and the consideration is actually received in foreign currency through a Korean foreign-exchange bank or by internationally accepted remittance. Services performed wholly for domestic principals or consumed within Korea remain taxable at 10%. The exporter must issue a 0% electronic tax invoice and retain the statutory export evidence bundle.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Export Declaration Certificate issued by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS for the re-exported merchandise
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation of the returned goods from Korea to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate or equivalent bank remittance document issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing actual receipt of export proceeds
  • Import Declaration (Bill of Entry), temporary import declaration, or bonded/transshipment customs declaration for the returned goods entering Korea
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and the reverse-logistics service contract/purchase order with the non-resident principal

South Korea operates the HomeTax electronic tax invoice system. Every registered business operator must issue an electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) for all VAT taxable supplies, including 0% export supplies, and transmit it to NTS through HomeTax by the day following issuance. There is no transactional digital-stamping pre-approval regime; however, the e-Tax Invoice filing obligation is mandatory and NTS performs near-real-time data matching against customs declarations, import/export records, and foreign-exchange remittance records. Cash receipts are also required for consumer retail transactions where Korean cash receipt rules apply.

NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-audit export declarations, e-tax invoices, service contracts, customs entry records, and bank remittance documents. If 0% export zero-rating is claimed without valid customs export evidence or without receipt of foreign currency within the statutory export settlement period, NTS will reverse the 0% treatment, assess 10% VAT on the full supply value, and impose statutory additional tax for underreporting or unfiled VAT, which can reach up to 40% of the unpaid tax in fraudulent concealment cases, plus statutory interest. Reassessment is generally available for 5 years, extended to 10 years for fraudulent omission, and criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may result in imprisonment or fines up to 2 times the tax evaded. Any input VAT refunded on the basis of a false or unsubstantiated export claim is clawed back with statutory interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a predominantly liberalised but formally regulated foreign exchange regime. Current-account transactions, including trade-related payments, service fees, and ordinary remittances, are generally freely convertible without prior approval when performed through a licensed foreign exchange bank; the bank books the transaction under a statutory purpose code and performs anti-money-laundering screening. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity injections and outbound foreign investment, are also permitted but are subject to mandatory reporting to the Ministry of Economy and Finance or Bank of Korea, and foreign direct investment must be notified under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act to qualify for capital verification and repatriation protection. Profits and dividends may be remitted overseas upon submission of audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, board resolutions, and foreign exchange bank documentation, but the bank must verify the underlying earning capacity and absence of outstanding tax or customs issues. Surrender requirements are limited: export proceeds must be collected through licensed banks and can be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted to Korean won at the market rate; there is no forced surrender percentage. Practical hurdles include strict KYC/BO identification for offshore shareholders, documentary proof of the source of injected funds, and the need to obtain a certificate of foreign direct investment report before opening escrow or operational accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected within 4 months from the date of export clearance unless an extension is granted by the foreign exchange bank; after collection, there is no mandatory surrender ratio, and 100% of proceeds may be retained as foreign currency deposits or converted into Korean won, with the chosen treatment reported to the relevant bank under the export receipt settlement code.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, with delegated foreign exchange administration and enforcement functions exercised through licensed foreign exchange banks and the Korea Customs Service for trader declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability for domestic KRW corporate treasury. Non-bank electronic money institutions and overseas fintech platforms such as Airwallex are not licensed banks under the Banking Act and cannot independently issue Korean won account numbers, receive foreign direct investment capital, or provide statutory FX purpose-code reporting to the Bank of Korea. They may serve only as supplementary payment collection services outside the regulated capital-account framework; all inbound capital injections, export proceeds, and repatriation flows must be housed in a tier-1 Korean commercial bank to satisfy regulatory capital verification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital for the Korean LLC must be reported as foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The overseas shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 for the capital amount to the Korean LLC's KRW or foreign-currency account at a licensed foreign exchange bank, quoting the statutory foreign-exchange purpose code for equity investment and the corporate name. The Korean receiving bank verifies the deposit, files the FDI report with the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency or the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy, and performs capital verification by comparing the remittance, the authorised share capital stated in the articles of incorporation, the investor shareholder list, and the certificate of foreign-investment report. Only after capital verification may the escrow balance be released for operational funding.Outbound payments for returned-merchandise settlement, logistics services, and quality-grading fees are executed through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers, commercial letters of credit, or documentary collections depending on transaction size and trade terms. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national headline rate plus 10% local income tax surtax, producing a 26.4% aggregate top effective corporate tax rate for taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate statutory withholding on outbound Korean-source dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, composed of 20% corporate/individual income tax and 2% local income tax surcharge; bilateral treaty relief commonly reduces the rate to 5% where the parent company owns at least 25% of the voting shares and meets the Limitation on Benefits clause, and to 15% in other shareholding cases

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion aggregate cross-border related-party transaction amount for a fiscal year, which triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation and submission to the NTS within 45 days of the corporate tax return deadline; below the threshold, contemporaneous TP documentation must still be prepared because the NTS can demand it and assess penalties for failure to produce records within 60 days

Economic substance scope: Although Korea does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime in the Gulf-country format, Korean tax law applies the substance-over-form doctrine through Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, the Korea International Tax Adjustment Act, and OECD BEPS Action 6 treaty-abuse protections. A Korean reverse-logistics company must demonstrate a real Core Income-Generating Activity inside Korea, including physical warehouse facilities, local managers, operational risk, inventory control decisions, grading teams, and customer interface; a shell entity with no employees and no commercial decision-making is subject to recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, withholding reassessment, transfer pricing adjustments, and criminal tax-evasion exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3; imprisonment for up to 5 years or a fine of not more than 3 times the evaded tax; additionally, a fraudulent-return penalty of 40% of the underreported tax plus daily interest of 0.022%; evasion above KRW 5 billion triggers aggravated imprisonment of 5 to 10 years and confiscation of tax benefits.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced when the managing director and foreign beneficial owners jointly engineer the fraudulent scheme; directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax, surcharges, and criminal fines from their personal assets; the NTS may impose a travel ban/blacklisting, seize bank accounts and real estate, and the responsible director and beneficial owner face imprisonment in Korea with possible Interpol red-notice coordination.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: NTS denial under the substance-over-form doctrine in Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, the Korea International Tax Adjustment Act, and OECD BEPS Action 6; the tax authority can reassess a deemed PE, impute arm's-length income, deny treaty relief on dividends and royalties, and impose a 40% unjustified-benefit penalty plus 0.022% daily interest; where sham transactions are proven, prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act follows.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders who intentionally used the Korean entity as a sham bear personal civil liability for the full reassessed taxes and penalties; Korean courts may pierce the veil where the entity is undercapitalized and lacks a separate identity; foreign owners may be personally blacklisted, have Korean assets restrained, and be subject to extradition or red-notice procedures, with imprisonment imposed on the local representative and, in egregious cases, on the foreign owner as co-principal.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Filing / Undisclosed International Fund Movements

Statutory sanction: Act on Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Transaction Information Article 33: imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to KRW 50 million for AML reporting violations; Foreign Exchange Transactions Act penalties up to 5 years or KRW 100 million for unreported capital transactions; combined NTS and customs review may add 20% undisclosed-income tax and 40% dishonest-return penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors, the compliance officer, and the ultimate beneficial owner are personally exposed to criminal prosecution as principal wrongdoers; the corporate veil does not block personal asset forfeiture, bank-account freezing, travel bans, or blacklisting by the Korean Financial Intelligence Unit and Korea Customs; management may face imprisonment and each responsible director may be jointly liable for all unpaid taxes, penalties, and the cost of any asset-tracing investigation.

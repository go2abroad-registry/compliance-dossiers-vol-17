Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa; the articles may fix the total capital in any amount. However, each member must fully pay the subscribed contribution amount in cash or in-kind before the incorporation registration is filed, and the bank must issue a deposit balance certificate evidencing full payment. Korean law does not require a blocked escrow account, but the company should treat the deposit as restricted until the registry issues the incorporation certificate. Non-resident members may subscribe and pay, subject to foreign exchange bank declarations and anti-money-laundering identification.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea applies an entity-level mandatory corporate income tax and VAT system; there is no check-the-box election and no tax consolidation for unrelated domestic subsidiaries. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Korean International Tax Adjustment Act apply for instrument/entity mismatches, but a Korean LLC may not elect pass-through status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 — no de minimis exemption. Both domestic and foreign suppliers of taxable goods/services, including cloud-based payroll automation SaaS, are required to register for VAT from the date of commencing business. For small traders, a simplified taxpayer regime may apply for annual taxable supplies below KRW 80,000,000, but registration itself remains compulsory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act and its Enforcement Decree, a supplier can claim zero-rated VAT on: (i) goods exported from Korea via the Korea Customs Service; (ii) services rendered to foreign non-resident businesses or foreign individuals, including international transport and digital services (SaaS, payroll automation) that are used or consumed wholly outside Korea; and (iii) services physically performed outside Korea. The supplier must hold written contracts identifying the foreign buyer, a zero-rated e-invoice, bank clearing evidence of foreign-currency receipt or SWIFT credit, and, where relevant, network/server logs proving the service was not used in Korea.

  • Customs export declaration (Korea Customs Service 'Export Declaration' / 'Cargo Clearance Report' proving physical shipment)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with the foreign buyer's full name, address, and foreign tax identification number
  • Bill of lading (B/L) or air waybill (AWB) evidencing the goods left Korean territory and arrived in the destination country
  • Foreign exchange bank certificate (equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate) issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank showing receipt of the export price in foreign currency, or SWIFT statement showing final credit
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice issued to the foreign purchaser and transmitted via the HomeTax e-invoice portal, together with the NTS real-time transmission record

South Korea mandates electronic tax invoices through the HomeTax e-invoice system. All taxpayers with annual prior-year turnover exceeding KRW 80,000,000 must issue e-invoices digitally through the NTS API within 1 day for B2B transactions and 5 days for B2C transactions; the system performs real-time transmission, validates the integrated tax invoice identification number, and applies cryptographic anti-fraud stamping. Taxpayers under the threshold may still issue fallback invoices but must migrate to e-invoices when turnover exceeds KRW 80,000,000 in the preceding year.

The NTS will match the zero-rated return with the customs export declaration, e-invoice transmission, and SWIFT credit history. If export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days or the zero-rating substantiation bundle is incomplete or forged, the NTS retroactively assesses 10% VAT on the export value, disallows associated input VAT credits, and imposes understatement penalties (typically 10%–40% of the omitted tax) plus statutory late-payment surcharges under the Framework Act on National Taxes. In cases of fraudulent export evidence, the responsible parties may be criminally prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, and the assessment period is extended to 10 years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a substantially open but regulated convertibility regime. Current account transactions, including software subscription fees, license royalties, management fees, and cross-border payroll-related expenses, are generally free from prior approval, but must be executed through a domestic foreign exchange bank and reported to Bank of Korea and Ministry of Economy and Finance through the Foreign Exchange Information System. Capital account transactions are more controlled. An inbound equity investment, shareholder loan, or capital deposit from a non-resident owner triggers reporting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; a foreign equity investment in a Yuhan Hoesa may also need a report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Profit remittance is allowed only after the Korean entity can evidence that corporate tax has been paid or withheld, that no outstanding customs or tax arrears exist, and that board approval has authorized the dividend; the bank then records the payment with an FX purpose code. The Korean won is not freely deliverable offshore for settlement in non-designated banking channels, and settlement timing differences can create liquidity friction. Export receipts are subject to statutory repatriation deadlines. Therefore, the treasury must maintain accurate FEIS records, isolate capital account injections, and keep documented current-account contracts to preserve full won convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, all foreign currency export proceeds must be repatriated through a certified Korean foreign exchange bank within 18 months of the export declaration date unless an extension is approved by the bank or relevant authority; for service exports by the payroll SaaS company, collection may follow the approved service-export settlement timeline and must still be received in full. The mandatory receipt ratio is 100% of the invoiced export value; there is no forced conversion percentage to Korean won, but the proceeds must be surrendered to the Korean banking system through a foreign currency account or KRW conversion and reported through FEIS.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea, as executing foreign exchange control agency under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and Korea Customs Service for export settlement enforcement

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs without a Korean banking license or e-money/funds-transfer authorization under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act are not viable as a primary treasury or corporate bank account. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires foreign-exchange business and settlement transactions to flow through a domestic foreign-exchange bank, and Korean AML rules require a local regulated identifier, real-name verification, and FEIS reporting. A licensed offshore EMI may only be used as an ancillary collection channel for small international payroll SaaS subscription receipts, and only if it routes actual settlement to the Korean Tier-1 bank account. Feasibility rating: Low for statutory capital, escrow, FX, or clearing functions; conditional Medium as a non-core receivables gateway.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: In South Korea, a Local File must be prepared if the aggregate amount of intercompany transactions with foreign related parties exceeds KRW 5 billion during the fiscal year. A Master File is required if the aggregate intercompany transactions exceed KRW 50 billion, or if the revenue of the multinational group exceeds KRW 100 billion and its aggregate intercompany transactions exceed KRW 5 billion. A Country-by-Country report is required if the consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion. Even if statutory thresholds are not met, the National Tax Service may demand contemporaneous documentation for any transaction that is individually above KRW 200 million in monetary value.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Doctrine in South Korea is embedded in the arm's length principle and the anti-abuse provisions of the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination. Article 4 of the Act permits the tax authority to disregard transactions lacking economic substance. For software and payroll automation companies, intangible related revenue and intercompany services must be supported by actual business substance, including personnel, office facilities, decision-making autonomy, risk assumption, and the conduct of Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) within Korea. A foreign parent or subsidiary that merely holds contracts and invoices without local substance will be recharacterized, and the income attributable to the Korean site will be taxed in Korea. The National Tax Service may apply upward adjustment based on the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and impose a 10% non-arm's-length penalty, plus compensation tax for unjustified transfer pricing adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 84(1) of the Individual Income Tax Act and Article 166(1) of the Corporate Income Tax Act, intentional false reporting to evade corporate income tax is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine up to KRW 200 million. For evasion exceeding KRW 1 billion, the punishment is imprisonment of up to 7 years. In addition, the tax authority adds a 30% penalty on the underpaid tax plus daily interest. The statute of limitation is 10 years for tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in tax evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act Article 167(2) and the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 43. Civil asset restitution requires directors to personally reimburse the evaded tax, penalties, and interest. A criminal conviction is entered on the director's record; the court may issue a stringent condition requiring prison time. Directors with outstanding criminal tax fines or unpaid tax obligations are subject to a travel ban (blacklisting) enforced by the Ministry of Justice, preventing departure from Korea. Persistent non-payment can lead to seizure of personal assets, including bank accounts, shares, and real estate. In serious cases, directors face imprisonment for up to 7 years and fines of up to KRW 300 million for the company.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 5(2) and Article 11 of the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination, tax authority may impose an adjustment to reflect the arm's length price. An additional 10% penalty on the understated tax amount applies (Article 20-2). A separate penalty of KRW 10 million (or KRW 5 million for small-medium enterprises) for non-compliance with documentation submission is set under Article 12(6). The adjustment may also trigger an 20% penalty on foreign related-party transactions if the tax authority determines that the taxpayer refused to cooperate.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally sign false transfer pricing declarations or direct the preparation of fabricated intercompany agreements may be charged with fraud under the Korean Penal Code (Article 347) and special tax law violations. If the transfer pricing fraud leads to corporate tax evasion, the director's personal assets can be attached. The National Tax Service may issue a 'Warranty of Collection' against the director's assets. Immovable property and bank accounts are frozen, and the director is placed on the National Tax Service's 'serious delinquent' database, which triggers asset seizure, credit damage, and the revocation of business licenses. Where a director is criminally convicted, the court may order imprisonment of up to 5 years, and a separate order to pay compensation to the tax authority will be levied against the director's estate.
Shell Company / Anti-Money Laundering Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Article 14 of the Act on Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information, failure to report a suspicious transaction is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine up to KRW 50 million. The Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) may impose fines and order administrative cessation of business. For unregistered financial services, the Electronic Financial Transactions Act Article 49 imposes a 15-year imprisonment maximum. The corporate entity may be dissolved and its tax benefits withdrawn. The National Tax Service may disregard the entity for tax purposes and tax the economic substance in the foreign parent's or beneficial owner's hands.

Director personal liability: The Legal Entity's directors are personally liable for AML violations under the Specific Financial Information Act and the Commercial Act Article 389(3) if they failed to exercise reasonable oversight. Directors can be held civilly liable for restitution of the full amount laundered and all related taxes. The prosecutor's office can issue a specific travel ban on directors suspected of money laundering, freezing all personal and corporate bank accounts without notice. On conviction, directors face imprisonment of up to 5 years for failing to report STRs, and up to 10 years for money laundering (Article 3 of the Act on Proceeds and Punishment of Crimes). Additionally, the director's directorial disqualification is automatic for 10 years after criminal conviction, and any assets acquired through the crime are subject to confiscation. If the shell company is used to facilitate tax evasion or sanctions evasion, the Commission on Good Administration may blacklist the director and company, barring them from public procurement contracts and banking access across Korea and potentially the EU/US via intelligence sharing.

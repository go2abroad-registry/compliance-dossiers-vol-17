Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS) - Home Tax Service (Hometax) online electronic portal at www.hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity seeking a Korean Tax Identification Number (TIN) must first secure a place of business in Korea or appoint a domestic tax representative if it engages in taxable domestic supply or maintains a fixed place of business. The foreign entity or its domestic tax representative must file a Business Registration Application (saeopja deungrok sincheongseo) with the competent district tax office (seomuseo) having jurisdiction over the place of business, attaching the foreign corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, power of attorney for the tax representative, and proof of domestic place of business. For VAT purposes, the NTS assigns a 10-digit business registration number. For non-business TIN purposes, a foreign entity may apply through the Hometax portal or via the tax representative. The application must be filed before commencing business, and the NTS issues the Business Registration Certificate (saeopja deungrokjeung) upon approval. The foreign entity must also report the opening of the domestic place of business to a foreign exchange bank. All NTS communications must be conducted in Korean or through an authorized tax agent; any change in address, business scope, or representative must be reported within 15 days.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover exemption threshold for domestic suppliers; any person supplying taxable goods or services in the course of business is required to register, regardless of revenue. A simplified taxable person status may apply to small businesses with annual gross sales not exceeding 80,000,000 KRW, but such persons are still registered and hold a VAT identification number. Non-resident electronic service suppliers without a domestic place of business must register under the simplified electronic service registration system on the Hometax portal when supplying electronic services to Korean customers, irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act, exports of goods are zero-rated when the goods are delivered to a foreign purchaser or moved from South Korea to a foreign country. Qualifying exports include direct export sales, sales to foreign tourists that are subsequently exported within the statutory time frame, supplies of services related to exported goods, international transportation services, and qualifying digital services provided to non-residents where the services are performed or used abroad and classified as exported services under the VAT Act. Zero-rating also applies to goods sold through foreign e-commerce platforms where the goods are exported and foreign exchange proceeds are received from the foreign buyer. The statutory criteria require: (1) the seller is a VAT-registered person; (2) the export is supported by documentary evidence of physical export; (3) title and risk of loss pass to the foreign purchaser; and (4) proceeds are received in foreign currency or in a manner acceptable under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. For offshore digital services, zero-rating requires supply to a non-resident outside South Korea, effective use and enjoyment outside South Korea, and evidence of the customer's foreign residency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Acknowledgment / Export Customs Clearance Certificate) issued by the Korea Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading (Master Bill of Lading and House Bill of Lading, if applicable)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) confirming receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Bank statement showing receipt of foreign currency from the overseas buyer
  • Export contract or purchase order from the foreign customer
  • Bonded transportation documents, if the export moved through a bonded area

South Korea operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) system. All VAT-registered persons issuing tax invoices for transactions exceeding 300,000 KRW (VAT-inclusive) must issue electronic tax invoices on the same day of supply and transmit them electronically through approved service providers or the Hometax API. The NTS receives invoice data in real time, validates business registration numbers and invoice formats, and performs cross-matching between issuer and recipient VAT returns. The anti-fraud framework includes digital authentication tokens embedded in e-tax invoice XML transmissions via the National Tax Information System. Cash receipts (hyeongeum yeongsujeung) must be issued through approved cash receipt devices and transmitted within 3 days. Non-resident electronic service suppliers must issue electronic tax invoices for B2B supplies and simplified electronic receipts for B2C supplies, and must file quarterly VAT returns through a tax representative or directly via Hometax. Failure to issue or transmit an electronic tax invoice attracts penalties of up to 2 percent of the supply value.

If the NTS determines that exported goods were not physically exported or that the export transaction is false or unsubstantiated, the zero-rating is reversed, and the exporter is liable for unpaid 10 percent VAT plus additional tax penalties and interest. Penalties include: up to 2 percent of supply value for failure to issue an electronic tax invoice; unfaithful declaration penalty of up to 0.75 percent of underreported taxable amount; negligence or fraud penalty of 10 to 40 percent of unpaid tax depending on intent; and possible criminal sanctions for fraudulent export declarations under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, including imprisonment and fines. The NTS may claw back previously refunded input VAT credits for zero-rated exports and assess unjustified refund penalties. Export proceeds not repatriated within the 180-day statutory deadline may be recharacterized as domestic taxable supply unless an extension is granted by the Ministry of Strategy and Finance or relevant foreign exchange bank; in such case VAT, additional tax, and foreign exchange penalties may apply. During audit, the NTS may demand original customs declarations, shipping documents, bank remittance certificates, sales and inventory records; failure to produce such documents within the requested period results in documentation penalties and can support criminal referral.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea has an open current account in line with IMF Article VIII status, and the Korean won is usable for bona fide trade and service payments without the need for central bank approval. For capital account movements, the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act operates on a negative-list system: eligible transactions are processed by a designated foreign exchange bank while unlisted transactions require prior approval from the MOEF or the Bank of Korea. A Yuhan Hoesa with non-resident ownership may receive paid-in capital under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act if the investment meets the FDI notification thresholds; the inflow is routinely registered by a designated Korean bank and capital verification is completed via the Certificate of Import of Capital. Profit remittance is permitted through bank channels after corporate tax withholding, but banks will require audited financial statements, shareholder meeting minutes, tax clearance certificates, and a Foreign Exchange Purchase/Sales application form. Practical hurdles include strict beneficial ownership disclosure, enhanced scrutiny of high-value goods such as jewelry and timepieces, and a statutory documentation retention obligation for every cross-border remittance. There is no unconditional surrender of export proceeds; exporters may keep foreign exchange balances, but settlement and repatriation are monitored through the Foreign Exchange Information System.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 28 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the related Regulations, export proceeds must be collected within 180 days of the export date, being the shipment date or customs clearance date for online cross-border parcels. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into Korean won; the proceeds may be deposited into the exporter's foreign currency account or converted as the company elects. Any expected delay beyond the statutory 180-day window requires a written application for extension made to the designated foreign exchange bank, and the bank must file it with the Foreign Exchange Information System. Non-compliance is treated as a foreign exchange violation with fines under the Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the monetary and FX statistics authority, with regulatory co-administration by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Financial Services Commission (FSC) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Offshore EMIs such as Stripe, PayPal, Airwallex and Currencycloud are not licensed deposit-taking institutions under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. They cannot provide the required KRW settlement account for a Korean legal entity, nor can they replace the designated foreign exchange bank for FDI notification, capital verification, or statutory FX reporting. Their current utility is limited to e-commerce merchant acquiring and card collection for online jewelry/watch sales; funds are then paid out to an onshore bank account. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For capital injection, the foreign founder must submit a foreign investment notification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before remitting funds when the investment meets the FDI threshold. The investor remits through SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary name exactly matching the Yuhan Hoesa registration, and the remittance field must identify the purpose as FDI capital injection or establishment of domestic enterprise. The designated domestic bank allocates the appropriate Foreign Exchange Transactions Act purpose code and, upon confirmation that the remittance matches the filed FDI notification, issues a Certificate of Import of Capital, which constitutes the capital verification instrument. If the initial capital is below the FDI threshold, the funds may be injected under a general capital transaction report through the Foreign Exchange Information System, but the same evidentiary chain and bank identification requirements still apply.Outbound trade payments for inventory, subcontract manufacture, design procurement and supply chain services should be settled through Korean bank clearing infrastructure: BOK-Wire+ for KRW domestic transfers; SWIFT MT103 for direct foreign-currency disbursements; irrevocable commercial documentary credits (LC) for high-value timepiece and jewelry shipments; and documentary collections for lower-value recurring trade flows. Each disbursement must be supported by a trade contract, commercial invoice, customs declaration and the correct Foreign Exchange Transactions Act purpose code. The company must retain the disbursement evidence for the statutory seven-year period.Foreign exchange forwards are permitted through a designated FX bank for actual trade, FDI or refinancing exposures with maturity aligned to the underlying settlement; speculative forwards are prohibited under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are available to Korean corporates for hedging USD/KRW or other deliverable currencies only when the position corresponds to committed export or import cash flows; the bank will require supporting trade documents and monthly revalidation.Currency swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps are permissible for longer-dated investment, borrowings, or refinancing obligations, but cannot be used to convert KRW into foreign exchange for unapproved capital account outflows.FX options are allowed to hedge contracted receivables and payables; the net premium paid must be commercially justified and must not create a synthetic leveraged position.

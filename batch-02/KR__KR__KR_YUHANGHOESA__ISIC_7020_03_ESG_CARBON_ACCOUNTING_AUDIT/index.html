Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. However, the full amount of every issued contribution unit must be paid in before the incorporation registration is filed. Monetary contributions must be deposited into a dedicated capital contribution account at a licensed bank, and the bank must not permit withdrawal of those funds until the company is registered and legal personality is conferred. In-kind contributions must be appraised and fully delivered before registration. No blocked escrow account is required, but the capital contribution deposit account is effectively ring-fenced by the bank until the company has been incorporated.

Occupational safety obligations under the Occupational Safety and Health Act administered by the Ministry of Employment and Labor apply only to ordinary office workplace safety and emergency plans.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating through the HomeTax electronic tax portal; for export clearance and customs-related substantiation, Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident shareholder forming a Yuhan Hoesa must appoint a Korean tax representative and establish a domestic place of business. The representative must prepare an apostilled or consular-legalized certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, certificate of good standing, board resolution approving the Korean establishment, passport copies, power of attorney for the Korean representative, local office lease, and evidence of the registered business address. The application is filed with the National Tax Service district tax office as a foreign corporation business registration application via HomeTax or in-person submission. NTS verifies the Korean business place and issues the Business Registration Certificate (Business Registration Number), which serves as the entity TIN and VAT registration number. If the entity intends to export, it must also register as an importer/exporter in the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system to obtain a customs registration number. The entity must then activate a HomeTax e-tax invoice account, obtain and install the NTS-issued digital certificate, and appoint a corporate accounting officer for VAT compliance.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not provide US-style check-the-box or entity classification elections. A Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic corporation by law and is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax under the Corporate Tax Act. The only elective mechanism remotely relevant is the statutory Korean tax consolidation regime for a qualifying domestic corporate group; no hybrid mismatch or entity-disregard election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 - no statutory economic turnover threshold; every business that supplies taxable goods or services in the Republic of Korea must register with the National Tax Service before commencing business, regardless of revenue. A Korean LLC is a general taxable corporate VAT person and is not eligible for the simplified small-business VAT regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated status is available on the export of goods physically removed from Korea, on supplies of services performed for non-residents where the service is used outside Korea, and on qualifying offshore digital services. For ESG carbon accounting and offset advisory exports, the Korean LLC must establish that the recipient is a non-resident enterprise, the carbon audit and advisory deliverable is performed for and consumed at the foreign client site, the contract is denominated in a foreign currency, and consideration is received offshore. If any part of the service is consumed by a Korean domestic fixed establishment or relates to Korean-located assets, that part is subject to VAT at 10%.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export clearance declaration (Export Customs Declaration) evidencing export from the Republic of Korea
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing physical movement of goods to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Foreign exchange settlement confirmation / Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank transaction report issued under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act) proving receipt of export consideration in foreign currency from the overseas buyer
  • Export contract, purchase order, and title transfer evidence showing the non-resident buyer accepted the goods or services
  • For intangible ESG/carbon advisory services: signed service agreement, documented scope of work, electronically delivered carbon audit report or carbon credit brokerage confirmation, and foreign recipient acceptance evidence demonstrating offshore consumption

Mandatory electronic tax invoicing through the National Tax Service HomeTax e-tax invoice system or an approved service provider. The Korean LLC must issue an electronic VAT invoice for each taxable B2B supply, include the NTS-validated business registration number, transmit the invoice data to NTS, and retain the standard XML retention copy. Korea does not operate a per-transaction real-time clearance block chain or fiscal stamping machine; integrity control is achieved through NTS electronic validation, encrypted authentication certificates, periodic transmission obligations, and cross-matching against customs and bank settlement data.

A zero-rated export claim that lacks credible customs, shipping, or foreign-exchange repatriation evidence is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply, and NTS will assess VAT at 10% on the full consideration. Previously refunded input VAT, including VAT refunds claimed on export-purchases, is subject to retrospective clawback with statutory interest and additional tax penalties. Fraudulent or falsely documented zero-rating, including inflated export invoices or false e-tax invoices, is subject to criminal prosecution under Korean tax penalty law, imprisonment, fines, and denial or suspension of export VAT refunds; repeated or aggravated cases may attract penalty surcharges calculated as a percentage of the false supply value plus collection proceedings under the National Tax Collection Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates an export-driven, regulated liberal current account and a partially liberalized capital account. The Korean won is convertible for documented current transactions such as trade settlements, service fees, and direct investment, but all cross-border transactions must be routed through a licensed foreign exchange bank and reported in the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Current account convertibility is effectively full once the bank verifies the underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration, or tax invoice; funds are credited to a resident foreign currency account. Capital account convertibility, including equity injection into a Korean LLC, debt financing, or acquisition of foreign assets, is permissible but requires a foreign exchange declaration and, for foreign investment of KRW 100 million or above, registration under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA). Profit remittance by way of dividends is freely convertible upon presentation of audited financial statements, board appropriation resolution, withholding tax calculation, and tax receipt; the remitting bank will not process dividends until the withholding tax has been paid. Excess funds in KRW may be converted to foreign currency only with a documented reason, and the bank must submit the transaction to FEIS. No broad capital flow localization or surrender ringfence exists for ordinary profits, but a treasury must maintain documentary integrity because the penalty for misclassified capital flows is heavy and can freeze the entity's banking access.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA and the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds must be received in foreign currency through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank generally within 18 months from the date of export declaration; an extension may be granted only by the Bank of Korea or the competent foreign exchange bank for justifiable commercial, judicial, or force majeure reasons. Once received, the proceeds may be credited to a resident foreign currency deposit account, and the mandatory conversion ratio to KRW is 0%; exporters are not obliged to surrender export proceeds. However, any conversion to Korean won must be executed at market rates through a licensed bank, and the receipt and any subsequent FX conversion must be reported in FEIS to maintain the audit trail.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), exercising powers under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations; the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) retains policy rule-making authority for capital transaction liberalization and foreign exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Very Low. Offshore digital EMIs and non-bank e-money institutions are legally barred from acting as the primary treasury rail for a South Korean resident corporation, because FETA requires foreign exchange and cross-border payment business to be conducted only by banks licensed as foreign exchange banks in Korea. Offshore EMIs cannot issue a resident Korean account, cannot access the Korean Won clearing infrastructure, and cannot make FEIS reports. An overseas EMI may be used only as a non-settlement ancillary invoicing/API layer, while all actual fund movement and FX conversion must remain in a Tier-1 licensed Korean bank; attempting to use an EMI for KRW settlement creates material risk of unlicensed money transmission and account termination.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be wired in foreign currency by SWIFT MT103 to the entity's capital escrow or designated operational account at a licensed Tier-1 Korean bank. The remitter must include the Korean foreign investment declaration number or foreign exchange bank capital transaction reference, and the remittance purpose must be tagged as paid-in capital / FDI equity injection. The beneficiary bank will verify the foreign investment report, credit the funds to the escrow account, and submit the receipt to FEIS. For any foreign investment over KRW 100 million, the company must first file a foreign investment report under FIPA and open the capital account in the same bank. The escrow balance may not be spent on operating expenses until the required capital verification has been issued by a licensed Korean certified public accountant or external auditor, unless the bank's policy permits an early-use waiver after FEIS confirmation. All conversion of the inbound foreign currency to KRW must be executed at market rate by the foreign exchange bank with a documented purpose code.For service-type disbursements under ISIC 7020 ESG carbon advisory activities, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 from the entity's Korean account to the counterparty's bank, with the payment memo referencing the invoice and underlying services agreement. If the payment relates to importation of stationary or commodity-linked carbon credits, a Commercial Letter of Credit or Documentary Collection can be opened through the same bank with presentation of commercial invoice, bill of lading or air waybill, insurance documents, and the carbon registry transfer statement. The bank will debit the KRW account, convert at market FX rate, and report the service or capital transaction purpose in FEIS. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal (progressive: 9% up to KRW 200m; 19% over KRW 200m; 21% over KRW 20bn; 24% over KRW 300bn)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

16.5% (15% corporate income tax withholding plus 1.5% local income tax surcharge); a bilateral tax treaty may reduce the rate to 10% or 5% depending on shareholding and beneficial ownership.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when total annual revenue equals or exceeds KRW 10 billion and cross-border related-party transactions equal or exceed KRW 5 billion; Master File is required when cross-border related-party transactions equal or exceed KRW 10 billion and total revenue equals or exceeds KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country Report is due for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea has no separate free-zone ESR filing, but substance is enforced through the substance-over-form rule under Corporate Tax Act Article 14, the real-transactions doctrine, the OECD transfer pricing framework, and treaty limitation-on-benefits / principal-purpose-test safeguards; a Yuhan Hoesa must maintain local office, payroll, director decision-making, risk control, and core income-generating activities in Korea to avoid profit re-attribution and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Carbon-Credit Accounting

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent tax evasion is subject to the Korean Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3(1): imprisonment for up to five years or a fine of up to twice the evaded corporate tax; NTS additionally imposes the 40% fraudulent underreporting surcharge and daily late-payment interest, and the corporate tax return can be recomputed without any deduction for sham carbon-credit invoices.

Director personal liability: The NTS can pierce the Yuhan Hoesa corporate veil where the director controlled the sham intermediary; responsible officers are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax and penalties, may be subject to civil asset restitution, travel bans and tax blacklisting, and face criminal imprisonment up to five years, with seizure of overseas bank accounts and carbon-offset proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Shell Invoicing

Statutory sanction: The International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 4 and 6 permit NTS to adjust the intercompany price to an arm's length result; the adjustment attracts a 20% negligence penalty or 40% fraudulent understatement penalty, daily late-payment interest, and denial of the deduction for undocumented intercompany service charges; failure to file transfer pricing disclosure forms triggers separate non-compliance penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or organize the false invoices can be held personally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties, subjected to overseas travel blacklisting, and prosecuted for tax evasion; where the corporation is insolvent after the adjustment, directors must make civil restitution from personal assets and may receive imprisonment up to five years.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Compliance Failure in Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: The Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Information Act and the Act on Prevention of Money Laundering impose criminal penalties of up to three years imprisonment or KRW 30 million fines for willful failure to identify beneficial owners or to report suspicious transactions; NTS and the Financial Intelligence Unit may blacklist the entity and impose administrative fines and registration cancellation penalties.

Director personal liability: For willful AML avoidance, the corporate veil is pierced and directors face personal civil liability for the amounts involved, travel bans and financial blacklisting, up to three years criminal imprisonment, and confiscation of the suspicious wire proceeds.

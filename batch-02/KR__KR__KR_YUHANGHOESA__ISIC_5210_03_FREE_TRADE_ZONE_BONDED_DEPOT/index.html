Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office, commercial mailbox, or shared drop-box address is rejected by Korean tax authorities, courts, banks, and the Korea Customs Service for this bonded warehouse industry. The Commercial Act requires a registered head office that is physically ascertainable; the National Tax Service requires a substantive business location for VAT and corporate tax registration; Korean banks require a physical presence, identifiable signage, and an in-person signatory meeting before opening corporate accounts. Limited shared-office space within the Free Trade Zone may be tolerated if it contains a dedicated lockable unit, business signboard, records room, and staffing, but a standalone virtual mailbox is legally insufficient.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic filing portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr). Customs-excise processing for the Free Trade Zone is administered by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Yuhan Hoesa LLC with a domestic fixed place of business (including a bonded warehouse depot in a Free Trade Zone) must file a business registration application under the Business Registration Act and the Value-Added Tax Act with the district head tax office that has jurisdiction over the principal or branch place of business. The application is submitted through HomeTax and must attach the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, evidence of the physical business address (lease or ownership proof), the FTZ operator license, and, where no domestic office has yet been opened, an appointment of a tax representative. The NTS examines the application, performs an on-site or documentary verification of the place of business, and issues the Business Registration Certificate containing the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which concurrently functions as the VAT identification number. Foreign entities that do not have a Korean place of business but are required to file VAT for digital services or import-related transactions must appoint a Korean tax representative and register separately with the NTS for VAT purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic corporation under the Korean Commercial Act; subject to Korean corporate income tax on worldwide income, 10% VAT obligations, local income tax surtax, and no pass-through classification)

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide a check-the-box election comparable to the United States. A Yuhan Hoesa LLC is automatically taxed as a domestic corporation; partnership-style pass-through, branch-equivalent classification, or hybrid entity election is not available. Separate consolidated corporate tax is available only to a qualifying domestic parent-subsidiary group and is not a tax classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 for a corporation; business registration is mandatory from commencement. Simplified taxable treatment (KRW 80,000,000 annual turnover threshold) is available only to individual sole proprietors, not to Yuhan Hoesa LLC. In the Free Trade Zone, import VAT and customs duties are suspended on goods entering the bonded depot until the goods are released into the domestic market; the depot operator must nevertheless be VAT registered for all domestic taxable sales and supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Korea, including further exports out of a bonded warehouse or Free Trade Zone after deposit, are zero-rated under the Value-Added Tax Act where the exporter holds the export declaration, the goods leave Korea through a customs-approved method, and the sale consideration is receivable in foreign currency. Qualifying cross-border services, international transport, and services directly connected with exported goods may also qualify for zero rating where supplied to nonresidents or performed with statutory evidence. In the bonded depot context, the 0% rate is substantiated at the time of actual export from Korea; earlier movement into the bonded area only suspends customs and VAT, and supplies released into the domestic market are subject to import VAT and customs duties.

  • Export Declaration Certificate issued by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing departure of the goods from Korea
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank evidencing actual receipt of export proceeds (equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List
  • Bonded warehouse release or departure record from the Free Trade Zone management system

Electronic e-Tax Invoice issuance is mandatory under the Value-Added Tax Act for all VAT invoiceable supplies. Yuhan Hoesa must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-Tax Invoice system, and the e-invoice data are transmitted to the NTS no later than the day after issuance. For final-consumer retail transactions, the cash receipt system must be used where required. The Korean system does not impose a pre-clearance fiscal-stamp or government-authorization API; anti-fraud control is embedded in the mandatory digital certificate, real-time reporting, invoice sequence monitoring, and matching of e-invoice data with customs and foreign-exchange declaration data.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-check e-Tax invoices, export declarations, bonded warehouse inventory records, and foreign-exchange receipts. If export zero-rating is not substantiated or the foreign-currency proceeds are not received within the required 180-day repatriation period, the zero rate is reversed, the 10% VAT on the supply is assessed retroactively, and additional tax (unpaid-tax additional tax and negligent or fraudulent reporting additional taxes) is imposed under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act. Where invoices are false or fraudulently zero-rated, criminal sanctions apply under the Tax Evasion Punishment Act, and the taxpayer may be barred from the expedited exporter VAT refund program.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized current account regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, meaning trade-related payments for goods, services, and current transfers are freely convertible through licensed foreign exchange banks. However, capital account transactions are subject to a notification, post-reporting, or prior approval framework administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance and delegated to domestic banks. For a Yuhan Hoesa operating a bonded warehouse in a free trade zone, inbound equity capital from non-resident shareholders must be routed through a foreign currency deposit account at a domestic foreign exchange bank, supported by a Foreign Investment Notification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act if the investment qualifies as FDI. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are generally permitted once corporate governance and tax clearance documentation, including audited financial statements, shareholder meeting minutes, withholding tax filings, and a certificate of tax payment, are submitted to the bank. Although no mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the central bank is currently imposed, all export-related foreign currency receipts must be collected within statutory deadlines and deposited or settled through a domestic foreign exchange bank. The Korean won is not freely tradable outside the regulated onshore and offshore FX markets for unlicensed non-bank entities, but authorized banks may execute KRW/USD and other major currency transactions for underlying trade and FDI liquidity. The overall convertibility risk is moderate, with strict documentation, transaction purpose verification, and intra-group transfer pricing scrutiny for trade-related flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds must be collected within 6 months from the date of export declaration unless an extension is granted by the foreign exchange bank. Extension beyond 18 months, up to 36 months, may be permitted for large-scale capital goods or deferred payment export transactions with supporting contracts. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to Korean won; all export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic foreign currency deposit account at a licensed foreign exchange bank. The exporter must submit a Foreign Exchange Payment Receipt Certificate and related customs declarations to the bank to evidence timely receipt. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline triggers a reporting obligation to the Ministry of Economy and Finance and potential administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Customs Act of South Korea

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs and digital-only banks are not viable as primary settlement institutions for this Yuhan Hoesa bonded warehouse mandate. Under the Banking Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act of South Korea, only domestic commercial banks or licensed foreign bank branches can operate resident Korean won corporate accounts, perform foreign exchange services, and interface with the Korea Customs Service electronic payment and bonded depot systems. Offshore digital EMIs are not authorised to hold KRW liquidity in the Hong Kong or Singapore clearing infrastructure for Korean customs duty payments, cannot issue compliant MT103 settlements into the Korean domestic clearing network, and are legally barred from servicing corporate treasury needs such as documentary LCs and import duty payments. The maximum permissible role for a fintech EMI is ancillary corporation payment facilitation through a duly licensed partner bank, but the Yuhan Hoesa must maintain its primary accounts, FX hedging lines, and customs payment rails at a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from non-resident shareholders will be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 through a licensed foreign exchange bank in Korea. Wire instructions must include the correct settlement currency, likely USD or KRW, and the purpose code indicating foreign direct investment or capital subscription under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. If the injection qualifies under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the Yuhan Hoesa must file a Foreign Investment Notification before the remittance, receive a Foreign Investment Notification Certificate, and open a foreign currency deposit account in the name of the Korea-resident entity. After funds arrive, the bank issues a foreign exchange purchase confirmation, converts funds to KRW if necessary, and completes a capital verification report verifying that the paid-in capital matches the submitted financial statements and corporate registry filings. The company must retain the SWIFT receipt and bank confirmation for MOEF and customs audits.Outbound trade disbursements for bonded warehouse operations will use SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for direct trade settlement, SWIFT MT700 series for confirmed or standard documentary letters of credit, and documentary collections via SWIFT MT400 series when transactions are conducted under collection terms. Offshore digital EMIs are not authorised to hold KRW liquidity in the Hong Kong or Singapore clearing infrastructure for Korean customs duty payments, cannot issue compliant MT103 settlements into the Korean domestic clearing network, and are legally barred from servicing corporate treasury needs such as documentary LCs and import duty payments. The maximum permissible role for a fintech EMI is ancillary corporation payment facilitation through a duly licensed partner bank, but the Yuhan Hoesa must maintain its primary accounts, FX hedging lines, and customs payment rails at a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Korea applies a progressive national corporate income tax (CIT) of 10% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 20% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, and 22% above KRW 20 billion; plus a local corporate income tax surcharge equal to 10% of the national CIT, producing an all-in top headline effective rate of 24.2% on the marginal taxable base above KRW 20 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory Korean withholding tax rate for dividends remitted by a Korean domestic corporation to non-resident shareholders is 22% in aggregate, comprising 20% national withholding tax and 2% local income tax surcharge; DTA-reduced rates commonly apply at 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for a foreign corporate shareholder holding at least 25% of the Korean company's shares, subject to documentary proof and limitation-on-benefits review.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10,000,000,000 in annual controlled transactions with foreign related parties involving tangible goods, or KRW 5,000,000,000 involving services, intangible property, and loans, triggers mandatory Local File preparation and filing in Korea under the International Tax Adjustment Act; Master File preparation is required when aggregate controlled foreign-related-party transactions equal at least KRW 50,000,000,000; and a Country-by-Country Report is required where the Korean ultimate parent or top-tier Korean constituent entity belongs to an MNE group with consolidated revenue of KRW 100,000,000,000 or more in the preceding fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not use the label 'Economic Substance Regulations' but enforces substance-over-form through Article 14 of the Basic Act for National Taxes, the beneficial ownership provisions of each double tax treaty, and the Korean General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) to deny treaty benefits and to disregard a corporation where the Korean Yuhan Hoesa cannot demonstrate day-to-day management, local employees, physical bonded warehouse facilities, real operational risk, and meaningful income-generating activity in Korea; for a bonded depot company, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include physical custody of goods, Korean customs clearance management, inventory control, storage and release decisions, revenue negotiation and invoicing, and operational risk assumption - all of which must be performed by the Korean entity rather than a shell-service provider, and the economic substance test will be applied in transfer pricing audits to confirm the entity is the true beneficiary of the Korean FTZ income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: This violates Article 60 of the Corporate Tax Act and constitutes tax evasion under Article 11 of the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, exposing the company to fines of up to five times the total evaded national corporate income tax and local surcharge, plus additional penalties of 20% for non-reporting and 10% for understatement; where the evaded amount exceeds approximately KRW 500 million, the Special Act on Aggravated Punishment of Certain Economic Crimes can raise the criminal imprisonment range to three to twelve years.

Director personal liability: Korean tax collection law pierces the corporate veil through the secondary tax liability doctrine of Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act, making a responsible director or actual oligopolistic shareholder personally liable for unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and interest where the company willfully manipulated returns and its assets are insufficient; the National Tax Service will place the director on the serious tax-offender blacklist, issue an overseas travel ban, freeze personal bank accounts, and seize personal real property; a criminal conviction also carries imprisonment of up to five years for ordinary evasion or up to twelve years for aggravated evasion, in addition to disqualification from serving on boards in Korea.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and False International Transaction Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 4 and 5 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, National Tax Service may re-determine the taxable income using the arm's-length principle and impose, in addition to the CIT difference, a 10% underreporting penalty and a 30% documentation penalty of the adjusted transfer-pricing amount, as well as statutory late-payment interest; knowingly preparing false transfer-pricing invoices or deliberately omitting controlled transactions with foreign related parties can be prosecuted as tax fraud under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, attracting fines of up to five times the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: A director who signed or instructed the false transfer-pricing documents may be jointly and severally liable with the Korean corporation for the adjusted tax, unpaid foreign withholding, and penalties under the secondary liability rules if the corporation cannot pay; Korean courts can impose personal asset recovery and require the director to repatriate shifted funds from the offshore parent, and the National Tax Service will issue an overseas departure prohibition pending payment; in egregious asset-shifting cases, directors face criminal imprisonment of up to five years for documentation fraud, with aggravated punishment when foreign banking channels were used to conceal taxable profits.
Bonded Depot Customs Duty Evasion and Sham Economic Substance / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: This triggers criminal penalties under Articles 270 and 271 of the Korean Customs Act for smuggling and duty evasion, including imprisonment of up to ten years and fines of up to five to ten times the evaded customs duties and applicable value-added tax; for the tax treaty dimension, Korean tax authorities will deny the DTA withholding reduction entirely, retroactively assess the full 22% withholding tax on outbound dividends and royalties, and levy neglected-tax penalties; the misuse of an FTZ license is also an administrative license-revocation event under the Act on Designation and Management of Free Trade Zones, and illegal bonded transactions can lead to closure of the warehouse.

Director personal liability: Korean Customs and tax authorities disregard the corporate form where the entity is a mere conduit or sham for tax-avoidance; directors who ordered employees to release bonded goods without duty payment or who attempted to conceal the shell nature of the Korean company will be pursued personally as principals in the smuggling offense, with travel bans, blacklisting, seizure of personal property, and imprisonment of up to ten years; a director who knowingly allowed the Korean LLC to be used for treaty shopping without genuine CIGA in Korea faces secondary liability for the entire unpaid Korean tax and civil restitution of diverted funds from the foreign parent, and the court can lift the corporate veil to attach the director's personal assets.

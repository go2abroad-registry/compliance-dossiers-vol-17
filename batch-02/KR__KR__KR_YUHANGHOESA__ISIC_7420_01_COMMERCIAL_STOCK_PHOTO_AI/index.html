Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. The capital amount and member contribution schedule are set in the Articles of Incorporation. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with online electronic operations administered through the HomeTax portal (hometax.go.kr) and the NTS Information Trading System (EDI).

E-government filing portal

A Korean-incorporated Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) is a resident domestic corporation and must apply for a Business Operator Registration Number at the district tax office having jurisdiction over its registered head office or domestic place of business. The application is submitted through HomeTax or to the competent head tax office using the statutory Business Registration application, attaching the articles of incorporation, certificate of corporate seal registration, certificate of incorporation or foreign business registration of non-resident shareholders where relevant, identity documents of directors, and evidence of the principal place of business. For a non-resident foreign entity that is not locally incorporated but is engaged in Korean VAT-taxable supplies, the foreign entity must appoint a domestic tax representative and file the same Business Registration application in the name of the foreign business operator through the NTS HomeTax portal. The NTS typically issues the Business Operator Registration Number within 3 to 5 business days, and that registration number simultaneously becomes the VAT registration identifier and the corporate income tax identifier. Passive non-resident shareholders of a Korean Yuhan Hoesa are not required to obtain a separate Korean VAT ID solely for their shareholding; the corporate entity holds the Korean TIN/VAT ID.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold applies to a Korean-incorporated Yuhan Hoesa; it must obtain VAT registration and issue tax invoices from business commencement regardless of revenue. The KRW 80,000,000 simplified-taxpayer annual turnover ceiling is available only to individual entrepreneurs and therefore cannot be used by the corporate Yuhan Hoesa. Foreign non-resident suppliers of digital services to Korean consumers are also subject to VAT registration without any turnover exemption threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The Yuhan Hoesa may claim 0% VAT on the supply of stock photography and AI-generated visual asset licenses to customers located outside the Republic of Korea, provided the services are supplied to and actually used by the non-resident customer outside Korea, are paid in foreign currency or otherwise as contractually agreed, and are properly evidenced as export service transactions. Physical export of a server or digital medium is not required; the critical statutory condition is that the benefit or legal license is entirely obtained outside South Korea by a non-resident party. Zero-rating is also available for supplies of goods physically shipped outside Korea after customs export formalities are completed. All zero-rated export supplies must be reported on the VAT return and accompanied by supporting documentation retained by the taxpayer.

  • Customs export declaration approved by Korea Customs Service, or the electronic export declaration number for physical goods exported from Korea
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other transport document evidencing physical movement of goods out of Korea (for goods exports only)
  • Commercial invoice describing the licensed digital content, license term, territory, price, and explicit indication that the supply is exported or supplied to a non-resident customer
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice issued through HomeTax or NTS-approved EDI showing the supply as tax-exempt export zero-rated transaction
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or inward remittance certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank demonstrating receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Executed license agreement or stock media purchase order demonstrating that the end customer is a non-resident and the license is for use outside Korea

VAT-registered taxpayers must issue electronic tax invoices for taxable supplies and transmit them to the NTS through HomeTax or NTS-approved EDI software by the day following supply. The NTS operates a real-time electronic tax invoice collection system; there is no third-party fiscal clearance stamping machine for VAT in Korea, but the e-tax invoice must carry a sequential supply number, supplier VAT ID, recipient VAT ID, supply value, VAT amount, and zero-rated export indicator where applicable. Failure to transmit a valid electronic tax invoice or intentional issuance of a false export invoice triggers statutory penalties and may invalidate the buyer's input VAT claim.

The NTS may open an export zero-rating audit and apply the standard five-year statute of limitations; if export documentation is missing, fraudulent, or inconsistent, the NTS will claw back the zero-rated treatment, reassess VAT at 10%, and impose statutory additional tax under the Value-Added Tax Act and Framework Act on National Taxes. Negligent under-reporting generally attracts an additional tax of 10% of the unpaid tax amount, fraudulent under-reporting attracts 20% to 40%, and knowingly fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating may also trigger the criminal penalty provisions of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment of up to three years or a fine of up to KRW 50 million. Late-payment interest accrues on all reassessed VAT, and fictitious export tax invoices may trigger separate tax invoice penalties as well as refusal/denial of export refunds. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day foreign exchange settlement deadline for export-related transactions, without an approved extension, may additionally constitute a violation of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and result in fines and further administrative sanctions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won (KRW) is freely convertible for current account transactions, while capital account transactions are subject to registration, declaration, or licensing under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. A foreign-invested stock photo marketplace operating as a Yuhan Hoesa may convert inbound paid-in capital to KRW after the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) report is filed with a designated foreign exchange bank; profit remittance in the form of dividends is regarded as a current account payment and may be remitted after the tax withholdings and the legal requirements of the Foreign Exchange Information System are satisfied. No mandatory percentage of realized foreign currency revenue must be exchanged into KRW; however, 100 percent of export proceeds must be repatriated through an authorized bank in Korea within the legally stipulated time limit, and any retention in an overseas account requires a separate overseas current account permit. For digital content and royalty income, the remittance of service proceeds requires a service contract, foreign currency invoice, and prescribed transaction report to the designated bank in advance. In practice, KRW convertibility is high for onshore licensed banks, but non-residents are not able to maintain free offshore KRW accounts, and settlement of domestic expenses creates a forced KRW conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds for digital content and services must be collected within 6 months from the date of service provision unless an extension is granted, while goods exports enjoy an 18-month collection period. The statutory surrender requirement is effectively 100 percent repatriation to a domestic foreign exchange account or settlement to an authorized foreign exchange bank, but the mandatory conversion ratio into KRW is 0 percent for proceeds held in a permitted foreign currency account. Proceeds must be reported via the Foreign Exchange Information System within the bank's reporting calendar; a separately approved overseas current account is needed if funding is to remain offshore.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Korea Federation of Banks' Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) registered as the delegated foreign exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMI accounts are not viable as primary banking rails for a Korean LLC. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires resident legal entities to carry out foreign exchange transactions with licensed foreign exchange banks, and foreign EMIs such as Wise or Revolut are not licensed by the Financial Services Commission (FSC) to provide Korean corporate accounts, local currency settlement, tax invoice support, or capital receipts verification. They may be used only as marginal collection aggregators for customer card payments, but the account must be funded and swept to a Tier-1 bank account, and any payment gateway must operate in compliance with the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Overall feasibility for treasury purposes is Low.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs, intercompany transactions with related parties exceeding KRW 10 billion per relevant year trigger the mandatory submission of Local File and Master File. The threshold is KRW 10 billion for the aggregate intercompany transaction amount. Small and medium enterprises are not automatically exempted if transactions exceed the threshold.

Economic substance scope: South Korea applies the substance-over-form doctrine under Article 2-2 of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs. The Korean entity must demonstrate sufficient economic substance through personnel, offices, decision-making functions, and risk-bearing capacity. For stock photography and AI visual asset business, Core Income-Generating Activities include AI algorithm research and development, content curation and quality control, platform server management, licensing contract negotiation, and assumption of content infringement liability. Where a Korean resident entity is treated as a foreign corporation's permanent establishment, domestic Korean income attribution must follow the Authorized OECD Approach. The Korean tax authorities may disregard a Korea-based paper LLC with no CIGA and reattribute income to the beneficial owner under the substance-over-form, resulting in personal and corporate penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act imposes imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine of up to KRW 100 million; additionally, up to 3 years' imprisonment for failure to comply with tax payment duties under Article 4 of the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act; the National Tax Basic Act imposes a 40% penalty for fraudulent underreporting and 20% for negligent underreporting plus daily overdue interest of 1.2% per month.

Director personal liability: Under Article 8 of the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act, directors are personally liable when the corporation's tax offense is committed with the director's knowledge and negligence; courts may pierce the corporate veil under Article 2 of the Commercial Act if the company is a sham or its separate legal personality is abused. Directors face civil tort liability for the full amount of evaded taxes, a travel ban and blacklisting by the Ministry of Justice, a ban from serving as a registered director of any Korean company, and up to 5 years' imprisonment; restitution of evaded taxes and fines may be enforced through attachment of personal assets.
Shell Company / Artificial Interposition for Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Article 2-2 of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs and Article 5 of the Corporate Tax Act deny treaty benefits and recharacterize the structure; withholding tax is reassessed at the domestic 22% rate on the full outbound flows, and penalty interest applies. If the interposed entity is deemed a sham, tax adjustment notices are issued to the beneficial shareholder.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax returns for a shell company may be jointly and severally liable for the reassessed corporate income tax under Article 3(1) of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs. Civil liability includes restitution of the entire tax shortfall, interest, and penalties; criminal exposure arises under Article 16 of the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act for aiding tax evasion, leading to imprisonment up to 3 years. A director may also be named on the National Tax Service's serious violator blacklist with publication of personal information and restrictions on foreign travel.
AML Failure in Cross-Border Payment Railing (Stripe/PayPal) and Suspicious Transaction Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 49 of the Financial Transaction Reports Act, failure to report a suspicious transaction is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine of up to KRW 50 million; the Financial Intelligence Unit may impose administrative sanctions, revoke the money-exchange business registration, and levy fines up to KRW 100 million under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. The Korean tax authorities may also adjust the unreported payment transactions as undisclosed gross income.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately fail to report suspicious transactions face criminal prosecution under the Financial Transaction Reports Act; personal imprisonment of up to 5 years is possible. Directors may be held civilly liable for losses arising from laundering proceeds; the corporate veil will be pierced where the company was operated as a tool for illicit foreign payment flows, allowing courts to order personal forfeiture of assets equal to the laundered amount. Travel bans are routinely imposed during the criminal investigation.

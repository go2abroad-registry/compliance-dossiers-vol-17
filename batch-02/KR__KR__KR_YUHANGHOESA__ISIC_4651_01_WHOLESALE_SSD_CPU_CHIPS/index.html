Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. Nevertheless, the entire subscribed contribution stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be paid in full into a blocked capital escrow account at an authorized Korean foreign exchange bank before the incorporation registration filing. The escrowed funds cannot be withdrawn or used until the company obtains its certificate of incorporation registration. If a foreign investor remits funds from abroad, a foreign exchange declaration is required; if the investment is reportable under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, foreign investment notification must be filed through KOTRA Invest Korea or an authorized foreign exchange bank before the deposit. The official state filing fee field is reported as zero only because the fee is variable by capital; actual registration tax of 0.4% of paid-in capital, local education tax of 20% of registration tax, and court fees are payable at filing.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea, operated through the Home Tax Service online portal at hometax.go.kr, with customs administration performed by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance portal at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Yuhan Hoesa in Korea must first complete commercial registration at the District Court having jurisdiction over the domestic registered office and obtain the Certificate of Registered Matters and certificate of corporate seal. Within 20 days from the day following business commencement, the entity must file the Business Registration Application with the District Tax Office having jurisdiction over the domestic place of business under the Framework Act on National Taxes. The application package must include the corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, shareholders registry and board resolution authorizing the Korean foreign-invested company setup, passport and identity documents of the foreign shareholder and the statutory representative, a power of attorney appointing a local tax representative, proof of the domestic office address such as a lease agreement or certificate of building registration, and, where applicable, the foreign investment report certificate issued under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Upon approval, the District Tax Office issues the Business Registration Certificate containing a single 10-digit Business Registration Number, which is the unified Korean Taxpayer Identification Number and VAT identification number used for corporate income tax, VAT, wage withholding tax, and all national tax compliance. After issuance, the entity must activate an electronic tax invoice profile in the Home Tax Service and register the local tax representative in the system. For a foreign non-resident corporation with no fixed domestic place of business but nevertheless carrying on a Korean VAT-taxable activity, the NTS operates a special simplified VAT registration procedure for foreign business operators, and foreign suppliers of electronic or digital services to Korean consumers may register through the Home Tax Service dedicated non-resident VAT registration channel.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Yuhan Hoesa is a Korean domestic corporation under the Commercial Act and is per se classified as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act, subject to entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide income. It is not eligible for pass-through treatment, no beneficial ownership transparency election is available for Korean tax purposes, and profits distributed to shareholders are separately subject to dividend withholding tax. The corporate tax liability is borne by the entity, not by the shareholders, and no territorial elective exemption is permitted for wholesale trading income; treaty relief, if any, applies only through the relevant income tax treaty and mutual agreement procedure.

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not maintain a check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime with respect to a domestically incorporated Yuhan Hoesa, and no hybrid mismatch election is available in the domestic statute. Hybrid mismatch consequences are to be addressed through the OECD BEPS inclusive framework and the applicable tax treaty using the competent authority and mutual agreement procedure where such double non-taxation or double deduction would otherwise arise.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary threshold applies to VAT registration in Korea; every business operator that supplies goods or services is required to register with the competent tax office within 20 days from the day following business commencement. The narrow statutory exclusion from registration for an individual small business with no fixed place of business and prior-year gross turnover below KRW 10 million is not available to the Yuhan Hoesa corporate entity or to the ISIC 4651 semiconductor wholesale brokerage activity, and therefore the Korean LLC must obtain a VAT Identification Number as part of its Business Registration Certificate without regard to anticipated turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 20(1) of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods are subject to VAT at the rate of zero percent. For the wholesale export of solid-state drives and server processors, the Korean exporter must establish that the goods physically left Korea pursuant to a customs export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service, that title and risk passed to a non-resident purchaser under the commercial contract, and that the consideration was paid in a foreign currency through a Korean foreign exchange bank. Qualifying offshore services, including digital or technology services supplied to a foreign service recipient outside Korea, may also qualify for zero rating where the services are consumed abroad and the consideration is received in foreign exchange, provided the statutory documentation is maintained.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration confirmation or export clearance certificate accepted and recorded through UNI-PASS
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Original bill of lading, sea waybill, or air waybill evidencing physical shipment outside Korea
  • Inward remittance certificate from a Korean foreign exchange bank confirming receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Export sales contract or purchase order setting out the parties, goods, quantity, price, and INCOTERMS
  • Bank foreign exchange transaction report and, where applicable, foreign exchange purchase certificate showing the disposition or repatriation of proceeds
  • Supporting correspondence and insurance documents demonstrating a genuine cross-border trade transaction

All VAT-registered business operators engaged in B2B supplies must issue an electronic tax invoice through the NTS Home Tax Service, and the electronic tax invoice must be transmitted to the National Tax Service no later than the day following the date of supply. The NTS operates a centralized e-tax-invoice clearing and validation system that verifies the Business Registration Number of both parties, reconciles issued and received invoices, and cross-matches declaration data with customs clearance records from UNI-PASS and foreign exchange receipt data from regulated Korean banks. Korea does not deploy real-time physical fiscal stamping devices, but the Home Tax Service electronic tax invoice system acts as a mandatory anti-fraud clearing mechanism and triggers automated risk scoring for unusual export transactions, zero-rated invoice patterns, and delayed or missing e-tax-invoice transmission.

The National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service conduct selective and post-clearance audits of zero-rated export transactions by reconciling customs export declarations, electronic tax invoices, bills of lading, and bank foreign exchange receipts. If a zero-rating claim is unsupported or the underlying export is determined to be fictitious, the National Tax Service will reclassify the supply as taxable domestic supply, assess VAT at the standard 10 percent rate on the export price, impose additional tax for late or underpaid VAT and for false or incomplete tax invoices under the Value-Added Tax Act, and add national tax interest and surcharges under the Framework Act on National Taxes. In cases involving intentionally false tax invoices, fabricated customs declarations, or fraudulent claims for refunds, the matter is referred to criminal enforcement under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and the entire VAT refund is clawed back together with interest, penalties, and potential criminal fines and imprisonment for responsible parties. Exporters must therefore retain the statutory documentation bundle for the full tax statute-of-limitations period and ensure that all export proceeds are repatriated to Korea within 180 days from the date of export declaration acceptance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalised current account and capital account regime, but the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act imposes reporting, designation, and approval requirements for certain cross-border flows. The Korean won (KRW) is convertible for trade and non-trade current account items, including international wholesale semiconductor transactions, provided the underlying economic substance is proven through customs declarations, invoices, and contracts. Capital account transactions, such as foreign direct investment, loans, and securities dealings, generally operate under a prior notification or post-reporting framework; however, large-scale fund transfers or changes in beneficial ownership can trigger enhanced review by the Bank of Korea and the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEBRIS). Profit repatriation through dividends is permissible once corporate tax is settled and audited financial statements are filed, but banks may request evidence of the original invested capital, tax clearance certificate, and board resolution. Surrender of export proceeds is not mandatory on a fixed percentage basis, but exporters are required to settle eligible current account receipts through a domestic licensed foreign exchange bank and report the collection method, amount, and overdue status within 30 days after the collection deadline. For a spot semiconductor wholesale trader, maintaining a robust documentary trail is essential because the BOK can request the underlying transaction documents to confirm the absence of disguised capital flows, third-party settlement without commercial rationale, or trade-based money laundering. The regime is therefore best described as fully convertible for legitimate trade but subject to granular reporting, monitoring, and periodic compliance audits, making adherence to statutory reporting timelines a non-negotiable treasury function.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (Presidential Decree No. 32770), Korean exporters are not required to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Korea; they may retain foreign currency in a domestic foreign exchange bank account. However, the exporter must ensure that all export proceeds are collected within a statutory term of 90 days from the loading or export declaration date, extendable up to 180 days with a prior report, and must be received through a licensed foreign exchange bank in Korea. The full amount of the export proceeds must be reported via the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEBRIS) within 30 days of actual collection. No mandatory conversion of foreign currency into KRW is imposed for export receipts; conversion is at the discretion of the entity. Non-resident-owned Korean entities are subject to the same settlement rules, and any delay beyond the statutory period without a valid delay report may result in penalties of up to 50 million KRW or criminal sanctions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Korea Customs Service under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech EMI (electronic money institution) cross-border viability is very low for a Korean-resident non-bank entity. Under the Korean Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Specific Financial Transactions Information Act, only licensed financial companies (banks, securities firms, or registered electronic financial business operators) may provide foreign exchange transfer or deposit-like services. The Financial Services Commission (FSC) does not issue standalone EMI licences for non-resident fintech firms; offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut Business cannot service Korean-domiciled business accounts without partnering with a local licensed bank and obtaining a report under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Operating an offshore EMI directly as the primary clearing rail would be considered an unlicensed foreign exchange business in Korea, exposing the entity to regulatory sanctions, bank account freezes, and potential criminal liability. A pragmatic arrangement is to use a foreign currency account at a Tier-1 Korean bank for settlement, while using an offshore EMI only for auxiliary low-value disbursements outside Korea, provided payments do not touch the KRW system and all underlying trade documentation is retained for the Korean bank's compliance review.

Revolut Fit: High

Fintech EMI (electronic money institution) cross-border viability is very low for a Korean-resident non-bank entity. Under the Korean Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Specific Financial Transactions Information Act, only licensed financial companies (banks, securities firms, or registered electronic financial business operators) may provide foreign exchange transfer or deposit-like services. The Financial Services Commission (FSC) does not issue standalone EMI licences for non-resident fintech firms; offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut Business cannot service Korean-domiciled business accounts without partnering with a local licensed bank and obtaining a report under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Operating an offshore EMI directly as the primary clearing rail would be considered an unlicensed foreign exchange business in Korea, exposing the entity to regulatory sanctions, bank account freezes, and potential criminal liability. A pragmatic arrangement is to use a foreign currency account at a Tier-1 Korean bank for settlement, while using an offshore EMI only for auxiliary low-value disbursements outside Korea, provided payments do not touch the KRW system and all underlying trade documentation is retained for the Korean bank's compliance review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound investment into KR_YUHANGHOESA, the non-resident shareholder must first file a foreign direct investment report (FDI report) under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (MOTIE) or a designated Korean bank. The Korean bank will issue a certificate of foreign investment (외국인투자신고확인서) after verification of the share capital, remittance sources, and ultimate beneficial owner. Funds must be remitted through a SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's bank to the entity's capital escrow account held at one of the Tier-1 domestic banks. The remittance purpose code must be 'Foreign Direct Investment' (code 3200) and the narrative should reference the FDI report number. Inward FX transactions exceeding USD 200,000 may be subject to an automatic receipt report to the Bank of Korea via FEBRIS; there is no prior approval requirement for a bona fide FDI. Capital verification (자금관리/자본금 검증) is handled by the bank and the foreign exchange broker, with the bank issuing a Foreign Exchange Demand Confirmation. The Korean entity must deposit the injected capital into a 'tax clearance account' and cannot disburse the funds until the bank's KYC review is fully completed. The entire capital transfer trail must be archived for at least five years, including the F/X contract, bank-certified report, share subscription confirmation, and the approval of the board of directors.For outbound trade disbursements, the preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for TT settlements, supported by the Korean 'Trade and Commerce Payment' purpose code (e.g., code 1210 for exports). For large or structured chip wholesale deals, the Korean bank will advise a commercial letter of credit (LC) as the most compliant rail, with the Korean entity as the beneficiary and the foreign buyer's bank issuing an LC. The entity must use the BK (back-to-back) LC or open a seller's guarantee if acting as intermediary. Documentary collections (D/P, D/A) are acceptable, but they carry a higher AML-review burden because the bank must confirm the underlying transportation documents. The treasury must ensure that all outbound wires include the exact trade invoice number and purchase order reference in the beneficiary information; any mismatch can trigger a 5-day compliance hold. For Korean won payments to domestic suppliers, the entity can use the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute (KFTC)-based payment system or the bank's virtual account; however, this is not cross-border and is outside the FX reporting scope. Cross-border payments must be settled in USD, EUR, or JPY as established in the trade contract; KRW is not an official settlement currency for third-country trades unless explicitly permitted by the bank under a KRW offshore settlement agreement.Forward exchange contracts are permissible only if the underlying exposure is evidenced by a real trade forecast or existing receivable/payable up to 12 months. The bank will require the entity to submit a valid tax invoice and a signed export/import contract; speculation is prohibited.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted for Korean residents on KRW cross-rate because KRW is a deliverable currency; however, if the underlying is USD/CNY or USD/EUR exposure arising from third-country procurement, the bank may allow an outright forward in those currencies only when the contract legs are fully booked with non-KRW settlement.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CIRS) are permissible only for medium-to-long-term debt or investment flows, not for short-term wholesale trading. The entity may enter into a rolling FX swap (외국환스왑) for up to 180 days with a Tier-1 bank, but the bank will treat the swap as a loan-equivalent facility and require collateral or a credit limit.Options are allowed only for hedging committed future FX cash flows; the entity must submit a hedging policy signed by the CEO and an annual utilisation schedule to the bank. The bank will reject any instrument designed to create a synthetic KRW exposure outside the reporting system.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Korean Commercial Act imposes no statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, the full amount of the contribution units stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be paid in before the incorporation registration is filed. The funds must be deposited into a pre-incorporation bank account opened in Korea in the name of the company or its formation escrow; the bank issues a certificate of payment of the full contribution amount. There is no permanent blocked escrow account after registration; once the court registers the company, the formation funds may be transferred to the active corporate operating account. For a non-resident member, the paid-in contribution must also be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before or within the prescribed post-registration window.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, online e-filing portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr). Customs-side export controls are handled by the Korea Customs Service through its UNI-PASS electronic clearance system.

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company): file a business registration application with the District Tax Office with jurisdiction over the Korean registered office or place of effective management within 20 days of business commencement, via HomeTax or in person. Attach the certificate of incorporation registration, articles of incorporation, lease or ownership evidence of the Korean address, capital contribution records, and shareholder identity documents including non-resident shareholders passports or corporate registration certificates. The NTS issues one unified 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) that simultaneously serves as the corporate TIN, VAT registration number, and electronic tax invoice identifier. For a foreign non-resident entity that has a Korean permanent establishment, the same domestic registration duty applies, and the BRN is issued on submission of a foreign corporation certificate, proof of Korean place of business, and evidence of the responsible domestic manager/representative. For a foreign non-resident entity with no Korean permanent establishment that nevertheless makes taxable Korean supplies or needs VAT refund substantiation, registration is available through HomeTax or via a domestic VAT representative, using the foreign entity certificate, tax residence certificate, and a written appointment of the VAT representative. These duties are grounded in the Corporate Tax Act, Value-Added Tax Act, and Framework Act on National Taxes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Zero monetary threshold for corporations: VAT registration is mandatory from business commencement. The simplified taxable person threshold (KRW 80 million of annual taxable supplies) applies only to certain individual entrepreneurs and cannot be used by a corporate M&A advisory boutique.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for exported goods that are physically cleared through Korea Customs Service, and for foreign currency earned services supplied to non-resident / foreign clients, including cross-border M&A advisory, international consulting, and qualifying offshore digital services, where the place-of-supply rules treat the service as supplied outside Korea or the service is actually used/consumed outside Korea. The Korean supplier must issue a zero-rated Electronic Tax Invoice, hold a written contract with the foreign counterparty, receive settlement in foreign currency through a Korean foreign-exchange bank, and maintain contemporaneous evidence of the foreign recipient's non-resident status and overseas consumption/use. Services supplied to Korean residents or consumed in Korea remain taxable at 10%. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports/services is refundable through the electronic VAT return after NTS substantiation review.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration record produced through UNI-PASS (required for exported goods; not required for pure service exports)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods from Korea
  • Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the Korean foreign-exchange bank's collection/export proceeds confirmation showing receipt in convertible foreign currency within the statutory repatriation period
  • Zero-rated Electronic Tax Invoice and signed cross-border service/export contract with the non-resident counterparty
  • Proof of the foreign counterparty's non-resident status (tax residence certificate or registration extract) and, for M&A advisory/offshore services, contemporaneous documentation of overseas client use, such as engagement letters, advisory deliverables, foreign payment trail, and project records

All corporate VAT suppliers must issue and transmit Electronic Tax Invoices (ETIs) for B2B supplies through the NTS HomeTax system using HomeTax web upload, API, or EDI channels within the statutory transmission window. The NTS validates and cross-matches the ETI data against VAT returns and the buyer's purchase records; late or missing transmissions trigger penalty surcharges and may disallow input credits. The mandatory cash receipt system covers B2C payments and functions with the ETI system as Korea's anti-fraud clearance architecture. Korea does not use a physical fiscal stamp; the ETI/Cash Receipt transmission to the NTS serves as the electronic fiscal seal.

The NTS (and for goods, Korea Customs Service) will claw back the zero rate if the export declaration, transport documents, FIRC/collection confirmation, foreign-use evidence, or electronic invoice records are missing, forged, contradictory, or not received by the statutory FX repatriation deadline (generally 180 days from export declaration or service supply, unless a lawful extension is granted under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act). The taxpayer is then assessed 10% VAT on the full supply value plus interest, additional tax for underreporting/unfiled returns under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and criminal fines and imprisonment for fraudulent zero-rating claims under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, South Korea maintains a liberalized but notification-heavy foreign exchange regime. Current-account transactions, including M&A advisory fees, management fees, and operating expenses, are freely permitted through registered foreign exchange banks provided the underlying transaction is verified and the bank files the required electronic report to the Bank of Korea. Capital-account flows, however, require ex-ante or ex-post reporting to the Ministry of Economy and Finance or the Bank of Korea. All inward foreign direct investment must be declared under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act through a KOTRA or a foreign exchange bank filing before the capital injection is received. The Korean won is substantially convertible on current account, but capital movements, including equity contributions, cross-border shareholder loans, and repatriation of invested proceeds, require the maintenance of a foreign exchange bank account, an investment registration certificate, and a capital verification procedure performed by the receiving bank. Profit repatriation for a Korean limited liability company is subject to corporate income tax, dividend withholding tax, a tax clearance certificate evidencing that applicable taxes have been paid, and an auditor-confirmed statement of distributable profits. The M&A boutique space is attractive because genuine advisory fee inflows do not require central bank prior approval, but any abnormal structure, round-tripping possibility, or non-resident beneficial owner connected to deal parties will trigger an extensive source-of-funds and underlying-transaction investigation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For merchandise exports, proceeds must be received within 15 months from the date of the export declaration and must be settled into the domestic banking system by either conversion into Korean won or deposit into a foreign exchange deposit account held with a Korean registered foreign exchange bank. The statutory obligation is to repatriate the full export invoice amount; there is no fixed percentage surrender ratio because the Korean regime does not impose a general mandatory conversion percentage, but the bank must receive evidence of collection and settlement before issuing export trade settlement confirmation. Exemptions for overseas retention or reinvestment of export proceeds require prior approval from the Bank of Korea or a delegated foreign exchange bank and are granted only for justified operational uses.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), operating in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as the primary clearing provider for onshore Korean won transactions. Foreign fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer do not hold a South Korean electronic financial business operator license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and cannot access the Korean won local clearing rail or Bank of Korea real-time settlement infrastructure. They may be used only as peripheral non-KRW collection or disbursement vehicles, and even then they require a partnership with a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank to move funds across the Korean border. Overall legal feasibility for a domestic Korean entity relying on an offshore EMI is Low; the compliant structure requires a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank as the principal account bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must first be declared under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act by filing a foreign investment report with KOTRA or a designated foreign exchange bank before the wire is sent. The remitter must use a clear payment reference stating the purpose as foreign direct investment into a Korean limited liability company, and the receiving Korean bank must open a dedicated foreign currency or won capital account to receive the funds. After receipt, the bank performs a capital verification by confirming the amount against the foreign investment report and issuing an investment registration certificate. The compliant wire protocol is SWIFT MT103 with purpose code FIRM or the bank-specific FDI purpose code, followed by submission of the corporate registration certificate, entity resolution authorizing the account opening, and the ultimate beneficial owner declaration. No inbound capital can be commingled with operating revenues before the capital verification is completed.Outbound disbursements for M&A advisory services should be executed through SWIFT MT103 with service fee purpose codes such as management or consulting service payments where the recipient is outside Korea. If the M&A engagement involves payment of a deferred purchase price or acquisition consideration, the preferred clearing rail is a commercial irrevocable letter of credit issued by the acquiring party's Korean bank, or a documentary collection where title documents and payment terms are settled through the banking channel. For recurring advisory fees to offshore affiliates or overseas experts, direct SWIFT MT103 wires supported by invoices, service agreements, and withholding tax certificates are required. All outbound payments must be matched against the bank's FX transaction reporting system and require a declaration of the underlying real demand transaction.Onshore Korean won forward contracts against USD, EUR, and JPY limited to confirmed underlying M&A fee receivables or operational payablesCurrency options on USD/KRW with a demonstrable real transaction exposureCross-currency swaps solely for hedging foreign currency debt or FDI capital commitmentsOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only where permitted for non-resident transaction counterparties; a Korean resident entity must execute hedging through an onshore Korean foreign exchange bank with underlying transaction proof

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as the primary clearing provider for onshore Korean won transactions. Foreign fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer do not hold a South Korean electronic financial business operator license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and cannot access the Korean won local clearing rail or Bank of Korea real-time settlement infrastructure. They may be used only as peripheral non-KRW collection or disbursement vehicles, and even then they require a partnership with a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank to move funds across the Korean border. Overall legal feasibility for a domestic Korean entity relying on an offshore EMI is Low; the compliant structure requires a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank as the principal account bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion in cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File preparation; Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds KRW 100 billion and the Korean entity's cross-border related-party transactions equal or exceed KRW 5 billion

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply the Caribbean/UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations as such, but enforces an equivalent substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 17 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. An M&A advisory boutique claiming treaty benefits, PE protection, or intercompany fee deductions must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) — deal origination, negotiation, financial modeling, execution of advisory mandates, board decision-making, and conflict resolution — are actually performed through genuine directors, employees, offices, bank accounts, and records in Korea; a shell entity without real economic substance is subject to re-characterization, denial of treaty relief, and piercing of the corporate veil.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, Article 3 and Article 9: imprisonment of up to five years or a fine of up to twice the evaded tax; additionally, the National Tax Service imposes a 40% bad-faith underreporting surcharge, a 20% or 40% non-filing surcharge, and daily late-payment interest at the statutory rate; criminal referral is automatic for aggravated evasion

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the company is operated as a sham: directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax under the secondary liability doctrine; personal assets are subject to civil restitution and confiscation; the directors are blacklisted as serious tax delinquents, which triggers a travel ban preventing departure from Korea until the deficiency is secured or paid; fraud-based evasion carries criminal imprisonment up to five years.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs penalties: failure to submit transfer pricing documentation attracts a monetary penalty of up to KRW 100 million; on re-determination, the tax authority imposes a surcharge of 10% of the adjusted underpaid corporate tax plus interest; the unfaithful-return surcharge may reach 40%; deliberate concealment of related-party transactions is referred for criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors who ordered the mispricing bear unlimited secondary liability for the resulting tax shortfall, with personal asset restraint orders and civil restitution demands; the director and the company are jointly designated as delinquent corporate taxpayers, generating a travel ban and blacklisting; if the scheme is proven fraudulent, the directors face criminal imprisonment up to five years.
Withholding Tax and DTA Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act withholding provisions make the Korean remitter personally liable for the un-withheld amount plus a 10% additional penalty; fraudulent claims are denied retroactively, requiring back-payment of the full 22% statutory rate with daily late-payment interest; Punishment of Tax Evaders Act criminal sanctions apply, including imprisonment up to five years and fines of up to twice the tax evaded; the National Tax Service may also impose civil penalties on the instrumentality used for treaty shopping

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for the withholding deficiency, holding directors jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; directors who signed or authorized the treaty-relief application face personal criminal liability, civil restitution of the diverted dividend or royalty amounts, asset confiscation, travel-ban blacklisting, and imprisonment up to five years where intent is shown.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. The capital stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be actually contributed in cash or in-kind and must be paid into or secured through a bank account opened in the name of the company or promoters before the registration court will accept the filing. The bank-issued deposit confirmation is submitted as evidence of payment. There is no blocked escrow account mandate; the deposited amount becomes corporate funds upon registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required for the office premises under the Fire Services Act, including a fire-fighting system completion certificate and compliance with fire resistance and evacuation requirements. Structural load inspection is required only to the extent of ordinary office occupancy. The office must obtain workplace safety and health compliance certification from the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency under the Occupational Safety and Health Act for all employees. Civil defense clearance is not applicable because the facility is a non-industrial professional office.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). Korea Customs Service (UNI-PASS single window) for customs export clearance.

E-government filing portal

The statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT ID is the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) issued by the competent district tax office under the Korean Framework Act on National Taxes. For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Korean Yuhan Hoesa (LLC), the procedure is: (1) incorporate under the Korean Commercial Act as a domestic limited liability company and comply with any foreign investment reporting/notification requirements; (2) register the entity and obtain the corporate charter/certificate of incorporation through the court registry; (3) within 20 days of business commencement, file a Business Registration Application with the district tax office of the NTS through the HomeTax portal or in person; (4) attach the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, office lease agreement, director identification (passport or alien registration card for foreign directors), any power of attorney appointing a domestic tax representative, and evidence of paid-in capital/funding; (5) receive the 10-digit BRN which simultaneously serves as corporate tax ID, VAT ID, and HomeTax digital identity; (6) if a foreign enterprise does not create a Korean incorporated entity but has a Korean fixed place of business (branch), register the branch under the same BRN procedure; if the foreign enterprise has no Korean fixed place of business but supplies taxable services in Korea, register as a nonresident VAT supplier through an appointed Korean tax representative using the simplified nonresident electronic service supplier registration pathway; and (7) after registration, activate an accredited HomeTax digital certificate to issue e-Tax Invoices and file VAT returns.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 for corporations: a Yuhan Hoesa LLC is a domestic corporation and has mandatory VAT registration before or within 20 days of commencing business, irrespective of turnover. For individual (non-corporate) suppliers, the simplified taxable person threshold is annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000, but a corporation is always a general taxable person.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Korean Value Added Tax Act, zero-rated supplies include exported goods, services supplied outside Korea, international transportation services, and certain designated foreign-earning supplies. For cross-border commercial litigation and arbitration services, zero-rating is eligible when the legal service is supplied to a non-resident recipient and the statutory place of supply is outside Korea, based on the service being performed or consumed overseas and supported by credible export documentation. Services rendered for Korean court proceedings, Korean regulatory advice, or for domestic clients are subject to 10% VAT. Qualifying offshore digital services are zero-rated when the recipient is a foreign non-resident without a Korean place of business and the service is genuinely consumed outside Korea. The exporter must preserve transactional records proving foreign destination, foreign recipient identity, service contract, work performance files, and receipt of foreign currency.

  • Customs export declaration receipt (export bill of entry equivalent) issued/approved through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement to a foreign destination
  • Foreign exchange transaction/settlement certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank, functionally equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), proving actual repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Signed cross-border legal services engagement letter/retainer contract and arbitration/litigation records evidencing the foreign recipient and overseas place of supply
  • NTS HomeTax e-Tax Invoice record marked as zero-rated export supply

All corporate taxpayers must issue tax invoices in the form of e-Tax Invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-Tax Invoice system or an NTS-approved electronic invoicing service provider/ERP system integrated via NTS APIs. Each e-Tax Invoice must be transmitted to the NTS no later than the day after issuance. The NTS validates the transaction dataset, assigns an issue/confirmation number, and cross-matches the buyer and seller transmitted data; this constitutes the fiscal clearance process. Physical anti-fraud stamps are not used. Anti-fraud compliance is enforced through mandatory digital issuance, statutory transmission timelines, NTS invoice-matching audits, QR/barcode-verified electronic invoice records, and an unbroken audit trail of the e-Tax Invoice supply chain.

The National Tax Service can disallow export zero-rating and assess 10% VAT plus statutory penalty additions if the exporter fails to produce credible customs, service-performance, or foreign-exchange repatriation documentation. False e-Tax Invoices and fraudulent zero-rated claims attract penalty tax for false/missing e-Tax Invoices, upward adjustment of assessed VAT, and severe additions including penalty tax at rates applicable to fraudulent tax evasion, retroactive reassessment of prior tax returns within the 10-year statutory assessment period for omitted or false returns, and criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. In addition, Korea Customs Service may impose export declaration penalties under the Customs Act, and the foreign exchange authorities may impose penalties for unrepatriated or overdue export proceeds under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized but statutorily controlled foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Ministry of Economy and Finance retains policy control, while the Bank of Korea and designated foreign exchange banks execute daily supervision. Current account payments are generally unrestricted once reported, but capital account transactions, such as inbound equity injections, loans from non-residents, and outbound repatriation, require a declaration, notification, or registration with the relevant foreign exchange bank or MOEF/BOK depending on size and nature. The KRW is not fully externally convertible and offshore digital platforms cannot freely clear KRW. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted to KRW; Korea does not mandate surrender ratios. Profit remittance is legal after corporate income tax and dividend withholding are satisfied, supported by audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and a Foreign Exchange Transactions Report. Banks review underlying commercial substance and may request proof that paid-in capital entered Korea through lawful banking channels before allowing dividend or intercompany remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For general export goods, the statutory export settlement period is six months from the date of export clearance, unless the exporter obtains an extension from the Ministry of Economy and Finance/Bank of Korea through its designated foreign exchange bank. Korea currently imposes no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio; exporters may hold 100% of foreign currency receipts in domestic foreign currency accounts or convert to KRW at their discretion. All export proceeds must be received through a designated foreign exchange bank and reported in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and the remittance must be traceable to a valid export customs declaration.

Central bank supervision: Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and Bank of Korea (BOK), acting as the delegated foreign exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital-only neobanks without a South Korean banking license are legally barred from servicing KRW-denominated corporate accounts, IOLTA/trust accounts, or local clearing rails. Domestic internet banks such as Kakao Bank and Toss Bank are separately licensed banks but do not provide the commercial-grade IOLTA/trust and cross-border litigation settlement infrastructure required for this treasury blueprint. The only compliant routing is through a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank designated as a foreign exchange bank, with SWIFT settlement and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inward remittances must be sent via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's regulated bank account to the Korean LLC's account at a designated foreign exchange bank. The remitter must specify the BOK/MOEF foreign exchange purpose code for capital contribution, loan, or operating funds, and the receiving bank will file the required Foreign Exchange Transactions Report. If the injection constitutes foreign investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the entity must submit a foreign investment notification through KOTRA or the foreign exchange bank, obtain the Investment Notification Certificate, and only then disburse the funds. After receipt, the bank issues a Foreign Exchange Purchase/Sale Certificate or Deposit Certificate, and a certified public accountant may prepare a capital verification report for corporate registration and capital augmentation. The funds must remain segregated in the IOLTA or operational account until the compliance and FX reporting checks are fully cleared.For cross-border commercial litigation settlements, legal fees, and trade-related payments, the compliant clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct traceable disbursements, commercial letters of credit (sight or usance) for goods-related trade obligations, and documentary collections (D/P and D/A) when no LC is required. All outbound transfers must be supported by the underlying award, court settlement, invoice, or cost judgment and must be reported by the designated bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. IOLTA disbursements require dual-authorization controls, positive pay, and matter-specific reference codes; MT202 may be used for institutional cover-to-cover transfers when a beneficiary bank routes via a correspondent, but the treasury instruction must preserve full auditability and reconciliation.Onshore deliverable FX forward contracts with a designated foreign exchange bank, permitted only when the entity holds a real underlying exposure such as an invoiced cross-border settlement, litigation payment, or investment receipt; the forward tenor cannot exceed the maturity of the underlying exposure without BOK/MOEF approval.Onshore FX swaps (currency swaps) between KRW and foreign currency executed through domestic Tier-1 banks to hedge cash-flow mismatches in the IOLTA and operational accounts, restricted to genuine commercial/trade exposure with speculation prohibited.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for offshore counterparties or parent-level hedging structures; an onshore Korean LLC may not use naked NDF positions and must evidence a compliant underlying claim or obligation before any offshore hedge is booked or back-to-backed through a local bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital-only neobanks without a South Korean banking license are legally barred from servicing KRW-denominated corporate accounts, IOLTA/trust accounts, or local clearing rails. Domestic internet banks such as Kakao Bank and Toss Bank are separately licensed banks but do not provide the commercial-grade IOLTA/trust and cross-border litigation settlement infrastructure required for this treasury blueprint. The only compliant routing is through a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank designated as a foreign exchange bank, with SWIFT settlement and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reporting.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Thresholds measured by aggregate intercompany transaction amount with each foreign related party: (i) Local File required when the transaction amount exceeds KRW 5 billion (goods/services transactions combined) or KRW 2 billion for intangible/loan transactions; (ii) Master File and Local File both required when the company's total annual revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and intercompany transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; (iii) per-country Country by Country Report required when consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations proprietary to Korea are applied through the substance-over-form doctrine under Article 4 of the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination; a foreign corporation with a Korean permanent establishment or a Korean incorporated entity in a zero- or low-tax holding structure without employees, premises, business decision-making, and risk-bearing capacity will be disregarded for treaty benefits; CIGA analysis for legal services requires that fee negotiation, contract execution, case strategy, disbursement of settlement proceeds, and client relationship management occur within Korea; passive holding companies with purely intra-group investment holdings are subject to the Korean CFC rules and may be denied treaty protection for dividend and capital gains flows

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Offshore Shell Structures

Statutory sanction: Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act imposes imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding KRW 100 million, or both; the National Tax Service recharacterizes the shell structure and disallows the diverted expenses under Article 41 of the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination; the unpaid corporate tax is assessed with a 40% negligent tax penalty plus sums and daily interest

Director personal liability: Korean courts pierce the corporate veil under Article 3(1) of the Commercial Act if a director uses the limited liability entity to evade tax and harms creditors; directors are personally liable for the full unpaid corporate tax, with asset restitution and confiscation of diverted proceeds; travel bans are issued under the National Tax Collection Act for unpaid tax exceeding KRW 100 million; criminal imprisonment up to 5 years is imposed on directors who signed false tax returns
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service exercises secondary adjustment powers under Article 8 of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs, imputing deemed distributions and assessing withholding tax on the excess; documentary penalties of up to KRW 50 million under Article 50 of the same law for non-filing or incomplete TP documentation; the corrected tax base is subject to a 10-40% underreporting false return penalty under the Framework Act

Director personal liability: Directors are deemed joint guarantors where the corporation is nominally a façade; civil liability extends to restitution of tax arrears, interest, and penalty surcharges; directors who certified false financial statements face imprisonment up to 7 years under the External Audit Act and Punishment of Tax Evasion Act; a personal blacklisting order forbids departure from Korea until all assessed taxes are settled
AML-Compliant Trust Account Clinics: abuse of IOLTA equivalent trust fund and unauthorized cross-border capital flows

Statutory sanction: Operating an unregistered legal trust service violates the Judicial Scriveners Act and the Framework Act on Real Estate and Financial Trusts; the Financial Services Commission levies an administrative penalty of up to KRW 200 million and confiscates the profit; violation of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act for unreported outward remittance above USD 10,000 imposes a fine up to KRW 100 million or imprisonment up to 3 years

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability for money laundering facilitation under Article 9 of the Proceeds of Crime Act with imprisonment of not less than 3 years; civil courts pierce the corporate veil where the trust account was used as the director's alter ego, exposing the director's personal assets, overseas real estate, and bank accounts to seizure; travel and banking restrictions apply under the Korean blacklist system, and the director may be disqualified from holding future directorships

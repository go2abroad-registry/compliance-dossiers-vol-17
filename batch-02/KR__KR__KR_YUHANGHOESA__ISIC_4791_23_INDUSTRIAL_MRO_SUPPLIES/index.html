E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity without a fixed place of business in Korea must appoint a domestic tax representative before any VAT registration. The representation agreement and a power of attorney must be submitted to the NTS district tax office that has jurisdiction over the tax representative. The foreign entity must file the Application for Business Registration (Sa-roe-p-ja-deung-rok) together with certified copies of the entity's Articles of Incorporation, the Certificate of Foreign Business Registration or equivalent incorporation evidence from the home country, a notarized copy of the foreign entity's corporation certificate, identification of the entity's representatives, and the domestic tax representative's appointment documents. For the Yuhan Hoesa LLC, which is a Korean domestic legal entity, the standard procedure applies: the company must complete business registration at the local NTS district tax office after company registration, and the NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Number (TIN/VAT ID) within several working days. A foreign non-resident seller of digital or e-commerce services without a fixed place of business must still obtain a VAT registration number through a tax representative and must report supply of services subject to Korean VAT, including via the HomeTax portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None under Korean tax law. A Yuhan Hoesa is statutorily treated as a domestic corporation for Korean corporate and VAT purposes and has no check-the-box or pass-through election. A wholly-owned Korean subsidiary of a domestic corporate group may elect consolidated corporate tax filing under the Framework Act on Corporate Taxation if all qualifying subsidiaries consent, but this affects income tax, not indirect tax. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available domestically; only treaty-based tie-breaker arguments may apply, and these are not registration elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All resident business entities are required to register for VAT before commencement of business. A simplified taxpayer with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000 may use simplified filing, but there is no exemption from registration for a domestic LLC. Foreign non-resident suppliers supplying goods or services in Korea or electronic services to Korean customers are subject to mandatory VAT registration without any turnover threshold; a tax representative is required. Thus, the only operative threshold for practical simplified registration is KRW 80,000,000 annual turnover, which does not defer registration for a limited liability company formed as a Yuhan Hoesa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 24 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act for the export of goods when the goods are physically moved out of Korea and the export declaration is accepted by Korean Customs, for the supply of services rendered to foreign persons for use outside Korea, and for the supply of goods or services incidental to international maritime or air transport. For the B2B e-commerce MRO catalog model, zero-rating can be applied to supplies of tangible MRO hardware sold to customers outside Korea, provided the export declaration is made and the products are delivered through an export process; digital services (if any) supplied to non-Korean B2B customers for use outside Korea may also qualify if the place of supply is properly substantiated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry or reference number as accepted by Korea Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing international carriage
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the shipped MRO items
  • Foreign Exchange Trading Certificate or Inward Remittance Advice issued by a foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in an eligible currency

All VAT-registered Korean businesses, including Yuhan Hoesa LLCs, must issue electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) for supplies to other VAT-registered businesses. E-tax invoices must be transmitted to the National Tax Service through the HomeTax real-time clearance system by the day after the date of issuance. The NTS assigns a unique tax invoice number and validates the invoice in its central system. For B2C supplies, cash receipts must be issued and reported through HomeTax if the customer requests them or if the transaction is paid by credit card. The Korean e-tax invoice system is mandatory regardless of sector, and there is no alternative paper-based clearance for VAT-registered entities; non-compliance triggers procedural penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Korean Customs and the National Tax Service closely scrutinize export zero-rating claims. The spatial movement of goods must be corroborated by the full export documentation bundle, and the exporter must maintain records of the transaction, including the customs export declaration, B/L, commercial invoice, packing list, and evidence of foreign exchange receipt. If the NTS determines that a zero-rated export was not actually exported or that documentation is false, it will assess the 10% VAT retroactively and impose an additional penalty of up to 40% of the unpaid tax for deliberate evasion, together with penalties for false or omitted tax invoices and a negligence surcharge of 10% for incomplete substantiation; the taxpayer is also liable for interest for late payment. Willful submission of forged export evidence constitutes criminal tax fraud under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and may result in up to three years imprisonment or a fine. The NTS also has a five-year statutory audit window (ten years for tax fraud or reasonable cause to suspect intentional concealment) for restoring excluded zero-rated supplies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely convertible foreign exchange regime, with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) providing for market-determined exchange rates and generally unrestricted current-account convertibility. The Korean won (KRW) is freely usable for trade and service transactions, and the Bank of Korea intervenes only under exceptional one-way volatility conditions to smooth disorderly market movements. Capital-account transactions, including inbound foreign direct investment (FDI), are also legally permitted but are subject to mandatory reporting and declaration mechanisms administered by the Ministry of Strategy and Finance and delegated to foreign exchange banks. Surrender requirements for export proceeds are not based on a statutory conversion ratio; rather, exporters are required to receive export settlement proceeds within a defined period and may hold such proceeds in designated foreign currency accounts. Profit remittance by a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) is legally permissible once the foreign-invested entity has filed the annual foreign exchange report and fulfilled corporate income tax and withholding tax obligations. Practical hurdles to dividend remittance include documentary demands for certified financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and proof of the original capital injection through the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) notification track. Overall, current-account profits are freely repatriable, but capital-account movements require impeccable paper trails and adherence to reporting timelines to avoid penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must receive export proceeds within 180 days from the date of export clearance, extendable through prior approval from the foreign exchange bank for justified operational reasons. No mandatory loan is made up to a fixed percentage of export receipts; instead, the proceeds may be deposited into a foreign currency account at a tier-1 domestic bank, and the exporter must present the underlying export customs declaration (Sinyoung and KCS movement) plus the export bill of lading to support the receipt and exemption from immediate conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) alongside the Ministry of Strategy and Finance (MOSF) as the coordinating Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Qonto, Airwallex) have low viability and material legal constraints for serving a Korean legal entity. Under the Real Name Financial Transactions Act and FETA, an authorized foreign exchange bank must be a licensed financial institution in Korea; offshore EMIs generally do not hold a Korean banking license and cannot provide local IBAN-like account rails for KRW clearing. In addition, KYC requirements mandate presence of the company's signatory in Korea, and banks are liable for identity verification. An overseas EMI cannot perform the required in-branch identification and foreign investment notification. Therefore, use of offshore EMIs is limited to ancillary payment collection via card acquiring or trade settlement facilitation through partners, but they are not acceptable as the primary corporate treasury account provider.

Therefore, use of offshore EMIs is limited to ancillary payment collection via card acquiring or trade settlement facilitation through partners, but they are not acceptable as the primary corporate treasury account provider.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national marginal rate; 26.4% combined headline rate when adding the 10% local income tax surcharge (progressive national tariffs: 9% for taxable income up to KRW 200m, 19% for KRW 200m-KRW 20bn, 21% for KRW 20bn-KRW 300bn, 24% above KRW 300bn)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% combined statutory withholding rate on outbound dividends paid by a Korean entity or branch to a non-resident corporation (20% national corporate withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge); a DTA may reduce the rate to 5-15%, normally 10% for a direct investor holding at least 10% and 15% otherwise in most Korean treaties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The mandatory Local File threshold is annual cross-border related-party transactions of at least KRW 30 billion (roughly USD 22 million); the Master File is additionally mandatory for a multinational group whose consolidated Korean revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and whose cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 30 billion, with the same trigger applying to the preceding business year; the Korean branch of the foreign MRO catalogue business must prepare contemporaneous documentation even below the threshold if the transaction amount is in a high-risk category.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not maintain a named Economic Substance Regulation regime like the UAE, but the economic substance doctrine is fully embedded in Korean tax law: Article 4 of the International Tax Adjustment Act authorizes the NTS to disregard substanceless transactions, Article 18 imposes CFC tax on base-company income that lacks real business activity, and the OECD authorized OECD approach is used for permanent establishment attribution. The MRO catalog e-commerce vehicle must demonstrate in Korea core income-generating activities (CIGA): local management, local warehousing/fulfilment, order-taking systems, credit-risk bearing, supplier contracts, and back-office staff. A Korean distribution entity with no Korean employees or business premises is a red flag and will be treated as having a Korean permanent establishment whose attributable profit and non-resident tax cannot be eliminated by re-domiciling the contract to a Hong Kong or Singapore shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act imposes imprisonment of up to 2 years and/or fines up to 3 times the evaded tax; Article 4 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and Article 47-3 impose a 40% erroneous-report penalty and daily overdue surcharge; once the amount evaded exceeds KRW 500 million, Article 3 of the Act on Aggravated Punishment of Specific Economic Crimes raises the imprisonment range to 3-20 years with a fine of 2-5 times the economic profit.

Director personal liability: The Korean courts have consistently allowed piercing of the corporate veil when the entity is used to avoid taxes and separate the property rights of shareholders; directors may be jointly and severally assessed for the unpaid corporate tax, restitution and civil damages; a director convicted of tax fraud faces personal imprisonment, disqualification from serving as a director, seizure of personal bank accounts and shares, and a Ministry of Justice departure restriction/blacklist that bars exit from Korea until the tax and penalties are paid.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Article 4 of the International Tax Adjustment Act and Article 51 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, the NTS may disregard the intermediary, reallocate income to the Korean operating entity, and require the arm's length amount plus an additional tax of 40% on the overstated deductions; the use of shell entities to claim a treaty reduction is treated as treaty abuse punishable under Article 12 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and may be referred to the prosecutor's office.

Director personal liability: Directors who are signatories to the shell company's bank account and contracts face personal liability when the corporate form is pierced: the tax lien attaches to personal and family assets; in addition to civil restitution, the director may be summoned for investigative interrogation, prohibited from leaving the country under the Immigration Control Act Article 4, and jailed for up to 5 years; de facto directors are held equally liable in administrative audits.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance and Illegal Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Article 98 and Article 111 of the Corporate Tax Act hold the Korean payer jointly liable to remit the unpaid withholding tax, with an additional 10% - 20% surcharge; a false withholding exemption under a tax treaty triggers the 40% erroneous report penalty under Article 47-3 of the Framework Act and, if the unpaid withholding tax exceeds KRW 500 million, criminal prosecution under the Act on Aggravated Punishment of Specific Economic Crimes with imprisonment of more than 3 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize the remittance without withholding are treated as withholding agents and are personally liable to the National Tax Service for the unremitted amount, interest, and penalties; criminal conviction carries personal imprisonment and a fine equal to 2-5 times the evaded amount; the director may be asset-frozen, default-listed, and deprived of travel rights by the Korea Immigration Service until all restitution is made, including cross-border enforcement through the Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax Matters convention.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under Korean statutory law: a Yuhan Hoesa may be formed with zero share capital. However, if the shareholders resolve to adopt a stated capital, the full amount must be paid into a frozen escrow deposit account at a licensed Korean commercial bank before the registration filing; funds remain blocked until the certificate of incorporation is issued and cannot be withdrawn without board authorization after the statutory registration.

Funding & unlock path: The facility must obtain a Fire Safety System Completion Certificate (Gyeongcheongso-bang) validated by the National Fire Agency, including a fire alarm system, portable extinguishers rated for at least 20 square meters per class, and an evacuation plan. A structural load inspection certificate for any rack storage over 5 meters must be submitted to the local government. Additionally, an Occupational Health and Safety Compliance Certificate from the Ministry of Employment and Labor is mandatory, confirming the assignment of a workplace safety manager, emergency drills, and fit-testing for personnel handling hazardous aircraft residuals.

Paid-in capital rule: None under Korean statutory law: a Yuhan Hoesa may be formed with zero share capital. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, administered through the Home Tax Service (HTS) electronic portal at hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Korean Yuhan Hoesa or a foreign non-resident entity with a domestic supply obligation, the statutory tax identification (Business Registration Number / VAT ID) is obtained through Business Registration under the Value-Added Tax Act. A foreign non-resident without a domestic place of business must first appoint a domestic tax representative with a notarized power of attorney under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Submit the Business Registration Application through the Home Tax Service (HTS) or to the competent district tax office, together with the articles of incorporation, corporate registry certificate, foreign investment report where required, director/shareholder identification, proof of place of business, and tax representative appointment letter. For a domestic Korean LLC, business registration must also follow completion of incorporation at the Korean Commercial Registry; NTS then issues the 10-digit Business Registration Certificate which serves simultaneously as the Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number and VAT ID. Registration must be completed before or within 20 days after business commencement, and failure to register does not relieve VAT liability.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident domestic corporation; profits taxed at entity level under the Corporate Tax Act, with no pass-through for a Yuhan Hoesa)

Available election forms

None. Korea has no check-the-box or comparable entity-classification election. The Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily a domestic corporation under the Commercial Act and Corporate Tax Act, and no statutory hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold; VAT registration is mandatory from commencement of taxable business in Korea. A simplified taxpayer threshold of KRW 80,000,000 annual gross revenue applies only to eligible small individual businesses, not to a corporate LLC distributing surplus commercial aircraft parts.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of tangible goods, including certified civil aircraft rotables and avionics surplus, is zero-rated under the Value-Added Tax Act only where the goods are declared to Korea Customs Service for export, physically leave Korea within the authorised export period, and the VAT-registered exporter retains full documentary proof of export and foreign-currency receipt. The recipient must be a non-resident buyer and title must pass to that buyer outside Korea. Domestic supplies within Korea, or goods diverted to the Korean market after leaving a bonded area, are not zero-rated. For qualifying offshore digital services, zero-rating applies only to B2B supplies to non-residents where place of supply and use are outside Korea; this payload is goods-based and therefore substantiated by customs export records.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Clearance / Export Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier evidencing actual physical export of the surplus commercial aircraft parts from Korea
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Korea Foreign Exchange Bank Export-Proceeds Settlement Receipt confirming receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency

Electronic Tax Invoices (e-Tax Invoices) are mandatory for all VAT-registered business persons under the Value-Added Tax Act. Each taxable supply must be issued through an e-Tax Invoice and transmitted to the Home Tax Service using an NTS-accepted channel: HTS web portal, integrated public or private e-invoice platforms, or API-linked accounting software. There is no per-supply pre-clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping regime; the NTS validates e-invoices in its central repository through real-time error checks after issue. E-invoice submission must occur within the statutory deadline prescribed by the Value-Added Tax Act, with severe additional taxes for late or omitted transmission.

If the National Tax Service determines that an export zero-rating claim lacks physical export evidence, transport documentation, or timely foreign-exchange repatriation, the zero-rated treatment is retrospectively clawed back and the supply is reclassified as a domestic taxable supply at 10% VAT. Under the Framework Act on National Taxes, the NTS may impose additional taxes of up to 40% of the underpaid tax for fraudulent underreporting, 20% for negligent underreporting, plus daily interest, and the deficiency assessment period may be extended to 10 years for fraudulent conduct. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Customs Act separately impose penalties for non-repatriated export proceeds and false export declarations, including criminal referral and customs fines. Any input VAT credits previously recovered on zero-rated exports are redetermined and recoverable, and the taxpayer must retain the customs export declaration, transport documents, FIRC/bank settlement evidence, commercial invoice, packing list, and aviation technical documentation for the full statutory record-keeping period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized current account and a registration-based capital account; the Korean won is widely convertible for genuine trade, service, and dividend flows through authorized foreign exchange banks, but it is not an absolutely unrestricted offshore currency. Current account items, including aviation parts import/export payments and after-tax profit remittances, can be cleared without prior central bank approval once the bank files the statutory foreign exchange transaction report. Capital account flows, including foreign equity injection and capital repatriation, are documented under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, or the prescribed foreign exchange information system notice, with timely reporting and purpose-code classification. Export receipts must be settled through an authorized FX bank within the statutory 18-month export collection window; there is no forced surrender to the government, and exporters may maintain the proceeds in foreign-currency accounts or convert them into KRW freely. Practical restrictions are documentary rather than administrative; an aviation spares trader must present invoices, shipping documents, end-user certificates, customs records, tax clearance, and transactional evidence to avoid mis-invoicing and sanction concerns. The Bank of Korea separately monitors excessive volatility and may smooth disorderly market conditions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its enforcement decrees, export proceeds must be collected and settled through an authorized foreign exchange bank in the name of the Korean exporter within 18 months from the date of export declaration; extension is available where justifiable grounds are accepted by the bank or regulator. There is no mandatory conversion ratio in the strict residency sense, because exporters are allowed to remain in foreign currency as a foreign-currency deposit or to convert to KRW at market rate; however, 100% of the export receipt must be routed to the authorized domestic banking channel and reported in the Foreign Exchange Information System.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (central bank) as statutory overseer of the foreign exchange market under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Ministry of Economy and Finance retains regulatory policy authority for cross-border capital transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Very Low. For a Korean resident entity, a foreign fintech EMI is not a permitted statutory account-holding institution under the Banking Act, the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, or the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. It cannot issue KRW settlement accounts, cannot verify inbound foreign investment capital for the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and cannot meet the authorized foreign exchange bank prerequisite for export settlement. Consequently, the treasury plan must use domestic commercial banking rails; foreign EMIs may be used only for secondary non-statutory information messaging or third-party payout outsourcing if local banking law permits, but never as the primary clearing venue.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use an authorized foreign exchange bank as the receiving statutory settlement bank. Funds must be remitted from the non-resident shareholder's own account in the shareholder's own name and credited to the Korean entity's capital escrow account. The SWIFT instruction must include the statutory foreign exchange purpose code for non-resident equity investment, the underlying subscription agreement reference, contract number, and the description Yuhan Hoesa inbound equity injection for aviation spares distribution. After receipt, the bank files the foreign exchange transaction report; the entity submits an FDI report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act if the shareholder is foreign and the investment will be finalized; then capital verification is performed via a certificate of inward foreign exchange remittance, bank statement, board resolution, equity subscription agreement, and audited balance sheet confirming the contribution is permanently available. No inbound funds may be split across EMIs.For low-value rotable purchases and sales abroad, use SWIFT MT103 with full invoice and underlying contract reference; for high-value or contractually structured aviation parts transactions, use an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit confirmed by a top-tier bank, with sight drafts and documents against payment; for medium-risk established counterparties, use Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) with a collection bank instruction. All outbound payments must be made through an authorized FX bank, matched with the underlying export or import declaration to Korea Customs Service, assigned the appropriate trade-settlement purpose code, and supported by invoices, packing list, aircraft part certificates, and delivery evidence. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000 for the Local File and KRW 10,000,000,000 for the Master File; documentation must generally be submitted with the corporate tax return or within a request period set by the National Tax Service

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a named Economic Substance Regulations regime identical to the UAE, but the National Tax Service and Korean courts apply the general anti-abuse substance-over-form doctrine. For an aircraft parts distribution company, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are procurement decisions, inventory risk, aircraft parts certification and airworthiness coordination, freight logistics, order processing, credit control, and contract execution. These functions must be performed in Korea by real employees, with actual board decisions and substantive contracts. A paper entity lacking personnel, premises, inventory, bank account, and risk exposure will be recharacterized, denied treaty benefits, and subjected to penalties and criminal tax investigation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, transactions may be recharacterized according to economic substance. Unsettled tax liabilities attract an additional tax of 20% or 40% under Articles 47-2 and 47-3. Criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act can result in imprisonment up to 2 years or fines equal to multiple amounts of evaded tax; aggravated penalties apply for evasion exceeding KRW 500 million.

Director personal liability: Korean courts pierce the corporate veil when a company is used as a fraudulent device. Directors can be personally liable under Article 401 of the Korean Commercial Act for damages caused to third parties and the tax authority. The representative director may be blacklisted as a delinquent taxpayer, restrained from overseas departure, face seizure of personal assets, and be sentenced to imprisonment for participating in tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Falsified Intercompany Documentation

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service will readjust taxable income to the arm's length price under the Corporate Income Tax Act transfer pricing provisions. The under-reported tax is increased by the unfaithful return penalty of 40% if egregious, plus daily late-payment interest. Failure to submit required transfer pricing documentation is separately penalized and may support criminal tax fraud charges.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly executes false invoices or dominates the Korean entity to channel profits abroad cannot hide behind limited liability. The commercial court will impose civil liability for lost tax revenue, the personal bank accounts of directors can be frozen, the director may be placed on the National Tax Service blacklist with travel restrictions, and the Tax Investigation Division may recommend imprisonment for aggravated tax evasion.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Non-Resident Dividend, Interest, and Royalty Taxes

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is liable for the uncollected tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Additional penalties of 20% or more, late-payment surcharges, and criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act may be imposed. The foreign recipient loses the ability to claim treaty relief because the withholding obligation was not fulfilled in a timely procedural manner.

Director personal liability: The representative director who ordered the unauthorized foreign payments is jointly and severally liable for the withheld taxes, penalties, and interest. Such conduct is treated as a breach of the director's fiduciary duty under the Korean Commercial Act, can lead to personal asset seizure, a departure ban, inclusion in the tax delinquent blacklist, and criminal imprisonment for intentional failure to comply with Korean withholding law.

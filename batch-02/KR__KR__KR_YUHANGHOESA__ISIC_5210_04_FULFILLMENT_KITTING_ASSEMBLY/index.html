Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under Korean corporate law; the paid-up capital declared in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully subscribed and deposited into a bank account in the name of the entity under formation before the registration application is filed. A blocked escrow account is not mandatorily required; however a Korean bank certificate of deposit or balance confirmation evidencing the contribution is the customary proof required by the registrar. The funds may not be withdrawn before completion of incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Fire Safety Certificate under the National Fire Agency and Building Act, including approved fire suppression systems, emergency exits, smoke ventilation, and resident fire safety inspections; structural load certification issued by a licensed structural engineer verifying racking weight capacity; and workplace safety clearance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act administered by the Ministry of Employment and Labor and KOSHA, covering ergonomic manual handling, hazardous materials labeling, electrical safety, warehouse stacking heights, and written safety procedures for the assembly and kitting lines.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity seeking to establish a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) in South Korea must first complete incorporation with the Commercial Registration at the local district court registry, obtain a corporate seal certificate, and then apply for a corporate Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) known as the Business Registration Number (Saupja Deungrokbeonho) through the National Tax Service's HomeTax portal. The application can be filed electronically or in-person at the competent district tax office. Required documents typically include the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, proof of registered address, identification/passport of the foreign representative, and, where applicable, a foreign entity registration certificate. The NTS assigns a 10-digit Business Registration Number and simultaneously registers the entity for Value-Added Tax (VAT) purposes. Foreign non-resident entities with a domestic place of business must appoint a domestic tax representative and may also need to register with the Korea Trade Statistics Promotion Institute (KTRP) or the Korea Customs Service for customs and trade statistics purposes. If the entity does not have a domestic place of business but is required to pay Korean VAT on certain supplies, it must register for VAT through a designated tax representative under the VAT Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The Korean tax system does not provide a general check-the-box partnership classification equivalent to the U.S. tax regime. A Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) is by default treated as a corporation for Korean corporate income tax purposes and is subject to entity-level taxation on its worldwide income if it is a domestic entity. There are no hybrid mismatch or check-the-box elections available under Korean domestic tax law; however, the entity should consider applying the OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules as implemented in Korean tax law, and may elect to be treated as a 'consolidated tax group' only for certain qualifying domestic group corporations under the Korean Corporate Tax Act. Under the Tax Treaty, a Korean entity may be subject to treaty tie-breaker rules if it is also considered resident elsewhere; state 'None' for statutory check-the-box elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No mandatory VAT registration threshold for entities with a domestic place of business; every business operator engaged in taxable supplies is required to register for VAT under Article 5 of the VAT Act. For non-resident or non-established foreign businesses supplying goods or services in Korea, registration is required when they have a domestic place of business or when their supplies are subject to Korean VAT; for newly incorporated entities, registration is required within 20 days after the business commencement date.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods is zero-rated under Article 19 of the Korean VAT Act when the goods are exported to a foreign buyer and leave Korea under an export customs declaration. Also zero-rated are international carriage services, certain ships and aircraft, and other goods/services earning foreign exchange with prescribed documentation. Businesses must file a zero-rated VAT return and evidence exports. For intra-company transfers of goods by a Korean entity to its foreign consolidated corporation, zero rating may apply if title passes and goods are physically exported. Qualified offshore digital services supplied to a foreign customer may be zero-rated when the place of supply is outside Korea or when the services are used outside Korea and paid in foreign currency.

South Korea mandates electronic tax invoices (e-invoices) for all business-to-business transactions under the electronic tax invoice system (Jeonja Sesayeongsu), transmitted in real time to the National Tax Service through the HomeTax system. Since 2022, the transmission window is mandatory within the day after the earlier of the invoice issuance date or the supply date, and penalties apply for late or omitted transmission. There is no government-operated real-time clearance or fiscal stamping model such as Italy's Sistema Tessera Sanitaria; instead, the NTS uses real-time electronic invoice data integration, VAT Declaration via HomeTax, and cash receipt machines for consumer transactions.

Under the Korean VAT Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes, any taxpayer who falsely claims zero-rated VAT treatment for exports that are not substantiated by the required customs declaration, foreign exchange receipt, and supporting trade documents is subject to a penalty of up to 40% of the fraudulent or erroneous zero-rated VAT amount, plus additional tax penalties of 20% for underreporting and 10% for non-payment. Retrospective clawbacks can be made for up to 5 years (10 years in cases of intentional fraud or false documentation) under the national tax statute of limitations. The NTS may also impose a penalty for lack of real-time electronic tax invoice transmission, and criminal liability may arise for tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act if the claim is fraudulent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including trade in goods and services, and official settlement of such obligations through authorised foreign-exchange banks. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment, portfolio investment and cross-border loans, are governed by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its enforcement decrees. Non-resident investors may freely bring capital into Korea after filing a foreign investment report; the initial funds can be converted into KRW at market rates without any surrender premium. Profit remittances – dividends, branch profits or loan interest – are permitted without prior approval, but must be supported by tax clearance certificates and proof of the underlying earnings. Banks apply a strict transaction-purpose review to forestall disguised capital flows. In practice, this regime is classified as non-restrictive but surveillance-heavy: there are no quantitative limits on capital inflows or outflows for bona fide corporate purposes, yet all transactions above threshold are reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Export receipts need not be converted into won; they can be held or used in foreign currencies. Overall, the Korean system balances market-convertibility with high regulatory visibility and occasional prudential measures during extreme volatility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic designated foreign-exchange account within 18 months after the export customs declaration date. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio is 0% – exporters are free to retain, deposit or sell foreign currency receipts, subject to threshold-based reporting through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Certain strategically sensitive exports may require accelerated settlement, but no general conversion quota is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) – acting in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for equity injection is routed through a designated foreign exchange bank. The company must file a Foreign Investment Report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) with the Ministry of Economy and Finance or the bank before remittance. Funds are remitted via SWIFT MT103 using settlement purpose code 'Investment' (e.g., code 70100 in the FEIS). The receiving bank credits the foreign currency escrow account; a certificate of foreign exchange is issued, followed by capital verification via articles of incorporation and auditor certification.Outbound payments for offshore suppliers and service providers are executed through the corporate bank’s trade finance desk using SWIFT MT103 (direct wire) for routine transfers or smaller settlements; Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) for bulk inventory purchases; and Documentary Collections (D/A, D/P) for lower-risk shipments. All outbound transfers require an underlying transaction document (commercial invoice, transport documents, customs receipts) and are referenced with the appropriate FEIS transaction classification code.Deliverable forward contracts on KRW/USD and KRW/EUR for current-account and trade obligations – available from licensed domestic banks.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) – permitted only for non-resident counterparties or offshore hedging structures; onshore corporates must use deliverable forwards because settlement in KRW is legally required for domestic transactions.Cross-currency swaps (KRW/USD, KRW/JPY) – permitted for hedging actual foreign-currency exposure; speculative use is prohibited. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top statutory Corporate Income Tax band under the Korean Corporate Tax Act (9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% on KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion), plus a 10% local income tax surcharge for an aggregate headline statutory rate of 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Korean domestic corporation to a non-resident shareholder, comprised of 20% dividend withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge; a Korean branch of a foreign corporation remitting profits is additionally subject to a branch profit tax of 10% on the after-corporate-tax retained earnings unless reduced by a bilateral treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A simplified Report of International Transactions must be filed with the annual corporate tax return when annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; full Local File and Master File transfer pricing documentation must be prepared and submitted where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion; Country-by-Country Reporting applies where the ultimate parent or the multinational group has consolidated revenue exceeding KRW 1 trillion; all documentation must be submitted within 45 days of the corporate tax return deadline, and failure to submit documentation shifts the burden of proving arm's length pricing to the taxpayer.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but the National Tax Service applies Economic Substance Doctrine and substance over form principles to deny tax treaty benefits and adjust taxable income where a domestic entity lacks real activity. For ISIC 5210 fulfillment kitting and assembly, Core Income-Generating Activities must physically occur in Korea: inventory purchasing and receiving, order pick and pack, kitting and assembly labour, quality control, packing equipment and warehouse premises, order dispatch, customer service and management decision-making. A contract-only LLC that acts as a no-risk agent or a conduit while the foreign parent retains inventory risk and management control will be disregarded for tax purposes or treated as a Korean permanent establishment of the foreign enterprise.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, Article 3(1): imprisonment up to 6 years or a fine up to two times the amount of evaded tax; additionally, under the Framework Act on National Taxes, a 40% negligence tax penalty on the fraudulent understatement, daily late payment surcharges at 0.022% per day, and disqualification from all tax settlement and payment extensions.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or caused the filing of a false corporate return are jointly and severally liable with the company for the evaded corporate tax, interest, and penalties; the National Tax Service may attach personal and family owned assets of the director, freeze bank accounts and securities, request an overseas departure restriction and blacklisting, and the director may be prosecuted individually for tax evasion, resulting in imprisonment up to 6 years and an order to pay restitution equal to the unpaid national taxes.
Shell Company and Discernible Lack of Economic Substance / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act, Article 9 (Limitation on Tax Treaty Benefits) and applicable anti-abuse rules: disregard of the shell entity, reallocation of income to a real Korean PE, denial of treaty-reduced withholding tax, recharacterization of the arrangement as a dividend or branch remittance, and re-computation of corporate tax with a 20%-40% understatement penalty, daily surcharges, and additional withholding assessments.

Director personal liability: Under the Korean corporate veil piercing doctrine recognized by the Supreme Court, where the LLC is a mere sham or the directors have used the corporate form to evade tax, directors and controlling non-resident shareholders are personally liable for the full amount of unpaid corporate tax, branch profit tax, dividend withholding tax and penalties; the directors may also face civil asset restitution, travel bans and blacklisting by the National Tax Service, and criminal conviction for fraudulent evasion if the sham documentation was knowingly used.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Cross-Border Invoice Fraud

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Article 8 and Article 9: administrative fines for failure to report international transactions, denial of the taxpayer's evidence position, discretionary estimation of the arm's length price by the National Tax Service, upward adjustment of Korean taxable income, and penalty surcharges of up to 40% of the additional tax assessment, plus daily late payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved false transfer pricing documentation are personally liable for the resulting corporate tax deficiency under the doctrine of joint tortfeasors and may be blacklisted and restrained from overseas travel; if the false documentation is used as a fraud device in the corporate tax return, the director faces criminal liability for tax evasion, civil damages to the shareholders and creditors, restitution of misstated intercompany balances, and potential imprisonment up to 6 years under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

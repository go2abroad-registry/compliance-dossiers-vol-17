None. The Korean Commercial Act sets no minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, using the HomeTax electronic tax portal (hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service is the counterpart authority for export declarations and customs evidence.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) must first incorporate under the Commercial Act and obtain a Korean registered address. For VAT and direct tax purposes, the representative or a retained resident tax administrator must file a business registration application with the District Tax Office having jurisdiction over the registered office, using the HomeTax portal or in-person. The application is accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, foreign registration certificate of the ultimate parent if any, passport or government-issued ID of the statutory representative, power of attorney, lease evidence of the virtual office, and proof of any required CMRA license or permit. Upon acceptance, National Tax Service issues the 10-digit Korean Business Registration Number, which serves as the TIN and VAT ID. The entity must then obtain a HomeTax electronic certificate (e-cert) for the signatory, register the address and bank accounts in HomeTax, and activate electronic e-tax invoice issuance. If no place of business is maintained in Korea and the non-resident entity is only indirectly present, a non-resident VAT registration can also be pursued through a Domestic Tax Representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes, but the operating Yuhan Hoesa is expected to register directly as a domestic corporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Yuhan Hoesa is a Korean domestic corporation and is automatically subject to Korean Corporation Tax on its worldwide income at the entity level. There is no pass-through or partnership tax classification for LLC-style limited liability companies in Korea.

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch classification election. The entity cannot elect to be disregarded or treated as a transparent partnership. Only separate elective provisions such as the domestic consolidated corporate tax regime for 100% owned subsidiaries exist, and they do not alter the entity's default entity-level classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold: a business must register from the first taxable supply of goods or services in Korea (KRW 0). A simplified VAT compliance regime applies to certain individual businesses with annual taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, but this is not a registration exemption and does not apply to a corporate Yuhan Hoesa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for goods physically exported from Korea and for services supplied in connection with export trade or supplied to a non-resident person where the place of supply is outside Korea. For virtual-office, mail-receiving, and digital scanning services, the business must demonstrate that the service recipient is a foreign business or individual located abroad, that the contract and performance evidence identify the service as consumed outside Korea, and that the consideration is paid from abroad. If the service is deemed used in Korea or is supplied to a Korean customer, it is taxable at 10%.

  • Korea Customs Service electronic export declaration with the Export Declaration Number and evidence of loading out of Korea
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, or another transport document evidencing international movement of goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list consistent with the export declaration
  • Bank-issued Foreign Exchange Acquisition Confirmation (often referred to as an Inward Remittance Certificate) proving receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency
  • HomeTax zero-rated electronic tax invoice carrying the export identifier or supporting service export details
  • For service exports: signed service contract, scope-of-work, output and service delivery logs such as digital mail-scanning records, and proof of the foreign customer's payment

All Korean registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax system for every B2B supply and zero-rated export supply. The e-tax invoice must be transmitted to HomeTax by no later than the day following the date of issuance; for monthly aggregated invoices, the same one-day post-issuance transmission rule applies. HomeTax validates the taxpayer identification number, invoice sequence, and issue date, and credit-card payments are matched through approved card acquirers. There is no separate physical fiscal stamp or real-time pre-clearance; the post-issuance HomeTax transmission is the anti-fraud control event. Third-party ERP and billing systems must submit via HomeTax EDI or use certified e-cert authentication and cannot alter transmitted invoice data.

The NTS automatically cross-checks Korea Customs Service declarations, HomeTax e-tax invoices, and bank foreign exchange remittance data. If the zero-rating substantiation bundle is incomplete, inconsistent, or fraudulent, the supply is reclassified as domestic taxable, VAT at 10% is assessed, and statutory interest runs from the original due date. The general assessment period is five years, extended to ten years for fraud or intentional concealment. Penalty additions under the Framework Act on National Taxes can range from 10% for a simple understatement to 40% for dishonest or unfair understatement, and failure to issue or late submission of an electronic tax invoice attracts additional tax equal to 1% to 2% of the relevant supply value. Intentional false export invoices or false documentation can result in criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment and fines, plus Customs Act penalties for false declarations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won (KRW) operates under a partially liberalized convertibility regime. Current account transactions are fully convertible and free of quantitative restrictions, while capital account transactions are liberalized but subject to declaration, reporting, or licensing through designated authorized foreign exchange banks. Foreign investors may inject capital into a Korean Yuhan Hoesa without prior approval except for sectors on the negative list, but each inward remittance must be reported under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations and logged in the Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts, but must be collected within the statutory deadline and reported upon settlement. Profit and dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted once the entity files corporate tax returns, proves settlement of taxes, and submits audited financial statements along with a Foreign Exchange Declaration at the remitting bank. There is no mandatory conversion of foreign currency export earnings into KRW, but all foreign exchange dealings must be routed through banks licensed by the MOEF, and any deferred collection or non-refund requires documentary justification. Transfer pricing and tax clearance certificates are required before foreign remittance of royalties or service fees. Overall, Korea is an open capital account economy, but the regulatory emphasis is on rigorous record-keeping, real transaction verification, and anti-money laundering vigilance rather than exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and relevant BOK/MOEF notifications, exporters must collect export proceeds within 15 months from the date of export declaration or the contractual payment due date, whichever is earlier. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters are free to retain proceeds in won or foreign currency. However, the exporter must report the receipt of export proceeds to the authorized foreign exchange bank through the settlement system, and any extension beyond the statutory timeline requires prior approval with valid justification. All collection must be evidenced with bank statements, invoices, packing lists, and customs declaration data.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed as authorized foreign exchange banks under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and they cannot provide KRW-denominated account numbers or IBANs for domestic corporate entities. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa must hold its operating bank account at a domestic tier-1 commercial bank. Fintech EMIs may only act as payment acceptance gateways, such as Stripe or PayPal, which route card and recurring subscription proceeds through domestic acquiring banks into a local Korean corporate account. Cross-border settlement with EMIs outside Korea is further constrained by Korean capital movement regulations requiring all foreign exchange transactions to be executed via authorized domestic banks. Therefore, the viable treasury architecture is Stripe for recurring card intake, with settlement falling into an onshore bank account at a tier-1 domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contribution for the KR YUHANGHOESA must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the bank's nostro account with a clear purpose code matching the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) declaration. For equity capital injection, use purpose code 400000 (capital acquisition by non-residents) and submit the Foreign Exchange Declaration Form to the branch before the funds are credited. The bank will issue a Capital Verification Certificate upon receipt, which must be reviewed by a licensed Korean accountant for entity capital verification. All shareholder remittances must be sourced to bank accounts under the shareholder's name, and rebates or letters of credit discharge must be avoided to prevent suspicion of circular flow. Funds should be held initially in a Capital Escrow Account and then transferred to the operating KRW account once the capital verification is completed and the corporate registry updates are filed.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed as authorized foreign exchange banks under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and they cannot provide KRW-denominated account numbers or IBANs for domestic corporate entities. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa must hold its operating bank account at a domestic tier-1 commercial bank. Fintech EMIs may only act as payment acceptance gateways, such as Stripe or PayPal, which route card and recurring subscription proceeds through domestic acquiring banks into a local Korean corporate account. Cross-border settlement with EMIs outside Korea is further constrained by Korean capital movement regulations requiring all foreign exchange transactions to be executed via authorized domestic banks. Therefore, the viable treasury architecture is Stripe for recurring card intake, with settlement falling into an onshore bank account at a tier-1 domestic bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal Korean Corporate Income Tax rate (progressive: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200M; 19% over KRW 200M to KRW 20B; 21% over KRW 20B to KRW 300B; 24% over KRW 300B). In addition, a 10% local income tax surcharge is levied on the national corporate income tax, producing a 26.4% combined top marginal burden.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate withholding (20% national corporate income tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge) on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders who do not have a Korean permanent establishment; the rate may be reduced under an applicable double tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10,000,000,000 (KRW 10 billion) in international related-party transactions generally triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation for the fiscal year; the annual International Business Transactions Report and attached financial schedules are required when cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5,000,000,000. The Master File also applies when the multinational group has consolidated global revenue of at least KRW 100 billion.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies an economic substance doctrine through the general anti-abuse rule (Framework Act on National Taxes Article 17), the International Tax Adjustment Act Article 3-2, the controlled foreign company regime, and substantive judicial substance-over-form precedent. A virtual-office and mail-scanning company must demonstrate that its core income-generating activities (CIGA) — client contracting, subscription billing, digital scanning, data storage, cybersecurity, and risk management — are actually performed in Korea using local employees, physical or cloud infrastructure, and Korean bank accounts. A pure mailbox or nominal office with no local activity, no decision-making, and no technical capacity can be disregarded or recharacterized as a conduit; treaty benefits and reduced withholding rates may be denied, and income may be reattributed to the foreign principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion and Transactional Shell Schemes

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act Article 3, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to 3 times the tax evaded. Under FANT Article 47-2, an unfaithful understatement penalty of 20% to 40% plus late-payment interest of 0.022% per day is assessed. Under the International Tax Adjustment Act, transfer-pricing adjustments may add 10% to 30% of the adjusted tax amount for failure to produce contemporaneous documentation.

Director personal liability: Korean Commercial Act Article 401 permits piercing the corporate veil where directors acted with intent or gross negligence. Directors are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax, penalties, and interest; personal bank accounts and assets may be seized; the National Tax Service may impose travel bans and immigration blacklisting; and a criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to 3 years and disqualification from corporate management.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service will apply FANT Article 17 and the International Tax Adjustment Act Article 3-2 to deny treaty benefits, recharacterize royalty payments as constructive dividends or Korean-source business income, impose 22% withholding plus local surcharge, and levy unfaithful-report penalties. Repeated use of a shell entity to evade Korean tax is subject to criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, and the Korean tax authority may cancel the entity's business registration and invalidate its postal/CMRA license.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly control the sham structure are personally liable for unreported withholding taxes and penalties under Commercial Act Article 401 and the National Tax Collection Act. Officials may be blacklisted from future government procurement, have Korean business licenses revoked, be subject to travel restrictions, and be criminally prosecuted with imprisonment up to 3 years. Civil restitution extends to the director's personal assets where the tax was collected from customers but intentionally not remitted.
AML/CMRA Regulatory Violation and Unlicensed Mail Forwarding

Statutory sanction: Under Korea's Act on Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Transaction Information, failing to file suspicious transaction reports can be punished by imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to KRW 100 million. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, unregistered or falsely reported cross-border remittances are subject to fines of up to 20% of the unreported amount and imprisonment up to 3 years. Under the Postal Logistics Act, operating an unlicensed mail-receiving agency triggers administrative shutdown orders, business registration cancellation, and criminal fines; the Financial Intelligence Unit may add the entity to a high-risk blacklist.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally control the unlicensed operation lose limited liability protection and may be held jointly and severally liable for fines, tax deficiencies, and restitution. Directors may face travel bans, asset freezes, criminal imprisonment up to 3 years for AML violations, and disqualification from serving as directors of any Korean corporation. Each director who knowingly enables the scheme can be charged as a co-principal under Korean criminal law.

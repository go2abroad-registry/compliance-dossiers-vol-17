Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, the entire subscribed capital must be fully paid before registration. The paid-in amount must be deposited into a temporary escrow bank account opened in the name of the company in formation and must remain blocked from all withdrawals until the Local District Court Registry Office issues the commercial registration certificate. After incorporation, the escrow bank releases the funds to the Company's operational account; there is no ongoing blocked-capital requirement, but the capital must be preserved under the Korean Commercial Act's capital maintenance rules.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must first obtain a Korean Tax Identification Number (TIN) by registering as a 'foreign corporation' with the competent district tax office. The procedure involves: (1) submitting an application for business registration (사업자등록신청서) using the prescribed form, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, and proof of foreign registration; (2) appointing a domestic tax representative (국세대리인) if the entity has no permanent establishment in Korea; (3) filing the application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the place of business or the location of the tax representative; (4) receiving the business registration certificate (사업자등록증) which contains the 10-digit TIN (also used as VAT registration number). For VAT purposes, the same TIN serves as the VAT registration number. The application must be filed within 20 days of the commencement of business activities in Korea, as defined under the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No threshold; any person or entity supplying goods or services in Korea, including foreign entities with a place of business or performing taxable supplies, must register for VAT. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under Article 21 of the VAT Act. Services supplied to non-residents or foreign entities are zero-rated if they are considered 'exported services' under the Enforcement Decree, including services provided to foreign clients that are used outside Korea. For digital services (e.g., video chat app), zero-rating applies if the service is provided to a non-resident who is not registered for VAT in Korea and the service is consumed outside Korea. The supplier must retain evidence of the foreign status of the recipient and the offshore consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (수출신고필증) or Electronic Export Declaration (전자수출신고서)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (수입대금지급확인서) or Foreign Exchange Bank Certificate (외국환은행 매매신고필증) proving receipt of foreign currency
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Contract or Purchase Order with the foreign buyer

Electronic tax invoices (e-세금계산서) are mandatory for all B2B transactions. The NTS operates a real-time electronic invoice clearance system (전자세금계산서 발급·전송 시스템) requiring submission of invoice data to the NTS within the day after issuance. For B2C transactions, cash receipts (현금영수증) must be issued when requested. There is no mandatory API-based fiscal clearance for all invoices, but the NTS requires electronic transmission via approved EDI or web services. Anti-fraud measures include digital stamping on electronic invoices and mandatory reporting of cash receipts.

The NTS may scrutinize export zero-rating claims through document reviews and on-site audits. If the required documentation is incomplete or the foreign exchange is not repatriated within the statutory deadline (generally 180 days from the export date, extendable under certain conditions), the zero-rated amount will be reversed and VAT will be assessed at 10% with additional penalties. Penalties include: (1) additional tax of 10% of the unpaid VAT for underreporting; (2) 0.75% per day of the unpaid amount for late payment; (3) criminal penalties for fraudulent claims, including fines up to 3 times the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won (KRW) is freely convertible on the current account, covering service royalties, user-generated content licensing, and profit remittances, but every cross-border payment must be supported by a valid transaction basis and reported via a licensed foreign exchange bank using the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Capital account transactions, including shareholder capital injections and vendor financing, are also liberalized but are subject to mandatory reporting, approval thresholds, and underlying economic substance verification. Korea does not impose a mandatory surrender of export proceeds; however, all foreign currency earnings derived from service exports, such as video match subscriptions and digital telephony credits, must be repatriated through local banks within twelve months and may be held in foreign currency accounts. Profit remittance is permitted provided the entity has fulfilled corporate income tax and withholding obligations; dividends are subject to 20% withholding tax (increased to 22% for amounts exceeding KRW 300 million, subject to treaty relief). The principal operational hurdle is not convertibility itself but the need to perfectly classify each transaction under the current account versus capital account matrix, and to ensure that the total gross settlement through payment aggregators such as Apple IAP and Paymentwall is represented in the FEIS filings with a single economically meaningful purpose code. For a social telephony app with consumer subscriptions, cross-border ATM withdrawals or virtual card purchases are prohibited without a dedicated reporting system, and any unmatched inflows from multiple foreign jurisdictions will automatically generate an exception report to the Korea Customs Service tax investigation unit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected from the overseas buyer and settled through a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank within 12 months from the date of export declaration. There is no fixed statutory conversion ratio for export receipts; resident entities may retain the foreign currency in a foreign currency account provided the inflow is reported to the bank and the source is verified. For digital service revenues derived from app stores and payment gateways, the same 12-month repatriation requirement applies, and the exporter must present settlement statements from the relevant payment processor (Apple, Paymentwall) to evidence that the funds originated from end users outside Korea. Mandatory conversion, if any, is limited to supervisory orders issued during balance-of-payments emergency, but in ordinary circumstances the company retains full discretion over the currency in which export receipts are held.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut, Wise Business, and Payoneer are not viable primary banking counterparts for a Korean Yuhan Hoesa. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only licensed financial companies or registered foreign exchange banks can legally open deposit accounts, supply FX inflow settlement, or issue won-denominated accounts for corporate entities resident in Korea. EMIs operating on a pass-through license from another jurisdiction are barred from accepting KRW deposits directly from domestic operations and from processing capital account wiring on behalf of Korean corporate entities. They may be used solely as an ancillary payment gateway for international customer refunds or for subsidiary treasury with a foreign entity, subject to bank approval and transaction-level reporting. The core compliance framework requires a local Korean bank to perform the initial capital verification, issue the foreign currency purchase confirmation, and file the foreign exchange reports; any attempt to route the initial share capital through a fintech EMI will trigger an automatic bank rejection and a potential Customs Service investigation due to the unlicensed handling of cross-border capital inflows.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut, Wise Business, and Payoneer are not viable primary banking counterparts for a Korean Yuhan Hoesa. 25% national corporate income tax on the top bracket, with progressive rates 10% (up to KRW 200 million), 20% (KRW 200 million-20 billion), 22% (KRW 20 billion-300 billion), and 25% (over KRW 300 billion); plus a 10% local corporate income tax surcharge on the national tax, resulting in a top combined effective rate of 27.5%.

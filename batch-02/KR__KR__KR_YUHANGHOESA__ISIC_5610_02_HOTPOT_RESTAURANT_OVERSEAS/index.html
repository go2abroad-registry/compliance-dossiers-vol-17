Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Korean Commercial Act; no statutory minimum paid-in capital applies to a Yuhan Hoesa. The amount stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully deposited into a blocked escrow account in a licensed Korean bank before registration, and a capital deposit certificate issued by that bank must be attached to the incorporation application.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Inspection and Facility Completion Certificate must be procured from the local fire authority, covering emergency lighting, exit signs, extinguishers, gas safety valve, automatic gas leak detection, and, if required, an automatic sprinkler system in the kitchen or dining area. Additionally, the municipal building authority must issue certification of general construction safety and electrical wiring approval. The employer must post occupational hygiene notices, conduct basic fire safety education, carry out workplace pollution health checks where relevant, and subscribe employees to industrial accident compensation insurance for the restaurant operation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax electronic tax filing system (https://www.hometax.go.kr/); customs administration: Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic customs clearance system (https://unipass.customs.go.kr/)

E-government filing portal

The operating entity is a Korean Yuhan Hoesa (domestic corporation) and must obtain a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) from the NTS, which serves as both the corporate TIN and VAT registration number. The entity must submit a business operator registration application with the head of the district tax office having jurisdiction over the actual domestic place of business within 20 days of business commencement, under Article 8 of the Value-Added Tax Act and Article 168 of the Corporate Tax Act. A foreign non-resident shareholder is not required to be physically present in Korea; the Yuhan Hoesa itself is the taxable person. If the foreign parent or a non-resident entity without a domestic place of business directly performs VAT-taxable supplies in Korea, it must appoint a domestic tax representative under Article 22 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and file the prescribed business registration application through the tax representative. Supporting documents for registration include: articles of incorporation and incorporation certificate (apostilled/consular-legalized as required), power of attorney for the tax representative or resident director, lease or ownership documents proving the Korean place of business, foreign investment report certificate under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act if a foreign investment report has been made, and identification/tax residence evidence for the representative. The NTS issues a Business Registration Certificate and assigns the BRN; registration is effective from the date of business commencement. The Korean Yuhan Hoesa is classified as a domestic corporation, so it is not eligible for individual simple-taxpayer registration and is mandatorily a general VAT taxable person.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 for an incorporated Yuhan Hoesa. There is no turnover exemption for a corporation; registration as a general taxable person is mandatory from day one of business commencement. The simplified taxable person threshold of KRW 80,000,000 for individual businesses is not available to this entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act where goods are exported from Korea and the export declaration is accepted by Korea Customs Service; title, risk, and physical possession must pass to the foreign buyer and the goods must leave the Korean customs territory. For qualifying exported services, zero-rating applies when the service is supplied to a non-resident, the recipient of the service is located outside Korea, the effective use and enjoyment of the service occurs outside Korea, and the service is classified as a service export eligible for zero-rating under the Value-Added Tax Act and its Enforcement Decree. Dining services rendered by a Korean restaurant to customers inside Korea are standard-rated at 10% and cannot be zero-rated merely because the customer is a foreign tourist. Condiment products delivered to overseas related restaurants or distributors can be zero-rated as exported goods only if accompanied by a valid customs export declaration and related evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Acceptance Certificate issued by Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS system)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Purchase Confirmation issued by a licensed foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Sales contract or purchase order with the foreign buyer
  • Certificate of Origin if required for customs clearance or FTA preference
  • Performance certificate or signed delivery receipt evidencing overseas receipt of the goods or services

A general taxable person must issue a VAT invoice for taxable supplies of goods and services. Electronic tax invoicing (e-tax invoice) is mandatory for all general taxable persons. The e-tax invoice must be issued through the NTS HomeTax electronic tax invoice system or through an integrated ERP/API connection that transmits the invoice data to HomeTax. The NTS validates the electronic serial number and uses real-time data matching across the issuer, recipient, customs, and payment systems as an anti-fraud control; there is no physical tax stamp or prepayment fiscal clearance device. For B2C restaurant and retail condiment sales, a VAT invoice must be issued when requested by the customer, and cash receipts must be issued through the approved cash-receipt system for cash transactions; credit card transaction data is automatically transmitted to the NTS by the payment acquirer. E-tax invoice data for supplies between businesses must be transmitted to the NTS by the statutory deadline prescribed in Article 32 of the Value-Added Tax Act, and inaccurate or late transmission exposes the entity to additional tax penalties.

Export zero-rating is scrutinized by matching the NTS VAT return, Korea Customs Service export declaration data, bank foreign exchange receipts, and the e-tax invoices issued. If the export declaration is absent, void, or corrected to domestic supply, if the Inward Remittance Certificate is not in the name of the Korean exporter, if export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days without a valid extension granted by the foreign exchange bank, or if the service export cannot reasonably prove that the recipient is non-resident and the place of use is outside Korea, the NTS will reverse the zero-rating and assess VAT at the standard 10% rate on the supply value. In addition, statutory additional taxes under the Value-Added Tax Act and Framework Act on National Taxes are imposed, including penalties for false or unfaithful e-tax invoices and underdeclared tax. Fraudulent or intentionally false export claims exposes the entity and its responsible officers to aggravated tax penalties and criminal prosecution under the Punishment for Tax Offenses Act. The ordinary statute of limitations for VAT assessment is five years, extended to ten years where the underreporting is deliberate and concealed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is deemed convertible for current-account items such as trade settlements, royalties, dividends, service payments, and operational branch transfers, provided the submitting entity can evidence the underlying transaction with commercial documents and the designated foreign exchange bank validates the filing. Capital-account items, including equity injections, cross-border loans, securities investments, guarantees, and related financial transfers, are conditionally convertible: they are permitted only with prior or simultaneous notification to the Bank of Korea or the delegated foreign exchange bank and are subject to post-transaction documentary audit. There is no mandatory ratio for forcibly converting export proceeds into KRW, but export earnings must be received through a licensed bank and reported with a valid export declaration and a matching collection settlement. Profit remittance from a Korean Yuhan Hoesa is lawful only after corporate income taxes and dividend withholding taxes are settled, the annual external audit is available, and the remittance classification is recorded in the Foreign Exchange Information System. Banks will require the entity's business registration certificate, corporate seal certificate, board or shareholder resolutions, audited financial statements, and the foreign exchange bank agreement before executing any capital or dividend outflow. In practice, the system is a highly documented, proportionate control regime: it neither prohibits convertibility nor permits unrestricted free-floating access; it instead disciplines all cross-border flows through banking intermediation, transaction coding, and BOK oversight.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Korean foreign exchange regulations, exporters must repatriate and settle export proceeds within six (6) months from the date of export declaration, unless an extension is approved by the designated foreign exchange bank for valid commercial reasons such as deferred payment terms. There is no statutory percentage requiring conversion of export earnings into KRW; the proportion held in foreign currency may remain in a foreign currency account, but the full amount must be reported under the Foreign Exchange Information System and supported by the customs export declaration, commercial invoice, and bank collection confirmation. For a hotpot chain also supplying condiments overseas, every shipment requires a matching customs export declaration and bank receipt to evidence bona fide trade, and any excess delay beyond the six-month window must be justified to the foreign exchange bank to avoid administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting as the central monetary and foreign exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with policy coordination by the Ministry of Economy and Finance and operational reporting through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) administered by the Bank of Korea and delegated foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Global fintech EMIs, digital wallets, and non-bank payment institutions are generally not viable as the primary treasury or clearing rail for a Korean-resident Yuhan Hoesa. Korea's Electronic Financial Transactions Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act require licensed domestic foreign exchange banks to execute cross-border remittances, maintain KRW clearing accounts, and file Foreign Exchange Information System reports. Non-bank EMIs cannot lawfully maintain a full corporate multi-currency treasury account for a Korean entity, cannot perform capital verification for foreign direct investment, and cannot issue a credible bank confirmation for dividend remittance or export proceeds collection. Such EMIs may only be employed as offshore collection points in the overseas restaurant markets, with automatic settlement into the Korean operating bank, and only after the Korean bank approves the arrangement as part of ancillary payment acceptance. For the core treasury mandate, a licensed domestic Tier-1 bank is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the Korean Yuhan Hoesa must be routed through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank via SWIFT wire transfer, with the remittance account name matching the investor's name and the payment narrative clearly identifying the transaction as a capital contribution to a Korean entity. The bank will require the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act purpose code for a foreign direct investment capital receipt, the underlying board resolution, the corporate registration documents, and the subscription contract before crediting the funds to the Capital Escrow Account or a foreign currency settlement account. Once the Bank of Korea notification or foreign investment acknowledgement is completed, the funds may be converted to KRW at the onshore market rate and transferred to the operating current account. The company's external auditor must subsequently verify the paid-in capital amount through a capital verification procedure, matching the bank confirmation, the general ledger, the shareholders register, and the BOK notification.

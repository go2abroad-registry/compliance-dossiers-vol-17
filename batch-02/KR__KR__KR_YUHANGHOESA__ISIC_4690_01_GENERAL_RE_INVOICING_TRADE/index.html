Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. The subscribed capital stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid or contributed before or simultaneously with the incorporation filing. The bank issuing the capital contribution certificate acts as the record keeper, but no separate blocked escrow account is mandated by corporate law. Where a foreign investor subscribes capital of KRW 100,000,000 or more, the Foreign Investment Promotion Act requires reporting through KOTRA and use of a foreign-investment account; foreign exchange transaction reporting applies to cross-border payments above applicable thresholds.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable as a standalone industrial clearance because the company operates no physical plant. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act for (i) export of goods definitively removed from Korea and cleared by KCS; (ii) international transport of goods/passengers; and (iii) qualifying services supplied to non-residents or used/consumed outside Korea, including certain foreign-currency-earning services. For the commercial re-invoicing trade model, goods transiting between foreign countries without entering Korea are not a Korean supply of goods; Yuhan Hoesa's commission, spread or intermediation fee can be zero-rated if the service is supplied to a non-resident customer and is used or performed outside Korea. Substantiation requires KCS export clearance evidence where goods are exported from Korea, transport documents, contracts, e-Tax Invoice showing 0%, and bank foreign-exchange receipt evidence.

  • KCS UNI-PASS Export Declaration and Export Clearance Certificate (customs export bill-of-entry equivalent) evidencing removal of goods from Korea
  • Bill of Lading, multimodal transport document or airway bill evidencing delivery to the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and VAT e-Tax Invoice showing the zero rate
  • Foreign currency acquisition certificate / foreign exchange receipt certificate / inward remittance certificate issued by an authorized foreign exchange bank evidencing collection of export proceeds
  • Underlying sales and purchase contracts, purchase orders and re-invoicing flow documents identifying Yuhan Hoesa as principal or intermediary

Korea operates the national e-Tax Invoice system through HomeTax. All VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices and transmit them to NTS by the day after issuance; there is no third-party pre-clearance fiscal stamping. Transmission is made through NTS HomeTax APIs or accredited ERP-to-e-invoice integrations using an NTS-approved digital certificate. B2C cash transactions require issuance of approved cash receipts, which are transmitted near real time. NTS cross-matches e-Tax Invoice data, cash receipt data, VAT returns and KCS customs declarations for anti-fraud review; sanctions apply for non-transmission or false issuance.

NTS audits zero-rating claims by matching e-Tax Invoice data, KCS export declarations and bank foreign-exchange receipts. If the export or foreign-service zero-rating is not substantiated, or export proceeds are not collected or evidenced within the applicable 180-day repatriation deadline unless an approved extension exists, NTS may retrospectively assess 10% output VAT, disallow input VAT credits, add statutory additional tax and interest under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes, and extend the assessment period to up to 10 years in cases of intentional fraud. Knowingly issuing false zero-rated invoices or false export declarations may also give rise to criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act and the Korea Customs Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is classified as a generally convertible currency for trade and current-account transactions; foreign exchange controls derive from the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree. The Bank of Korea maintains supervisory oversight, while the Ministry of Economy and Finance issues administrative guidance and the designated foreign exchange banks carry out documentary checks. Trade-related settlements, including export proceeds, advance payments, and re-invoicing payments, can flow through designated foreign exchange banks without prior central bank approval; however, every receipt and payment must be reported within the statutory filing window. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment into the Korean LLC, intercompany shareholder loans, and dividend repatriation, are subject to prior declaration or post-event notification. Equity injection funds must be brought into the Korea Foreign Exchange Information System before or at the time of remittance, and the entity must complete tax clearance and capital verification before remitting profits. Dividend repatriation is lawful after the payment of Korean corporate income tax and withholding tax, with treaty benefits available if documentation such as a tax residence certificate and certificate of withholding is filed. In practice, re-invoicing hubs face enhanced documentary scrutiny because physical goods do not cross South Korean borders; banks require complete trade flow maps, pricing rationalization, counterparty KYC, and customs declarations. The Korean won is not freely deliverable offshore at Seoul market rates, so hedging instruments must be subject to the real-demand trade principle and should be booked only against verified underlying trade exposure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory export proceeds must be received within 15 months from the invoice or export declaration date unless an extension is approved by the Bank of Korea or a designated foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory surrender requirement and no compulsory conversion ratio; exporters may retain 100 percent of foreign currency receipts in a foreign-currency account or convert into Korean won at the onshore market rate. The beneficiary must report each receipt to the designated foreign exchange bank and retain supporting trade documents, including invoices, bills of lading, and customs declarations, for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea; the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Korea Customs Service jointly administer the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the Foreign Exchange Information System, and cross-border payment monitoring.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. South Korea does not grant a standalone electronic money institution licence to unregulated fintech firms. A foreign digital EMI cannot act as the primary treasury account of a Korean LLC because it does not satisfy the Bank of Korea and Financial Supervisory Service requirements for a designated foreign exchange bank. Offshore EMIs, such as global multi-currency business wallets, are legally constrained from completing Foreign Exchange Information System declarations, customs trade evidence, or Korean withholding-tax attestations. The re-invoicing entity may use an EMI only as a non-primary sub-ledger for convenience payments to foreign merchants when the ultimate settlement remains with a tier-1 Korean bank; even then, the EMI must be regulated in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction and the Korean bank must be informed of all downstream payment activities. For corporate formation and FX compliance, onshore Korean banks are mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for equity or shareholder loans must be routed through a designated foreign exchange bank using SWIFT MT103 with a clear foreign exchange purpose code under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Equity injections require a prior direct-investment declaration through the Foreign Exchange Information System before the remittance date; shareholder loans require a signed intercompany loan agreement and Bank of Korea reporting when the loan exceeds regulatory notification thresholds. The receiving bank must issue a foreign exchange receipt confirmation, and the company must obtain a certified public accountant's capital verification report. No cash, cryptocurrency, or unbanked value transfer may be used; the remittance field must identify the Korean entity, the payer, the underlying investment title, and the purpose code.Outbound trade disbursements must be executed from the Korean LLC's designated foreign exchange bank against verified underlying invoices, purchase orders, and settlement instructions. For standard open-account payments, use SWIFT MT103 with the invoice number, contract reference, and destination jurisdiction in field 70. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% to 24% progressive corporate income tax plus 10% local income tax surcharge; effective headline rate for the highest bracket is 26.4% and for the lowest bracket 9.9%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000: a Local File and Master File must be prepared and submitted when the total annual intercompany transactions with foreign related parties exceed KRW 5 billion. A Country-by-Country Report is additionally required where the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue is at least KRW 100 billion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulation regime similar to the UAE, but the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 5 and the Corporate Tax Act Article 17 permit the NTS to disregard low-substance arrangements and recharacterize income. A re-invoicing entity must demonstrate that core income-generating activities such as contract negotiation, invoicing, inventory risk, foreign exchange risk, credit risk, and management control are actually performed in Korea. A letterbox company with a registered office, nominee director, and no employees is treated as a conduit; the NTS may disallow deductions, recharacterize sales as commission, and apply Controlled Foreign Corporation rules to include retained profit in the Korean shareholder's tax base.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate concealment of offshore re-invoicing income - tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Unpaid corporate tax is assessed together with a 10% understatement penalty, or a 20% penalty for intentional concealment under Article 76 of the Corporate Income Tax Act. Daily overdue interest of 0.025% accrues, and evaded tax exceeding KRW 500 million triggers aggravated criminal punishment.

Director personal liability: The representative director is jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act when the non-payment is caused by willful default or gross negligence. Criminal liability includes imprisonment of up to three years, or up to five years where the evaded amount exceeds KRW 500 million, and a fine of up to twice the tax evaded. The NTS may also issue a travel ban and asset freeze order against the director.
Transfer pricing documentation and arm's-length misstatement - international profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, a penalty of 20% of the tax deficiency may be imposed; nonsubmission of required documentation attracts an additional penalty of up to KRW 10 million per document.

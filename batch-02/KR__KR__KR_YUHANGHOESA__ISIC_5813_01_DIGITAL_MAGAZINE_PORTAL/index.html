For a low-risk digital magazine publishing LLC, Korean tax authorities and banks generally accept a registered agent or licensed serviced office as the statutory registered office, but the National Tax Service may challenge a purely virtual commercial mailbox if no supporting administrative substance exists. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Korean Commercial Act for a Yuhan Hoesa. The articles of incorporation must state a fixed capital amount and the class and value of each member's contribution. Cash consideration must be fully deposited into a provisional corporate bank account in the company's name before the court filing, and contribution-in-kind must be reported and examined by a court-appointed inspector where required. The deposited funds are not state escrow but are blocked for the company's exclusive use until incorporation is registered; withdrawal before registration is a disqualifying defect. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS) - HomeTax System (hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service (UNI-PASS) for export declaration processing.

E-government filing portal

A Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company) is a domestic corporation for tax purposes. Within 20 days of commencing the digital publishing business, the legal representative must apply for business registration through the HomeTax system at the competent district tax office; required documents include the certificate of corporate registration, the company's articles of incorporation, foreign shareholder identity and foreign capital reporting documents (if any), and the executed business premises lease or ownership proof. Upon approval, the NTS issues a Business Registration Certificate (business registration number) that serves as the unified TIN for corporate income tax and VAT. For a foreign non-resident supplier without a Korean place of business that supplies electronic/digital services to Korean consumers, a separate simplified foreign business VAT registration must be made via HomeTax, and the foreign supplier is required to appoint a Korean tax representative under the Framework Act for National Taxes. Procurement of the TIN is not subject to minimum capital; a physical address for the deemed Korean place of business must be established.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold for a Yuhan Hoesa corporation; every corporate business must register for VAT. A KRW 80,000,000 annual turnover threshold applies only to simplified-taxable individual entrepreneurs, not to a Yuhan Hoesa. Foreign non-resident suppliers of electronic services to Korean consumers are required to register for VAT if their annual Korean electronic-service supplies exceed KRW 10,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, export of goods and services supplied to foreign countries is zero-rated. For the digital magazine portal, digital subscription and advertising receipts qualify for 0% VAT only where the recipient is a non-resident or foreign entity, the place of supply is outside Korea, the service is effectively consumed abroad, and the export proceeds are received in foreign currency from abroad. If the digital advertising or sponsorship service is deemed to be consumed in Korea or to serve a domestic advertiser, the general 10% VAT applies.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration (Export Declaration) via UNI-PASS and the customs export clearance certificate for any physical promotional or fulfilment goods accompanying the digital publishing service
  • Signed digital service export contract, purchase order, or written agreement with the foreign counterparty, together with itemised commercial invoices identifying the foreign end-user and service period
  • Foreign-exchange receipt confirmation issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank, evidencing that the export or supply payments were received from abroad within the statutory settlement period
  • Bill of lading or air waybill when physical goods are exported; for pure digital services, a digital delivery report or metrics report showing the foreign IP or customer location may be substituted for transport documentation

All corporate VAT taxpayers must issue and submit electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-Tax Invoice system for B2B supplies. Submission is not a real-time fiscal clearance; the NTS processes and reconciles invoice data using its integrated API/EDI interface, and no anti-fraud stamping or pre-approval is required. For B2C digital magazine subscriptions and AdSense payouts, a cash receipt may be requested by the consumer; electronic tax invoices are mandatory for B2B sales and must be reported in the VAT return. Failure to transmit through HomeTax exposes the taxpayer to statutory penalty tax and interface rejection penalties.

On audit, the NTS and Korea Customs Service will verify the export declaration, place-of-supply, customer tax and foreign-residence status, payment flow, and repatriation within 180 days. Missing, inconsistent, or fraudulent export evidence leads to reversal of the zero-rating, reassessment of 10% VAT, statutory additional taxes for underreporting/unpaid tax, and daily delinquency charges; deliberate false documentation may additionally be prosecuted under the Act on Punishment of Tax Evaders, with criminal fines/imprisonment and retrospective clawback of VAT credits and refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberal current account convertibility framework but reserves prior-approval and reporting obligations that companies must satisfy. Capital account transactions are subject to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, which requires foreign direct investment, inbound equity capital contributions, non-resident subscriptions, and repatriation of proceeds to be reported through designated foreign exchange banks or, in certain circumstances, to the Ministry of Economy and Finance. The KRW is convertible for ordinary business transactions; dividends, service fees, and trade settlements can be remitted provided the underlying transaction is evidenced and the borrowing or investment was reported. Nevertheless, the Financial Transaction Reports Act, the BOK's Foreign Exchange Information System, and the Know Your Customer obligations of domestic banks create practical friction for rapid cross-border movements. Profit remittance hurdles are manageable but require an audited financial statement, tax clearance, and a certified foreign exchange transaction report. Surrender requirements are not applied as a blanket 100% conversion; however, exporters must bring proceeds into Korea within statutory deadlines and may retain balances in authorized foreign currency accounts. The system is therefore liberal with supervision, not a strict control regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and BOK regulations, export receipts must be collected through a domestic foreign exchange bank and settled within 90 days of the export declaration date. The exporter may hold foreign currency in an authorized foreign currency account without mandatory immediate conversion into KRW, but upon settlement into Korean won, the exchange bank must report the transaction electronically through the Foreign Exchange Information System. Extensions are possible for projects with long payment cycles or construction/installment contracts, subject to prior bank or BOK approval depending on the amount.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank and operator of the Foreign Exchange Information System, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance serving as the principal foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut Business are not authorized by the Financial Services Commission or the Bank of Korea as domestic foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. They can neither issue Korean IBANs nor act as settlement banks for KRW or foreign currency capital accounts. For a Yuhan Hoesa, using a fintech EMI would fail regulatory KYC and undermine the ability to receive foreign investment registration and capital verification certificates. Local tier-1 commercial banks are mandatory at account opening; EMIs can only be auxiliary for low-value expense cards if accepted by local partners, but never for treasury flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions exceeding KRW 10 billion trigger the Korean Local File requirement; related-party transactions exceeding KRW 50 billion combined with group revenue above KRW 100 billion trigger the Master File requirement; a Country-by-Country report is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above KRW 100 billion

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations directly, but the doctrine of substance over form is embedded in the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs and the National Tax Basic Act; the National Tax Service will scrutinize Core Income-Generating Activities and will disregard an entity lacking real personnel, premises, decision-making, IP ownership, and risk-taking when deciding DTA beneficial ownership and Korean permanent establishment exposure; a pure shell intermediary is treated as fiscally transparent and all income is reattributed to the Korean LLC or the ultimate non-resident principal

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Direct Tax Evasion and Cyber Revenue Concealment

Statutory sanction: Article 3 of the Korean Punishment of Tax Evaders Act imposes imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to 5 times the tax evaded; the Framework Act for National Taxes also imposes a 20% understatement penalty, increased to 30% for concealment, plus 0.022% daily late-payment surcharges and full reassessment

Director personal liability: A director or beneficial owner who controls the diversion is personally liable under the secondary tax obligation doctrine of the National Tax Collection Act, allowing the NTS to seize personal assets, attach Korean bank accounts, and impose an overseas departure restriction or travel ban where delinquent tax exceeds KRW 50 million; criminal imprisonment is personal and cannot be discharged by corporate liquidation
Transfer Pricing Fraud, Shell Company Abuse, and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 4 and 21 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the NTS may disregard the shell parent, recharacterize excessive royalty payments as constructive dividends, adjust the taxable income on an arm's-length range, and impose penalty tax on the corrected income; failure to submit transfer-pricing documentation further permits evidentiary presumption and denial of internal comparables

Director personal liability: Directors who sign sham agreements and knowingly allow profit stripping can be jointly and severally liable for the assessed corporate tax, interest, and penalties as officers actually managing the business; chronic delinquent status leads to blacklisting, travel bans, and seizure of personal property; fraudulent transfer or false documentation may trigger imprisonment under the Special Economic Crimes Act, with terms up to 5 years
AML, Foreign Exchange Violation, and Payment-Rail Misuse

Statutory sanction: The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Act on Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information impose criminal penalties of imprisonment up to 3 to 5 years and fines of up to KRW 500 million; the NTS and Financial Services Commission may also impose administrative fines, freeze accounts, and disallow all associated expenses for Korean corporate tax purposes

Director personal liability: A director who directs unregistered outward remittances faces personal monetary fines, freezing of personal assets, passport revocation, and travel restrictions; civil restitution of the unlawfully transferred foreign exchange may be ordered; if the corporate form is used only as a facade for personal payment-rail laundering, the court will pierce the corporate veil and impose imprisonment directly on the director

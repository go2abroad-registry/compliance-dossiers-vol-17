Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. South Korea imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, the total contribution stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid or performed by the incorporation date. Monetary contributions must be deposited in full into a designated bank account and evidenced by a bank certificate; contributions in kind must be fully delivered or transferred and, in certain cases, appraised by a court-authorized expert. There is no general blocked escrow account requirement under the Commercial Act, but the members may voluntarily agree to an escrow arrangement in the Articles of Incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a Fire System Completion Inspection Certificate from the local Fire Station under the Fire Services Act, a Civil Defense facility registration for the dispatch and parking site, and a Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency workplace safety inspection approval for the dispatch center, vehicle maintenance bay, GPS sealing station, and fuel storage area.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification. Korea has no check-the-box election. A qualifying wholly-owned Korean subsidiary may elect to file a consolidated corporate tax return with its parent if 100% ownership is maintained for the whole taxable year, but the election does not alter the entity-level taxation of the LLC. Hybrid mismatch regulations under the OECD BEPS Action 2 are incorporated in the Korean International Tax Coordination Act and impose automatic adjustments, not elective treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Corporations: mandatory VAT registration from the first day of business with no turnover threshold. Simplified taxpayer threshold of KRW 30,000,000 annual taxable supplies is available only to natural persons, and a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) is ineligible; accordingly, the effective registration threshold for this corporate entity is nil.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% rate may be applied by the Yuhan Hoesa to its GPS-sealed bonded road feeder service when the service qualifies as a supply of services for export under Article 21 of the VAT Enforcement Decree. The criteria are: (a) the entity holds a valid bonded transporter licence from KCS; (b) the service physically moves bonded cargo into or out of an international airport customs-bonded area or between bonded facilities as part of an international import/export movement; (c) the cargo is accompanied by a customs export declaration or is otherwise under customs-bonded transit status; (d) the service is rendered to a foreign-domiciled counterparty or to a Korean exporter/importer incurring a deductible export cost; (e) the consideration is received in foreign currency (or in KRW via a legitimate foreign exchange settlement); and (f) full documentary evidence as listed below is maintained. Pure goods export trading would need the exporter, not the transporter, to file the customs export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry) issued by the Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Master Air Waybill (MAWB) for the international leg of the freight movement
  • Commercial invoice for the bonded transport service, cross-referencing the customs export declaration and the AWB/B/L number
  • GPS movement and seal report generated by the bonded transport tracking system and certified by KCS
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate (equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate) issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Bank wire or ACH credit advice showing receipt of the foreign currency export proceeds within 180 days

All VAT-registered businesses must issue an electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) for each taxable supply, transmit it to NTS via HomeTax (E-Scro service) no later than the following business day, and retain the digitally signed transmission record. There is no physical clearance stamp; the successful transmission and NTS receipt number constitute fiscal clearance. Additional anti-fraud control for bonded trucking: the GPS seal event data and the cargo transfer record must be logged in UNI-PASS/KCS and reconciled with the e-Tax Invoice before a zero-rated supply is declared.

Zero-rating claims are subject to joint NTS/KCS audit by matching e-tax invoices with UNI-PASS export declarations, bonded GPS seal activity, Bill of Lading/Air Waybill data, and bank foreign exchange settlement records. If any required document is absent or the transaction is found to be fictitious, NTS will disallow the zero-rating and assess VAT at 10% on the supply, plus additional tax penalties (ranging from 10% for unintentional errors up to 40% for fraudulent evasion), monthly overdue interest (0.66% per month), and potentially a civil penalty of up to the VAT amount. Criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act can result in imprisonment for up to five years and fines of up to five times the evaded tax; any VAT refund already disbursed is immediately clawed back with statutory interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is generally convertible for current account transactions, including trade settlements, service fees, and routine repatriation of dividends, subject to filing obligations with an authorized foreign exchange bank and documentary evidence confirming the underlying transaction. Capital account transactions (e.g., foreign direct investment in equity, loans, and securities) are liberally regulated under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and require notification or prior approval for certain sensitive flows. Export proceeds are not subject to a mandatory statutory conversion ratio; however, all export payments must be received through an authorized foreign exchange bank and settled within the prescribed export due date, typically one year from the transport date, unless extended by the bank. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders require proof of the distributable reserve, audited financial statements, and tax clearance certificates, but there are no surrender requirements. The practical hurdle is not convertibility itself but the need to demonstrate the bona fide nature of the cross-border flow through purpose codes and supporting documentation. Repatriation restrictions may be imposed only under exceptional national security or balance-of-payments crises, and current account payments are highly liberalized. For a customs bonded trucking entity earning KRW from domestic logistics services, the foreign exchange exposure is minimal; capital injection from non-resident shareholders is treated as foreign direct investment and must be notified via a bank or KOTRA before or within 30 days of the transaction, and a certificate of foreign exchange receipt is issued after the funds are banked and verified. Therefore, the regime is best characterized as a free-flow system with stringent recordkeeping and real-time compliance verification rather than a surrender-driven system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds must be collected within the export payment due date (usually 1 year from the goods' departure date) through an authorized foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory percentage surrender to the Bank of Korea; however, the exporter must deposit the foreign currency proceeds into a foreign currency account or convert them to KRW within 1 business day of receipt, and the bank must report the receipt under the Foreign Exchange Information System. In practice, for customs bonded trucking operations that are primarily domestic, export-related foreign currency receipts might arise from cross-border freight fees; these must be settled within the statutory period and documented against the transportation invoice and customs manifest.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) pursuant to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act of 1961 (amended), administered jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Korea Customs Service for trade-related foreign exchange controls

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Progressive corporate income tax: 9% for taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% between KRW 200 million and KRW 20 billion; 21% between KRW 20 billion and KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% gross dividend withholding on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders: 20% national dividend withholding tax plus a 10% local surtax on the national withholding tax, resulting in 2% local surtax and a combined 22% rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return must be filed by the last day of the third month following the fiscal year-end. For a December 31 fiscal year-end, the filing deadline is March 31 of the following year. If the financial statements are reviewed or audited by a qualified Korean tax accountant or auditor, the deadline is extended to the last day of the fourth month following fiscal year-end. Foreign corporations with a Korean permanent establishment must file on the same statutory deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion in annual cross-border intercompany transactions with any foreign related party triggers the mandatory Local File; KRW 50 billion in group-wide cross-border related-party transactions triggers the Master File; KRW 100 billion in consolidated group revenue triggers the Country-by-Country Report. Established Korean-source domestic related-party transactions also require contemporaneous documentation if they exceed KRW 10 billion. Bonded trucking intercompany fees must be benchmarked to independent international road feeder services performing comparable airport-to-customs-warehouse hauls.

Economic substance scope: Although South Korea does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, the Korean tax authorities apply substance-over-form, the Framework Act on National Taxes general anti-abuse rule, treaty limitation-on-benefits and principal-purpose tests, and judicial doctrines that disregard artificial entities. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for the Yuhan Hoesa are physically conducted where dispatch decisions, driver supervision, customs-bond inspection, maintenance scheduling, fuel procurement, billing, and risk management occur. If CIGA are performed in Korea, the Korean LLC cannot claim that no Korean PE exists. If CIGA are deliberately located in a low-tax offshore office while trucks and drivers remain functionally controlled in Korea, the Korean tax authority can recharacterize the structure, deny treaty benefits, and assess Korean corporate income tax plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Act on the Punishment of Tax Crimes Article 3(1): a person who evades corporate tax by fraudulent or unlawful acts is subject to imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding five times the amount of tax evaded. The National Tax Service will additionally impose the 40% unfair underreporting penalty under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-3 and delinquency surcharge on unpaid tax. The Korea Customs Service may revoke the customs bonded transport operator license under the Customs Act and impose customs penalties for false or incomplete transport records.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil if the company is found to be an alter ego of the directors. Directors who signed false returns or directed the concealment of revenue are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax, additional penalties, and delinquency charges to the extent of the diverted corporate assets. The National Tax Service blacklists the directors as serious delinquent taxpayers, which triggers travel bans, passport restrictions, asset seizure, and bank account freezing. Criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to 5 years, and the director is disqualified from serving as a registered officer of any Korean corporation.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping / Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: DTA relief is denied for lack of beneficial ownership and absence of economic substance; the full 22% dividend withholding tax is re-collected from the Korean payer or shareholder. The Framework Act on National Taxes general anti-abuse rule and substance-over-form doctrine recharacterize the offshore entity as a sham; management fee deductions are disallowed and reclassified as constructive dividends, resulting in a 40% improper-report penalty plus late payment interest. The shell's bank accounts may be blocked under the Financial Information Act and the tax treaty's principal-purpose test may also operate to deny all treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: When the corporate form is used as a fraud on creditors or the revenue authority, personal liability attaches. Directors who established the sham offshore owner and signed the intercompany agreements are personally liable for the additional corporate tax, denied withholding relief, penalties, and interest. The tax authorities can obtain a criminal conviction under the Act on the Punishment of Tax Crimes for preparing a bogus beneficial ownership claim; imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to five times the tax evaded apply. The director's personal assets may be attached, and the National Tax Service blacklisting imposes travel bans and prevents the director from leaving Korea until the assessed liabilities are secured.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and False Intercompany Documentation

Statutory sanction: The Act on Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 5 and Article 6 empower the National Tax Service to re-determine the Korean taxable income using the arm's-length price based on the most appropriate transfer pricing method. The assessment can add transfer pricing additional tax equal to a percentage of the understated corporate tax, and failure to submit contemporaneous documentation triggers documentation penalties. False or incomplete transfer pricing disclosures are treated as improper tax returns subject to the 40% underreporting penalty. Accumulated corrections for three consecutive years may trigger formal tax criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified the false intercompany services and undocumented management fees cannot hide behind the limited liability company. The court may pierce the veil where the transfer pricing structure is merely a device to strip profits from the Korean carrier; directors are jointly and severally liable for the corrected tax and penalties. The National Tax Service may issue a travel ban against the directors and list them on the public delinquent taxpayer register. Criminal false-document charges under the Act on the Punishment of Tax Crimes can lead to imprisonment of up to 5 years and personal forfeiture of assets equivalent to the evaded Korean tax.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None - the Commercial Act imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, each member's subscribed contribution must be fully paid in cash before the incorporation registration is submitted. The full amount must be deposited in a licensed Korean bank in the name of the company or its promoters and cannot be withdrawn for operating expenses until registration is complete; the bank must issue a balance certificate. For non-resident members, a blocked escrow account is not statutorily required, but a foreign-investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act must be made through a designated bank or KOTRA before or at the time of remittance.

Funding & unlock path: Civil defense and fire safety clearance must be obtained from the National Fire Agency or the local fire station before operating any warehouse or fulfillment center where portable power stations and lithium-ion batteries are stored. The clearance includes active fire suppression, automatic smoke detection, emergency exit markings, and segregation of battery charging areas. The company must file an initial KOSHA risk assessment under the Occupational Safety and Health Act before employee operations. If the warehouse floor area exceeds 4,600 square meters, a separate Fire Safety Plan and civil defense inspection may be imposed by the local fire department.

Paid-in capital rule: None - the Commercial Act imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For the Korean-incorporated Yuhan Hoesa LLC, the domestic business operator must complete business registration at the district tax office having jurisdiction over the principal place of business within 20 days of commencing business under the Value-Added Tax Act. The application must be submitted through HomeTax and supported by the Certificate of Incorporation, registered articles of incorporation, corporate seal certificate, lease or ownership document for the Korean business place, and evidence of foreign non-resident ownership (e.g., foreign incorporation certificate and passport/corporate ID of the foreign shareholder). NTS then issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as the entity's domestic TIN/VAT ID. For a foreign non-resident entity with no Korean business place, the entity must appoint a Korean tax representative to file a simplified VAT registration through HomeTax under the Value-Added Tax Act and ancillary Enforcement Decree, attaching the foreign certificate of incorporation, evidence of the foreign business place, bank account details for refunds, and the tax representative's mandate. If the foreign entity has a domestic place of business or permanent establishment, it must instead complete full domestic business registration through the same district tax office procedure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary registration threshold. All business operators in Korea, including the online retailer of portable battery generators and solar panels, must register for VAT within 20 days of commencement of business under Article 8 of the Value-Added Tax Act. Foreign non-resident electronic service suppliers are separately required to register and file VAT on taxable electronic services supplied to Korean consumers, with no separate turnover threshold applied for the domestic goods-export business at issue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods from Korea to a foreign destination qualifies for zero rating under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act if the physical export is proven by a customs declaration, the goods are actually delivered to a foreign purchaser, and the business retains documentary evidence of the export flow. For direct online retail of portable battery generators and solar panels, zero rating is available only where the goods are physically exported through Korea Customs Service formal clearance; domestic sales or digital-only services supplied in Korea remain standard-rated at 10%. Where qualifying offshore services are supplied to a foreign purchaser, zero rating may be claimed only if the services are actually used or consumed outside Korea and payment is received in foreign currency.

  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing international carriage
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign Exchange Bank Certificate of Export Proceeds / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent documentary proof of foreign currency repatriation

Mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) system administered through HomeTax. For domestic business-to-business supplies, e-Tax invoices must be issued and transmitted to NTS no later than the day after the statutory tax invoice issuance date. For exports, a zero-rated e-Tax invoice must be transmitted to NTS within the applicable filing schedule and is electronically cross-matched with Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declarations. Korea is not yet aligned with full real-time transaction clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping regimes, but mandatory NTS electronic submission via HomeTax/electronic data interchange constitutes the required fiscal clearance control.

If export zero-rating is claimed without valid customs clearance, delivery documentation, or timely foreign exchange repatriation, NTS will disallow the zero rating and assess VAT at 10% on the export consideration. Under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, fraudulent or unverified claims may incur additional tax up to 40% of the evaded VAT, while negligent underreporting attracts up to 20% penalty, together with statutory arrears interest. Deliberate export-transaction fabrication or failure to substantiate foreign exchange settlement within the 180-day period can result in retrospective clawback of the entire VAT refund, additional tax, and criminal referral for tax evasion and customs fraud.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a deeply liberalized but carefully regulated foreign-exchange system. Current-account transactions, including cross-border trade settlement and operational expenses, are convertible and require only post-transaction reporting through a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital-account transactions are not fully free: foreign direct investment into Yuhan Hoesa, shareholder loans, and portfolio flows require notification/registration, must be routed through a domestic foreign-exchange bank, and are captured in the Foreign Exchange Information System. There is no compulsory surrender of export proceeds into Korean won; exporters maintain foreign-currency deposit accounts and convert at their own discretion. Profit remittance is permissible through dividends once audited retained earnings and corporate tax obligations are documented; the Korean tax administration requires advance withholding tax settlement and a tax withholding certificate before a foreign-exchange bank will approve the remittance. Practical hurdles arise where the UBO and shareholding structure is opaque, where equity funding sources are not documented, or where beneficial ownership changes are not reported. Those features directly trigger enhanced due diligence and may suspend foreign-exchange approval until the bank is supplied with verified capital-formation records and evidence of genuine e-commerce operational substance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no fixed statutory surrender percentage in South Korea; the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. Export proceeds must be collected through state-approved settlement methods such as letters of credit, documentary collections, or approved open-account terms, and must be credited to an onshore foreign-currency deposit account or a Korean-won account within the trade-settlement due date, normally not exceeding one year from the export declaration date. Deferred receipts beyond the standard period require prior or ex-post reporting to the Ministry of Economy and Finance/Bank of Korea delegated system. Conversion from foreign currency to Korean won is at the exporter's discretion, but every conversion must be executed through a domestic foreign-exchange bank and reported in the Foreign Exchange Information System.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the principal foreign-exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility is low to nil for treasury management. Stripe can act as a front-end card acquiring gateway for e-commerce, but it is not a licensed Korean bank and cannot open a Korean-won settlement account, receive inward direct investment, issue letters of credit, or perform foreign-exchange purchase and sale for a Korean legal entity. Affirm BNPL is an offshore consumer-credit product; deploying BNPL to Korean customers would require a consumer-finance license under the Credit Finance Act if offered locally, and it cannot serve as a corporate liquidity rail. Offshore digital EMIs that are not licensed under the Banking Act or the Electronic Financial Transactions Act are legally barred from taking Korean deposits or performing Korean-won foreign-exchange brokerage. Therefore all regulated treasury flows must be seated in a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank; Stripe and similar fintech rails may be used only as collection switches with settlement swept onward into the local Korean bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital should be moved by SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder to the company's foreign-currency investment deposit account at the designated domestic foreign-exchange bank. The remitter name, beneficiary name, FDI notification number, and the applicable purpose code must be shown in the message fields so the bank can report the transaction to the Foreign Exchange Information System. After the bank verifies the FDI notification, the foreign currency is converted into Korean won at the market rate and credited to the operational KRW account. The required audit trail is the inward remittance certificate, the foreign-currency investment deposit certificate, a shareholder subscription receipt, and a Korean CPA-issued capital verification certificate.Use SWIFT MT103 for straightforward supplier payments and smaller trade purchases; use confirmed irrevocable letters of credit for large imports of solar panels, lithium battery cells, and finished portable power units; and use documentary collections (documents against payment or documents against acceptance) for established medium-risk vendors. Domestic Korean-won supplier payments should flow through the bank's electronic settlement platform, connected to KFTC and BOK-Wire+ for high-value transfers. Every outbound disbursement must be supported by purchase orders, commercial invoices, customs declarations, and Korean tax filings, and by the bank's underlying trade-transaction documentation so that the cross-border payment is not reclassified as a suspicious capital outflow.Only hedging with documented real demand is permissible: deliverable foreign-exchange forwards (USD/KRW and CNY/KRW) for underlying inventory import and profit remittance; FX swaps to manage conversion timing on FDI equity and shareholder loans; currency options or collars approved against named actual exposures; cross-currency swaps for long-term non-KRW liabilities; and non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) restricted to licensed onshore banks or offshore counterparties hedging non-repatriable positions, always subject to Korea's real-demand and transaction-evidence rules. Speculative trading, uncovered option writing, and crypto-linked hedging are not permitted.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is low to nil for treasury management. Stripe can act as a front-end card acquiring gateway for e-commerce, but it is not a licensed Korean bank and cannot open a Korean-won settlement account, receive inward direct investment, issue letters of credit, or perform foreign-exchange purchase and sale for a Korean legal entity. Affirm BNPL is an offshore consumer-credit product; deploying BNPL to Korean customers would require a consumer-finance license under the Credit Finance Act if offered locally, and it cannot serve as a corporate liquidity rail. Offshore digital EMIs that are not licensed under the Banking Act or the Electronic Financial Transactions Act are legally barred from taking Korean deposits or performing Korean-won foreign-exchange brokerage. Therefore all regulated treasury flows must be seated in a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank; Stripe and similar fintech rails may be used only as collection switches with settlement swept onward into the local Korean bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared and submitted when cross-border intercompany transactions with foreign related parties exceed KRW 5 billion; Master File is required when a consolidated group has total revenue exceeding KRW 100 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion. Even below thresholds, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is required for DTA access and audit defense.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies economic substance as part of the Corporate Income Tax Act, the Framework Act on Taxes, and tax treaty anti-abuse rules. There is no separate Caribbean-type ESR form, but a company claiming treaty benefits must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea: functions performed, assets used, risks assumed, and key management decisions. A captive distributor with no real inventory risk or sales-support team is subject to recharacterization of taxable income and denial of treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion through unreported e-commerce sales

Statutory sanction: Under Framework Act on Taxes Article 47-2, a 30% additional tax of the underreported amount plus unpaid taxes and daily surcharge of 0.03% capped at 73 days; under Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3, imprisonment up to three years or fine up to KRW 200 million; if tax evaded exceeds KRW 500 million, aggravated sentencing may apply.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly directed the false reporting becomes personally liable with the LLC for unpaid tax and penalties. Korean courts will pierce the corporate veil where formalities were abused; National Tax Service may blacklist the individual, impose a travel ban, freeze personal assets, and criminal conviction is served personally.
Shell company creation and DTA treaty-shopping abuse

Statutory sanction: The Principal Purpose Test under the OECD DTA, as implemented in the International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 2, 5 and 11, authorizes denial of treaty benefits; National Tax Service will assess the 22% withholding tax shortfall with interest and a 30% additional tax; the related-party transaction may be disregarded or recharacterized under substance-over-form.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed the sham structure can be jointly assessed for unpaid withholding and penalties; they may face blacklisting, asset seizure, travel restrictions, and criminal liability for aiding tax fraud where fraudulent concealment of beneficial ownership is proven.
AML and payment rails violation through BNPL and refund abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Korea's Financial Transaction Reports Act, failure to identify customers and report suspicious transactions can lead to criminal fines and imprisonment; tax authorities may impose an additional 30% tax under the Framework Act on Taxes if the transfers are recharacterized as non-deductible disguised dividends or laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors and the AML compliance officer are exposed to personal criminal fines; personal bank accounts may be frozen and included in the blacklist. Courts can pierce the veil where the entity was used for illegal financial flows, imposing civil restitution and imprisonment.

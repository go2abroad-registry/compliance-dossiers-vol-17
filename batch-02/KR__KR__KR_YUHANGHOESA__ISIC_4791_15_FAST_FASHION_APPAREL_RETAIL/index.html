Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Korean Yuhan Hoesa. The amount of capital is fixed by the members in the Articles of Incorporation. Every monetary contribution must be paid into a domestic bank account opened in the name of the corporation before the registration application is submitted, and a certificate evidencing receipt of the subscribed capital must be attached to the registration application. Korean law does not require a blocked escrow account; however, the subscribed capital must be identifiable in the company's bank account and must remain in the company name after registration.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for a pure electronic retail head office and administrative workplace; however, the Occupational Safety and Health Act general duty to provide safe working conditions applies once employees are hired. A Civil Defense fire permit will become mandatory only if the Company later operates a physical warehouse, distribution center, or mixed-use retail storage area exceeding the exempted square footage; in that case, the municipal fire department must issue a fire protection completion certificate and conduct a structural review before occupancy.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Determine whether the foreign non-resident entity has a domestic place of business in South Korea. If yes, the entity must file an Application for Business Registration with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the domestic place of business. Step 2 - Prepare the foreign entity documentation pack: notarized and apostilled (or consular-legalized) certificate of incorporation, foreign business license, company statute or articles of association, board resolution appointing a domestic agent or tax representative, and proof of the domestic business address (lease contract or ownership certificate). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Korean tax system does not provide a check-the-box election analogous to the United States. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa is automatically classified as a corporation for all Korean tax purposes and is subject to entity-level corporate income tax. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are imposed by statute under the Corporate Tax Act and its Enforcement Decree; they are non-elective in nature.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no mandatory economic turnover threshold for VAT registration in South Korea. Any person engaged in a taxable business, including a foreign e-commerce seller with a Korean nexus, must register for VAT from the first taxable supply. The simplified taxpayer status may apply only to individual sole proprietors with annual taxable supplies not exceeding KRW 80 million; it is not available to a Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) or any other corporate exporter. Therefore, all corporate exporters must obtain a full VAT registration regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act, the supply of exported goods is zero-rated when the following statutory criteria are satisfied: (1) the goods are delivered or shipped to a foreign buyer and physically depart from South Korea; (2) the supplier obtains an export declaration confirmation (export bill of entry) from the Korea Customs Service via the UNI-PASS system; (3) the sales proceeds are generated from a non-resident and are repatriated to South Korea through a registered foreign exchange bank in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; and (4) for qualifying offshore digital or electronic services, the recipient is a non-resident and the services are not effectively used in Korea. The supplier must retain all relevant evidence, including e-Tax Invoices issued at 0%, and must report the export sales on the VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration Bill of Entry - Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declaration confirmation
  • Bill of Lading (including airway bill or multimodal transport document evidencing physical export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or foreign exchange receipt certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Commercial Invoice and signed Packing List
  • Purchase order or overseas sales contract with the non-resident buyer
  • Electronic tax invoice issued for the zero-rated export supply

South Korea does not operate a centralized real-time invoice clearance or digital stamping regime of the Latin American type. However, all VAT-registered taxpayers must issue electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoices) under the National Tax Service HomeTax system. Every e-Tax Invoice must be transmitted to the NTS within one day after the date of invoice issuance, and the transmission is completed via a secure API using XML or JSON payloads authenticated by an accredited digital certificate (mutual SSL). B2C retail sales to Korean consumers are typically evidenced through the mandatory cash receipt system, which is also transmitted to NTS in real time. Anti-fraud scrutiny is applied by NTS through cross-matching of e-Tax Invoices with customs export declarations, foreign exchange bank records, and periodic VAT returns.

If the exporter fails to obtain the foreign exchange receipts within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration, or fails to substantiate the export zero-rating with the required customs and banking documentary bundle, the zero-rate is denied retroactively and the 10% VAT becomes payable immediately. NTS may assess additional tax as national basic additional tax for underreporting, plus a penalty of 40% of the underpaid VAT in cases of fraudulent or intentional evasion. Criminal prosecution may also be initiated under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment and fines. In repersistence, NTS can reopen audit assessments for up to five years and claw back any VAT refunds already paid, with applicable interest and penalty surcharges.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates an open but disciplined foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is freely convertible for current account transactions, including export settlement, cross-border service fees, royalties and dividend remittances, provided the underlying transaction documentation is submitted to a Foreign Exchange Bank. Capital account operations, including inbound equity contributions, intercompany loans, securities purchases and derivative transactions, are liberalized under a negative-list system but must be reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System, with certain high-value or related-party operations requiring prior approval by MOEF or BOK. Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory conversion into won; companies may retain foreign currency receipts in approved Foreign Currency Deposit Accounts. However, exporters must collect and settle export proceeds within six months of shipment, subject to extension approval for legitimate commercial reasons. Dividend and profit remittances by a Yuhan Hoesa LLC to non-resident shareholders require evidence of distributable reserves calculated under Korean GAAP or K-IFRS, corporate income tax clearance, and a board resolution authorizing the distribution. In practice, for a fast fashion e-commerce entity, accurate purpose-of-payment codes, transparent invoicing and a stable domestic banking relationship are critical to avoid friction in cross-border clearing and FX settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected within six months from the date of export. There is no mandatory conversion ratio requiring surrender to the central bank; proceeds may be retained in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account or voluntarily sold to a licensed Foreign Exchange Bank. If the six-month collection deadline cannot be met, the exporting company must seek an extension through a designated Foreign Exchange Bank or MOEF approval, and failure to comply may trigger administrative penalties, delayed export license renewal or adverse classification for FX transaction privileges.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange policy authority, Korea Customs Service for trade-related enforcement, and designated Foreign Exchange Banks for reporting and settlement under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. South Korean law restricts deposit-taking, KRW clearing and cross-border money transmission to licensed banks and registered payment service providers under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Most foreign digital EMIs such as Revolut Business or Wise Business hold no Korean banking license and cannot open a domestic KRW settlement account or act as the primary treasury bank for a Yuhan Hoesa LLC. Online payment platforms like Shopify Payments and Klarna BNPL are useful merchant rails but they settle through acquirers or cross-border PSPs and are not substitutes for a domestic corporate account. The entity should therefore maintain its primary KRW concentration account and FX hedging relationship with a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank, using foreign EMIs only as supplementary expense card or low-value remittance rails where permitted.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident investor should remit equity capital by SWIFT or telegraphic transfer to the Yuhan Hoesa LLC’s Foreign Investor Capital Account maintained at a designated Foreign Exchange Bank in Korea. The remittance instruction must reference the foreign investment report filed under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and the purpose description “Paid-in capital contribution for equity investment in a domestic corporation.” The receiving bank will assign the appropriate Foreign Exchange Information System purpose code, register the inward remittance, and provide an inward remittance advice and foreign currency deposit certificate. Before conversion into KRW and issuance of shares, the company must complete the Foreign Investment Enterprise registration with the reporting bank. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion; plus 10% local income tax surcharge, yielding combined effective rates of 9.9% to 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% statutory outbound dividend withholding tax for non-resident corporations and individuals under Korean domestic law, composed of 20% corporate/income withholding plus 2% local income tax surcharge; DTA relief may reduce the rate to 0%, 5%, 10% or 15% depending on the treaty jurisdiction, the class of shareholder and the level of shareholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion aggregate cross-border related-party transactions is the statutory threshold triggering mandatory Korean Local File preparation; a Master File is additionally required when cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion and worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the National Tax Service applies a substance-over-form doctrine and the OECD BEPS anti-abuse framework to related-party structures. A fast-fashion e-commerce Yuhan Hoesa must demonstrate real staff, inventory, logistics decision-making, order-processing systems and management control consistent with Core Income-Generating Activities; absent that substance, cross-border fee payments and treaty benefits are denied and the offshore profit is reattributed to Korea.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes, the evaded corporate tax is collected with a 40% fraud penalty, a mandatory criminal complaint is filed when omitted tax exceeds KRW 300 million, the entity is registered as a tax delinquent, business tax invoices are restricted and the company can be suspended from e-commerce payment gateways.

Director personal liability: Korea courts can pierce the limited liability of the Yuhan Hoesa when the corporate form is used for fraudulent evasion; directors who signed the false return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and damages under Commercial Act Art. 401; the director is subject to a travel ban/blacklist, civil asset attachment is ordered, bank accounts and foreign real estate are frozen, and imprisonment of up to 5 years can be imposed.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service recharacterizes such transactions under Art. 183 of the Corporate Tax Act and the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs; the excessive payments are added back to taxable income, a transfer-pricing penalty of 20% and overdue interest of 0.0255% per day apply, the payments may be recharacterized as deemed dividends subject to 22% withholding, and the shell counterparty is stripped of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced as a fraudulent intermediary scheme; directors who approved the excessive related-party payments are personally liable for corporate income tax shortfalls, the Korean tax authority obtains a ruling on restitution of the extracted profits, personally liable directors face departure restrictions for outstanding tax over KRW 100 million, and criminal charges under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act can result in imprisonment up to 7 years for aggravated fraud.
AML / Unreported Cross-Border Payments and Foreign Financial Accounts

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act subject the company to a fine up to 100% of the repatriated amount, the National Tax Service imposes a 20% additional penalty on unreported foreign financial account balances and refers the matter to the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit, and the AML reporting failure carries imprisonment for responsible executives under the Act on Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Information.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct unreported remittances or hold undisclosed overseas accounts are personally fined up to KRW 50 million and imprisoned up to 5 years; civil asset restitution is ordered to the Korean entity, the director is placed on a departure blacklist and Korean passport restrictions are issued, and where the hidden funds derive from VAT or customs fraud associated with imported fast-fashion inventory, the imprisonment ceiling rises to 7 years with share seizure and court-ordered confiscation of foreign real estate.

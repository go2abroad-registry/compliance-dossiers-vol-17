Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under Korean court practice and tax law, a Yuhan Hoesa must maintain a fixed physical address within the Republic of Korea for the receipt of official notices and the preservation of books. A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not prohibited when the company is dormant or engages only in passive holding activities, but for a multi-family office engaged in discretionary portfolio advisory under ISIC 6430 and NAICS 523920, the Financial Services Commission and the National Tax Service will reject a virtual office as the registered office because the economic substance requirement of the Korea International Taxation Act and FSC customer due diligence guidelines require a dedicated office with staff, records, and an AML policy manual. Banks also decline to open SWIFT interbank accounts when the registered office is a post office box or an address-only service. Therefore, a virtual office is permissive only for the initial filing, but it must be replaced within thirty days with a physical office in an approved commercial zone or banking will be refused.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. The incorporators may set the initial capital in the Articles of Incorporation. The declared capital must be fully paid into a provisional capital-subscription bank account in the name of the company being formed before filing, and the account is blocked by the bank until the certificate of incorporation registration is issued. If the MFO will conduct discretionary portfolio management under the FSCMA, the FSC capital-adequacy rules require a substantially higher operating capital, which must be evidenced before the financial license is granted.

Competent tax authority

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover exemption for a corporate VAT obligor; a Korean corporation must register from the commencement of the taxable business. Individual/small entrepreneurs (simplified taxpayers) have a KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable supply threshold for simplified taxation status. Non-resident suppliers of electronic/digital services to Korean end-users must register when the annual Korean cross-border revenue exceeds KRW 1,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Korea qualify for zero-rating under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act when the goods are actually shipped outside the Republic of Korea, title passes to a non-resident buyer, and the export is declared through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS. Services supplied for use/benefit outside Korea to non-residents, including qualifying offshore digital content and game exports, are zero-rated when the service is not effectively used in Korea and the consideration is earned in foreign currency. VAT-exempt financial services (e.g., certain portfolio-management/fiduciary functions of a multi-family office) do not accrue output VAT and cannot use export zero-rating relief; the zero-rating substantiation regime below applies only to taxable export supplies.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate) with the unique export declaration number and confirmed customs clearance date
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list signed by the Korean exporting entity
  • Bill of Lading (ocean shipment) or Air Waybill (air shipment) evidencing the cargo carrier, departure port/airport in Korea, and discharge/arrival location outside Korea
  • Foreign exchange bank proof of receipt: bank remittance advice or a certificate from a Korean foreign-exchange bank evidencing the export proceeds were received in an external convertible currency for the exact export invoice
  • For non-goods service exports: written global service agreement, a service delivery/benefit analysis confirming foreign consumption, the KRW/foreign-currency invoice issued to the non-resident client, and a foreign exchange bank receipt certificate for the inward remittance

Every Korean VAT taxable person with a domestic place of business must issue an electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) for B2B supplies and transmit it to the NTS via HomeTax, no later than the day after issuance. The e-tax invoice includes the seller and buyer 10-digit Business Registration Numbers, the supply description, the supply date, KRW supply value, and KRW VAT. The HomeTax e-tax invoice system assigns an official NTS registration/approval number and stores the transmission in the NTS database; the buyer must verify this registration number before taking an input VAT credit. Korea does not operate a Latin-American-style continuous fiscal printer/real-time stamping system; a supplementary paper invoice system applies for B2C transactions, but all B2B electronic tax invoices are captured centrally for anti-fraud cross-matching.

The NTS will deny zero-rating and reclassify the supply as taxable at 10% if the export documentation bundle is incomplete, the service is found to be consumed in Korea, or the foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated to Korea within the statutory settlement period (generally 180 days from export declaration or, for services, from the contractual receipt date, without an approved extension). Administrative corrections add the unpaid VAT, interest from the date the VAT actually arose under the National Tax Collection Act, and additional tax equal to 40% of the evaded or erroneously zero-rated output tax where NTS finds a false invoice pattern or unreasonable tax benefit, plus a 20% late-payment additional tax for underpayment. Fraudulent export zero-rating can also trigger criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (imprisonment of up to three years or a fine not exceeding three times the tax evaded) and separate fines under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act for unlawful non-repatriation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized current account regime with full convertibility for trade and ordinary business transactions, but capital account transactions remain regulated under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won (KRW) is not fully convertible outside the jurisdiction; offshore NDF markets exist but onshore convertibility is restricted to legitimate underlying economic activities. Capital account operations such as equity injections, loans, and portfolio investments must be reported through designated foreign exchange banks, and certain transactions require prior notification or approval from the MOEF. Profit remittances are generally permitted without tax clearance if proper tax returns and documentation are filed, but the bank will require evidence of the underlying transaction, tax compliance certificate, and a certified translation of audited financial statements. Foreign investors face no mandatory surrender of export proceeds for current receipts, but they must repatriate funds through a registered foreign exchange bank and settle within the statutory reporting period. For high-risk industries such as multi-family office discretionary portfolio advisory, the bank may impose enhanced scrutiny on the source of wealth and the ultimate beneficial owner, and any capital inflow classified as portfolio investment must be registered with the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA), coupled with a foreign exchange purchase/sale confirmation from the custodian bank. In practice, strict beneficial ownership disclosure, the Kimchi Premium arbitrage controls, and the real-name settlement system for foreign exchange transactions create formidable hurdles for unstructured capital movements. The treasury blueprint must therefore assume pre-cleared, evidenced, and purpose-tagged flows through Tier-1 banks with in-house foreign exchange desks capable of handling regulatory filings in real time.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Korea abolished mandatory export proceeds surrender for current account receipts; exporters must receive proceeds within 6 months from the shipment date and may hold foreign currency in domestic foreign exchange accounts, but all receipts must be reported to the designated foreign exchange bank within the prescribed reporting window. Conversion to KRW is optional for the exporter, but any conversion must be executed through the bank's interbank FX desk with a valid transaction code (e.g., code 01001 for export receipts under standard letters of credit). For high-risk sectors and large transactions exceeding USD 50,000 per case, simultaneous reporting to the Korea Customs Service is required.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), administered by the Korea Customs Service for trade-related reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Unviable. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex, or similar e-money institutions) are not licensed to provide multi-currency local account solutions for Korean-domiciled entities. The Financial Services Commission (FSC) requires any institution handling KRW settlement to hold a bank charter or an electronic financial business license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Non-resident fintech EMIs cannot interface with the domestic real-time settlement network (BOK-Wire+) and lack the statutory authority to issue Korean account numbers for corporate foreign exchange reporting. They may, at best, act as an unregulated intermediary for the initial cross-border remittance, but the Korean entity must maintain a domestic Tier-1 bank account for all regulatory filings, tax withholding, and capital verification. Therefore, no fintech EMI should be placed in the treasury liquidity structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign capital injections must be routed through the designated domestic Tier-1 bank using SWIFT MT103 with purpose codes assigned by the MOEF. For a foreign-owned Korean entity, the initial equity investment is classified as 'Foreign Direct Investment' under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) and must be reported via the bank's Foreign Investment Support Center. The remitter must clearly designate the transaction type as 'Acquisition of Shares' or 'Capital Increase' and include the CEO/EBITDA narrative in the payment details to avoid misclassification. The bank will issue a Foreign Exchange Certificate (외화증명) which is a prerequisite for the capital verification process. The inbound payment must land within 60 days of the filing date, and the KRW conversion is performed at the bank's interbank rate with the bank automatically filing the capital transaction report to the MOEF. For multi-family office operations, the equity injection must be distinguished from discretionary asset custody flows — the latter must be routed through the separate Custody Account and tagged with code 'F-3' (overseas institutional investment) to prevent blanket capital restrictions. All large cash injections exceeding KRW 500 million must be preceded by a pre-onboarding committee clearance to avoid the foreign exchange bank rejecting the wire upon arrival.Outbound disbursements for investment management fees, trade settlements, or profit distributions must be executed through SWIFT MT103 with the appropriate Korean standard transaction codes. For trade-related payments (e.g., purchases of securities infrastructure or external advisory services), use code 02810 for service charges, or 03120 for business management and consulting fees. For dividend repatriation, use code 01310 and attach the board resolution, audited financial statements, and tax withholding certificate (the bank will withhold 20% for non-network jurisdictions unless a tax treaty provides a lower rate). For settlements involving connected party services, the bank requires a transfer pricing study under Article 53 of the Korean Corporate Tax Act, confirming the arm's length nature of the fee. Standby LCs and documentary collections are permissible for physical goods transactions, but for financial services, the standard clearing rail is an SWIFT interbank transfer using a bilateral correspondent route (typically via New York or Hong Kong for USD and Asia-Pacific jurisdictions). The entity should maintain a minimum operational balance of KRW 100 million in the basic account to satisfy the bank's liquidity stress threshold and to ensure immediate activation of the real-time gross settlement (RTGS) gateway without intraday overdraft limits.Onshore USD/KRW forward contracts via the designated bank (under FETA General Directive 9-3) limited to the notional amount of the underlying equity injection and profit remittance forecast with a bank-confirmed exposure schedule.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in offshore markets are permitted only for hedge of non-KRW currency exposures where the Korean entity has a documented overseas portfolio asset; outright speculative NDF positions are strictly prohibited by the FSC.Currency swaps are allowed for long-term debt or capital accounts exceeding one year, subject to prior notification to the MOEF when the notional exceeds USD 10 million equivalent; such swaps must be executed with the opening bank or a licensed Korean securities house.Options (vanilla calls/puts) are permitted solely for the purpose of hedging foreign currency receivables and payables, but require evidence of the exact commercial invoice and the bank's treasury committee approval.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Unviable. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex, or similar e-money institutions) are not licensed to provide multi-currency local account solutions for Korean-domiciled entities. The Financial Services Commission (FSC) requires any institution handling KRW settlement to hold a bank charter or an electronic financial business license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Non-resident fintech EMIs cannot interface with the domestic real-time settlement network (BOK-Wire+) and lack the statutory authority to issue Korean account numbers for corporate foreign exchange reporting. They may, at best, act as an unregulated intermediary for the initial cross-border remittance, but the Korean entity must maintain a domestic Tier-1 bank account for all regulatory filings, tax withholding, and capital verification. Therefore, no fintech EMI should be placed in the treasury liquidity structure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion (10,000,000,000) aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File; Master File and Country-by-Country Report thresholds are generally KRW 100 billion and KRW 1 trillion for group consolidated revenue, respectively, or lower where the entity is part of a multinational group designated by the NTS; MFOs must prepare contemporaneous documentation even below threshold when a valuation of management/performance fees or loan transactions is challenged.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation (ESR), but the tax authorities and courts apply the OECD core income-generating activities (CIGA) doctrine through the beneficial ownership requirement, substance-over-form rule, and general anti-avoidance provisions in the International Tax Adjustment Act. A Korean Multi-Family Office claiming DTA benefits or management-fee deductions must demonstrate local employees (portfolio managers, compliance officers, investment committee), local policymaking, real office, bank/custody accounts, and discretionary investment authority. Absent these CIGA factors, the NTS may disregard the entity, deny treaty relief, reclassify income, and penalize the director. For licensed MFO operations, the Financial Services Commission licensing and AML (Custody/Wire/SWIFT) registrations are strong evidence of local substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company structured solely for treaty shopping and dividend stripping

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of treaty benefits under Article 5 of the International Tax Adjustment Act; reclassification of all Korean-source earnings as dividends to a non-resident; withholding at statutory 22% instead of 5%; additional 40% unreasonable tax benefit penalty; mismatch penalty for failure to file foreign-owned entity income; administrative fine up to KRW 100 million.

Director personal liability: The NTS may pierce the corporate veil under the Commercial Act and Corporate Tax Act, holding the director personally liable for the unpaid 22% WHT and penalties; travel ban (blacklisting) under Korean tax collection law; civil restitution of illegally streamed dividends with interest; criminal liability for directors under Article 114 of the Individual Tax Law for facilitating tax evasion, resulting in imprisonment up to 3 years and fine.
Failure to withhold and remit non-resident WHT on outbound interest, royalties, or management fees

Statutory sanction: The Korean payer is liable for the un-withheld 22% tax plus a 10% (or 20% if intentional) withholding penalty, daily late payment surcharge, and administrative audit; under Corporate Tax Act Article 128, the payer’s statutory tax obligation is assessed; in fraudulent cases, under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, fines of up to 5 times the evaded withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company’s withheld amounts and penalties under Corporate Tax Act Article 112 and Commercial Act Article 401; the NTS may obtain a civil judgment against the directors' personal bank accounts; foreign-travel restrictions are imposed on the representative director for unresolved tax liabilities; criminal imprisonment up to 2 years applies where the failure is intentional.
Fraudulent transfer pricing mispricing of discretionary portfolio advisory fees to a foreign affiliated MFO

Statutory sanction: NTS adjusts the arm's length price under Article 4 of the International Tax Adjustment Act; disallows the inflated fees; adds 10% underreporting penalty and 40% negligence penalty for fraudulent misstatement; imposes the mandatory TP documentation penalty for missing Local File (KRW 100 million or 5% of the transaction amount); assesses corporate tax at 24% on the adjusted base plus 2% local surcharge.

Director personal liability: The thin capitalization and excess fee structure pierce the veil of the Korean entity: directors are severally liable for restitution of the deducted management fees; criminal indictment for aggravated tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act carries imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine not exceeding five times the evaded tax; as a criminal red flag, foreign travel is banned until the judgment is satisfied, and directors' personal bank, real estate, and equity assets are subject to compulsory execution.

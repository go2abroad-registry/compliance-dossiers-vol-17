Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a Yuhan Hoesa. The incorporators may fix the member capital contributions in the Articles of Incorporation at any amount, but all subscribed cash contributions must be fully deposited into a blocked escrow account at a licensed Korean bank before the registration application is submitted. The bank certificate of capital contribution deposit must be filed with the registry court, and the escrowed funds cannot be released or used for operations until the Certificate of Corporate Registration has been issued by the district court. Non-cash contributions, if any, must be verified by an independent appraiser and described in the Articles of Incorporation. Capitalization should nevertheless be set at a level commercially sufficient for the intended LME warrant transfer and documentary letter of credit trading operations.

Funding & unlock path: For office and trading operations, Yuhan Hoesa must be covered by the landlord statutory fire safety completion certificate and must comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including a basic workplace safety plan and emergency evacuation plan. If the company operates a copper cathode warehouse in Korea, the facility must obtain a fire-fighting system completion inspection and fire safety certificate from the National Fire Agency, complete a structural load and rack stability inspection by a qualified engineer, and satisfy all storage and escape route requirements before commencement of operations; for workplaces with employees, annual workplace safety and health inspections through the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency may apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No minimum turnover threshold for general taxpayers; a Yuhan Hoesa, being a corporation, must register for VAT before commencing business. The simplified-taxpayer threshold of KRW 48,000,000 in annual taxable supplies applies only to natural persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 24(1)(1) of the VAT Act, exports of South Korean goods are zero-rated when the goods are deposited in a bonded area, loaded, and transported abroad under an accepted export declaration. For the Grade A refined copper cathode wholesale activity, zero-rating requires an LME warrant transfer or delivery order accompanied by physical export through a Korean customs port; the warrant transfer alone does not justify 0% VAT. Qualifying offshore digital services are zero-rated only if supplied to a non-resident customer and consumed outside Korea, with supporting evidence retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export declaration submitted to Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS and accepted with a unique export serial number)
  • Bill of Lading (or multimodal transport document) evidencing loading at a Korean port and discharge at a foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List corresponding exactly to the copper cathode grade, warehouse location, and LME warrant details
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate (Inward Remittance Certificate, FIRC) issued by a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank upon collection of export proceeds
  • LME warrant transfer or delivery order records evidencing title transfer to the non-resident buyer (supporting evidence; does not replace physical export proof)

All VAT invoices must be issued as electronic tax invoices under Article 32 of the VAT Act and transmitted to the NTS HomeTax system no later than the day following issuance. Each e-invoice carries an NTS-assigned electronic tax invoice number, a digital certificate, and an anti-fraud electronic token. Korea does not impose a continuous real-time fiscal clearance API for B2B wholesale transactions; however, export declarations are validated through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS, and VAT e-invoices are submitted using HomeTax DTD with API/EDI integration. Non-compliance triggers the statutory additional taxes (e.g., 2% of the supply value for failure to issue, 2% for incorrect issuance, plus applicable penalty interest).

NTS audit teams cross-match the Export Declaration status, shipping manifests, and inflows of export proceeds through the foreign exchange information system. If the export zero-rating is not substantiated by the customs and FX settlement documents within the 180-day repatriation deadline, or if the documents are false or backdated, the NTS reverses the zero rating, assesses 10% VAT, adds statutory additional tax under Article 48 of the VAT Act for fraudulent or negligent refunds, imposes a false-invoice additional tax up to 2% of the supply value, and may pursue criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, carrying imprisonment up to 10 years and a fine up to 50 times the evaded tax. The VAT refunded is clawed back with interest; records must be retained for at least five years after export.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised convertibility system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The won is fully convertible for current-account needs, including international trade and business services, and such settlements require only post-event reporting through the Foreign Exchange Information System. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity/debt investment and cross-currency funding, are subject to notification, registration, and use of authorised foreign exchange business banks; they are not subject to prior approval in the ordinary course. There is no centralised surrender requirement for export proceeds, which may be retained in foreign-currency accounts at domestic banks. However, profit remittance and repatriation of capital require evidence of the original foreign-investment registration, tax clearance certificates, and audited financial statements, and should be routed through the same banking corridor used for capital injection. The Bank of Korea and MOEF retain macro-prudential levers, including monitoring of speculative inflows and temporary recovery measures. For a copper cathode wholesaler, credibility is enhanced by demonstrating LME-backed invoicing, real trade flows, and corporate tax compliance, allowing unrestricted current-account clearing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, exporters must recover export proceeds within one year from the export declaration date; extensions are possible with Korea Customs Service or BOK approval for justified causes such as deferred payment terms. Mandatory surrender ratio is 0%: South Korea does not require conversion of export receipts into KRW. Proceeds may be held in foreign-currency accounts or foreign exchange bank accounts and used for trade settlement, FX hedging, and approved capital transactions, subject to Foreign Exchange Information System reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low / not legally viable as a primary banking rail. South Korea reserves deposit-taking, foreign exchange, and cross-border settlement services to domestic banks and licensed foreign exchange business entities under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, or Airwallex are not authorised to maintain KRW corporate accounts or clear trade payments for a Korean-domiciled entity absent a partnership with a licensed domestic bank. Therefore, all treasury operations for KR_YUHANGHOESA should be anchored in Tier-1 Korean commercial banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared and submitted when cross-border related-party transaction values exceed KRW 5 billion (approx. USD 3.8 million) in a business year; Master File is required if the consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion; CbCR applies for groups with revenue of KRW 1 trillion or more.

Economic substance scope: Although South Korea does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) law similar to UAE, the Korean Tax Authorities apply the core income-generating activities (CIGA) concept via Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules, substance-over-form doctrine under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, and the domestic-source income deeming provisions. The Korean LLC must demonstrate that it performs the actual commercial risk-taking and decision-making for copper trading (e.g., negotiating terms, taking delivery risks, holding LME warrants ownership) and has sufficient personnel, offices, and bank accounts. Otherwise, profits can be reallocated to the non-resident parent or treated as constructive dividends subject to WHT.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Violation of Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (2019 rev.); penalty of up to 3 times the evaded tax (administrative negligence surcharge of 40% of the underreported tax). Aggregate withholding non-compliance adds 15% surcharge.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil under Article 42 of the Framework Act on National Taxes: the director who actually controlled the evasion is jointly liable for the corporate tax plus penalties. Criminal liability: up to 5 years' imprisonment or a fine equal to 5 times the evaded tax; when the violation is committed by the corporation, the director can be personally blacklisted by the NTS and prohibited from leaving the country (travel ban/출국금지) pending investigation.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The NTS may disregard the entity as a sham under Article 14, apply the substance-over-form principle, and impute all income to the ultimate non-resident parent, leading to a 40% negligence penalty on underreported income and denial of VAT input credits. Additionally, the company's registration can be cancelled under the Commercial Act for sham management.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties via joint liability under Article 42; criminal prosecution for breach of duty under the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies (similar to fraud). The court may issue a compensation order against the director for the full underpaid corporate tax. Travel restrictions and immigration blacklisting are routinely applied to directors of offshore shell structures.
AML / Money-Laundering through Trade Financing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 6 of the Act on Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information, failure to file a suspicious transaction report (STR) is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years or a criminal fine of up to KRW 20 million (approx. USD 15,000). The Korea Customs Service may also impose administrative penalties up to KRW 100 million for false trade declarations under the Customs Act, and seize commodities.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who intentionally conceal trade-based money laundering are criminally liable; imprisonment up to 10 years under the Proceeds of Crime Act for the predicate offense money-laundering. The court may order asset confiscation or retrieve equivalent value from the director's personal estate. Directors face blacklisting from the financial system and are barred from openings bank accounts; daily fines of KRW 10,000 per day for noncompliance of reporting obligations may be imposed.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: Home Tax Service (HTS) at hometax.go.kr; export goods physical movement clearance and customs declaration acceptance are supervised by Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic customs portal.

        
E-government filing portal
For the Korean domestic entity Yuhan Hoesa (LLC), the Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) issued after incorporation under the Commercial Act. Step 1: file incorporation with the local court registry and obtain the certificate of incorporation. Step 2: apply for Business Registration (BRN) with the National Tax Service through the Home Tax portal or by visiting the competent district tax office within the statutory commencement period. Step 3: NTS accepts the application and issues the BRN certificate, which simultaneously serves as the corporate TIN and the VAT identification number for electronic tax invoices. Step 4: register for electronic tax invoice issuance in the Home Tax e-Invoice System using an NTS-accredited digital certificate. Step 5: open a corporate bank account, including a foreign-exchange repatriation account, and complete payroll and withholding tax registration with the NTS if the entity hires personnel. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a local establishment, the domestic BRN route is unavailable; the foreign entity must instead complete simplified VAT registration through a resident tax representative using the foreigner Business Registration procedure applicable to overseas electronic service suppliers, with the representative filing returns and receiving refunds on its behalf.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation; the Yuhan Hoesa is taxed under the Corporate Tax Act as a domestic corporation on worldwide income with no general pass-through treatment.

        
Available election forms
None; South Korea does not afford a check-the-box election for entity classification. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily treated as a separate corporate taxpayer, and no domestic statutory hybrid mismatch election is available.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No general VAT registration threshold: every Korean corporation supplying taxable goods or services must register for VAT from its first taxable supply. A de minimis annual revenue threshold of KRW 300 million applies only to foreign non-resident suppliers of electronic services under the simplified overseas businessperson VAT registration regime; below that threshold, such foreign suppliers are not required to register in Korea.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Under the VAT Act, 0% VAT applies to exports of goods actually moved out of the Republic of Korea upon acceptance of an export declaration by Korea Customs Service, and to services exported to non-resident enterprises or overseas users where the service is deemed supplied offshore and is paid for in foreign currency. For the virtual card issuer's cross-border B2B SaaS and platform services, zero-rating is available for fees, interchange, or licence revenues charged to non-Korean customers where the contract with the foreign counterparty demonstrates the place of supply is outside Korea, the payment is received in foreign exchange, and the service is not physically used by a Korean-resident establishment. Payment card network settlement fees attributable to foreign banks or foreign merchants may qualify if the underlying transaction is an exported financial service; however, domestic Korean merchant services remain standard-rated at 10%.

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for all corporations. The Yuhan Hoesa must issue and submit tax invoices through the NTS Home Tax e-Invoice System, either directly via API or through a certified ASP before the statutory due date. Each electronic tax invoice carries a unique sequential invoice number and an NTS-generated association number, is digitally certified, and is transmitted to NTS in near-real time for fiscal validation. There is no per-transaction government digital stamping model; compliance is enforced through transactional data matching, mandatory electronic delivery to the customer, and automated cross-checks of issuance, payment, and VAT return data.

      
NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-verify export declarations, foreign-exchange receipt records, bill-of-lading data, and electronic tax invoices. If a zero-rated export is unsupported, the supply is recharacterized as taxable domestic supply and VAT is reassessed at 10% on the full consideration. NTS will claw back any output VAT refund previously granted and impose an additional tax equal to the prescribed statutory penalty for under-reported sales, false tax invoices, or failure to submit evidence; penalties generally range from 10% to 100% of the tax depending on fraud. Where proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period without a valid extension, zero-rating is revoked and VAT is exigible with the additional tax. Criminal penalties for deliberate tax evasion or fictitious export refund claims under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act include imprisonment for up to five years and fines up to fifty times the evaded tax, plus revocation of the business registration and public disclosure measures when applicable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Korean won convertibility operates under a bifurcated regime. Current account transactions, including payments for imported services, royalties, and operating expenditure, are liberalised: they may be made through a registered foreign exchange bank subject only to a FEIRS filing. Capital account transactions, however, are subject to reporting thresholds and, in certain categories, prior approval. A Yuhan Hoesa virtual card issuer receiving foreign equity capital must file an FDI report through a foreign exchange bank; the inflow is then registered and capital verification is performed by the appointed accountant. Profit remittance hurdles are manageable: a non-resident shareholder may remit dividends after audited financial statements, annual corporate tax clearance, and a board-approved dividend resolution are submitted to the remitting bank. Surrender requirements are essentially absent today, as export proceeds can be held in foreign currency accounts, but any conversion to KRW must occur through a licensed domestic bank at the prevailing market rate. The real friction for a fintech card issuer is the need to prove that every individual virtual card transaction is backed by a permissible underlying trade or service; otherwise the registration of the payment under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act can be rejected and the clearing bank will block the flow.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Export proceeds must be repatriated and settled through a domestic foreign exchange bank within 12 months after the date of issue of the export declaration certificate. If the export is settled through a foreign currency account at a Korean bank, the bank will require a FEIRS report within 30 days of receipt. Statutory mandatory conversion ratio: 0% - there is currently no requirement to convert foreign currency export proceeds into KRW in any fixed proportion.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Information Reporting System (FEIRS)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Viability: Low. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, the Financial Services Commission restricts deposit-taking and settlement activities to licensed financial institutions. Offshore digital EMIs are not permitted to open Korean domiciled settlement accounts, and they are legally barred from acting as the settlement institution for KRW-denominated corporate card flows. A Yuhan Hoesa must maintain its primary treasury accounts with a licensed Korean commercial bank; offshore EMIs can only function as card program managers, never as custodian of corporate funds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: KRW 30,000,000,000 (USD equivalent) of combined intercompany transactions with foreign related parties triggers mandatory Local File; group-wide consolidated turnover exceeding KRW 100,000,000,000 triggers Master File and Country-by-Country reporting under Article 111 of the International Tax Coordination Act

      
Economic substance scope: Korea applies the arm's length principle under OECD BEPS Action 10; while Korea has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime, the Korean National Tax Service applies CIGA principles to attribute profit to the entity performing risk-taking, strategic decision-making, and operational card-issuance activities; a shell company with nominee directors and no local head office staff will face denial of treaty benefits and a deemed-dividend adjustment under General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Article 45 of the International Tax Coordination Act)

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion via Transfer Pricing Manipulation
        
Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 12: imprisonment up to 7 years and a fine not exceeding 3 times the evaded tax; additionally, the International Tax Coordination Act Article 49 authorizes the NTS to impose a false-documentation penalty of 5% of the controlled transaction value

        
Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties under Article 11 of the National Tax Collection Act; the NTS may seize director bank accounts and freeze overseas assets; the director's personal passport may be blocked via the 'Restriction on Departure for Tax Evader' mechanism for an initial 6 months, and a criminal sentence of up to 7 years' imprisonment applies to those who sign false financial statements

      

      
      

        AML and Licensed Fintech Breach
        
Statutory sanction: Electronic Financial Transactions Act Article 49: imprisonment up to 7 years and fine up to KRW 300 million; additionally, Act for Reporting and Use of Specific Financial Information Article 12 imposes a fine of up to KRW 200 million for omission of virtual currency or card settlement reports

        
Director personal liability: The Financial Supervisory Service may blacklist the director from serving in any financial company; the director faces personal criminal liability including imprisonment up to 3 years for AML reporting gaps, and civil compensation to cardholders for unauthorized transactions under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act; a travel ban is imposed under Article 38 of the Act on Regulation of Financial Company Supervision

      

      
      

        Shell Company Abuse and Economic Substance Fraud
        
Statutory sanction: International Tax Coordination Act Article 45 applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to disallow all treaty benefits and demand tax on the entire global income at the maximum 24% rate; Corporate Income Tax Act Article 121 requires non-residents to file a branch return; non-compliance triggers a punitive 35% gross tax on deemed distributions; the firm is placed on the NTS 'Fraudulent Delinquent Taxpayer' blacklist which is publicly disclosed

        
Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil under Article 200 of the Civil Procedure Act and Article 11 of the National Tax Collection Act makes directors personally liable for 100% of unpaid tax plus penalties; directors face criminal prosecution under Article 16 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for using a sham corporation, with imprisonment up to 5 years; civil flight-risk measures include impoundment of shares, seizure of director's real estate, and travel ban until the tax debt is fully settled

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. The capital is set in the Articles of Incorporation and must be fully subscribed and paid in before the corporate registration application is filed. The paid-in amount must be deposited into a blocked capital-verification account at a licensed South Korean commercial bank, with the bank issuing a capital verification certificate; funds are released only after corporate registration is completed and a corporate registration certificate is obtained. Non-resident shareholders may contribute in KRW or permitted foreign currency after completing the required foreign exchange declaration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea - HomeTax e-Services portal at https://www.hometax.go.kr; Korea Customs Service - UNI-PASS portal at https://www.unipass.customs.go.kr for export declarations and clearance

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Korean Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) obtains its TIN and VAT registration in one combined procedure. Within 20 days after commencement of business, file the statutory Business Registration Application with the competent District Tax Office of the National Tax Service having jurisdiction over the registered head office, using HomeTax or in-person submission through a certified tax accountant. The application must be accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, a power of attorney if filed by an agent, proof of the Korean business premises, identification of the domestic representative, and, where applicable, the foreign investment notification confirmation. After review, the District Tax Office issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (TIN), which is simultaneously the VAT registration number and the corporate income tax reference number. A foreign non-resident entity with no Korean establishment that supplies electronic services to Korean end customers must appoint a domestic tax representative and file the statutory business registration application for foreign electronic service suppliers through HomeTax; NTS issues a foreign VAT identification number after validation. All VAT registrants must issue electronic tax invoices through HomeTax from the effective registration date.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold is applied to domestic business operators; VAT registration is mandatory upon business commencement. Simplified taxable status applies to businesses with annual taxable supplies below KRW 80,000,000, but it is a reporting status rather than a registration exemption. Foreign non-resident suppliers of electronic services with no Korean establishment must register under the NTS foreign-supplier registration path when supplying electronic services to Korean consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Korean VAT Act for qualifying exports of enterprise SaaS when the service is supplied to a non-resident business customer, the service is actually used or consumed outside Korea, and the Korean exporter receives consideration from abroad in a convertible foreign currency. The exporter must retain the service contract, offshore delivery and usage evidence, and foreign exchange remittance documentation. For pure cloud-delivered SaaS, no physical customs movement is required, but the offshore service and foreign-currency collection evidence must be complete.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) accepted through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system for any physical goods exported
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing outward movement of physical media or hardware, if applicable
  • Commercial invoice and written service agreement with the non-resident customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Foreign Exchange Settlement Record issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Service delivery and offshore usage records, such as cloud access logs, license agreement, or usage report, showing the SaaS is consumed outside Korea

Korean VAT law mandates issuance of an electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) through HomeTax for B2B and export transactions. Registered suppliers transmit invoice data in real time to NTS via the HomeTax web service, NTS-certified API, or government-approved EDI, and each successful transmission receives an NTS reference number. The regime uses real-time filing and cross-matching with VAT returns rather than a pre-approval digital stamp; anti-fraud controls include automated abnormal-transaction detection and tax invoice audits.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to NTS export VAT review and tax invoice matching. If the customs, foreign-exchange, or offshore-usage evidence is missing, unverifiable, or false, NTS retroactively reclassifies the supply as taxable Korean supply, assesses VAT at 10% on the full invoice value, and imposes statutory additional tax of 20% for negligent under-declaration or 40% for fraudulent under-declaration, plus late-payment interest. Criminal penalties under the Korean tax crime punishment statutes can include imprisonment and fines, and the Framework Act on National Taxes permits retroactive reassessment for the fraudulent periods. Foreign-exchange repatriation violations may also draw penalties under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized foreign exchange system anchored in the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account transactions are freely convertible for cross-border payments of services, software license fees, royalties, and cost reimbursements, requiring only routine reporting through a designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment in the Korean LLC, are permitted under a positive-notification regime: an inward direct investment report must be filed with KOTRA or the designated bank before or within 30 days of remitting equity. Profit remittances, dividends, and liquidation proceeds are freely transferable after corporate tax settlement and documentary proof of the final beneficial owner's foreign residence, although the bank will require confirmation that no Korean tax arrears exist. KRW is subject to convertibility restrictions for non-resident bank accounts; non-residents cannot open won accounts solely for speculative trading, and our entity will need a permitted purpose such as payroll, vendor payments, or dividend reserve. The authorities may also verify that the SaaS transactions do not represent disguised cross-border lending or unlicensed money transmission. Overall, the regime is not a strict surrender currency system, but the entity must retain a professional transaction history and comply with the electronic reporting requirements under the Foreign Exchange Information Network to avoid lawful administrative penalties or currency clearance delays.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds must be collected within 18 months from the export declaration date; there is no statutory percentage that must be converted into KRW. All proceeds must be credited to a designated foreign exchange account at a domestic bank and a certificate of export receipt must be lodged electronically with the bank. If collection exceeds 18 months, a notice of delay and prior approval schedule must be submitted to the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central monetary authority, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the designated foreign exchange regulator under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; daily administration delegated to designated foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

EMI cross-border viability is legally constrained. Because the Korean Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic resident entity, global fintech EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex or Stripe Treasury are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Korea under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. They cannot offer a legally recognised KRW IBAN, won settlement relationship, or cross-border clearing access to the Korean interbank system without a licensed banking partner. A foreign EMI may act as an ancillary collection agent only if the payment is initiated outside Korea, but that does not apply to revenue collected from Korean enterprise customers. Therefore, fintech EMIs are rated Low as a treasury anchor; their permitted role is limited to supporting small-value overseas market expenses, and all critical liquidity must be held through the Tier-1 domestic commercial banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion (approximately USD 7.5 million) of aggregate cross-border transactions with foreign related parties in a fiscal year triggers mandatory contemporaneous Master File and Local File preparation; multi-national groups with consolidated annual revenue above KRW 100 billion are also required to file a Country-by-Country Report (CbCR).

Economic substance scope: Korea applies a broad substance-over-form doctrine under the Framework Act on Taxes, the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, and the general anti-abuse rule. Although Korea does not apply formal UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, the NTS will disregard or re-characterize transactions that lack commercial substance and valuable economic effect. A foreign-owned Korean SaaS entity must maintain real board meetings, substantive employment, office premises, servers or infrastructure in Korea, and genuine risk-management functions in Korea. For treaty relief and transfer pricing defense, the overseas related party must satisfy a CIGA-like test by having actual employees, premises, and decision-making authority over the relevant intangible asset and investment functions; a shell company without real activity will be denied treaty benefits and may trigger penalty assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Articles 3 and 9: imprisonment for up to five years and a fine not exceeding five times the evaded corporate income tax; additionally under Framework Act on Taxes Article 47-3, fraudulent concealment attracts a 60% additional tax penalty on the underreported amount, plus daily interest and corrective assessments.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under Korean Commercial Act Article 401(2): directors who intentionally or grossly negligently perform their duties and cause harm to the tax authority or third parties are jointly and severally liable to compensate the state for unpaid taxes, penalties, and costs. NTS may impose travel bans (blacklisting) and issue compulsory investigation referrals for criminal contempt; the responsible director can be arrested and imprisoned for up to five years.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse (DTA Fraud)

Statutory sanction: Under Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 6(2) and the general anti-abuse rule, the NTS may deny the DTA benefit and re-impose the full 22% withholding tax on the entire distribution, plus interest charges; the Framework Act on Taxes Articles 15 and 42 support re-characterizing the shell as transparent or invalid, effectively making the Korean entity liable for the shortfall and civil fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or directed the treaty-abusive shell structure are jointly and severally liable for the shortfall in withholding tax and penalties under Commercial Act Article 401(2). NTS will blacklist the directors, impose travel restrictions, and may prosecute them for aiding tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, carrying imprisonment up to five years and personal monetary restitution of all mispaid treaty benefits.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 4 requires the NTS to re-determine the arm's length price and adjust the Korean taxable income accordingly; the additional tax will bear an unfaithful-return penalty of 40%-60% under Framework Act on Taxes Article 47-2 and 47-3, plus interest and daily unpaid-tax surcharges. Massive transfer pricing adjustments may also trigger referral to the Tax Tribunal and criminal investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorized non-arm's length pricing are personally liable under Korean Commercial Act Article 401(2) for the resulting unpaid corporate tax and penalty amounts; the NTS can attach directors' personal assets and impose a travel ban (blacklisting) pending compliance. In cases of willful falsification of transfer pricing documentation, the director faces criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with imprisonment of up to five years and fines up to five times the manipulated tax amount.

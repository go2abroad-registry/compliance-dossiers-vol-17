Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-in capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa. The full subscribed capital must nevertheless be physically deposited into a temporary escrow bank account opened in the company's name or in an incorporator's verified escrow account before filing. A Certificate of Capital Deposit issued by the bank, bearing the branch manager's seal, must accompany the registration application. A practical initial capital of KRW 10,000,000 is recommended to facilitate opening business bank accounts and executing rail-freight agency contracts. For non-resident shareholders, the capital remittance must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before corporate bank accounts are activated.

Funding & unlock path: The company must obtain a Fire Safety Certificate for Business Premises from the local fire station for its registered office, confirming acceptable fire exits, extinguishing equipment, electrical wiring, and occupancy load under the Fire Services Act. Civil Defense clearance is limited to the standard premises inspection because no chemical, explosive, or hazardous-material storage is involved. If a warehouse or intermodal terminal is later operated, a more detailed civil-defense fire-defense facility permit under the Fire Services Act and an occupational safety approval under the Occupational Safety and Health Act will become mandatory before operations commence.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS); online electronic administration via HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service (KCS) via the UNI-PASS single window for customs clearance and export declaration processing.

E-government filing portal

A Korea-resident Yuhan Hoesa must first complete court registration under the Commercial Act, then file a business registration application with the local district tax office within 20 days after business commencement; the NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Certificate number used simultaneously as the corporate tax identification number and the VAT identification number. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Korean place of business, the entity must appoint a Korean tax representative and submit a foreign business operator business registration application through HomeTax or the competent district tax office, attaching a certificate of incorporation or legal existence, articles of incorporation, a board resolution appointing the tax representative, and a power of attorney. The NTS issues a foreign business operator Business Registration Number in a separate series; the same number is used for electronic tax invoice issuance, VAT filings, and VAT refund claims. A foreign non-resident electronic-service supplier may complete the simplified registration procedure in HomeTax without a monetary threshold. Without registration, a foreign entity cannot issue zero-rated VAT invoices or recover input VAT and remains subject to Korean-source withholding taxes where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide a check-the-box election; a domestically incorporated Yuhan Hoesa is automatically a domestic corporation taxable at entity level on worldwide income. The only relevant group election is the consolidated corporate income tax filing election for wholly owned domestic parent and subsidiary groups, which does not alter individual entity classification. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold. Any person commencing a taxable Korean business must register within 20 days; registration is mandatory without regard to turnover. A foreign non-resident supplier of electronic services is obligated to register for Korean VAT when it begins supplying electronic services to Korean consumers, also without a monetary threshold. Simplified taxable person status is limited to qualifying individual sole proprietors whose annual VATable turnover does not exceed KRW 80,000,000 and is not available to Yuhan Hoesa as a corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods that are physically exported from Korea with an accepted Korea Customs Service export declaration, sold to a foreign purchaser, and settled in foreign exchange. Zero-rating also applies to exported services supplied to non-residents and paid in foreign currency where the place of supply is outside Korea, including international freight transport and international freight forwarding services such as transcontinental container block train and intermodal rail expeditions that carry cargo from Korea to foreign destinations. The supplier must issue a zero-rate electronic tax invoice and retain evidential title files showing the goods crossed the Korean border, the freight service was used outside Korea, and the foreign currency consideration was collected. Qualifying Korean supplies of digital services to non-residents may similarly be zero-rated if the export service and foreign exchange settlement conditions are met. If any condition is not met, the supply is subject to 10% Korean VAT.

  • KCS accepted Customs Export Declaration (export declaration or export manifest filed and confirmed through UNI-PASS)
  • Commercial invoice covering the exported containers and freight services
  • Negotiable Bill of Lading, multimodal transport bill, or CIM/SMGS rail waybill evidencing the intercontinental block train movement from Korea to the foreign destination
  • Packing list and container seal records for the rail intermodal shipment
  • Contract for freight forwarding/logistics services with the non-resident customer and, if used, the letter of credit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or foreign exchange bank settlement certificate evidencing actual collection of foreign currency proceeds
  • Korea Customs Service or NTS export performance certificate if required on audit
  • Copy of the zero-rated electronic tax invoice transmitted through HomeTax

Korea requires electronic tax invoicing for all corporate suppliers and for all tax invoices issued between registered VAT traders. Electronic tax invoices must be issued under the NTS HomeTax e-invoice system or using an NTS-certified electronic tax invoice API; data is transmitted to the NTS no later than the day after issuance and is cross-matched against the purchaser's VAT return. Korea does not operate a mandatory pre-clearance anti-fraud digital stamping system. For B2C supplies made by foreign electronic service providers using simplified registration, the NTS-administered reporting system is used in lieu of full electronic tax invoice issuance where required.

The NTS verifies zero-rated export claims by matching UNI-PASS customs declarations, HomeTax zero-rate e-invoice data, foreign exchange settlement records from banks, and the exporter's title documents. If foreign exchange proceeds are not collected within the applicable statutory settlement period, if the export declaration is rejected or matched to false documents, or if the supporting pack is incomplete, NTS will reverse the zero-rating, assess 10% VAT on the supply, impose statutory additional taxes for non-payment or fraudulent misdeclaration, claw back any input VAT already refunded, and charge daily late-payment interest. Fabricated export declarations, counterfeit FIRC certificates, or knowingly false e-invoices may additionally lead to criminal tax fraud penalties, Customs Act penalties, and director liability for the freight forwarder.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberal but monitored foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions, including cross-border freight invoices, operating expenses, and dividends, subject only to a post-transaction reporting obligation to the designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, such as equity injections by foreign shareholders and loan repayments, are subject to prior declaration or report, but standard inbound FDI is not restricted for entities in the freight and logistics sector. There is no surrender requirement for export earnings or service receipts; companies can retain foreign currency in designated accounts. The practical friction lies not in conversion itself but in the documentation and purpose-code validation required by the banks. For profit remittance abroad, the company must prove that corporate tax obligations have been settled by presenting a certificate of tax clearance, and the remittance must be declared under the appropriate purpose code. In emergency situations the authorities may impose temporary conversion limits, but such measures have not been active since the Asian Financial Crisis. Thus, for a rail freight forwarding entity, the treasury should expect routine monitoring rather than discretionary approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds from rail freight and intermodal services must be received within the statutory settlement period (generally two years from exportation, unless extended). Upon receipt, the company must report the inflow to the appointed foreign exchange bank within 15 business days. There is no mandatory conversion of foreign currency into Korean won; the proceeds may be kept in a corporate foreign currency account (e.g., KRW, USD, EUR). If the proceeds remain outstanding beyond the settlement due date, the company must file a delayed receipt report and provide evidence of enforceable receivables. The term 'surrender' is not applied in Korea; effective controls are achieved via reporting thresholds and periodic banking supervision.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of the Republic of Korea) and the Foreign Exchange Information Network managed by the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, or similar are legally disqualified from functioning as the primary banking counterparty for a Korean-domiciled limited liability company. The Banking Act of Korea restricts the acceptance of deposits and the issuance of trade-level outward payments to locally licensed banks. EMIs can only operate by piggybacking on a licensed Korean bank through wrapper cards or local partner accounts, but they cannot provide the essential documentary permissions (Foreign Exchange Information Network reporting, capital verification reports, or customs-related trade remittance processing). For a low-risk freight forwarding entity, an EMI can at most serve as an auxiliary low-value payment tool for refunds or micro-disbursements, but only after the primary treasury chain is kept within a tier-1 Korean bank. Overall viability: Very Limited - requires a sponsor bank, and should not be considered for FX hedging or regulatory compliance.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, or similar are legally disqualified from functioning as the primary banking counterparty for a Korean-domiciled limited liability company. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% for the 2025 Korean CIT rate schedule (plus a 10% local income tax surcharge, giving a combined marginal headline rate of 26.4%). The rate schedule is: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% on KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; and 24% on taxable income above KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% outbound dividend withholding tax for a Korean corporation paying dividends to a non-resident shareholder, composed of 20% national corporate withholding under the Corporate Tax Law plus a 2% local income tax surcharge. The domestic rate is reduced by an applicable bilateral double tax treaty, normally to 15% for portfolio dividends and to 5% for a corporate shareholder owning at least 25% of the voting shares for a continuous holding period of not less than six months (subject to specific treaty wording). If the beneficial owner qualifies as a foreign government or treaty-protected institution, a complete exemption can apply under selected treaties.

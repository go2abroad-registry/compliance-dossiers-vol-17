Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, electronically via the HomeTax portal at www.hometax.go.kr

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Korean Yuhan Hoesa must first complete incorporation under the Korean Commercial Act and obtain a domestic place of business. The LLC representative (or authorized resident tax representative) must then apply through the HomeTax portal or in person at the competent district tax office for a Business Registration (Sa-eop-ja Deung-nok). The application requires the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, lease or ownership proof for the domestic business premises, representative identification/power of attorney, bank account details, and initial capital documents. NTS issues a single 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) that simultaneously operates as the VAT registration number; the applicant is registered as a standard taxable person from the date of business commencement. For a foreign company without a domestic place of business, no domestic VAT registration is required; Korean counterparties self-assess VAT and resolve TIN obligations as a non-resident seller. The Korean LLC is, however, a resident taxpayer and must obtain the BRN within 20 days of business commencement.

Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No registration exemption threshold in KRW; every supplier of goods or services is required to register before or within 20 days from commencement of business. A simplified taxpayer threshold of KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable supplies exists only for certain unincorporated small businesses; a data-center colocation LLC is a standard taxable VAT registrant.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, the supply of exported goods and certain exported services/offshore services may be zero-rated. For data-center colocation and bare-metal hosting, zero-rating is available only if the service is contractually provided to a foreign recipient and the place of use or performance is conclusively outside Korea or the supply qualifies as an exported service under the Enforcement Decree. If the server/rack space or hosting service is performed or consumed within Korean territory for a foreign customer, the supply is generally treated as a domestic taxable supply at 10% VAT. The taxpayer must separately document and establish cross-border dispatch/delivery or offshore consumption.

The electronic tax invoice (ETI) is mandatory for tax invoices issued by registered business operators. Invoices are issued through the NTS HomeTax electronic invoice system, using an NTS-issued digital certificate or a registered tax processing/API connection. The ETI must be transmitted to the NTS without additional third-party certification; there is no state-operated real-time VAT clearance, fiscal stamp, or continuous transaction control anti-fraud stamp in Korea. NTS and Korea Customs Service may jointly audit zero-rated export claims. If the required customs clearance or foreign-exchange receipt documentation is missing, incomplete, or inconsistent, the claimed zero rate is denied and the supply is assessed at 10% VAT, plus penalty tax and daily interest by overdue period. Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea operates under a nominally liberalized but closely monitored current account regime. Current account transactions such as service fees, data center leasing receivables, trade payments, and ordinary operating settlements are permitted without prior approval provided that genuine underlying documents exist and the settlement is effected through a domestic licensed foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, however, are subject to declaration, ex-ante report, or authorization depending on the category. The Korean won is not a fully freely convertible offshore currency; non-residents cannot maintain won-denominated accounts outside the licensed domestic banking system, and offshore KRW activity is heavily constrained. Foreign direct investment into a Korean Yuhan Hoesa requires a foreign investment report through MOEF/KOTRA or a designated domestic bank, followed by the opening of a temporary foreign currency deposit account and capital verification. Profit and dividend remittance is treated as a current account transaction once the relevant withholding tax, audited financial statements, and board approval are filed; however, banks will demand the foreign investment registration certificate and will verify that the funds were sourced from retained earnings or recognized capital surplus. Export proceeds must be repatriated through licensed domestic banks within prescribed collection deadlines, while capital remittances outside the originally declared purpose will be blocked by FEIS reporting. From a treasury standpoint, the KRW is convertible for underlying trade and FDI purposes but not as a free offshore deliverable currency; all material FX activity must flow through domestic banks and must be tagged with valid FEIS purpose codes.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds from goods and services must be received into a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank account within the statutory collection period, generally 18 months from the date of export declaration, with extension possible only through prescribed MOEF/BOK procedures. There is no mandatory fixed percentage surrender to the won in the current Korean regime; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a permissible foreign currency account or convert to KRW through a licensed FX bank. Nevertheless, any foreign currency not needed for approved foreign-currency working capital should be converted promptly under prudent treasury policy, and every receipt must be supported by the export invoice, service contract, and FEIS declaration referencing the colocation or leasing arrangement.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with all reporting executed through licensed foreign exchange banks and the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Offshore fintech EMIs have minimal viability in South Korea. They are legally barred from opening KRW accounts for domestic corporate entities and from executing foreign exchange declarations under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. An EMI cannot function as the primary operating or treasury bank for a Korean Yuhan Hoesa. Any use of an offshore EMI would be limited to non-KRW auxiliary transactions, always through a licensed domestic Korean bank, and would still be subject to Korean tax, customs, and FX reporting overrides. The only compliant treasury architecture is a tier-1 Korean bank onboarding model.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Nominee shareholder or multi-layer non-resident ownership structure obscuring the ultimate beneficial owner of the Korean LLC.
        
Root cause: Nominee shareholder or multi-layer non-resident ownership structure obscuring the ultimate beneficial owner of the Korean LLC.

        
Defense evidence: Apostilled and certified corporate register, full share register, UBO declaration form, ownership chain diagram to the ultimate natural persons, copies of passports for every UBO holding 25% or more, tax residency certificates for all shareholders, notarized board resolution of the ultimate parent, audited financial statements of the direct and ultimate parent, and a signed undertaking confirming no nominee arrangements or bearer shares.

      

      
      

        Inbound capital injection or three-party remittance from a jurisdiction or account unrelated to the declared colocation business, creating suspicion of money-laundering layering.
        
Root cause: Inbound capital injection or three-party remittance from a jurisdiction or account unrelated to the declared colocation business, creating suspicion of money-laundering layering.

        
Defense evidence: Twelve months of bank statements from the remitting entity, signed third-party payment declaration explaining the economic rationale, source of wealth letter, source of funds file to the original wealth generation, commercial contract between the remitter and the Korean LLC, sanctions and PEP screening certificates from an internationally recognized screening provider covering UN/EU/OFAC/Korean designations, and legal entity identifier (LEI) documents where available.

      

      
      

        Business profile inconsistency between high-frequency colocation leasing receipts and the absence of verifiable physical infrastructure, staff, or tenant contracts.
        
Root cause: Business profile inconsistency between high-frequency colocation leasing receipts and the absence of verifiable physical infrastructure, staff, or tenant contracts.

        
Defense evidence: Executed colocation service agreements for each tenant, rack and power allocation schedule, service level agreement schedule, facility lease with utility invoices, Tier-3/4 certification documentation, server and networking equipment invoices, depreciation schedule, payroll records, and a detailed projected cash-flow waterfall showing monthly ACH and SWIFT incoming receipts against operating expenditures.

      

      
      

        Foreign shareholder funding or shareholder loan is disproportionate to the company's stated capital and operating scale, creating an unexplained equity risk.
        
Root cause: Foreign shareholder funding or shareholder loan is disproportionate to the company's stated capital and operating scale, creating an unexplained equity risk.

        
Defense evidence: MOEF/KOTRA foreign investment report, FEIS declaration number, licensed FX bank capital verification certificate, board resolution authorizing the capital increase or shareholder loan, investor audited financial statements and solvency evidence, Korean tax clearance certificate, the executed shareholder loan agreement with approved interest rate, and an independent valuation of the colocation equipment or facility if the loan is linked to asset acquisition.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The foreign investor must file the foreign investment report with KOTRA/MOEF or a designated foreign exchange bank prior to remittance. After approval, the investor or the Korean LLC opens a temporary foreign currency deposit account at a tier-1 Korean bank and receives the capital via SWIFT MT103, clearly identifying the investor name, investor registration number, transaction purpose, and FEIS purpose code for direct investment equity subscription. Upon receipt, the bank releases the foreign exchange declaration into FEIS, the LLC completes the foreign investment company registration, and the bank issues a capital verification certificate confirming the paid-in capital. Only then may the full amount be converted to KRW and transferred to the operational account. No part of the capital injection may be commingled with operating revenue, and any deviation from the declared purpose requires a post-change report to the MOEF.Outbound disbursements must be executed through the licensed Korean bank using SWIFT MT103 for services, colocation lease payments, licensing fees, and supplier settlements, always with the underlying contract, tax invoice, and FEIS purpose code. Imported server hardware and data center equipment may be settled through Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700 with reimbursement via MT103/MT202) or Documentary Collections (DP/DA using SWIFT MT400/MT410) when the bank relationship and LC terms support it. Dividend, royalty, and interest remittances require board approval, withholding tax payment proof, audited financials, and FEIS capital transaction filings.Deliverable FX forwards with a documented underlying colocation, trade, or service transaction, executed only with licensed onshore Korean banks and limited to genuine exposure amounts.Korean won non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only where permitted by MOEF/BOK position limits and executed via a licensed onshore bank against a proven real trade or capital transaction exposure, never on a naked speculative basis.Currency swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps permitted for eligible underlying capital transactions such as foreign currency shareholder loans or equipment financing, subject to FEIS reporting, board approval, and bank credit/ISDA documentation.FX options with real-demand underlying exposure, used as hedging instruments only, with premiums and notional amounts tied to the actual colocation or trade contract.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
21% top marginal corporate income tax rate (progressive brackets: 9% on the first KRW 200 million; 19% on the next KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% on taxable income above KRW 20 billion), plus a 10% local income tax surtax on the national corporate tax, yielding an aggregate top statutory rate of approximately 23.1% before treaty limitations

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
22% on gross dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders: 20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax; where a bilateral Double Tax Agreement applies, the rate may be reduced to 15%, 10%, or 5% depending on the treaty and the shareholder's ownership percentage and substance

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File and simplified documentation are mandatory for Korean entities whose gross revenue for the fiscal year exceeds KRW 100 billion or whose cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion; a consolidated group with revenue of at least KRW 100 billion must prepare a Master File, and a Country-by-Country Report is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds KRW 1 trillion. These thresholds are reviewed annually by the National Tax Service and should be monitored on a real-time basis for a data center colocation entity due to the high volume of international recharges and equipment lease transactions.

      
Economic substance scope: South Korea applies the substance-over-form principle under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 2-5 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs. Although Korea has not enacted UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, the National Tax Service consistently applies the OECD Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis when determining whether a Korean subsidiary involved in data-center colocation has actual personnel, decision-making functions, operational control, asset management, and risk assumption. A Yuhan Hoesa that merely holds invoices, routes dividends, or invoices rack-space fees without local technical staff, network monitoring, physical security oversight, and independent contract negotiation will fail the substance doctrine and may be denied treaty benefits.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Fictitious Cost Inflation
        
Statutory sanction: A 20% to 30% negligence tax surcharge on the understated tax amount under Article 47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, plus late-payment daily interest charges. Criminal prosecution under Article 10 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act and Article 13 of the Act on Aggravated Punishment for Specific Crimes applies where the evaded tax exceeds statutory thresholds, carrying imprisonment of up to ten years and fines of up to five times the tax evaded.

        
Director personal liability: Where the corporate form is used as a sham to evade taxes, a court may pierce the corporate veil and hold the representative director and managing directors jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and interest. Directors who directly prepared false books may be personally blacklisted by the National Tax Service, subject to a travel ban, have assets attached, and be sentenced to criminal imprisonment. The companies law also permits civil creditors and minority shareholders to sue directors for compensatory damages arising from intentional or negligent compliance failures.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing and Arm's Length Violation
        
Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service is entitled to adjust taxable income to the arm's length result under Article 5 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs and impose an additional 20% to 30% unfaithful-reporting negligence tax on the adjusted under-reported income. Failure to submit transfer pricing documentation triggers separate administrative penalties, and the NTS may apply penalties of up to 100% of the tax difference in aggravated non-compliance situations.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who approve or execute the false intercompany pricing scheme are jointly liable for restitution of the tax gain when the company is unable to pay after the adjustment. In egregious cases, a court may disregard the limited liability of the Yuhan Hoesa and hold the directors personally liable for the entire adjusted tax, penalties, and interest. Travel restrictions, asset seizures, and civil damages actions are routinely combined with criminal charges for violation of the International Tax Adjustment Act and fraud under the Korean Criminal Act, leading to imprisonment terms of up to five years.

      

      
      

        Treaty Shopping, Shell Ownership and False Beneficial Owner Representation
        
Statutory sanction: The tax authority will deny treaty benefits under Article 2-5 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs and the domestic General Anti-Avoidance Rule. The company must pay the full statutory 22% withholding tax on all distributions, together with a 20% negligence penalty for non-withholding, daily overdue charges, and interest. Submitting false beneficial ownership declarations constitutes tax fraud and deception, which triggers criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, including imprisonment up to three years or fines up to five times the tax evaded.

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the Korean entity is operated as a conduit with no genuine business purpose. Each director who signed or knowingly facilitated the false treaty application is personally liable for the un-withheld tax and penalties if the company becomes insolvent. The NTS will impose individual blacklisting and a travel ban for responsible directors, and the courts may enter personal civil restitution judgments for the full amount of tax advantage obtained. Criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment, disqualification from serving as a director in Korea, and deportation if the director is a non-resident.

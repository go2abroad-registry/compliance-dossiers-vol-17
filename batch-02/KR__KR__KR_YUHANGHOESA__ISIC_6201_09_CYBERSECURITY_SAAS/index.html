Governing statute

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. A Yuhan Hoesa has no statutory minimum paid-in capital and no blocked escrow account requirement. The total stated capital must be specified in the Articles of Incorporation, and each member's subscribed contribution must be fully paid before the incorporation registration is accepted: cash contributions must be deposited into a bank account in the name of the company under incorporation, and non-cash contributions must be fully transferred and evidenced by an independent valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for the digital SaaS activity. If the Company later leases physical office premises, the building owner must supply the current building fire safety certification, and the Company must complete the workplace safety and health management duties under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including accident prevention plans and employee safety training. No Civil Defense permit is triggered by an office-based information security company.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with electronic tax administration via the HomeTax portal (www.hometax.go.kr) and administrative support from the local district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered place of business.

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Yuhan Hoesa LLC, the company's representative files a business registration application with the competent district tax office after incorporation. Supporting documents include the articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation, registered address or lease agreement, and owner's identification. The tax office issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number that simultaneously constitutes the corporation's TIN and VAT registration ID. For a foreign non-resident entity with no Korean domestic place of business that supplies digital services or other taxable goods or services in Korea, the foreign entity must appoint a Korean tax representative and register through the NTS HomeTax portal under the special simplified foreign business registration procedure; the issued foreign business registration number is then used for VAT filings, zero-rated export substantiation, and electronic tax invoices. Changes in business status or registered place of business must be updated on HomeTax within the statutory period.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Yuhan Hoesa is a Korean resident domestic corporation liable to corporate income tax on worldwide income and is not eligible for pass-through taxation.

Available election forms

None under Korean check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election rules. A qualifying domestic parent corporation may apply for group taxation with its wholly-owned Korean subsidiaries subject to NTS approval under the statutory consolidated filing conditions, but this is an entity-level filing regime rather than a pass-through election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based VAT registration threshold applies to Korean VAT. Every supplier carrying on business in Korea by selling goods or services must register with the NTS. A foreign supplier of digital services, SaaS, or other taxable supplies to Korean non-registered recipients has a mandatory VAT registration requirement from the first Korean supply; no low-value exemption or KRW-turnover threshold is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the VAT Act for goods that are physically exported from Korea and for certain services supplied to a non-resident where the place of supply is outside Korea. For the qualifying cybersecurity SaaS product, the statutory zero-rating criteria are: the recipient must be a non-resident customer, the SaaS services must be consumed or used outside Korea, the contract and invoices must identify the offshore recipient, the payment must be received through foreign-exchange-recognized settlement channels, and contemporaneous evidence must prove that the service was supplied outside Korea. Digital services supplied to a resident Korean non-registered customer are taxable at 10% and do not qualify for zero-rating.

  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Final commercial invoice and international sales contract or service agreement
  • Foreign exchange transaction proof and bank SWIFT remittance records
  • Service delivery and offshore-use evidence for SaaS such as customer IP addresses, geo-location logs, server records, and statement of work documentation
  • NTS e-tax invoice issued at zero percent for the qualifying export supply

All VAT-registered businesses must issue a tax invoice for each taxable supply, and electronic tax invoices must be issued through the NTS HomeTax e-tax invoice system. E-tax invoice data are transmitted to the NTS in real time upon issuance for every taxable supply, and the NTS operates a mandatory e-tax invoice validation and matching anti-fraud control. High-volume taxpayers and platform operators may transmit invoices through NTS-required API integrations or licensed e-tax invoice service providers, but every invoice must carry the valid NTS e-tax invoice number and the issuer and recipient Business Registration Numbers. Korea does not operate a legacy fiscal stamp clearance mechanism; the real-time electronic tax invoice transmission itself is the statutory anti-fraud digital clearance mechanism. Foreign electronic service suppliers registered under the simplified foreign business VAT registration must also issue electronic tax invoices through HomeTax.

The NTS applies intensive audit review to zero-rated export claims by cross-matching customs declarations, foreign exchange settlement records, e-tax invoices, and service delivery documentation. If any required export document is missing, inaccurate, or inconsistent, the zero-rated treatment is disallowed and the full 10% VAT is assessed with statutory interest and penalty taxes. Fraudulent, forged, or intentionally manipulated export documents are treated as unlawful tax refund claims and tax evasion, leading to additional penalty assessments, retrospective clawback, criminal prosecution, and fines or imprisonment under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. The statutory collection period is five years generally, extended to ten years for intentional or fraudulent tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but monitored convertible-currency regime. The won is fully convertible for current-account transactions such as import settlements, royalty payments, and software-as-a-service subscription fees, where authorization is effectively automatic once the underlying commercial contract and invoice are submitted to a licensed foreign exchange bank. Capital-account transactions, including non-resident equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, and repatriation of capital gains or loan principal, require ex ante or ex post reporting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; in practice, inbound foreign direct investment is safeguarded by ex post verification rather than licensing, provided the foreign investment is reported within three months of capital inflow. Profit and dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is permissible after corporate tax settlement, supported by audited financial statements, board dividend resolution, and a foreign exchange purchase record at a domestic bank. However, practical hurdles remain: financial institutions expect documentary proof of the underlying business purpose, source of funds for the capital injection, and consistency between the declared ISIC 6201 cybersecurity SaaS activities and the payment flows. Cross-border settlement must be routed through a bank possessing a foreign exchange dealing license; unauthorized electronic money institutions or overseas payment firms cannot provide permanent corporate treasury settlement as primary bank accounts. Overall, convertibility is high for compliant current-account flows, while capital flows remain subject to detailed documentation and reporting discipline.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Bank of Korea's Foreign Exchange Regulations, export proceeds must be received and settled within the statutory receipt period prescribed by the BOK for the relevant export category, with the general expectation that proceeds are recovered within the timeline stated in the export declaration and subject to extension only for documented cause. There is no blanket mandatory percentage conversion into Korean won; the exporter may hold foreign currency in a domestic foreign-currency account, but the receipt must be reported to the licensed foreign exchange bank via the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) without undue delay, and any conversion into won must be executed through a domestic bank with a foreign exchange dealing license. For the Yuhan Hoesa cybersecurity SaaS model, export receipts consist largely of subscription fees which are treated as service export proceeds and are not subject to compulsory surrender ratios; the treasury must nevertheless maintain a perfect reconciliation file between each inbound SWIFT credit and the corresponding export invoice in order to clear FEIS reporting and bank compliance review.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central monetary and exchange-rate authority, with foreign exchange policy administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance and executed under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act through licensed foreign exchange banks reporting to the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility is low for offshore fintech EMIs as the primary treasury settlement rail. Under the Banking Act, only licensed banks may accept KRW deposits; under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, money transfer services beyond payment collection require registration as licensed e-money issuers or payment gateways and are not permitted to maintain corporate operating accounts or provide foreign exchange sweeping services without a foreign exchange dealing license. Cross-border EMIs such as Wise or Payoneer can be used narrowly as invoice collection agents or disbursement tools in external markets, but they lack local branch presence and cannot discharge Korean statutory capital verification, FEIS reports, or bank-level KYC obligations. Consequently, the determining rail must be a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank, with EMIs limited to ancillary functions such as collecting overseas SaaS subscription proceeds from non-Korean clients, provided the flows are separately reconciled and justified under the entity's treasury policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The purpose of payment should be stated as equity investment in Korean limited liability company (Yuhan Hoesa) with reference to the Foreign Investment Report. After receipt, the investor must submit the capital injection to the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) through the bank within the statutory reporting window, generally three months for inward foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national headline rate, plus 10% local income tax surtax on the national corporate tax, giving an aggregate maximum effective rate of 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% for cash dividends paid by a Korean resident corporation to a non-resident corporation (20% national withholding plus 2% local income tax surtax).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after the fiscal year-end; a calendar-year entity files by March 31. External-audit companies may extend by one month to April 30. If the ordinary or extended deadline falls on a weekend, filing is timely on the next business day.

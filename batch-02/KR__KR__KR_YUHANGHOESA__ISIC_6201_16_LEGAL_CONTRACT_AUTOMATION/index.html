Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or blocked escrow account requirement is imposed by the Korean Commercial Act. The Articles of Incorporation must state the total capital and each member's contribution; all contributions must be subscribed and paid in before or at incorporation in a Korean bank or a designated foreign exchange bank. In-kind contributions must be fully delivered and, where required, supported by valuation or court inspection evidence. For a nonresident shareholder, inward remittance should be reported through a Foreign Exchange Bank, and if the investment is intended to be recognized as foreign direct investment, a Foreign Investment Promotion Act notification is required. There is no escrow amount blocked with the commercial registry; funds become available to the company after commercial registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea, acting through the HomeTax electronic tax administration portal (www.hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service (KCS) operates the UNI-PASS electronic customs clearance system for export declarations.

E-government filing portal

For a Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) incorporated in South Korea, the foreign non-resident entity or its domestic tax representative must apply to the district tax office under the NTS for a Business Registration Certificate (Business Registration Number, 10 digits) using the statutory Application for Business Registration, within 20 days after the business commences. The application must be filed with: (1) certified articles of incorporation and corporate certificate demonstrating completed incorporation registration; (2) evidence of the domestic place of business, typically the lease agreement or ownership certificate; (3) certified Korean-language translation of the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation or equivalent proof of legal existence, apostilled/consularized if required; (4) photographic identification and passport copy of the foreign legal representative; (5) a power of attorney appointing a licensed tax accountant (sesamusa) or a domestic tax representative where the foreign entity has no resident officer; and (6) any banking due diligence documents evidencing the initial capital contribution if the entity maintains a corporate bank account. The NTS issues the Business Registration Number through the HomeTax portal, and that number functions simultaneously as the national Tax Identification Number for corporate income tax, the VAT registration ID, and the electronic tax invoice identifier. Foreign non-resident entities without a domestic place of business that supply electronic services or other Korean-source supplies are required to complete the simplified VAT registration procedure available to foreign business operators through the HomeTax portal and must appoint a domestic tax representative for VAT and corporate income tax compliance; the NTS will issue a separate non-resident tax registration number rather than a domestic Business Registration Certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic Korean corporation taxed at the entity level on worldwide income under the Korean Corporate Income Tax Act and local income tax acts; pass-through treatment is not available under Korean law. The entity is also an independent VAT taxable person subject to the Value-Added Tax Act.

Available election forms

None for entity classification - Korea does not operate any check-the-box or comparable elective entity-classification regime. A Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily classified as a domestic corporation. The only relevant election is the Korean consolidated corporate tax return regime, available under Articles 76-2 to 76-8 of the Corporate Income Tax Act to a fully owned domestic subsidiary and a qualifying resident parent corporation; this is not a pass-through election. Hybrid mismatch and other anti-avoidance elective mechanisms are governed by the Adjustment of International Taxes Act and do not permit a taxpayer to change its Korean tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW; there is no general registration exemption threshold. Every supplier making taxable supplies of goods or services in South Korea must register for VAT. A simplified taxable person computation scheme is available to small businesses with aggregate annual turnover or revenue not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, but that is a computational relief and does not eliminate the registration obligation. Foreign non-resident electronic services suppliers making B2C supplies to Korean customers must register under the simplified foreign business operator VAT registration procedure; there is no turnover threshold for that registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for goods exported to foreign countries under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act when the goods are physically cleared from South Korea through the Korea Customs Service and formally exported under a customs export declaration. Zero-rating also applies to services supplied to non-resident recipients where the service is consumed, used, or exploited wholly outside South Korea, and to qualifying international transportation and ocean/air freight services. For export zero-rating to be substantiated, the Korean supplier must be registered for VAT, the transaction must be evidenced by a commercial invoice issued to the foreign purchaser, the goods must depart Korea under KCS customs clearance, and the export proceeds must be repatriated in a lawful foreign-exchange transaction within the statutory period. A Yuhan Hoesa engaged in LegalTech SaaS, cloud document automation, and e-signature services may zero-rate its exported digital subscriptions and electronic-signature services only if it can establish that the non-resident recipient received and used the services outside Korea, invoicing is made to the overseas customer, and the consideration is received through a licensed foreign-exchange bank or permitted payment rail.

  • UNI-PASS Customs Export Declaration Certificate (Export Clearance Report) issued by the Korea Customs Service for each export shipment
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List executed by the Korean exporter and addressed to the foreign buyer
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment of goods from a Korean port/airport to a foreign destination
  • Foreign Currency Inward Remittance Certificate or Export Proceeds Confirmation issued by an authorized foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, showing receipt of export consideration in convertible foreign currency
  • Export Performance Confirmation Certificate issued by the handling foreign exchange bank or Korea Customs Service where required for the export transaction to prove the foreign-exchange earning event

Korea operates a mandatory national electronic tax invoice system administered by NTS through HomeTax and NTS-certified API/EDI service providers. A VAT-registered supplier must issue an electronic tax invoice to the recipient no later than the day following the time of supply and transmit the invoice data to the NTS; the transaction is then centrally validated and cross-matched by the NTS. The system is not a Latin-American-style real-time digital fiscal stamping clearance system, but it imposes mandatory daily transmission and acceptance confirmation. Anti-fraud controls include centralized invoice number audit, recipient confirmation obligations, and penalty surcharges for non-issuance, delayed issuance, missing transmission, or false invoice data. Supplies made through credit card or payment gateways such as Stripe or credit card acquirers additionally generate transaction-level data that NTS may reconcile against VAT returns.

NTS VAT audit teams cross-check export zero-rating refund claims against Korea Customs Service export declarations, bank foreign-exchange receipts, and corporate income tax records. If the required export declaration, shipping documents, or foreign-exchange repatriation evidence are missing, unverified, or fraudulent, the zero-rating is rejected and the 10% VAT is assessed retroactively on the export supply, with interest and late-payment surcharges. The NTS may impose aggravated additional tax penalties up to 40% of the unlawfully refunded or understated tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes, including the 40% unfair refund/unjust enrichment penalty, the 20% underreporting penalty, and the 10% non-filing penalty, plus daily statutory interest at 0.022% for overdue tax debts. Where fraudulent tax invoices or forged export declarations are used to obtain an export VAT refund, criminal prosecution may be pursued under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with seizure and clawback of the refunded amount and additional monetary penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but regulated foreign exchange environment under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account remittances, including SaaS subscription fees and legal contract automation services, are freely allowed and may be settled through a licensed foreign exchange bank; the requirement is a transaction report and an appropriate purpose code rather than a prior license. Capital account movements, including inbound foreign direct investment into a Yuhan Hoesa, are subject to notification through the Ministry of Economy and Finance and Bank of Korea. In practice, FDI funds can be remitted under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; after investment report filing and business registration are recorded, capital can be converted into KRW by a licensed foreign exchange bank, subject to beneficial owner identification. There is no surrender requirement for export receivables; exporters may keep foreign currency in domestic foreign currency deposit accounts. Profit remittances are treated as current transactions and can be wired abroad as dividends after corporate governance documentation and tax compliance are completed; the practical brake is documentation-heavy, not a currency shortage. The main hurdles are verifying the underlying trade/capital nature, proving the UBO chain, calculating withholding tax (20 percent dividend withholding unless reduced by treaty), and satisfying mandatory reporting to tax, customs, and foreign exchange authorities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and MOEF regulations, export proceeds for goods must be received within five years from the export declaration date unless a longer deferred-payment period is separately approved; there is no mandatory conversion ratio, meaning 0 percent of export foreign currency receipts must be surrendered to the Bank of Korea, and the exporter may retain the proceeds in a resident foreign currency deposit at a licensed foreign exchange bank. Receipts must be settled through a licensed bank, purpose-coded, and overdue collection beyond the statutory window requires filing a report to the foreign exchange bank and MOEF.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business or Payoneer are not licensed foreign exchange banks in South Korea and cannot replace a domestic Tier-1 bank for FDI capital custody, KRW conversion, export proceeds collection, trade settlement, or statutory corporate banking. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route is to open a foreign-currency FDI receipt account at a Tier-1 Korean foreign exchange bank after filing an investment notification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (via KOTRA or the receiving bank). The foreign investor remits by SWIFT MT103 using the exact corporate legal name, Korean business registration number, investment notification number, direct-investment foreign exchange purpose code, and the bank's foreign-currency intermediary details. No sanctioned bank or unlicensed intermediary may be used in the chain. After the remittance is credited and matched to the FDI notification, the bank performs capital verification and permits conversion into KRW through an onshore USD/KRW spot trade. The Yuhan Hoesa must complete share subscription, board approval, registration in the Korean commercial registry, and any post-closing notification to KOTRA.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Korean Commercial Act for a Yuhan Hoesa. The Articles of Incorporation must state the total capital and the value of each contribution. The entire subscribed capital must be deposited by bank transfer into a blocked corporate-formation escrow account opened in the Company's provisional name; the bank issues a certificate of capital deposit and freezes the funds until the court registry certificate of incorporation and, if applicable, business registration certificate have been issued. For non-resident members, the capital remittance must be reported to a foreign-exchange bank or KOTRA under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and the escrow release is conditioned on completion of registration and compliance with foreign-investment reporting.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate for the warehouse, fire-fighting facility inspection, civil defence evacuation and emergency preparedness approval, structural load certification for pallet racks, electrical safety inspection, and occupational workplace safety approval under the Occupational Safety and Health Act. Any storage area housing combustible cartons and edible-fat-containing soup base bricks must maintain clear aisle spacing, installed extinguishers, and sprinkler coverage. If the warehouse floor area is 3,000 m2 or greater, additional civil-defence storage approval and a fire-defence permit from the competent fire station are mandatory.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS), Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: Home Tax Service (HTS) at www.hometax.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity or a Korean Yuhan Hoesa applying for its Korean tax identity must first complete incorporation with the Korean company registry under the Commercial Act. A foreign non-resident entity without a Korean place of business must appoint a Korean tax representative (tax agent) with a power of attorney under the Framework Act on National Taxes, then file the Business Registration Application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the Korean place of business, or file through Home Tax Service using an accredited digital certificate. Supporting documents include: certified foreign corporate registration certificate or articles of incorporation (apostilled/notarized and translated into Korean), lease contract or evidence of a domestic place of business, identification documents of the representative director and foreign shareholders, and the tax representative appointment certificate. The NTS issues a Business Registration Certificate under a 10-digit Business Registration Number (000-00-00000), which also serves as the VAT registration number; no separate VAT ID is issued. The Yuhan Hoesa must then register for electronic tax invoicing in Home Tax Service and open a corporate bank account for export foreign exchange receipts.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

0 KRW (no monetary threshold); every business person supplying taxable goods or services must register from the commencement of business under the Value-Added Tax Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act when goods are actually exported to a foreign country and leave the Korean customs territory under an export customs declaration. For pre-packaged spicy tallow hotpot base bricks, 0% VAT applies only if the sale qualifies as an export of goods to a foreign destination; services qualify only when supplied to a non-resident recipient under the place-of-supply rules. Domestic wholesale supplies remain subject to 10% VAT.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate) showing HS code, quantity, value and export destination
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of the hotpot base bricks out of South Korea
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List fully cross-referenced to the export customs declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Bank Confirmation) evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency

All B2B suppliers must issue an electronic tax invoice through the NTS Home Tax Service portal or an NTS-approved API-integrated e-invoicing solution. The e-tax invoice must be transmitted to the NTS electronic tax invoice central clearing system within one business day after the supply date, and the counterparty must validate it. No physical fiscal stamp is used, but anti-fraud control is achieved through NTS digital validation of business registration numbers, electronic signatures, automatic cross-referencing of issued invoices with VAT returns, and real-time querying of invoice authenticity. B2C supplies may be evidenced by cash receipts or simplified tax invoices where applicable.

Korea Customs Service and the National Tax Service cross-match export declarations, e-tax invoices, customs manifest data, shipping records, and bank foreign exchange remittance records. If the export zero-rating is not substantiated, VAT at 10% is retrospectively assessed on the transaction, and statutory additional tax is imposed under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, false export declarations, or false e-tax invoices may trigger monetary penalties, retrospective VAT clawback, and criminal referral for tax evasion, and the exporter will not retain the 0% rate unless the physical export and foreign exchange repatriation are fully demonstrated within the statutory period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized current account convertibility regime for trade and service transactions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA). Export collections and import payments for the pre-packaged hotpot soup base distribution business can be settled freely through Authorized Foreign Exchange Banking Institutions by SWIFT or letter of credit, subject to documentary review and the reporting of underlying customs declarations. The capital account is open but administratively regulated; equity subscription, shareholder loans, and other capital movements must be declared via the Foreign Exchange Information System to obtain a certificate for onward remittance. There is no blanket surrender or mandatory conversion rule for export proceeds in foreign currency; exporters may retain receipts in Foreign Currency Accounts or convert at market rate, but every conversion is reported to the Bank of Korea. The principal profit remittance hurdles are the need to document an actual business profit through certified or audited financial statements, the payment of corporate tax and withholding tax at treaty rates on dividends, and the absence of a prolonged unrepatriated export receivable, because FETA penalizes receipts older than six months unless a delay report is filed. Dividends are otherwise freely convertible after settlement of tax liabilities. For non-resident shareholders, no prior exchange-control approval is required if the bank has verified the foreign investment declaration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA, export proceeds must be received within 6 months from the date of export declaration unless an extension is filed in advance through the settling bank. Korea does not require the surrender of export proceeds to the central bank; the statutory mandatory conversion ratio is 0%, meaning the exporter may hold the foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Account. If converted to KRW, the transaction automatically enters Bank of Korea reporting and must match the export customs declaration.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the implementing authority for the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with policy supervision by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and operational reporting through the Foreign Exchange Information System.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs cannot open accounts or provide Korean local clearing on behalf of a domestic entity without holding a Korean Electronic Financial Business license and a foreign exchange brokerage registration with the Bank of Korea. The regulatory perimeter of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act prohibits unlicensed fintech vehicles from acting as settlement agents for export proceeds or supplier disbursements in KRW. A limited viable use exists for a non-Korean parent company to hold pre-funding in an offshore EMI, but the Korean operating company must clear through a licensed domestic authorized bank. No offshore EMI can provide the statutory export settlement certificate needed to complete the foreign exchange verification process.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion by suppressed export revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, imprisonment for not more than three years or a fine of not less than one time and not more than three times the evaded tax; the National Tax Service imposes additional tax of 30% of unpaid tax plus 0.022% daily interest and disallows carried-forward losses.

Director personal liability: A directing mind who ordered or benefitted from the evasion becomes jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid tax under the National Tax Collection Act Article 25; assets of the director may be seized, departure from Korea may be banned, and the director may be subject to criminal imprisonment and debarment from corporate management.
Transfer pricing and profit stripping through intercompany payments

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities may make transfer pricing adjustments under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, restore the Korean tax base, impose an understatement penalty of 10% to 20% on the recomputed tax, and assess daily late-payment interest; falsified TP documentation may trigger criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false related-party transaction statements and intercompany agreements can be personally assessed for the additional corporate tax if they acted intentionally or negligently in causing the underpayment; the NTS may impose a departure blacklist, freeze personal bank accounts, and pursue civil restitution of diverted profit through the National Tax Collection Act.
Shell entity / treaty shopping without economic substance

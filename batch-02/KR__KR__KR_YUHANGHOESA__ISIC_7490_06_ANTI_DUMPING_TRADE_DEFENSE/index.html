Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; official online electronic portal: HomeTax at hometax.go.kr. Customs clearance and export declaration processing are administered by Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS system at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing or participating in a Korean Yuhan Hoesa must apply for business registration with the competent local district tax office through HomeTax within 20 days from the commencement of the Korean business. The application is the statutory Business Registration Application under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, and must be accompanied by the articles of incorporation, proof of foreign investment registration under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, paid-in capital evidence, registered office lease or title evidence, identification documents of the representative, and a corporate seal certificate. The NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves simultaneously as the entity TIN and VAT registration number for a general taxable person. There is no separate VAT certificate. A non-resident foreign supplier with no domestic Korean place of business but with a Korean VAT nexus must register under the NTS non-resident VAT registration procedure through a designated tax representative and obtain the same 10-digit registration number for VAT filing, electronic tax invoices and refund claims.

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available under the Value-Added Tax Act Article 21 for goods that are physically exported from Korea and for services, including professional anti-dumping and trade-remedy defense consulting, supplied to a non-resident recipient and actually used or consumed outside Korea. The claim requires the supplier to demonstrate that the service was performed for a foreign principal, that no domestic Korean taxable use occurred, that consideration is payable in foreign currency, and that the foreign exchange proceeds are actually repatriated and settled through a Korean foreign exchange bank. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is creditable and recoverable through the periodic VAT return and refund procedure on HomeTax.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Cargo Declaration) filed through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS; for exported services without physical customs clearance, the substitute evidence is the executed service agreement, service delivery or completion report, and evidence of the non-resident recipient status and actual use of the service outside Korea
  • Bill of Lading for sea cargo or Air Waybill for air cargo, plus commercial invoice and packing list, for goods exports
  • Zero-rated tax invoice issued under the Korean electronic tax invoice system and transmitted to NTS via HomeTax, expressly marked as an export or foreign-currency-earning supply
  • Foreign Exchange Bank Export Proceeds Settlement Certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank, being the Korean statutory equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), evidencing timely receipt and settlement of the export consideration in foreign currency

A Yuhan Hoesa general taxable person must issue tax invoices for all taxable supplies and transmit electronic tax invoice issue details to the NTS through HomeTax or an NTS-certified API/commercial e-invoicing system. Korea does not operate a physical fiscal stamping or continuous real-time central clearance model; anti-fraud control is instead achieved through mandatory electronic tax invoice transmission, NTS invoice registry verification, the cash receipt system for consumer supplies, and real-time data matching against Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declarations and foreign exchange bank settlement records. Zero-rated export invoices must be distinctly recorded as export/foreign-earning supplies and linked to the underlying export evidence.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-match HomeTax zero-rated VAT returns with UNI-PASS export declarations and foreign exchange bank settlement records. If export or foreign-use substantiation is incomplete, if foreign exchange is not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period, or if the invoice value does not reconcile with the settled export proceeds, the zero-rating is retrospectively denied and the supply is reclassified as taxable domestic supply. The NTS will assess VAT at 10% on the consideration, impose statutory penalty tax additions including up to 40% penalty tax for fraudulent or grossly negligent underreporting, and assess e-invoice issuance and transmission penalties. False zero-rated invoices or export declarations also expose the entity and its representative to criminal sanctions, fines, and imprisonment under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act and related customs legislation. Retrospective assessments follow the national tax limitation rules: generally 5 years from the date of assessment, extended to 10 years for fraudulent evasion cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a highly liberalised current-account regime; the Korean won is fully convertible for trade in goods, services, dividends, interest, royalties, and ordinary business expenses, provided settlement occurs through an authorized foreign exchange bank. Capital-account transactions remain subject to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and require either direct reporting, ex-ante notification, or licensed prior approval depending on the specific financial instrument. A capital contribution into a Korean LLC by a foreign investor is treated as foreign direct investment and must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; subsequent profit remittances are permitted once corporate tax and withholding obligations on dividends are satisfied. There are no general surrender requirements obliging a Korean exporter to convert foreign exchange, but export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange bank within the statutory collection period. During crisis, Korea may reimpose conversion or payment deferral measures, but the current peacetime regime is open current account plus managed capital account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be received through an authorized foreign exchange bank and repatriated into Korea within 18 months from the date of the export declaration. There is no fixed statutory ratio for mandatory conversion into KRW; the obligation is focused on collection, receipt, and repatriation of the export foreign currency. The exporter may retain export proceeds in a foreign currency deposit or convert at will, provided the transaction is properly declared through the Foreign Exchange Information System. Extensions beyond the 18-month period require prior approval or reporting to the relevant authorized bank with supporting justification such as trade dispute resolution or financing arrangement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the central administrative agency under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; authorized Korean foreign exchange banks handle reporting and control.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Non-viable as principal banking rail. Offshore digital EMI providers are not licensed as Korean electronic financial service providers under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and are not authorized foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. They cannot maintain domestic settlement accounts, provide escrow-capable KRW current account services, or perform corporate KYC for foreign direct investment. Using such EMIs for entity treasury would bypass regulated FX reporting and constitute a compliance violation; rated Low viability.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national headline CIT rate on taxable income above KRW 300 billion (combined 26.4% including the 10% local income tax surcharge; lower progressive brackets apply at 10%, 20% and 22%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% combined on gross dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge), unless reduced by an applicable Double Tax Treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions equal or exceed KRW 10 billion; Master File is required where aggregate related-party transactions equal or exceed KRW 100 billion or the MNE group satisfies the CbCR threshold (consolidated revenue at the statutory threshold, approximately KRW 1 trillion). Simplified contemporaneous documentation is required for all related-party transactions below these thresholds, and documentation must be produced to the NTS within 30 days of request.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply Gulf-format ESR, but the substance-over-form principle (Framework Act on National Taxes Article 14) and the statutory GAAR allow recharacterization of transactions lacking real economic substance. For anti-dumping defense consulting, the Korean entity must perform Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA), maintain premises and employees, control contracts and decision-making, and bill clients at arm's length. Offshore shell affiliates without substance will be disregarded, and the Korean CFC regime may currently tax passive income accumulated abroad.

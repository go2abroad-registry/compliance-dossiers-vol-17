Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Korean Commercial Act requires an actual registered head office address in Korea. A virtual office or commercial mail-forwarding mailbox is not acceptable for corporate registration with the registry court; the registered office must be a physical premise with land-use permission for office use. Local tax offices may visit and may reject the registration if the office is merely a post-office box. For the vertical micro-drama industry, banks and app-store payment aggregators such as Apple, Google Play, and Stripe require a physical address in Korea and in-person KYC; a standalone virtual address will likely be rejected for corporate bank account opening and for foreign exchange merchant registration. A full-service serviced office with reception, lease documentation, and physical presence may satisfy the substance test, but a pure virtual mailbox will fail.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. South Korea does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. The Articles of Incorporation must state the precise contribution amount allocated to each member. Paid-in capital does not need to be held in a blocked escrow account. However, contributions made in kind must be examined by a court-appointed inspector unless an exemption applies under Article 287-15 of the Commercial Act. For a foreign-invested company, remittances from non-resident members should be received through a domestic foreign exchange bank and reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, but no escrow blockage is mandatory.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic registration, filing, payment and electronic tax invoice transmission are performed through the statutory HomeTax system at hometax.go.kr; customs export declarations are lodged through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must first determine whether it has a domestic place of business in Korea; if it does not, it must appoint a tax manager or statutory tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes before submitting the Business Registration Application to the local competent district tax office or through the HomeTax portal. The application must attach the foreign company certificate of incorporation, constituent documents, passport copies of directors or controlling shareholders, the tax representative designation letter, and proof of the Korean service address for any domestic branch. After validation, NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number which also serves as the VAT identification number. A non-resident supplier of offshore digital services with no Korean place of business may instead apply for Simplified VAT Registration for Foreign Suppliers of Electronic Services through HomeTax, disclosing its platform channels, payment methods, and estimated Korean turnover. Once registered by a Korean company, the HomeTax digital certificate must be used for quarterly VAT filing even if no taxable activity occurred in a quarter.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or pass-through classification for a Yuhan Hoesa; a Yuhan Hoesa is a resident corporation taxed at entity level under the Corporate Tax Act, while shareholders are separately taxed on dividends. The only relevant elective regime is the group consolidation system for wholly-owned resident subsidiaries under the Corporate Tax Act, which is an optional consolidated filing mechanism and not a pass-through election; hybrid mismatch adjustments are mandatory under the International Tax Adjustment Act and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a resident corporation such as the Korean Yuhan Hoesa, no monetary VAT registration threshold applies; registration is compulsory from the date of business commencement. For a non-resident supplier of offshore electronic or digital services to Korean consumers, simplified VAT registration is required only when annual Korean-taxable turnover exceeds KRW 100,000,000; however, if the business is also a Korean resident corporation for corporate tax purposes, Business Registration is mandatory regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the VAT Act for goods that are physically exported from Korea and for services supplied for use or enjoyment outside Korea to a non-resident. For the vertical micro-drama streaming business, the Korean company may claim the 0% export VAT rate if the end consumer is located outside Korea, the digital content is delivered through overseas platforms or application stores for access abroad, the subscription or in-app purchase is billed in foreign currency or via foreign settlement systems, and the foreign exchange is repatriated to Korea within the statutory period. For physical merchandise, the goods must pass Korean customs export clearance and be evidenced by an electronic export declaration. Zero-rating is not automatic for cross-border digital services unless the entity can prove the place of use is outside Korea through geolocation, app-store region, payment card country, contracts and platform sales reports.

  • Korea Customs Service Electronic Export Declaration (Export Declaration Confirmation submitted via UNI-PASS)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill for physical goods consignments
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign Exchange Acquisition Certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank, equivalent to an inward remittance certificate, evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • App Store or Google Play settlement statements and payment gateway reports showing foreign-currency payments repatriated to Korea for digital content

General taxpayers must issue an electronic tax invoice for every taxable supply to another business or to any person who requests one, and must transmit it to NTS through HomeTax within one day after issuance. The clearance architecture is API/XML based and near real-time with D+1 mandatory transmission; all electronic tax invoices carry a HomeTax digital signature and are validated against NTS systems, and post-transmission alteration is a criminal offence. For business-to-consumer transactions, the mandatory Cash Receipt Card system instantly reports cash receipts to NTS, and for online app-store distribution using Apple IAP, Google Play Billing or Stripe, the Korean seller must retain the payment gateway settlement reports, platform tax invoices, and transaction logs as source evidence. Foreign simplified VAT suppliers must also issue an electronic tax invoice for Korean business customers and transmit it through HomeTax under the same clearance rules.

If NTS determines that the export was not actually completed, that the services were consumed in Korea, or that the foreign exchange proceeds were not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, the zero rating is retrospectively cancelled and standard VAT of 10% is assessed on the gross supply consideration from the original supply date. The taxpayer becomes liable for the overdue unpaid VAT plus statutory additional tax penalties ranging from 20% for negligent misdeclaration to 40% for fraudulent misdeclaration, along with daily default interest accruing until full settlement. The input VAT credits related to the transaction are also clawed back. Where fabricated export documentation or deliberate evasion is demonstrated, NTS is obliged to refer the matter to prosecution under the Act on the Punishment of Tax Evaders, which carries imprisonment for up to five years and fines up to KRW 50,000,000, with increased penalties for repeat offenders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a largely free-floating current account for goods, services, and ordinary trade settlement, while capital account instruments remain subject to declaratory or approval-based controls under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is convertible on a visible-transaction basis without a surrender obligation, but conversion and repatriation require a legitimate underlying economic transaction and must be filed through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Corporate profit remittance from a Yuhan Hoesa is permissible once annual statutory audits, tax filings (corporate income tax, local income tax), and shareholder resolution are completed; dividend remittance is classified as a current-account payment and enjoys automatic permission only if the foreign-invested company has properly notified FDI and submitted the end-of-use statement. Practical hurdles include documentary burdens from commercial banks, average processing windows of 2-5 business days, and strict anti-money laundering substantiation of shareholder source-of-funds. Non-resident capital injection above USD 30,000 must be reported; equity contributions above KRW 100 million may trigger foreign investment notification; transfer pricing documentation may also be requested. There is no central bank surrender of export proceeds, but all export receipts must be collected within statutory timelines and matched to customs declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, Korean exporters are obliged to collect export proceeds within 18 months from the date of export declaration for general goods and services. No mandatory conversion of export foreign currency into KRW is imposed, provided the foreign currency is deposited with a designated foreign exchange bank in Korea. However, if proceeds are held as foreign currency deposits, the bank must report the receipt to the FEIS; exporters must maintain documentary evidence such as export declaration, bills of lading, invoices, and settlement receipts for at least five years. Late collection beyond the 18-month period requires a reasoned application to the bank for extension with proof of lawful cause such as trade dispute or collection trial.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, serves as the central monetary and foreign exchange authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability for Korean KRW settlement. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Airwallex generally cannot provide KRW IBANs without a domestic partnership and are not recognized as designated foreign exchange banks for corporate FX payments in Korea. They may facilitate non-KRW collections from SaaS or IAP streaming channels but cannot serve as primary treasury clearing rails for KRW disbursements, payroll, VAT tax payments, or profit repatriation. Any fintech payment service provider must be registered with the Financial Services Commission under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and a pure offshore EMI is legally barred from servicing the domestic treasury of a Korean entity without a licensed Korean bank acting as the underlying clearing intermediary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound shareholder loans or capital contributions into the Korean Yuhan Hoesa must be routed by SWIFT MT103 using a foreign-exchange purpose code for foreign direct investment or shareholder loan under FEIS. Funds must be received into a designated foreign-exchange bank account. If the equity contribution exceeds KRW 100 million, an FDI notification must first be filed with the designated bank and reported to the Bank of Korea and FEIS before remittance. The bank will issue a Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation; a licensed Korean certified public accountant then prepares a capital verification report. The inward remittance cannot exceed the amount stated in the investment notification; any shortfall or excess greater than 20 percent requires an amended notification prior to further transfer.Outbound disbursements for content licensing, royalties, and production fees should be settled via SWIFT MT103 with MT202 COV for bundled multi-leg payments. Commercial letters of credit are unsuitable for intangible digital content, so documentary collections at D/P at sight or electronic payment instructions are used for contractual settlement. For recurring profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders, the Korean entity must execute MT103 together with applicable corporate income tax withholding receipts and pay the dividend remittance fee to the remitting bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability for Korean KRW settlement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCIAA), Article 11, a Korean company must prepare and submit (upon NTS request) a Transfer Pricing Local File and Master File when the annual aggregate amount of related-party cross-border transactions (goods, services, IP royalties, interest, and financial transactions) with each foreign related party exceeds KRW 10 billion (approximately USD 7.6 million) for the fiscal year. If the total aggregate amount exceeds KRW 50 billion (approximately USD 38 million), a Master File must be prepared also for the multinational group; if any related-party transaction is with an entity in a designated tax haven jurisdiction, the documentation threshold is zero (i.e., mandatory documentation regardless of amount). The Korean entity must also file an Annual Transfer Pricing Disclosure Form (Schedule 6) and the 'International Transactions Information Statement' when any related-party transaction exceeds KRW 300 million (approximately USD 230,000).

Economic substance scope: The Republic of Korea does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation comparable to that in the UAE, but the Korean tax authorities robustly apply the substance-over-form doctrine, anti-fragmentation rules, beneficial ownership requirements, and the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) under Article 19 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs. For a vertical micro-drama holding and distribution company, Korean-source income from the exploitation of IP will be recognized where Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are actually performed. If the Korean entity is merely a shell claiming treaty benefits or passing through app-store revenue to an offshore IP owner without real office, staff, contracts, and decision-making, the NTS will deny treaty relief and reattach Korean-source income to the Korean company; the directors would face personal liability for unpaid taxes. In addition, the OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI) provisions (Principal Purpose Test, PPT) operate to deny treaty benefits where obtaining the treaty benefit was one of the principal purposes of the arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Underreporting Foreign App-Store Revenue

Statutory sanction: Violation of Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (Korea) and Article 66 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. An additional tax of up to 40% of the underreported tax is imposed, and the unpaid tax attracts daily delinquency penalties (0.022% per day). Criminal sentencing: up to 3 years imprisonment or a fine of not more than three times the evaded tax; if tax evaded exceeds KRW 300 million, imprisonment of up to 5 years and a possible fine of up to five times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil, the NTS may disregard the company's separate legal personality if the company is a mere alter ego of the directors, particularly where corporate formalities are nonexistent and assets are drained to offshore accounts. Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, surcharges and penalties; their personal assets can be seized. In criminal proceedings, the director is criminally liable with imprisonment; the NTS will also report the individual to the immigration authorities, resulting in a travel ban (blacklisting) and refusal of passport renewal.
Transfer Pricing Mis-Pricing of IP Royalties to Offshore Affiliate

Statutory sanction: Articles 4 through 9 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCIAA) allow the NTS to recharacterise the transaction, compute the arm's length price, and assess additional corporate income tax plus the 10% (or 40% in fraudulent cases) understatement penalty. Interest penalties accrue at a rate of 0.022% per day. The agency may also fine the taxpayer for failure to submit transfer pricing documentation under Article 11; non-compliance fines are up to KRW 50 million.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany agreements or knowingly submit ineffective documentation are personally liable for the re-assessed corporate tax under the 'alter ego' doctrine; the NTS may issue a demand for payment against the individual. In criminal cases, the director may be convicted under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for intentional omission of income or false accounting, with imprisonment up to 3 years. Additionally, if the mis-priced royalty was remitted out of Korea without withholding tax and without treaty relief, the director is personally liable as the withholding agent for the unpaid 22% withholding tax, and is subject to a travel ban until the tax is settled.
Failure to Withhold and Report Non-Resident Withholding Tax on Outbound Dividends, Interest and Royalties

Statutory sanction: Under Article 156 and Article 157 of the Corporate Income Tax Law, the withholding agent is personally liable for the unwithheld tax; the NTS is empowered to issue a demand covering the unpaid tax amount plus an additional charge of 10% of the unwithheld tax (if due to failure to withhold) or 20% if a false withholding return was filed. Delinquency penalties of 0.022% per day also apply. The corporate payment is deemed a 'prohibited deduction' and disallowed, triggering further corporate tax assessment. Fines for intentional failure to withhold may amount to 22% of the relevant income plus possible criminal sanctions under Article 13 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: a fine not exceeding three times the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable as statutory withholding agents if the company fails to withhold or to remit. Civil liability attaches to the director's personal estate, and the NTS may seize bank accounts, wage income, and real property. The director will also be subject to a blacklisting travel ban until the tax and penalties cleared. If the offence was committed fraudulently (false invoice, disguised beneficiary, prior concealment), criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment of 2 to 5 years, and the director is disqualified from serving as a director of any Korean company for a period set by the court. The corporate veil is pierced to the extent of the unpaid tax and any income diverted offshore, plus appropriate punitive restitution.

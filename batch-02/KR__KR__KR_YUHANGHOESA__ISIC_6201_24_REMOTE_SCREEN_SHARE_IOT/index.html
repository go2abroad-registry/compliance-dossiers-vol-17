Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. However, the full amount of the subscribed contributions stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be paid or contributed in property before the formation registration is filed. The bank will place the paid-in capital in a blocked provisional incorporation escrow deposit account and issue a capital deposit certificate. Funds in this escrow account may not be withdrawn, disbursed, or pledged prior to the completion of incorporation registration, and the deposit certificate must be submitted to the registry as proof of payment. If incorporation is abandoned, the funds are released only to the persons who remitted them, after lawful cancellation of the formation process.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS), Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); export customs clearance supervised by Korea Customs Service through the UNIPASS electronic system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: A Yuhan Hoesa is incorporated in Korea; the court registration office issues a Corporate Registration Certificate. Step 2: The entity must file a Business Registration Application with the district tax office that has jurisdiction over its registered place of business, or submit the application online through HomeTax, within 20 days of the business start date. Step 3: The applicant must attach the articles of incorporation, Corporate Registration Certificate, proof of identity of the representative, lease agreement or other proof of a domestic business place, and a bank account record. Step 4: The NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Number which operates as the VAT identification number and the corporate tax reference number. Step 6: A foreign supplier of remote digital services to Korean consumers who has no domestic establishment may use the NTS special simplified registration for foreign electronic service suppliers through HomeTax, which is used exclusively for VAT filing and issuance of electronic tax invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic Korean corporation taxed on its worldwide income under the Corporate Income Tax Act, and is the VAT taxable person under the VAT Act. It is not a pass-through entity and cannot elect to flow income through to shareholders. Non-resident shareholders are not taxed on operating profits directly, but Korean withholding tax may apply to dividend distributions.

Available election forms

None. The Korean tax system does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election for Yuhan Hoesa. The only statutory elective relief relevant to indirect tax is the simplified VAT taxpayer regime available when gross revenue is below the statutory threshold, but that election does not change the entity's corporate classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No exemption threshold: every business operator must register with the National Tax Service from the date of business commencement regardless of turnover. For small businesses, the simplified taxpayer regime is available only when annual gross supply value is below KRW 80,000,000 (excluding VAT), but business registration remains mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from South Korea and cleared through customs are zero-rated under the VAT Act. Services supplied to non-residents and consumed outside South Korea, including remote desktop access, online screen-sharing, SaaS, and IoT telemetry software, are zero-rated when the recipient is a non-resident, the place of service use is outside Korea, the supply is evidenced by a 0% electronic tax invoice, and the foreign currency proceeds are received or repatriated within the statutory foreign-exchange collection period. Inward payments in KRW from a non-resident are not automatically zero-rating; the taxpayer must prove the actual non-resident counterparty and offshore consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration processed in the Korea Customs Service UNIPASS electronic clearance system)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or combined transport document evidencing physical exportation
  • Commercial Export Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or documentary foreign exchange receipt evidence issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank
  • Executed service agreement/purchase order identifying the non-resident buyer and the exact remote screen-share and IoT software services; for pure digital services, platform service logs, IP addresses, and user telemetry may supplement or replace customs documents if the NTS accepts the service export substantiation package

All VAT-registered businesses must issue an electronic tax invoice for each supply to another business and transmit it to the NTS through the HomeTax electronic invoice system by the day after the supply. Transactions with final consumers must be documented through the NTS Cash Receipt System where the customer is a domestic consumer. There is no notarial real-time fiscal stamping; instead, the NTS enforces anti-fraud compliance through mandatory electronic tax invoice validation, automated cross-matching of invoices with VAT returns, and the mandatory use of HomeTax EDI/API interfaces by ERP and certified accounting software. Foreign non-resident electronic-service suppliers registered under the simplified procedure must file VAT returns electronically through the HomeTax non-resident portal and issue/download electronic tax invoices without any physical stamp or local fiscal device.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-match export declarations, shipping documents, electronic tax invoices, foreign exchange receipt records, and service audit logs. If the zero-rating documentation is missing, incomplete, forged, or inconsistent, the NTS will disallow zero-rating, reclassify the supply as a taxable domestic supply, and retroactively assess 10% VAT together with statutory additional tax for underreporting/unfaithful returns, delinquency interest, and possible input VAT clawback. Fraudulent export zero-rating or evasion of VAT may be referred for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, exposing responsible officers to imprisonment and fines, and customs authorities may also impose export clearance sanctions and revoke export benefits.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a market-determined exchange rate system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account transactions are generally liberalized and require no prior approval; residents may freely receive and remit funds for trade, services, income, royalties, and current transfers. The Korean won is highly convertible for commercial bank transactions at market rates, and there is no mandatory surrender requirement for export proceeds. Capital account transactions, including inward foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, intercompany loans, and derivative exposures, are liberalized but subject to notification, registration, or reporting to a designated foreign exchange bank or the Ministry of Economy and Finance. This registration structure is not a convertibility barrier, but it imposes real-time compliance documentation and beneficial-ownership verification. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are permitted after corporate income tax and withholding tax are settled; in practice the foreign investor must present a certificate of foreign investment registration or the bank's foreign-exchange purchase confirmation. For a remote desktop and IoT software company operating under ISIC 6201, recurring cross-border subscription receipts will be classified as current-account services revenue and may be received in foreign currency without restriction. Conversion into Korean won is immediately available through the foreign exchange bank at the prevailing market rate, with no quota, holding-period, or forced conversion framework. Overall, the KRW regime is best assessed as full current-account convertibility and high-but-controlled capital-account convertibility, with documentation and reporting discipline rather than government approval being the decisive liquidity constraint.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds and cross-border service-supply revenue must be collected and brought into Korea within six months from the date of export clearance or completion of the underlying service performance; an extension of up to two years may be approved by the Ministry of Economy and Finance for installment supply, open account contracts, dispute, or documented commercial reasons. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; 0% of the foreign currency proceeds must be surrendered to the authorities. The registered exporter or service provider may retain 100% of the foreign currency in a designated foreign currency deposit account at a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank and may convert the balance into Korean won at any time.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank and monetary authority, acting in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Transaction Council under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and e-money institutions have low legal viability for serving as the Korean entity's primary treasury or settlement bank. They are not authorized under the Korean Banking Act to open domestic KRW current accounts or provide local bank sort codes. A non-bank payment service provider must hold an electronic financial business license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and must also be registered with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act to conduct cross-border money-transfer services. Foreign EMIs such as European e-money institutions or Singaporean major payment institutions rarely hold these Korean licenses, so their use is limited to offshore collection perimeter services. For the target payment rails, Stripe and Paddle may be used as offshore merchant acquirers and global payfac providers, but they are not bank rails in Korea. The Korean Yuhan Hoesa must instruct Stripe or Paddle to remit the net merchant payout by SWIFT MT103 into the company's own designated foreign currency deposit account at a Korean Tier-1 bank, with a clear service-revenue purpose code and a payout report matching the subscription transactions. Without the underlying merchant settlement report, the Korean bank will refer the receipt for enhanced due diligence. Recommended treasury architecture is therefore a licensed domestic FX bank as the sole primary account holder, with Stripe and Paddle used solely as off-balance-sheet collection gateways.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection for the Yuhan Hoesa must comply with the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The foreign investor first files a foreign-investment report with a designated foreign exchange bank and receives an 'investment report certificate.' The bank opens a designated foreign currency investment deposit account in the name of the company or the investor, and the remitter wires the capital via SWIFT MT103 using a purpose code for acquisition of equity in a Korean company. The sender must include the foreign-investment notification number, the investor's full registered name, the Korean entity name, and the beneficiary account number. Upon receipt, the bank carries out interest and sanctions screening, confirms the source of funds, and issues a capital-contribution certificate or foreign-capital payment statement, which serves as the capital verification document for Korean tax and corporate registry filings. The funds may then be converted to Korean won at the market rate and transferred into the operating KRW account. For related-party capital contributions from high-risk jurisdictions, the bank will require the full evidentiary defense bundle before posting value.Outbound treasury disbursements should be routed through the Korean entity's designated foreign exchange bank using fully documented settlements. For software royalties, licensing, and professional services to foreign suppliers, the standard clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 corporate-to-corporate wire with the appropriate services-payment purpose code and a remittance instruction referencing the underlying contract. For physical IoT hardware procurement, a commercial Documentary Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) is preferable for higher-value vendor contracts, with the beneficiary presenting commercial invoice, packing list, bills of lading or air waybills, and inspection certificates. For pilot equipment or recurring hardware purchases, Documentary Collections under D/P or D/A terms provide a lower-cost rail but require confirmed acceptance from the Korean buyer and clear unpaid-document handling clauses. All outbound KRW and foreign-currency debits must be supported by the master purchase agreement, vendor proforma invoice, Korean corporate tax invoice, title documents where applicable, and completed foreign-exchange justification forms; payout remittances received from Stripe and Paddle should enter as MT103 service-revenue collections and be reconciled to the merchant payout report before onward disbursement.Deliverable foreign exchange forwards in USD/KRW and EUR/KRW, available only with underlying actual or anticipated trade, service, or equity exposure, documented to the Korean bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act real-demand rule.Foreign exchange swaps and FX swap points (USD/KRW) to cover timing mismatches between inbound subscription receipts and outbound supplier payments, provided the swap matches genuine cash-flow obligations.Plain-vanilla cross-currency swaps and interest-rate swaps for medium-term foreign-currency capital injections or long-term licensing arrangements, requiring bank approval and annual exposure reporting to the Ministry of Economy and Finance.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for the onshore Korean corporate as a primary hedging instrument because the KRW is a deliverable currency and internal real-demand restrictions apply; any NDF transaction should be rejected by the treasury policy unless separately licensed and supported by a documented offshore presence requirement.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs and e-money institutions have low legal viability for serving as the Korean entity's primary treasury or settlement bank. They are not authorized under the Korean Banking Act to open domestic KRW current accounts or provide local bank sort codes. A non-bank payment service provider must hold an electronic financial business license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and must also be registered with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act to conduct cross-border money-transfer services. Foreign EMIs such as European e-money institutions or Singaporean major payment institutions rarely hold these Korean licenses, so their use is limited to offshore collection perimeter services. For the target payment rails, Stripe and Paddle may be used as offshore merchant acquirers and global payfac providers, but they are not bank rails in Korea. The Korean Yuhan Hoesa must instruct Stripe or Paddle to remit the net merchant payout by SWIFT MT103 into the company's own designated foreign currency deposit account at a Korean Tier-1 bank, with a clear service-revenue purpose code and a payout report matching the subscription transactions. Without the underlying merchant settlement report, the Korean bank will refer the receipt for enhanced due diligence. Recommended treasury architecture is therefore a licensed domestic FX bank as the sole primary account holder, with Stripe and Paddle used solely as off-balance-sheet collection gateways.

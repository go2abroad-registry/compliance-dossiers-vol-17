Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: Hometax (hometax.go.kr); export/customs administration: Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Korean entity: first execute entity formation before the Commercial Registry; the foreign company must then file a Business Registration Application (with articles of incorporation, foreign certificate of incorporation/analogous legal existence certificate, board resolution appointing an in-country representative or tax representative, proof of domestic place of business, and power of attorney) with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the domestic place of business, or online through Hometax. The NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Number used for corporate income tax, VAT/payroll withholdings, and e-Tax invoice issuance. If the foreign entity has no domestic place of business but is within the scope of Korean VAT for remote/electronic services, register as a foreign non-resident VAT operator through Hometax, identify a Korean tax representative, and obtain the NTS-issued Business Registration Number under the foreign-registration procedure; domestic and non-resident VAT registrations are mandatory before the first taxable supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Yuhan Hoesa LLC is taxable at entity level under the Corporate Tax Act; no pass-through eligibility)

Available election forms

None (Korean tax classification is statutory; no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections). A foreign corporation can elect to appoint a tax representative for procedural compliance, and a qualifying wholly-owned domestic subsidiary may elect consolidated corporate tax filing under the Corporate Tax Act, but no US-style entity classification election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No mandatory turnover threshold; VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable business activity in Korea. The simplified-taxpayer ceiling of KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable supplies (KRW 48,000,000 for certain prescribed businesses) affects simplified tax calculation but does not exempt registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for 0% VAT if the goods are sold to a non-resident purchaser, physically exported under a Korea Customs Service electronic export declaration, invoiced at zero VAT, and paid for by the foreign buyer in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Export of services is zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident and the services are deemed consumed outside Korea, consideration is received in foreign currency, and the prescribed export-evidence package is retained. International transport and related services qualify as zero-rated by statute; offshore digital services sold to Korean consumers remain within the domestic VAT scope and are not zero-rated.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic Export Declaration / cargo clearance printout with export entry number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing export of the goods from Korea to the foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and HS-code master data matching the export declaration and 0% VAT e-Tax invoice
  • 0% VAT e-Tax Invoice addressed to the foreign buyer and transmitted through Hometax
  • Foreign exchange bank certificate of export-proceeds collection (bank-issued foreign currency receipt certificate equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate)

Mandatory national e-Tax Invoice administration through Hometax: every registered VAT operator issuing a tax invoice must transmit the e-Tax Invoice into Hometax (portal, EDI, or government-certified open API) no later than the day after issuance; NTS validates issuer registration, buyer confirmation, and invoice amount in real time. Retail and prescribed consumer-service operations must use approved cash-receipt (credit-card / cash-receipt device) equipment and transmit those transactions to NTS by the following business day. Korea does not currently apply fiscal stamping; anti-fraud controls are supported by NTS Big-Data cross-matching between purchase/supply e-invoices, customs declarations, and foreign-exchange receipts.

NTS examination will verify the export declaration, B/L, and foreign-exchange receipt trail; if the 0% claim lacks documentary support, is not substantiated, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the prescribed period without an approved extension, the NTS will reassess 10% VAT on the unproven export revenue, impose statutory penalties for false/unsubstantiated tax invoices and unfaithful return, add interest/overdue amounts under the National Tax Collection Act, and can refer the matter for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Refunds erroneously paid are retroactively clawed back with interest; the fraud limitation period of ten years applies, and concealment/false documentation will not benefit from any shortened assessment period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a fundamentally current-account convertible regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA). Current account settlements in respect of medical device consulting fees, licensing royalties, and trade-related disbursements are generally free from prior approval, provided that the underlying contract and supporting documentation are retained. Capital account transactions are largely liberalized under a positive-list system; declared direct investments, equity injections, and intra-company loans may be routed through authorized foreign exchange banks with post-transaction reporting. There are no surrender requirements compelling mandatory conversion of export proceeds into Korean won; exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic foreign-currency accounts. Profit remittances and dividend outflows are permitted after verification of accumulated undistributed earnings, audited financial statements, and valid tax clearance certificates. During balance-of-payments stress, the Bank of Korea may reinstate emergency approval measures, but such measures have not been actively invoked in the current cycle. The central bank operates the Foreign Exchange Information System to monitor large transactions and ensure statistical reporting. Non-resident LLCs are permitted to hold KRW current accounts and foreign-currency accounts, but full convertibility is contingent on clean AML/KYC onboarding and submission of purpose-of-remittance codes. The overall assessment is high convertibility for transactional flows with moderate compliance friction only for suspicious or unusually structured capital injections.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 26 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Enforcement Decree, export proceeds must be received and settled, deposited in a foreign currency account, or sold to an authorized foreign exchange bank within one year from the date of exportation. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; the exporter may elect to retain the foreign exchange liquidity in a bank-operated foreign currency account. However, the settlement must be reported to the foreign exchange bank within the applicable reporting window, and any overdue export proceeds require an extension from the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (Central Bank of the Republic of Korea), acting in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Very low viability for offshore digital EMI providers. Foreign electronic money issuers and non-bank fintech institutions are not authorized to open resident Korean won settlement accounts or act as primary clearing members of the local payment system. The Electronic Financial Transactions Act requires a domestic FSC license for e-money issuance, and no passporting regime exists for overseas EMIs. At the treasury stage, such providers may be used solely as ancillary payment initiators, but they cannot host the entity’s operating liquidity or satisfy Korean foreign exchange bank reporting obligations. A Tier-1 Korean commercial bank remains mandatory for statutory inbound capital verification and outbound trade settlement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the national CIT, producing a top combined effective rate of 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders under the Corporate Tax Act plus 2% local income tax, making the aggregate statutory domestic rate 22%; a bilateral tax treaty may reduce the rate, typically to 10% to 15% depending on ownership and residency.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, a Korean entity must prepare and submit a local (contemporaneous) transfer-pricing file where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion, and a Master File where annual related-party transactions exceed KRW 100 billion; the international transaction report schedule must be attached to the corporate income tax return for all reportable cross-border related-party transactions, and documentation is required to satisfy the arm's-length standard even below the KRW 50 billion threshold if NTS requests justification.

Economic substance scope: Korean tax authorities apply a substance-over-form doctrine under Article 14 of the Corporate Tax Act and Articles 4 through 6 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs. Transactions that lack commercial rationality and economic substance, or are arranged to take advantage of foreign shell entities, can be disregarded and recharacterized at arm's length. Core Income-Generating Activities are analyzed by reference to where the entity's staff, premises, capital, financial authority, and substantive decision-making reside; if the Yuhan Hoesa or its affiliate is a shell with no meaningful CIGA, the NTS can deny treaty benefits, apply controlled foreign corporation rules to passive income, impose adjustment penalties, and hold the managing director personally accountable.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Fraudulent Tax Return and Corporate Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Violates Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and Article 60 of the Corporate Tax Act. The fraudulent omission triggers an additional tax of 40% of the understated corporate tax plus local income tax surcharge, daily late-payment surcharges, and criminal prosecution. The director may be sentenced to imprisonment up to 5 years and ordered to pay fines up to 3 times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced when the company is a sham or an alter ego of the director: the director becomes jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and civil restitution of diverted assets. The NTS transmits the director to the Justice authorities for blacklisting and travel-ban enforcement, and a criminal conviction brings imprisonment plus permanent disqualification from serving as a corporate director.
Transfer-Pricing Misstatement and Inadequate International Documentation

Statutory sanction: Violates Articles 4, 5, 6 and 7 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs. The NTS may disregard the intercompany payments, re-determine taxable income at arm's length, impose an adjustment additional tax of up to 30% of the adjusted tax base, and add delinquency interest. Knowingly submitting false transfer-pricing schedules also falls within the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, carrying imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to 3 times the avoided tax.

Director personal liability: The director who knowingly signs false transfer-pricing documentation loses limited-liability protection; personal assets are reachable for the tax deficiency, penalties, and interest. The director is also subject to professional disqualification, blacklisting, travel ban, and criminal imprisonment for up to 5 years for aggravated fraudulent transfer-pricing violations.
AML, Shell Company Abuse, and Unreported Cross-Border Accounts

Statutory sanction: Violates the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the Act on Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information, and the Act on the Registration and Reporting of Overseas Banking and Financial Accounts; penalties include up to 20% of the amount held in unreported foreign accounts, confiscation of the unreported accounts, and, for money-laundering or concealment through shell companies, imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to KRW 100 million or up to three times the criminal proceeds, depending on the court's application.

Director personal liability: Because the shell entity is used as the alter ego of the director, the limited liability of the Yuhan Hoesa is disregarded. The director is personally liable for restitution of the converted funds and unpaid taxes, the Ministry of Justice imposes an exit restriction/blacklist, and a criminal conviction results in imprisonment, forfeiture of assets, and permanent damage to the director's legal capacity to manage corporate entities.

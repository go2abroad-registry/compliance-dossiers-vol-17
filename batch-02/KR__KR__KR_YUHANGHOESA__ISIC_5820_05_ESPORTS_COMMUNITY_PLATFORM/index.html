Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act; however, the full amount of the contributed capital set forth in the Articles of Incorporation must be actually paid in before the registration application is submitted. The funds must be deposited into a domestic bank account in the name of the company under formation, and a deposit certificate or balance certificate must be attached to the registration filing. There is no statutory blocked escrow account requirement. For non-resident investors, if the capital is remitted from overseas and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act procedures are used, a foreign-investment declaration may be filed with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy or through KOTRA, but this does not create a locked escrow condition.

Standard fire safety and Occupational Safety and Health Act obligations may apply to any occupied administrative office under the relevant municipal fire code and labor law, but no facility-specific Civil Defense certification is triggered by the corporate registered office.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax at www.hometax.go.kr. Customs-related export verification is administered separately by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS.

E-government filing portal

A Yuhan Hoesa is incorporated under the Commercial Act at the competent Korean court registry. Immediately after incorporation, the legal representative must apply to the NTS district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered head office for a business registration certificate. The application must be filed within 20 days after business commencement and can be submitted through HomeTax. Required documents include the court-issued certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, evidence of head office location (lease or title certificate), representative identification, and, for foreign investors, notarized or legalized foreign registration documents. The NTS will issue a 10-digit business registration number (the entity TIN and VAT registration number in one). A non-resident foreign company with no Korean place of business but carrying on specified electronic or digital service supplies to Korean customers must instead apply through the HomeTax simplified VAT registration procedure for foreign electronic service providers; the NTS issues a simplified VAT registration number. The foreign entity must also appoint a domestic tax representative for tax administration if required by the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. A Yuhan Hoesa is treated as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and is taxed on its worldwide income at entity level; Korea does not permit a check-the-box or pass-through election. A group consolidation tax election exists under the Corporate Tax Act only when all conditions for qualifying wholly-owned domestic subsidiaries are met, and certain incentive elections are available under the Special Tax Treatment Control Act, but these do not alter the entity classification itself.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for VAT registration; every taxable business must obtain a VAT registration within 20 days of business commencement. A simplified taxable person status is available to a domestic business with annual taxable supplies not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, but this is an optional simplification category, not an exemption from registration. A foreign non-resident supplier of electronic services to Korean consumers must register under the VAT Act simplified foreign electronic services registration regime regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods and supplies of services for use wholly outside Korea are zero-rated. For this esports community platform, zero-rating may apply to platform subscription fees, tournament entry fees, digital goods, advertising, and other platform services supplied to non-resident users where the service is deemed supplied and consumed outside Korea. The company must obtain and retain evidence of non-resident status and overseas consumption, including the customer's country of registration or residence, payment card payment address, IP address data, platform account records, and the underlying service contract. Supplies to Korean residents are subject to 10% VAT, while supplies to a foreign business may also be zero-rated where the Korean place of supply rules characterize the service as exported.

  • Export Declaration filed with the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic customs clearance system (the Korean statutory proof of physical export; a Bill of Entry is not used for exports).
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight, issued by the carrier in the exporter's name.
  • Certificate of Foreign Exchange Receipt (Korean statutory inward remittance evidence, not FIRC) issued by a licensed foreign exchange bank evidencing actual collection of export proceeds in foreign currency.
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order or platform contract, and the zero-rated e-tax invoice issued under the VAT Act.
  • For digital services requiring no customs clearance: written contract or electronic service order, platform transaction logs, payment settlement reports from Stripe or PayPal identifying the foreign customer, and the bank certificate of foreign exchange receipt.

Korea operates an electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) mandate under the VAT Act. A tax invoice must be issued on the supply date; for B2B supplies and for businesses above the statutory enforcement threshold in the Enforcement Decree, the e-tax invoice must be transmitted to the NTS through HomeTax or an NTS-certified EDI/API interface by the day following issuance. The NTS does not operate a Latin-America-style real-time pre-clearance system or require an anti-fraud digital stamp; compliance is enforced through mandatory e-tax invoice database filings, periodic VAT returns, and automated cross-checking of invoice details against declared output VAT.

If the required foreign exchange receipt, export declaration, or non-resident consumption evidence is not obtained within the statutory 180-day period (or an NTS-approved extension), the NTS will deny the zero-rating, reassess the 10% VAT, impose statutory additional taxes for under-declaration or unfaithful payment and for false or non-issued e-tax invoices, and claw back any previously refunded input VAT. Fraudulent or collusive documentation, circular remittances, or sham export schemes can trigger criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with imprisonment and monetary penalties in addition to tax reassessment; the burden of proof for zero-rating eligibility rests on the taxpayer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized current account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Bank of Korea (BOK). Convertibility for current transactions, including software licensing, esports tournament services, and platform subscriptions, is unrestricted and does not require prior approval; only a post-transaction filing via a foreign exchange bank is necessary. Capital account transactions, including inbound foreign direct investment into the Yuhan Hoesa LLC, are permitted without amount ceilings but require registration/reporting to the BOK or the delegated foreign exchange bank. Export proceeds must be collected and deposited in a domestic foreign exchange bank within six months of the export declaration date; actual conversion to KRW is at the entity's discretion, with no mandatory surrender ratio. Profit remittance hurdles arise from documentary requirements: an audited financial statement, tax withholding certificates for corporate income tax, and a foreign exchange bank report confirming the distribution; dividends cannot be remitted unless the local corporate tax obligation is discharged. For inbound equity injections, a Foreign Investment Report must be filed with KOTRA or the bank, triggering issuance of the Foreign Investment Certificate for capital verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds must be received in the entity's foreign exchange bank within six months from the date of export declaration (or from the actual loading date for certain goods). The statutory receipt period can be extended by up to six months upon application to the foreign exchange bank; failure to collect within the deadline may trigger administrative penalties. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; proceeds may be held in foreign currency or converted at market rates. In practice, the bank will demand the Export Declaration (Customs Cargo Clearance) report to reconcile the incoming SWIFT credit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange regulatory authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. South Korean law reserves the power to take deposits, handle foreign exchange settlements, and open corporate accounts to banks licensed under the Financial Holding Companies Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, and similar systems are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions for Korean corporates; they may offer ancillary payment cards or collection partners, but cannot serve as the primary treasury or capital escrow venue. Relying solely on an offshore EMI would fail the Capital Verification requirements and could invalidate the Foreign Investment Report.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top national CIT rate; 10% local income tax surtax raises combined headline to 26.4% for taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion. Progressive bracket rates are 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, and 21% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% combined statutory withholding on outbound dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder: 20% national corporate income tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surtax, subject to reduction under an applicable bilateral Income Tax Convention or Double Tax Agreement (DTA).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation is triggered when cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion in the preceding fiscal year; Master File preparation is required when such transactions exceed KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country Report filing is required for a multinational group whose ultimate parent's consolidated revenue reaches KRW 1 trillion. A related-party transaction disclosure schedule must nevertheless be attached to every annual corporate income tax return regardless of these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a formal UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the NTS applies the substance-over-form principle, the beneficial ownership doctrine, and the general anti-avoidance rules under Article 23 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and Article 4/5 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as tournament software development, matchmaking algorithm management, sponsor contracting, user acquisition, payment settlement, and corporate decision-making must actually be performed in Korea; if the Yuhan Hoesa merely holds contracts while offshore affiliates perform all activities, the NTS will recharacterise income, deny DTA benefits, and assess transfer pricing and evasion penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Framework Act on National Taxes Articles 47-2 and 47-3 impose under-report additional taxes of 20% or 40% depending on fraud and intent; Article 47-4 adds daily late-payment surcharges; the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 provides for imprisonment up to five years or fines up to five times the evaded tax; in egregious cases the NTS may publicly disclose the taxpayer under Article 85-5 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and impose the 10% local income tax surtax.

Director personal liability: Under the Korean Commercial Act Article 399, directors who negligently or intentionally violate their duties are jointly liable to the company and creditors; the National Tax Collection Act Article 24 permits the tax authority to collect taxes directly from directors where corporate assets were transferred or dissipated to evade collection; responsible directors may be subjected to departure restriction orders, blacklisting, asset seizure, and criminal imprisonment if the evasion was committed knowingly.
Transfer Pricing Abuses and False Intercompany Documentation

Statutory sanction: The Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Articles 4 and 5 authorise the NTS to make arm's-length adjustments and recharacterize abusive transactions; Article 7 of LCITA imposes an additional tax equal to 30% of the corporation tax avoided from the transfer pricing adjustment, plus the Framework Act on National Taxes non-payment surcharges; failure to submit transfer pricing documentation can trigger separate fines and evidentiary adverse inferences before the Tax Tribunal.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false intercompany records and tax schedules face secondary liability for the additional tax, penalties, and interest under Commercial Act Article 401; if the false documentation is used to suppress taxable income, criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may be asserted personally against the responsible director, and travel bans or departures from Korea can be ordered while the investigation is pending.
Shell Company Substance Failure and Non-Withholding on Outbound Payments

Statutory sanction: The Corporate Tax Act Article 98 and Local Tax Act Article 103-8 impose a combined 22% withholding obligation; the withholding agent is liable for the uncollected tax, additional tax, and daily late-payment surcharges under the Framework Act on National Taxes; treaty benefits are denied for conduit companies and Limitation-on-Benefits provisions are applied; intentional submission of a false beneficial ownership declaration can trigger criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and tax-evasion sanctions against the payer.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause or participate in the failure to deduct and remit withholding taxes are personally liable for the withheld amounts and surcharges as withholding agents under the National Tax Collection Act Article 24; civil joint liability exists under Commercial Act Article 399; the NTS may impose departure restrictions and blacklisting for large non-payment; criminal imprisonment is available for directors who intentionally use a shell entity to defeat Korean tax claims and for those who falsify DTA relief applications.

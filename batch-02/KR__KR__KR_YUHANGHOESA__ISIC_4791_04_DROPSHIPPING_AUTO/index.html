Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, accessed electronically via the Home Tax Service (HTS) portal at www.hometax.go.kr, with physical customs clearance and border cargo alerts handled by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that creates a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) domestically first obtains corporate registration from the competent court registry office and then applies to the relevant local district tax office for Business Registration using the standard NTS application. The applicant must file the business registration form together with the articles of incorporation, proof of corporate registration, lease or address proof for the Korean place of business, identity and passport/corporate equivalent of the representative, and a power of attorney if a Korean tax representative is appointed. After acceptance, NTS issues the Business Registration Certificate containing the 10-digit business and VAT registration number, which serves as the corporate TIN. If the foreign non-resident entity has no domestic place of business but supplies electronic or digital services to Korean individual consumers, it must complete the special foreign business operator VAT registration through the Home Tax Service by submitting the foreign registration certificate, charter or equivalent constitutional document, certificate of tax residence from the foreign jurisdiction, statement of Korean consumer revenue, and a power of attorney to a resident tax agent. NTS then issues a foreign business operator registration number for VAT filing and withholding purposes. For entities that will physically clear goods through Korean customs, an additional importer/exporter code must be obtained through UNI-PASS before any export declaration or import clearance is accepted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic corporations: registration is mandatory with no turnover threshold; any Yuhan Hoesa engaged in VAT-taxable business must register with NTS within 20 days of business commencement. Foreign non-resident electronic or digital service suppliers: mandatory registration is triggered when annual Korean consumer digital-service supplies exceed KRW 30 million; below that threshold, special foreign registration is not required unless the foreign entity opens a Korean place of business. Physical dropshipping imports into Korea are taxed at the border through customs import VAT regardless of supplier turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Korea qualify for zero rating under the Korean Value Added Tax Act when the goods have been shipped to a foreign buyer and the actual departure from Korea is evidenced by an accepted export customs declaration. Services supplied to non-residents may likewise be zero-rated when the service falls within the foreign-earned services or offshore digital service categories and is actually consumed outside Korea. In a direct dropshipping model where goods are sent from a third-country supplier directly to an overseas consumer without ever entering Korean customs territory, Korean export zero-rating is not applicable because the transaction lacks Korean indirect tax nexus; such a supply must be documented by commercial invoices, courier waybills, and bank settlement records from the source jurisdiction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate) accepted through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system
  • Bill of Lading or multimodal transport document evidencing physical departure from Korea
  • Commercial invoice showing the overseas buyer and export terms
  • Packing list matching the export declaration and transport documents
  • Foreign exchange settlement certificate or inward remittance certificate proving receipt and repatriation of export proceeds

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for business-to-business supplies made by a registered Korean LLC. A Yuhan Hoesa must issue an electronic tax invoice using the NTS Home Tax Service or an approved certified ASP and transmit it to NTS no later than the day after the supply date. The electronic tax invoice must state the supplier registration number, recipient registration number, supply date, supply value, VAT amount, and transaction description. Korea does not operate a physical fiscal stamp or real-time cash-register clearance; instead, anti-fraud controls are based on mandatory electronic tax invoice interlocking, the Cash Receipt System, and automatic cross-matching of purchase, sales, banking, and customs declarations by NTS.

If NTS or Korea Customs Service detects a false export declaration, forged transport documents, black-market exchange conversion, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the required 180-day period, the zero-rated export is retroactively recharacterised as a taxable domestic supply. The taxpayer then becomes liable for the 10% VAT on the suppressed export value, interest under the National Tax Collection Act, additional penalty taxes of up to 20% for dishonest filing or up to 10% for underpayment, and possible criminal referral for customs fraud or foreign exchange violations. The burden of proof rests on the exporting entity, and substantiation documents are subject to post-clearance audit and demand notices in the course of VAT reassessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a managed but de facto functional convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is convertible for current-account trade and service-related cross-border payments, including digital content subscriptions, software licensing, logistics, and dropshipping inventory procurement, provided there is a valid underlying commercial contract, electronic trade declaration, and proper bank documentation. Capital-account transactions, however, are subject to a negative-list system: foreign direct investment, external borrowing, and foreign-currency loans require notification, registration, or approval through a designated foreign exchange bank or the Bank of Korea. There is no general statutory surrender obligation compelling conversion of export proceeds into Korean won when proceeds are lawfully retained in a foreign-currency account at an authorized domestic bank; nevertheless, export proceeds must be repatriated to South Korea within the prescribed statutory period and documented through the Foreign Exchange Information System. Profit remittance by a foreign shareholder is generally permissible after external audit, corporate tax settlement, and dividend board approval, but the remitting bank will require evidence of the foreign investment report, tax clearance, and the board resolution declaring dividends. In practice, banks treat cross-border digital dropshipping revenue with heightened scrutiny because of the absence of physical goods movement and the complex aggregation of payments through third-party payment service providers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds arising from dropshipping transactions must be received within 18 months from the date of the export declaration or electronic trade receipt, unless an extension is granted by the authorized foreign exchange bank for justified commercial reasons. There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio to Korean won; exporters may retain foreign currency in an authorized foreign-currency account at a domestic bank. However, each receipt must be reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System, and any conversion into Korean won is executed at the prevailing market rate. For automated e-commerce platforms where sales are aggregated and settled through Stripe or PayPal, the consolidated foreign-currency settlement into a Korean bank account must be fully reconcilable to underlying order data, product descriptions, cross-border customer and logistics records, and, where applicable, customs clearance documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Bank of Korea) acting in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; operational reporting and monitoring are executed through the Foreign Exchange Information System operated by the Korea Federation of Banks and delegated authorized foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Foreign fintech Electronic Money Institutions and offshore digital payment platforms do not constitute authorized foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and cannot directly open Korean won settlement accounts or execute FX operations in South Korea. Such EMIs are legally barred from servicing a domestic corporate entity in a manner equivalent to a licensed bank. The entity may use global payment service providers such as Stripe and PayPal solely as customer collection tools, but all treasury concentration, conversion, and disbursement must ultimately occur through a domestic licensed commercial bank or a locally licensed financial services company with a bank-backed payment gateway partnership.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax top marginal rate plus 10% local corporate income tax surtax (2.4%), producing a 26.4% aggregate top marginal rate on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; progressive national rates are 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 21% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion, and 24% above KRW 300 billion

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax on dividends paid by a Korean domestic corporation to a non-resident beneficial owner under Article 98(1) of the Income Tax Act, plus a 10% local income tax surcharge equal to 2% of the gross dividend, resulting in a 22% aggregate statutory withholding tax rate absent treaty reduction

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50 billion aggregate cross-border related-party transaction amount in a fiscal year is the current statutory threshold under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Enforcement Decree for mandatory simultaneous submission of the Local File and Master File; a Korean group member with prior-year revenue of KRW 100 billion or more and related-party international transactions at or above KRW 50 billion is also within the Master File obligation; group consolidated revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion triggers Country-by-Country Reporting notification; below the thresholds, taxpayers should still prepare contemporaneous TP documentation because the NTS may request it upon audit and absence of documentation shifts the burden of proof on the arm's length price

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply a Gulf Cooperation Council-style Economic Substance Regulations filing, but Korean tax law and BEPS minimum standards apply the economic substance doctrine through the substance-over-form rule, step-transaction doctrine, and Article 17 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs under which the tax authority may deny or recharacterize an unreasonable transaction or incorporation; for the automated dropshipping Yuhan Hoesa, Core Income-Generating Activities CIGA include the actual performance of order-matching algorithms, payment reconciliation, customer relationship intake, logistics control, and contracting decisions; if those activities are performed by the foreign non-resident parent and the Korean entity has no employees, no premises, no board authority, and no operating records, the Korean entity is effectively a shell and the NTS may reattribute taxable income to the real earner, applying severe penalties and treaty anti-abuse rules

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company and economic substance failure

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service is empowered under Article 17(1) of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs to disregard the artificial entity or recharacterize the transaction; a 20% negligence penalty applies for underreporting under Article 47-3(1) and a 40% penalty for fraudulent underreporting under Article 47-3(2) of the Basic Act for National Taxes; criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act carries imprisonment up to five years or a fine up to KRW 100 million, aggravated to three to ten years and a fine of up to three times the tax evaded where the tax deficiency exceeds KRW 500 million.

Director personal liability: Directors may have the corporate veil pierced through fraudulent conveyance rules and the substance-over-form recharacterization, making personal assets available for collection of the Korean corporate tax liability; directors who knowingly authorized the shell structure can be named as joint and several collection targets, blacklisted as delinquent taxpayers, subjected to overseas travel restrictions, and prosecuted as co-principals in tax evasion or fraud; where the director prepares or falsifies corporate books, Article 8 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act imposes imprisonment up to three years or a fine of up to KRW 50 million
Transfer pricing and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: The NTS may make a transfer pricing adjustment under Article 2 and Article 5 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, increase taxable income, add a 20% negligence penalty under Article 47-3(1) of the Basic Act for National Taxes, and impose a 40% penalty for repeated or fraudulent international transaction mispricing; non-compliance with documentation standards permits the NTS to rely on its own estimate and deny burden-of-proof protections; criminal consequences can arise through false books and records under Article 8 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act with penalties of imprisonment up to three years or a fine of up to KRW 50 million, and where the transfer pricing adjustment is part of intentional evasion, Article 3 provides the aggravated imprisonment and fine regime.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or execute the false intercompany pricing may be personally liable to the extent they participated in causing the tax deficiency under Korean joint tort and company law creditor remedies; NTS collection authorities may seize personal bank accounts, vehicles, and real estate where assets were transferred out of the Yuhan Hoesa to shareholders; directors may be designated as delinquent high-value obligors and prohibited from overseas travel or denied foreign exchange conversions; criminal conviction can bar a director from service on Korean corporate boards and expose the director to extradition-assistance proceedings in treaty partner countries
Withholding tax evasion on profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service recharacterizes the mislabeled payments as dividends or royalties under the substance-over-form rule and imposes the full 22% withholding tax, plus a 20% penalty for underwithholding under Article 47-3 of the Basic Act for National Taxes and daily delinquency interest at 0.022%; the failure to file withholding returns may be classified as an intentional concealment of tax under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, carrying imprisonment up to five years or a fine up to KRW 100 million, and up to ten years' imprisonment and a fine of three times the evaded tax when the amount exceeds KRW 500 million.

Director personal liability: A director who participates in the disguised dividend scheme is personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax as a responsible withholding officer under Korean tax collection rules; civil asset restitution extends to personal bank transfers where the dividend was paid into directors' personal accounts; the director and the non-resident beneficial owner may be jointly blacklisted by the National Tax Service, and the Criminal Sentencing Committee may order seizure of director assets, travel bans, and imprisonment; in egregious cross-border cases, the director may face criminal extradition under treaty frameworks and personal directors' disqualification on grounds of breach of fiduciary duty and complicity in tax fraud

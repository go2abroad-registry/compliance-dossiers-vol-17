Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act does not impose any minimum paid-up capital requirement for a Yuhan Hoesa and there is no statutory blocked escrow account rule. Directors are, however, required to deposit the total subscribed contributions with a licensed bank and obtain a capital deposit certificate, which must be presented to the court registry. If a marine bunkering license under the Harbor Transport Business Act is pursued, the applicant may be asked to demonstrate financial solvency, operational assurance capacity, and oil pollution liability insurance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea and its electronic portal HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service (KCS) and its UNI-PASS electronic customs clearance portal (unipass.customs.go.kr) govern all export-side documentation.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must first appoint a domestic tax agent or establish a Korean place of business for Korean tax purposes. The entity then files an online business registration application through the NTS HomeTax portal with the competent district tax office. The submission package includes the foreign certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, evidence of the Korean place of business or valid tax agent appointment, authenticated signature or corporate seal documentation, identification of the responsible representative or agent, and any bunkering or maritime fuel supply statutory licence. The NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) that simultaneously serves as the VAT registration number; there is no separate VAT-only registration procedure. The same BRN must be used for electronic tax invoices, customs export declarations, VAT returns, foreign exchange reporting, and all indirect tax compliance communication with the NTS.

The entity then files an online business registration application through the NTS HomeTax portal with the competent district tax office. Entity_Level_Taxation

None; the Republic of Korea does not permit check-the-box pass-through elections for a Yuhan Hoesa. The entity is taxed as a separate juridical person under the Corporate Tax Act. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Zero KRW threshold for juridical persons; a Korean-incorporated LLC must complete VAT registration before or upon starting business if it makes any taxable supply. The Korean VAT Act does not provide a general monetary registration exemption for juridical persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available when goods are physically exported from Korea, evidenced by an accepted customs export declaration, and when the supply qualifies as an export supply under the Value-Added Tax Act. For marine bunkering and refuelling, the supply of bunker fuel, lubricants, and marine stores to a vessel engaged in international navigation or foreign trade qualifies for the 0% export VAT rate, provided the goods are delivered as stores for use on that vessel, are declared to customs as exports, and the export consideration is repatriated to Korea within the statutory deadline. Qualifying offshore services, including certain exported business-to-business digital and maritime auxiliary services performed outside Korea, are also zero-rated under the separate export-service provisions of the VAT Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration recorded and accepted in UNI-PASS (satisfying the Korean export declaration requirement)
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Yuhan Hoesa bunkering entity
  • Packing list for the bunker fuel or marine lubricant supply
  • Bill of Lading or sea waybill evidencing ocean carriage from Korea
  • Bunker Delivery Receipt / marine fuel delivery note signed by the vessel master or chief engineer and stating vessel name, IMO number, port of supply, and delivered volume
  • Bunker supply contract, voyage charter order, or purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC/FIRC) or Certificate of Export Proceeds Settlement issued by a Korean licensed foreign-exchange bank

Electronic tax invoicing is mandatory for all VAT invoice issuers. Every tax invoice must be created and transmitted through NTS HomeTax or an NTS-approved ASP/ERP channel, and must be delivered to the NTS by no later than the day after the date of issuance. Each transmitted electronic tax invoice receives a unique NTS approval number, creating a real-time anti-fraud clearance trail. No physical stamping or paper fiscal seal is used. B2C cash sales are separately reported through the NTS cash receipt system, and the NTS automatically cross-validates Business Registration Numbers, declared invoice amounts, VAT return data, and customs export declarations.

The NTS will deny the 0% export VAT rate and reclassify the transaction as a taxable domestic supply if the required customs, transport, delivery, and inward-remittance documentation is not obtained and retained contemporaneously or if the foreign-currency export proceeds are not evidenced by a bank certificate within the statutory 180-day repatriation window. The denied VAT is assessed at 10% on the supply, together with statutory additional tax for underreporting or unreported tax, monthly overdue interest, and, in cases involving false or fraudulent export documentation, aggravated penalty tax and criminal exposure under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including fines and imprisonment. Customs-side irregularities may also result in suspension of export-ledger privileges, denial of future zero-rated treatment, and penalties imposed by Korea Customs Service.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea applies a substantially open current account regime but retains a managed floating capital account framework. Current account payments for goods, services, and ordinary trade settlement are freely convertible and can be executed through licensed foreign-exchange banks, requiring only accurate purpose-of-payment declarations and compliance with documentary rules. Capital account transactions such as equity injections, shareholder loans, and the issuance of foreign-currency debt are generally permitted for foreign investors but must be routed through an authorized foreign-exchange bank and reported to the Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance via FEIN. The Korean won is freely usable onshore for trading, but offshore KRW clearing is restricted and usually requires onshore bank participation; offshore NDF activity exists but onshore corporates must prove genuine trade exposure. Profit remittance is permitted through either the Foreign Investment Promotion Act or the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act regimes, provided that the initial foreign investment was properly registered and the remittance is supported by audited evidence of earnings. There is no mandatory percentage conversion of export proceeds into KRW; exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic foreign-exchange accounts, but all export proceeds must ultimately be repatriated and reported to Korean banking authorities within the statutory settlement window. The capital verification process is strict, requiring the bank to certify the actual receipt of foreign currency against a registered foreign investment declaration. Thus, while Korea is not a hard-currency-control jurisdiction, the MFEO and BOK impose rigorous transaction-level reporting, and any deviation from the declared purpose of a cross-border flow can trigger civil and criminal penalties under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from general cargo shipped from South Korea must be repatriated to a licensed domestic foreign-exchange bank within two (2) years from the date of export declaration; for shipbuilding, offshore plant, heavy machinery, and manufacturing-intensive projects including marine bunkering vessels and fuel infrastructure, the statutory settlement window extends to five (5) years. The full invoice value must be received; however, the mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent, as Korea does not require conversion into KRW. Exporters may hold receipts indefinitely in an approved bank foreign-currency account, subject to quarterly FEIN reporting and supporting export documentation. If the buyer fails to remit within the permissible period, prior approval from the Ministry of Economy and Finance via the Bank of Korea is mandatory to avoid administrative fines.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), and the Korea Customs Service (KCS), with all foreign-exchange reporting executed through the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Statutory non-viability for full treasury operations. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the FSC licensing framework, no offshore fintech EMI or virtual-bank patchwork may open a KRW-denominated corporate account for a Korean resident entity or act as a settlement institution for capital account flows. Domestic e-money companies may only perform limited pass-through functions and cannot substitute for the licensed foreign-exchange bank required for FDI registration, capital verification, and trade payment reporting. Therefore, the Korea business must utilize a traditional tier-1 commercial bank; overseas EMIs are confined to supporting non-resident FX conversions outside regulated KRW clearing rails and may not be used for statutory import/export settlement or profit remittance reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital must be injected via a licensed foreign-exchange bank using a SWIFT MT103 wire from the overseas corporate bank account. The remitter must use the foreign direct investment (FDI) declaration approved by the Korean Foreign Exchange Bank and filed with the Bank of Korea through FEIN. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Statutory non-viability for full treasury operations. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax on taxable income above KRW 300 billion, with lower brackets of 9%, 19%, and 21%; a local income tax surcharge equal to 10% of national corporate income tax applies, producing a top all-in marginal rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate outbound dividend withholding (20% national withholding plus 2% local income tax surcharge) on distributions to non-resident shareholders; applicable bilateral DTA rates commonly reduce this to 5-15%, with lower rates available for qualifying substantial shareholdings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory Master File and Local File preparation and production within 45 days of a National Tax Service request; Country-by-Country Report filing triggers at KRW 1 trillion consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations on ordinary Korean bunkering companies, but economic substance remains a dispositive tax issue. Korean corporate residence is determined by the place of head office and effective management; treaty relief requires beneficial ownership and satisfaction of the principal-purpose test; and for a marine bunkering entity, the core income-generating activities (CIGA)—fuel procurement, storage, physical bunker delivery, invoicing, and treasury decisions—must be performed and documented in Korea. Artificial offshore arrangements, non-resident shell shareholders, and substance-less intermediary companies are vulnerable to GAAR, CFC attribution, treaty denial, and recharacterization of the entire transaction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Marine Fuel Tax Evasion / False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 9: imprisonment not exceeding 3 years, or 5 years where the evaded tax exceeds KRW 300 million, and a fine not exceeding 3 times the tax evaded. The Framework Act on National Taxes adds 20% negligence and 40% fraudulent under-reporting surcharges, daily overdue surcharges, and VAT false-invoice penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for damages to the state under Korean Commercial Act Article 401. The National Tax Collection Act Article 12 permits secondary collection against directors who transferred or concealed company assets; the National Tax Service may blacklist the director and impose an overseas travel ban once unpaid taxes exceed KRW 30 million; the decision-maker faces criminal imprisonment, personal fines, and seizure of personal assets.

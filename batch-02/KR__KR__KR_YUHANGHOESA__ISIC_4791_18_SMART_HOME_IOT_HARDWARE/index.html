Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, acting through the Home Tax Service (HTS) online electronic portal located at hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that engages in or owns a Korean business entity must ensure that the Korean domestic entity, including a Yuhan Hoesa limited liability company, files for a Business Registration Number with the local district tax office or through the Home Tax Service within 20 days after business commencement. For a foreign entity that itself carries on trade or business through a permanent establishment in Korea, an ex officio registration as a foreign corporation is required through the competent tax office, accompanied by the designation of a domestic tax representative, the articles of incorporation, certificate of foreign registration or equivalent authentication, power of attorney, and proof of appointment of the representative. The Business Registration Number issued by the NTS serves as the unified corporate tax identification number and the Value Added Tax registration number for indirect tax purposes. For foreign non-resident entities without a Korean place of business, no Korean TIN is obtained unless the entity is required to file Korean income tax or VAT in respect of domestic supplies; in such cases, a withholding agent or domestic tax representative must complete the relevant registration via HTS before any tax filing obligation arises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None under any US-style check-the-box election; a Korean Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic corporation taxed at the entity level on its worldwide income. No hybrid mismatch elections are available. A statutory group tax consolidation election may be applied by a domestic controlling parent over its wholly-owned domestic subsidiaries subject to approval under Article 50 of the Corporate Tax Act, but this does not alter the entity-level classification of the LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold; every person who independently supplies goods or services in the course of business, whether resident or non-resident, must register for VAT with the National Tax Service. A Simplified Taxpayer regime may apply to small businesses with annual taxable supplies below KRW 80,000,000, but VAT registration remains mandatory and the simplified regime does not exempt the taxpayer from reporting obligations.

All VAT-registered businesses are mandated to issue electronic tax invoices (e-invoices) for supplies to other businesses through the Home Tax Service (HTS). The issuance details, including supply value and calculated VAT, must be transmitted electronically to the NTS in real time or no later than the next business day using HTS API integration or the HTS web platform. Each e-invoice contains a mandatory transaction-specific invoice number and a generated electronic verification code; there is no separate physical fiscal stamp. A penalty of up to 2% of the supply value for non-issuance or late issuance of the e-invoice applies, and failure to submit correct data may trigger further administrative penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates an open-market regime with the current account fully convertible and the capital account subject to registration or reporting. For an IoT hardware retail Yuhan Hoesa, import payments for inventory, software licensing, and platform service fees are current account transactions and are freely permitted after the submission of standard supporting documents to a licensed foreign exchange bank of the entity's choice. Equity injections by non-resident shareholders are capital account transactions that require advance FDI registration under Article 23 of the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA); once registered, conversion into KRW is automatic, and no individual central bank approval is needed. Profit remittances via shareholder dividends require tax clearance and the submission of a foreign exchange purchase confirmation to the settlement bank; the key limitation is the documentary burden, not permission. Korea does not impose export proceeds surrender quotas; however, all export receipts must be either retained in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or converted to KRW within a statutory settlement period of 15 months from the export declaration date, and each conversion must be reported to the settlement bank in the Electronic Foreign Exchange Information System. Treasury liquidity is therefore highly mobile for bona fide trade flows, while regulatory scrutiny is concentrated on UBO transparency, trade substance, and proper tax clearance before capital account transactions such as dividend remittances are completed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 33(1) of the Enforcement Decree of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export receipts must be collected within 15 months from the date of export declaration, with extensions up to 6 years available for capital goods or defense-related transactions. The collected foreign currency proceeds may be deposited into a Foreign Currency Account at a domestic DMB; the mandatory conversion ratio into KRW is 0%, meaning full retention in foreign currency is permitted. However, any optional conversion to KRW must be executed at a licensed foreign exchange bank and submitted through the bank's e-reporting system under the proper trade-related foreign exchange purpose code. Balances held in the FCA are subject to quarterly reporting to the Bank of Korea through the designated bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

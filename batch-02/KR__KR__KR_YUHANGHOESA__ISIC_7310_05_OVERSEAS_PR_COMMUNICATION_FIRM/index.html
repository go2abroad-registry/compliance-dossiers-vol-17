Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Commercial Act mandates a physical office address in Korea. A virtual office or commercial mailbox can be registered for media/PR businesses, but the National Tax Service and Korean banks apply a strict substance-over-form analysis. Where no staff, business equipment, or client-facing activity is located at the address, the NTS may reject business registration and banks will refuse corporate account opening. Therefore, a virtual office is permissible only when accompanied by a lease agreement, utility bills, and a qualified local resident agent for tax notices; without these, it is a serious red flag and may also invalidate the Korean corporate registration.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is prescribed for a Yuhan Hoesa. The Articles of Incorporation must state the subscribed capital amount. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked capital subscription bank account in the Republic of Korea, and the bank must issue a certificate of deposit confirming the paid-in capital before the registration application is submitted. The deposited funds remain blocked and may not be withdrawn for corporate operations until the court issues the Certificate of Registration of Incorporation. In-kind contributions, if any, must be described and valued in the Articles in accordance with the Commercial Act.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No revenue-based VAT registration exemption for corporate businesses; a Yuhan Hoesa must apply for Business Registration through HomeTax upon commencement even if initial turnover is zero. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for goods physically exported from Korea after an export declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service, and for services supplied to non-resident persons in the course of earning foreign exchange, including overseas public relations and communication-agency services, as long as the recipient is outside Korea and the consideration is collected through a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank. The offshore digital-services zero rating requires the foreign recipient to have no Korean place of business and the payment to be received in foreign currency; the claim must be supported by the service contract and foreign-exchange receipt.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration Receipt (Export Declaration Confirmation) issued on UNI-PASS for physical exports
  • Transport evidence: Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill and any required cargo manifest
  • Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation or Inward Remittance Voucher from a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank, evidencing receipt and purchase of foreign currency export proceeds

E-invoicing is mandatory for all business-to-business VAT supplies. The supplier must issue an electronic tax invoice in the NTS HomeTax electronic invoice system by the 10th day of the month following the taxable supply month; the system issues a digital audit stamp and approval number. There is no separate real-time fiscal device pre-clearance requirement, but invoices must be transmitted to NTS electronically, and cancellation or correction requires a blocking or rejection code under NTS rules.

If export zero-rating is claimed without the statutory documentation, NTS will retroactively deny the 0% rate and assess VAT at 10%, with national-tax additional surcharges of up to 40% for unjustified or fraudulent refunds and 20% for negligent underreporting, plus late-payment interest and penalties for false e-tax invoices under the Value-Added Tax Act. False customs declarations are also subject to fines or imprisonment under the Customs Act, and failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory deadline may trigger penalties under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The tax statute of limitations is generally five years and is extended to ten years for fraudulent or non-arm’s-length transactions.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a heavily liberalised but strictly supervised foreign exchange system. Current account receipts and payments for services such as international media relations, retainer fees, royalties and reimbursed expenses are generally free to move across borders without approval, provided the underlying trade is evidenced and the transaction is handled by a licensed foreign exchange bank. Capital account items are less liberalised: inbound equity injection, capital increase, shareholder loans and profit repatriation are subject to BOK reporting, beneficial ownership declarations and tax withholding verification. The Korean won is not freely deliverable offshore, so no non-bank intermediary can execute true KRW clearing outside the domestic banking system. In practical treasury terms, the corporate must open a main KRW operating account at a domestic bank, convert foreign inward remittances at market rates, ensure that all outward remittances carry correct foreign exchange purpose codes, and preserve source documents for at least five years. Dividend or liquidation proceeds can be transferred out only after the bank has confirmed the existence of cumulative investment registration, payment of capital gains tax if applicable, and notarised board approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export service proceeds are required to be collected within six months from the invoice or service-performance date. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, there is no per-transaction fixed surrender percentage; rather, 100% of export receipts must be repatriated and cleared through a licensed foreign exchange bank. The bank will credit the amount to a foreign currency export account or automatically convert it to KRW. In the absence of a separately approved foreign currency export account, the receiving bank applies a de facto 100% KRW conversion at the bank's market rate. If a pre-registered foreign-currency treasury account is used, the entity can retain the foreign currency for forward hedging, but every subsequent FX conversion must be reported in BOK's FX information system.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low and legally constrained. Under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only institutions licensed as foreign exchange banks by the Financial Services Commission may execute KRW cross-border settlements and corporates fund transfers. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex and Currencycloud operate as e-money institutions outside that licence and are not eligible to settle through BOK-Wire+ or provide full KRW current accounts to Korean-resident legal entities. The Korean LLC must rely on at least one Tier-1 domestic bank account for KRW clearing, capital operations, trade disbursements and FX hedging. An offshore EMI may be used only as an ancillary non-KRW collection lockbox for clients in limited corridors without SWIFT access, never as the primary treasury rail.

The remitting parent transfers funds by SWIFT MT103; the domestic bank credits the inward USD or other permitted currency to the capital escrow account only after the wire contains a clear purpose classification of equity contribution, not trade invoice. The Korean external accountant then issues a capital verification certificate and the funds are converted into KRW at the bank's market rate and swept into the operating account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top national Corporate Tax Act Article 55 progressive rate (9% up to KRW 200m; 19% from KRW 200m to KRW 20bn; 21% from KRW 20bn to KRW 300bn; 24% above KRW 300bn) plus 10% Local Income Tax surcharge on the national corporate tax for a combined top effective marginal rate of 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate withholding on outbound dividends paid by a Korean Yuhan Hoesa to a non-resident corporate shareholder (20% corporate income tax under Corporate Tax Act Article 98 and 2% local income tax surcharge), reducible by bilateral DTA rates that are commonly 5% for shareholdings of at least 10% and 15% for portfolio dividends, with other negotiated rates before the applicable OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when cross-border related-party transactions with foreign related parties exceed KRW 5 billion in the fiscal year; Master File is mandatory when the aggregate cross-border related-party transaction amount exceeds KRW 10 billion and total revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion, and the documentation must be submitted within 60 days of a request by the National Tax Service; for a Yuhan Hoesa PR agency, intercompany retainers, media buying recharges, management fees, and intellectual property royalties all count toward the threshold

Economic substance scope: Although Korea does not operate Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, Korean tax law applies substance-over-form doctrine under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Law on the Coordination of International Tax Affairs, and treaty benefits are subject to the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test and beneficial ownership analysis; for an overseas PR communications Yuhan Hoesa, the Core Income-Generating Activities include negotiation of client contracts, creative content development, campaign execution, media relationship management, assumption of reputational and currency risk, and final authority over spending; a Korean entity without employees, office, bank accounts, managerial capacity, or real client-facing functions will be treated as a shell, its intercompany invoices disregarded, and its income reallocated to the jurisdiction of the actual decision-makers.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3: imprisonment for not more than 5 years or a fine not exceeding three times the evaded tax, aggravated to 7 years when the evaded tax exceeds KRW 300 million; additional charges under the Framework Act on National Taxes include 20% for false non-filing, 10% for under-reporting, and 0.03% daily late-payment surcharge; the NTS may also revoke the company's electronic tax invoice registration and refer the case to the public prosecutor

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under judicial application of Korean Commercial Act principles and the Framework Act on National Taxes secondary liability provisions; a director who signed the fraudulent return may be personally assessed for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties, personal bank accounts and real estate may be attached, the director may be placed on the NTS tax-delinquent blacklist and subjected to an overseas travel ban, and criminal conviction can result in 5 to 7 years imprisonment and disqualification from serving as a director in South Korea
Withholding Tax Evasion on Outbound Dividends, Interest, and Royalties

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act Articles 98, 98-2 and 98-3, together with Local Tax Act Article 103-10, impose 22% withholding tax on the gross remittance; failure to withhold makes the Yuhan Hoesa the statutory withholding agent liable for the uncollected tax, plus the Framework Act on National Taxes late-payment surcharge (0.03% per day) and an additional 10% to 20% penalty; deliberate collection and diversion of government withholding tax is punished under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act as embezzlement of national tax and carries imprisonment exposure of up to 3 years or a criminal fine in addition to full restitution

Director personal liability: A director who instigated or knowingly authorized the under-withholding may be held civilly liable for the withheld tax as a fiduciary breach under Korean Commercial Act Article 399, with personal assets subject to restitution and attachment; the NTS can impose a travel ban and blacklist the director as a serious tax defaulter; because the source tax was never paid, the non-resident recipient remains taxable, and if the NTS determines the offshore arrangement was a sham, the director faces criminal indictment for embezzlement of tax and fraudulent evasion, which pierces the limited-liability shield of the company
Shell Company Substance Failure and Transfer Pricing Mismatch

Statutory sanction: Law on the Coordination of International Tax Affairs permits the NTS to disregard the shell entity and re-determine the arm's-length price using any appropriate method; where reportable international transactions are not disclosed or supporting TP documentation is not produced, the NTS may impose a penalty tax related to the unreported international transaction amount, apply a 20% understatement penalty on the resulting tax deficiency, and, for refusal of evidence, use aggregate estimation; repeated or fraudulent cross-border TP manipulation is referred for criminal tax prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act with imprisonment exposure up to 5 years, aggravated to 7 years for large deficiencies

Director personal liability: When a Yuhan Hoesa is only a facade and foreign shareholders exercise actual management through the directors, Korean courts may disregard separate legal personality and order the directors and beneficial shareholders to pay the company's tax liabilities out of personal assets; civil liability includes damages to the company under Korean Commercial Act Article 399 as well as unjust enrichment claims; the NTS may issue a demand against the director under the secondary tax liability regime, impose a travel ban and tax-delinquent publication, and, in aggravated cases, the director may be named as the principal offender in a criminal fraudulent-evasion indictment and sentenced to up to 7 years of imprisonment with mandatory civil restitution of the evaded tax and penalties.

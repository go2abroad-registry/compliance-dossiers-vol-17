Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election. A Yuhan Hoesa is per se a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and is taxed at the entity level; it cannot elect pass-through treatment. No hybrid mismatch election exists, and hybrid mismatch outcomes are adjusted mandatorily under the Act on Adjustment of Taxes of International Transactions. The only tax-group election available is the Korean consolidated corporate filing regime for a resident parent with wholly owned resident domestic subsidiaries, and that regime does not alter the separate legal or tax classification of the Yuhan Hoesa.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory turnover threshold in KRW for corporations; VAT registration is mandatory upon business commencement under Article 5 of the VAT Act, and the 10-digit Business Registration Number constitutes the VAT registration. Simplified taxable person status is available only to individual business operators with annual taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80 million, and a Korean Yuhan Hoesa LLC does not qualify for that simplified regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export supplies under the Korean VAT Act include goods physically exported from Korea to foreign countries; services supplied to a foreign country or to a non-resident where the place of supply and actual consumption are outside Korea; international maritime and air carriage of goods or passengers; and other foreign-currency-earning supplies prescribed by Presidential Decree. Offshore digital services qualify for 0% VAT when the recipient is a non-resident, the service is not legally or practically consumed in Korea, and the consideration is paid in foreign currency to a Korean bank account. Any service consumed or used in Korea is subject to the standard 10% VAT even if invoiced to a foreign person, so a single family office must evidence the overseas place of benefit to substantiate zero-rating.

  • Korea Customs Service Electronic Export Declaration approved in the UNI-PASS export clearance system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the goods description, quantity, and export value
  • Foreign Exchange Bank Confirmation of Foreign Currency Settlement or Receipt (inward foreign exchange remittance certificate) issued under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act
  • Bank statement or Foreign Exchange Information System (FES) record showing that the export proceeds were received and settled in Korea

All tax invoices must be issued as mandatory e-Tax Invoices under Article 32 of the VAT Act and transmitted to the National Tax Service through the HomeTax e-tax invoice system no later than the day after issue, unless the transaction is a B2C cash receipt eligible for the real-time cash receipt mechanism. The taxpayer's ERP/accounting system must transmit the e-tax invoice data through the NTS-certified XML/API channel or the HomeTax web portal; Korea does not operate a pre-clearance digital stamping model. Anti-fraud integrity is achieved through mandatory sequential e-tax invoice transmission, NTS data matching, automated VAT return reconciliation, and, for B2C cash transactions, real-time cash receipt device transmission to NTS. Late, false, or non-transmitted e-tax invoices trigger VAT Act penalty taxes and, for intentional falsification, criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Export zero-rating claims are subjected to NTS matching with UNI-PASS customs declarations, bank foreign exchange records, and e-tax invoice data. If the customs export declaration, bill of lading, or foreign exchange receipt is missing, mismatched, or insufficient to trace the shipment to the repatriated payment, the NTS rejects the 0% rate and reassesses VAT at the standard 10% on the export consideration, with statutory interest from the original VAT filing date under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Penalty tax is additionally imposed for the issuance of false e-tax invoices or for fraudulent VAT refund claims under the VAT Act, and intentional manipulative export refund schemes are prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, which carries imprisonment and fines up to the statutory evaded-tax multiples. Retrospective clawback can extend up to ten years for fraudulent refund activity, and the assessed VAT, penalties, and interest are collected through national tax enforcement and asset seizure procedures.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea applies a liberalized but supervised foreign exchange regime. Current account transactions are effectively freely convertible and are subject to post-verification by domestic foreign exchange banks, while capital account transactions require filing or declaration with the Bank of Korea under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Foreign direct investment, investment in private equity funds, and share subscription transmissions for a single family office are classified as capital account transactions; they must be filed through a domestic bank with Know-Your-Customer documents and the underlying investment contract. Gross proceeds from liquidation, dividends, and interest may be repatriated after tax clearance, but banks require evidence of the initial foreign investment registration and the report of the transaction in the Foreign Exchange Information System. No surrender requirement exists for inbound or outbound remittances, but all cross-border transfers above USD 5,000 per transaction must be reported to the bank for monthly transmission to the Bank of Korea. The main convertibility hurdles are not administrative in terms of conversion but documentary: a UBO with heavy beneficial ownership concentration, unexplained family wealth from prior digital export receipts, and insufficient proof of economic substance for the holding entity all serve as de facto blockers. Practical convertibility is High once the entity has opened a corporate currency account and established an approved FIPA foreign investment registration number, allowing full conversion of USD into KRW and repatriation in USD without surrender for a single family office. A single-family office is expressly not a licensed financial company, and it avoids the Foreign Exchange Concentration Base System, but the restriction on CIKs may create a payment rail bottleneck. Overall, South Korea permits convertibility subject to robust FIU reporting, true economic-beneficiary disclosure, and demonstrable underlying assets, making it a transparent, upper-band OECD jurisdiction with no centralised blacklist risk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected within one year from the date of export declaration, extendable to three years with Bank of Korea approval for capital goods export, and zero percent are required to be surrendered; the full amount may be credited to a foreign currency account held with a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank. Once credited, conversion to KRW is optional. All receipts must be settled through a domestic designated foreign exchange bank and filed in the bank's official settlement records to satisfy the export proceeds repatriation timeline.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Ministry of Economy and Finance delegated foreign exchange administration protocols

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and non-resident electronic money institutions are legally barred from servicing a domestic corporate treasury in South Korea without a banking business license under the Banking Act and an Electronic Financial Business Provider registration under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Foreign EMIs such as Mercury, Intergiro, or Airwallex cannot hold KRW clearing accounts, cannot issue local IBANs, and cannot perform cross-border FX brokerage with the Bank of Korea's payment system. The only viable path for fintech-style rails is through a domestic licensed bank's open banking APIs, so the overall feasibility rating is Low for offshore EMIs. Domestic online banks like Toss Bank operate under full banking licenses but focus on retail and do not offer the multi-currency account structure required for a single family office, making them impractical for private investment holding and securities custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a single-family office foreign direct investment, the family office will wire USD or KRW via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's account abroad to an escrow account at KB Kookmin or Hana Bank. The bank applies the appropriate Bank of Korea Foreign Exchange Information System settlement-purpose code for direct investment capital increase based on the FIPA notification. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs and non-resident electronic money institutions are legally barred from servicing a domestic corporate treasury in South Korea without a banking business license under the Banking Act and an Electronic Financial Business Provider registration under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Foreign EMIs such as Mercury, Intergiro, or Airwallex cannot hold KRW clearing accounts, cannot issue local IBANs, and cannot perform cross-border FX brokerage with the Bank of Korea's payment system. The only viable path for fintech-style rails is through a domestic licensed bank's open banking APIs, so the overall feasibility rating is Low for offshore EMIs. Domestic online banks like Toss Bank operate under full banking licenses but focus on retail and do not offer the multi-currency account structure required for a single family office, making them impractical for private investment holding and securities custody.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50 million for the annual International Transactions Statement; KRW 5 billion of cross-border related-party transactions for the Local File; KRW 100 billion group consolidated revenue for the Master File (as applicable under the International Tax Adjustment Act).

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations registration, but substance is enforced through statutory anti-abuse doctrines, the beneficial ownership rules in the Income Tax Act, and Article 4 of the International Tax Adjustment Act which disregards contracts whose legal form does not reflect economic reality. A single-family office must demonstrate actual economic presence through office premises, local board decisions, identified employees, and Korean-based custody and portfolio management; otherwise treaty benefits can be denied and investment income recharacterized as Korean-source business income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Unreported Offshore Investment Income

Statutory sanction: Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to 3 times the evaded tax when the concealed amount exceeds KRW 300 million; plus 40% fraudulent underreporting penalty and daily overdue surcharge.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil under Article 401 of the Commercial Act for willful misconduct or gross negligence; directors can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate tax and penalties under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act, face overseas travel blacklisting and asset seizures, and may be sentenced to criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Round-Trip Fee Arrangements

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service adjusts taxable income to the arm's length price under Article 5 of the International Tax Adjustment Act; disallowed fees attract corporation tax, 10% ordinary underreporting penalty, and a documentation penalty under Article 11 of the same Act; interest accrues daily.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the sham fee structure breach fiduciary duty and are liable for civil restitution under Article 401 of the Commercial Act; if the arrangement is found intentional, directors may also face criminal tax-evasion charges and joint liability for tax deficiencies.
Withholding Tax Default on Outbound Distributions

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is liable for the unwithheld tax under the Income Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes; a 10% penalty on the un-withheld amount is imposed, plus 0.022% daily late-payment interest, and a separate penalty for failure to submit the withholding return.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and jointly liable for withheld taxes collected but not remitted, under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act; personal assets may be seized, travel restrictions enforced, and diverting withheld tax may constitute criminal embezzlement under Article 355 of the Korean Criminal Code.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korean law prescribes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. The subscribed capital stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid before registration, with the deposit placed in a bank escrow or blocked capital account at a licensed Korean bank. The bank's deposit certificate must be attached to the court registration filing. If the LLC is not registered, the deposit arrangement is void. For non-resident investors, inward remittance must be reported to the receiving bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and, if applicable, under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the competent fire department after inspection of extinguishing systems, exit routes, emergency lighting and sprinkler systems; required when total floor area exceeds the Fire Act thresholds. A structural load inspection certificate and building use approval must be obtained from the local government, with particular attention to high rack storage and seismic loads. Occupancy and operation require an occupational safety clearance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including a workplace risk assessment, hazard communication for alcohol vapors and manual handling risks, worker safety training, evacuation drills, and a fire prevention plan coordinated with the local civil defense authority.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No Korean Won threshold for corporate entities: a domestic Yuhan Hoesa LLC is required to register for VAT/Business Operator Registration immediately upon commencement of business, irrespective of turnover. Unincorporated small operators have simplified taxpayer statuses, but these do not apply to a corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% VAT applies when goods are exported from Korea to a foreign purchaser; a valid Korea Customs Service electronic export declaration must evidence the actual departure, and in bonded-warehouse wholesale trade, the wines/spirits must move from the bonded warehouse to export under customs supervision. The title, risk, and economic ownership must pass to the non-resident counterparty, and the export sale must be supported by the foreign-exchange receipt within the prescribed deadline. For qualifying offshore digital services, zero-rating requires that the service is supplied to, and received by, a person outside Korea and consumed entirely outside Korea; the supplier must retain contracts, work orders, and usage logs. Mere shipment to an overseas intermediary without actual export, or goods diverted into domestic consumption, cannot be zero-rated.

  • Korea Customs Service electronic Export Declaration (UNI-PASS export clearance record)
  • Original Bill of Lading (or air waybill) evidencing physical shipment from Korea
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act
  • Commercial invoice and packing list corresponding to the export declaration
  • Bonded warehouse customs transfer/export approval record (for wines/spirits under bonded trade licence)

All VAT-registered businesses, including the Yuhan Hoesa LLC, must issue electronic tax invoices via the NTS HomeTax system. Each B2B electronic tax invoice must be transmitted to the NTS within the day immediately following the supply date. No physical stamping or pre-clearance approval is required; the NTS performs real-time data clearance using the national e-Tax Invoice network and cross-matches invoice data with Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS records for bonded trade. Accredited digital certificates are mandatory, and third-party service providers may use NTS-certified transmission channels. Failure to issue, late issuance, or false issuance of e-Tax Invoices triggers additional tax and digital anti-fraud penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act.

Zero-rated export claims are automatically cross-checked by the NTS against Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS departure records and foreign exchange bank reporting. If any mandatory document is missing or inconsistent, the 0% rate is reversed and VAT is assessed at 10% on the total export consideration, plus the additional tax penalties and interest prescribed by the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act. False export invoices, circular transactions, or fraudulent export declarations constitute tax evasion and may result in criminal sanctions and imprisonment under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, revocation of bonded-warehouse licences, and retrospective clawback of all VAT refunds previously claimed on the zero-rated transactions for the statutory limitation period. Non-receipt of foreign exchange within the deadline without an approved extension can also invalidate the zero-rating and trigger fines under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea is an OECD member with a substantially liberalized, yet still documentation-heavy, foreign-exchange regime. The Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible on the onshore interbank market but is not freely deliverable offshore. Current-account transactions, including trade settlement for bonded wine and spirits, service fees, royalties, and salaries, are generally permissible without quantitative ceilings, provided the authorized foreign-exchange bank receives the underlying contract, invoice, transport documents, and customs declaration. Capital-account transactions such as foreign direct investment, equity injections, intercompany loans, and profit remittances require a report or prior declaration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. In practice, repatriation of dividends is achievable after corporate tax and withholding tax requirements are met, but the treasury must demonstrate that the profits are real, audited, and properly distributed. Export proceeds must be repatriated into Korea within the statutory time frame and either credited to a licensed foreign-currency account or converted into KRW. The Korean tax and customs authority examines bonded wine and spirits flows through the UNI-PASS system, meaning global treasury transfers must align precisely with customs invoices, LC documentation, and KCS bonded warehouse records. There are no current-account quotas, but the KRW serves as a managed float, and the compliance cost is primarily procedural: purpose codes, underlying exposure, entity verification, and post-event reporting to the Bank of Korea and MOEF.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected and repatriated to an authorized foreign-exchange bank in Korea within 18 months from the date of export declaration. There is no fixed statutory percentage surrender ratio into KRW; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a designated foreign-currency account or voluntarily convert it to KRW at the onshore market rate. However, 100% of the export proceeds must physically return to a Korean authorized bank account within the 18-month window unless prior advance approval is obtained from the MOEF or Bank of Korea for exceptional deferment. For bonded warehouse trade, the same repatriation obligation applies, and any retention of export proceeds abroad without proper reporting is treated as an unresolved foreign-exchange violation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) is the central bank and custodian of foreign-exchange settlement statistics, while the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) retains policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Korea Customs Service (KCS) separately supervises cross-border trade declarations and bonded warehouse controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and legally non-viable for this treasury blueprint. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed as authorized foreign-exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and cannot perform the mandatory reporting to the Bank of Korea, MOEF, or Korea Customs Service for bonded import settlements, capital injections, or VAT/duty payments. The alternate payment service provider license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act is limited to domestic payment facilitation, not cross-border corporate treasury or bonded warehouse clearing. Therefore, all inbound equity, trade settlement, and outbound payment flows must route through a Korean commercial bank or a licensed foreign-exchange bank branch with physical presence in Korea. EMIs may be used only as a supplementary information layer, never as a legal account-holding treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital must first be declared through a designated authorized foreign-exchange bank in Korea under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The foreign shareholder should remit via SWIFT MT103 with an investment remittance purpose code and the FDI report number in the reference field. The Korean bank will credit the funds to the company’s capital escrow account in KRW or an approved foreign currency account; if received in a foreign currency, the company may convert to KRW at the onshore market rate. After the deposit is verified, a certified public accountant issues a capital contribution verification report used to update the company registry. Profit distributions follow a separate route: audited financial statements, tax clearance, dividend withholding tax settlement, and a corporate resolution of the Yuhan Hoesa must be presented to the bank so that the dividend can be converted and remitted abroad through the SWIFT platform with a valid royalty or dividend settlement purpose code.For imports of aged Scotch and Bordeaux, the primary clearing rail is the irrevocable documentary letter of credit opened by the Korean bank in favor of the foreign supplier, using SWIFT MT700 to issue the LC, MT799 for amendments, and MT103 for final reimbursement at maturity. For lower-risk repeat transactions, direct SWIFT MT103 wire payments against pro-forma invoices and scanned shipping documents may be used. Documentary collections on D/P and D/A terms are acceptable for established counterparties, with the presenting bank instructed to release documents only against payment or acceptance. All customs duty and VAT liabilities associated with bonded warehouse operations must be settled through the Korean bank’s virtual account integrated with the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

21% national Corporate Income Tax top bracket for taxable income exceeding KRW 20 billion; the combined top effective rate is 23.1% after adding the 10% Local Income Tax surcharge (national rate brackets: 9% up to KRW 200 million; 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% above KRW 20 billion).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% on outbound dividends paid by a Korean Yuhan Hoesa to a non-resident shareholder, composed of 20% national withholding tax plus 2% Local Income Tax surcharge, unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty (DTA), typically to 5%, 10%, or 15% depending on the treaty, the shareholding threshold, and the recipient's beneficial ownership and holding period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in aggregate annual foreign related-party transactions triggers the mandatory Local File; KRW 50 billion triggers the Master File; consolidated group revenue of KRW 1 trillion triggers the Country-by-Country Report. The threshold is measured by the value of intercompany purchases, sales, loans, royalties, and service fees during the fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code, but the corporate veil and treaty benefit claims are scrutinized under the substance-over-form doctrine, the OECD BEPS beneficial ownership standards, and Article 51 of the International Tax Coordination Act (GAAR). A Yuhan Hoesa engaged in bonded wine and spirits trade must demonstrate real Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea: contractual negotiation, purchase buying and inventory decision-making, bonded warehouse lease and license holding, physical custody and risk of loss for beverage inventory, staff payroll, board meetings, accounting records, and portfolio risk management. A business that performs only booking entries in Korea while foreign shareholders or affiliates manage the wine sourcing, marketing, pricing, and financing from outside Korea is vulnerable to being treated as a shell company; the NTS may deny treaty protection, disallow related-party deductions, and pierce the entity to hold the ultimate beneficial owner and directors personally liable.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Customs Duty Evasion and Illicit Bonded Diversion

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Customs Act provisions against false declarations and unlawful removal from customs bonded areas; the NTS/Customs authorities assess customs duty, individual consumption tax, VAT, and a surcharge of up to 40% of the evaded amount. Criminal penalties under the Customs Act and the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Economic Crimes include imprisonment for up to 5 years and fines of up to 5 times the amount evaded. The bonded warehouse license is revoked, and import-export privileges are suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in, order, or knowingly benefit from the bonded diversion are joint and severally liable for the unpaid duties, additional taxes, and penalties. Civil seizure of personal assets, bank accounts, and residences is permitted. Directors are subject to criminal imprisonment up to 5 years and, where the evasion exceeds KRW 500 million, up to 10 years in aggravated cases. The NTS imposes immigration control blacklisting and a travel ban on the director and beneficial owner, and the company's name and the director's personal identity are published as a serious tax defaulter.
International Profit Shifting / Transfer Pricing Offense

Statutory sanction: The NTS makes an arm's length transfer pricing adjustment under Articles 4 and 5 of the International Tax Coordination Act, increases the Korean taxable income, and imposes an additional tax of 10% for underreporting or 30% to 40% for fraudulent manipulation under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Criminal sanctions under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act include imprisonment for up to 2 years or a fine not exceeding 3 times the tax evaded; repeated or large-scale evasion involving more than KRW 500 million carries imprisonment of up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the director signed a false corporate tax return or instructed the use of a sham intermediary; directors may be held personally liable for the additional tax, penalties, and interest via civil judgment under the Commercial Act. The NTS attaches personal bank accounts and overseas estate assets through tax collection procedures, imposes a travel ban and immigration blacklisting, and refers directors to prosecutors for criminal fraud. In aggravated restitution cases, the court orders personal assets forfeited or sold to satisfy the evaded corporate tax.
Shell Entity and Abusive Treaty Shopping / Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Article 51 of the International Tax Coordination Act empowers the NTS to disregard the abusive arrangement, deny the treaty benefit, and recharacterize the payments as taxable dividends, interest, or ordinary income. The company is assessed additional CORPORATE income tax, withholding tax at the statutory 22% rate, and punitive penalties up to 40% for fraudulent underreporting. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act penalties for unauthorized capital outflows and false remittance declarations also apply, and the scheme is reported to the Financial Intelligence Unit as potential money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed nominee agreements, false beneficial ownership certificates, or abusive conduit invoices are treated as co-conspirators. Courts pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for restitution of all unpaid taxes, interest, and penalties to the NTS. Civil asset restitution reaches personal properties in Korea and abroad; travel bans, immigration blacklisting, and asset freezes are imposed on controlling directors. Criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act can yield imprisonment up to 10 years for aggravated evasion, with mandatory confiscation of personal assets acquired through the unlawful scheme.

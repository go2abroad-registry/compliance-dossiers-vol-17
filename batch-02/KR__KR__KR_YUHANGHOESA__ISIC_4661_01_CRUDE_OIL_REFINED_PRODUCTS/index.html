Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) under the Commercial Act; the incorporators are free to fix paid-in capital at any amount in the articles of incorporation. There is no mandatory blocked escrow account under general company law. However, before filing the establishment registration, the full amount of cash capital contributions must be deposited into a domestic Korean bank account opened in the name of the promoter or prospective company, and a bank-issued certificate of capital deposit must be obtained and submitted to the registration court. Non-cash contributions must be inspected and valued by a notarized valuation report with the consent of the incorporators. For foreign investors, the capital subscription and remittance must be reported under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and a Foreign Investment Report Certificate may be required. Although company law prescribes no minimum capital, the petroleum wholesale license under the Petroleum and Petroleum Substitute Fuel Business Act may impose separate financial capacity requirements, solvency thresholds, or storage facility commitments based on the annual trading volume.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a fire safety inspection certificate and a completed firefighting systems inspection report from the local fire department under the Firefighting Systems Installation and Maintenance Act, including an operational self-defense firefighting and civil defense emergency plan approved by the local civil defense coordination committee. If the business site or any associated storage area contains petroleum or hazardous chemicals, secure a hazardous facilities fire protection authorization and a chemical accident prevention plan under the Hazardous Chemicals Management Act and the Chemicals Control Act. Register the workplace with the Ministry of Employment and Labor, conduct a workplace risk assessment under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, and appoint a workplace safety and health manager if the number of workers meets the statutory threshold. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic tax portal: HomeTax System (hometax.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

Under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, the Yuhan Hoesa must file an Application for Business Registration with the district tax office having jurisdiction over its registered head office within 20 days from the commencement of business, attaching its corporate formation documents such as the articles of incorporation, certificate of capital contribution, and identification of the representative director. The NTS then issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which functions simultaneously as the corporate Tax Identification Number and the VAT registration number, and the electronic business registration certificate is retrievable through the HomeTax portal. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Korean domestic place of business that nevertheless incurs mandatory Korean VAT obligations in respect of cross-border supplies or imported transactions, the entity must appoint a tax representative, file a simplified VAT registration application with the NTS through HomeTax before commencing taxable activity, and obtain a simplified business registration number for VAT purposes. Because the Yuhan Hoesa is a domestically incorporated Korean limited company, the standard domestic BRN registration route is the governing procedure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration exemption threshold exists in Korea; every business operator engaged in taxable supplies of goods or services must register with the NTS and obtain a Business Registration Number. An annual taxable turnover of KRW 80 million in the preceding calendar year is the statutory cutoff under the VAT Act between the general taxable person regime and the simplified taxable person regime, but it is a compliance classification threshold and not a registration exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, zero-rating is available for goods physically exported out of the Korean customs territory, including marine fuel oil and refined petroleum distillates that are laden on board a vessel departing from a Korean port for an international destination, as well as bunkering supplies to ships engaged exclusively in international voyages. Zero-rating also extends to foreign-exchange-earning services supplied to non-resident counterparties, such as oil brokerage and commission services for cross-border physical fuel trades, provided the recipient is a non-resident, the service is performed in the context of exports or foreign trade, and the invoice is issued in foreign currency or evidence of foreign-exchange earning is maintained. The Yuhan Hoesa must demonstrate through the statutory documentation bundle that title and beneficial ownership passed to the foreign buyer, that the goods departed the Korean customs territory under a valid export declaration, and that the sales proceeds were repatriated to Korea in foreign currency within the statutory deadline.

  • Certificate of Export Declaration (export clearance certificate) issued by the Korea Customs Service
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Multimodal Transport Bill of Lading evidencing export shipment
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the exported fuel shipment
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice (VAT e-invoice stated at 0 percent)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Purchase Certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Bunker Delivery Note or marine fuel delivery receipt for fuel loaded on international-bound vessels

Korea requires VAT-registered general taxable persons to issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax electronic invoice system and to transmit the e-invoice issuance data to the NTS no later than the day following invoice issuance. Machine-to-machine submission is enabled by NTS Open API and accredited ERP/accounting integrations using accredited digital certificates and electronic seals. Korea does not operate a real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) pre-clearance portal or an anti-fraud digital stamping regime; instead the NTS applies post-transaction e-invoice reporting supplemented by automated cross-matching between e-invoice data, customs export declarations, import records, and VAT refund validation algorithms.

The Korea Customs Service and the NTS cross-verify e-invoice data against export declarations, vessel loading records, Bill of Lading duplicates, port records, bunker delivery receipts, and bank foreign exchange ledgers. If a zero-rated export claim is found to be unsubstantiated, false, or if the export proceeds are not repatriated in foreign currency within the statutory 180-day deadline, the NTS retroactively claws back the previously refunded VAT, imposes 10 percent VAT on the supply, assesses understatement penalty tax of 10 to 40 percent of the tax evaded or a 20 percent non-filing penalty when applicable, adds daily overdue interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and may refer the case for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, which carries imprisonment of up to three years and/or fines of up to three times the amount of tax evaded for fraudulent export zero-rating schemes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, administered by the Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance. The current account is broadly convertible: payments for imports, trade-related services, and profit remittances are permitted through a registered Foreign Exchange Bank without individual licensing, subject to documentary verification and the filing of transaction reports. The capital account operates on a negative-list prior-declaration framework; foreign direct investments, cross-border loans, and securities purchases require a declaration or approval from the Foreign Exchange Bank or the Bank of Korea, with FDI additionally reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act to obtain a certificate of registration and capital verification. There is no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio for export proceeds; Korean-resident enterprises may retain USD or other foreign currency in Foreign Exchange Bank accounts and forward-sell only against real underlying trade exposure. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal but real: dividends and branch profits can be remitted after the resolution of the shareholders' meeting, evidence of corporate tax withholding at the applicable 20-22 percent dividend withholding tax subject to treaty relief, and the submission of the Form 26-25 withholding tax payment certificate. Banks retain discretion to require additional FATCA and CRS self-certifications and economic substance documentation before allowing frequent large-value dividend or loan repayments to non-resident treasury centers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 8 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be received domestically within six months from the date the export declaration is accepted; the deadline can be extended by up to six additional months upon application to the responsible Foreign Exchange Bank, for a maximum of one year total. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may hold the foreign currency indefinitely in foreign currency deposit accounts, but any conversion into KRW or derivative hedging must be executed through the reporting bank and supported by the original export contract, the UNIPASS customs export declaration, and the trade settlement evidence. Failure to meet the collection deadline triggers an administrative fine and a compliance audit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (the central bank of the Republic of Korea), jointly administering the foreign exchange regime with the Ministry of Economy and Finance through the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the network of registered Foreign Exchange Banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable as a primary treasury rail for this high-risk energy-commodity licence. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation/submission is triggered when the aggregate gross amount of cross-border related-party transactions in a fiscal year is KRW 10 billion or more. In addition, a Country-by-Country Report is required for Korean ultimate parent entities or Korean constituent entities of MNE groups with consolidated revenue of KRW 100 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: Korea enforces economic substance through the Corporate Tax Act, the International Tax Adjustment Act, the substance-over-form doctrine, and the Beneficial Ownership rule in the tax treaties. An energy-trading Yuhan Hoesa must have real premises, qualified staff, board decision-making at Korean board meetings, risk-bearing capital, and active contract negotiation, voyage-risk management, credit control, and payment-settlement functions in Korea. It must not be a mere booking center for a foreign principal; the Korean entity's OECD-compliant Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) have to match the profit allocation. The CIGA standard is now applied by the Korean National Tax Service when recharacterizing commission income as a taxable permanent establishment, denying DTA benefits, or applying controlled foreign corporation (CFC) rules to passive income parked abroad.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Fabricated Bunker Supply and Brokerage Documentation

Statutory sanction: Violation of Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and Article 121 of the Corporate Tax Act: imprisonment up to 5 years, a criminal fine up to three times the evaded tax, plus civil penalties of 10%-40% of the understated tax, daily late-payment surcharges, and recovery of the full tax, local surtax and interest.

Director personal liability: The Representative Director and any director who participated in the false filing can be personally named as secondary taxpayers; the National Tax Service may levy on personal bank accounts, real estate, and vehicles, block director departure through a foreign-travel blacklist, and refer the director to criminal prosecution. Under Korean Commercial Act Article 401, directors who intentionally or negligently cause harm to third parties are jointly and severally liable for damages, effectively piercing the corporate veil.
Transfer Pricing and Paper-Only Intermediary Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 4 and 18 of the International Tax Adjustment Act and the principal purpose rule, the National Tax Service adjusts taxable income to arm's-length levels, adds a TP documentation penalty, applies a 40% fraudulent understatement penalty, denies DTA protection, and assesses 24% national plus 2.4% local surtax on the adjusted profit.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TP schedules face secondary liability for the adjusted tax and penalties; the corporate veil is pierced if the entity was formed as a sham; NTS can attach director assets and issue departure restrictions pending payment. Willful false TP documentation is treated as tax evasion and can result in incarceration of up to 5 years.

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Act on Reporting and Use of Specific Financial Transaction Information (AML) and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act: criminal imprisonment up to 5 years and criminal fines up to KRW 500 million or three times the laundered proceeds for each transaction; in addition, the Energy/ISIC license for wholesale fuel brokerage may be revoked and the customs duty/tax benefits forfeited, and tax deductions for illegal bribes or black-market payments are disallowed, generating additional corporate tax and penalties.

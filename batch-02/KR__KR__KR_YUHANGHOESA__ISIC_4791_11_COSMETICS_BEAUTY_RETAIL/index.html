Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS) via the HomeTax electronic tax portal at hometax.go.kr; export customs clearance is administered by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

A Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company) must first complete commercial registration with the court registry and obtain a certified copy of the corporate register. The company, through its statutory representative or authorized tax agent, must then file the Business Registration Application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the principal place of business. This filing must be completed within 20 days from the commencement of business. Required supporting documents include the articles of incorporation, certified commercial register certificate, corporate seal certificate, lease agreement or proof of business premises, evidence of paid-in capital, and passport or foreign nationality documents for each non-resident representative. Upon satisfactory review, the NTS issues the Business Registration Certificate containing the unified 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the corporate Tax Identification Number, VAT registration number, and electronic tax invoice identifier. A foreign non-resident entity that is not incorporated in Korea but maintains a domestic business place follows the same registration procedure through a domestic tax representative; where the statute requires it, the entity may also obtain a non-resident registration number through HomeTax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act applies when cosmetics goods are physically exported from South Korea pursuant to a valid export customs declaration, are transferred to a foreign buyer outside Korea, and the sale is concluded with a non-resident counterparty. The Yuhan Hoesa must prove the genuine cross-border nature of the supply; without such proof, the NTS will recharacterize the sale as a domestic taxable supply and assess VAT at the standard rate of 10%.

  • Export Declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service in the UNI-PASS system, including the unique export declaration number
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued in the name of the exporting Yuhan Hoesa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Currency Acquisition Confirmation issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act
  • Foreign exchange settlement records evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from the overseas buyer

Electronic tax invoicing is compulsory for all VAT-registered corporate taxpayers. The Yuhan Hoesa must issue e-tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-tax invoice portal for business-to-business supplies. If the entity uses integrated accounting software, issuance is performed through an NTS-certified EDI or API service provider that transmits invoice data to HomeTax at the time of issuance. The transmitted dataset is automatically validated against the counterparty Business Registration Number and taxpayer status, but South Korea does not operate a pre-clearance fiscal invoice approval or digital stamping model. For business-to-consumer beauty retail, credit card payment data is automatically reported to the NTS through licensed VAN operators, and cash receipts must be issued through the HomeTax Cash Receipt system whenever requested by the customer.

The NTS may scrutinize any zero-rated export claim and apply the additional tax and penalty provisions of the Value-Added Tax Act if the required customs, transport, and foreign exchange documentation cannot be produced. Unsupported export claims are recharacterized as domestic taxable supplies, resulting in an assessment of unpaid VAT plus statutory negligence surcharges and interest. Where fraudulent documents or deliberate concealment are found, the penalties may include increased additional tax and criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Incorrectly claimed VAT refunds are subject to retrospective clawback through reassessment; the general tax assessment period is five years for non-fraudulent underreporting and ten years for tax evasion or false documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a broadly open current account convertibility regime, but capital account operations are monitored by the Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA). For a Korean Yuhan Hoesa engaged in online cosmetics retail, ordinary sales of beauty products into Korea via Shopify/PayPal will likely be collected as KRW through local payment gateways; where foreign consumers pay in USD/EUR, those receipts are classified as services/export proceeds and require settlement through an authorized foreign exchange bank. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are not blocked for registered foreign-invested companies, but require corporate registration with a designated FX bank, proof of paid-in capital, audited financial statements, tax compliance, and a foreign investment repatriation notification. Companies that fail to register under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before capital injection may face enforcement penalties and be unable to receive foreign currency or later transfer assets abroad. The KRW is not fully internationally convertible outside the banking system and offshore trading of KRW deposits is limited to certain financial centers; however, trading between domestic resident and nonresident businesses is permissible with underlying trade and finance evidence. At present, surrender requirements do not include mandatory conversion; exporters may maintain their foreign currency proceeds for a statutory period.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters subject to FETA must collect export proceeds in foreign currency through an authorized foreign exchange bank. For goods, the standard settlement period is 18 months from the export date, or as extended by the bank based on trade terms; for services, 12 months is common; if collection cannot be achieved within the allowed period, prior permission from the MOEF/BOK is required. There is currently no mandatory surrender ratio: 0% of export proceeds must be converted into KRW. The exporter may hold the proceeds in a foreign currency deposit account, converted to KRW at market rates at its option, provided each receipt is reported through the bank's electronic foreign exchange system under the appropriate export settlement purpose code.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Korea Customs Service under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low as primary banking rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Payoneer, Airwallex, or Wise are not licensed banks in South Korea under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and cannot issue standard KRW IBAN-equivalent settlement accounts, act as a receiving bank for foreign investment notifications, or provide the formal trade settlement reporting required by the Bank of Korea. An unlicensed EMI can only be used as an ancillary collection channel for Shopify/PayPal sales from selected foreign countries, with the EMI account usually being a segregated e-money account outside Korean jurisdiction. Any KRW clearing would have to pass through a licensed domestic bank, which negates the cost savings and still requires this entity to onboard at a Tier-1 bank. The FSC treats unlicensed cross-border payment service providers as prohibited banking-like activities; therefore the treasury blueprint must route all statutory capital and trade flows through licensed FX banks.

An unlicensed EMI can only be used as an ancillary collection channel for Shopify/PayPal sales from selected foreign countries, with the EMI account usually being a segregated e-money account outside Korean jurisdiction. Any KRW clearing would have to pass through a licensed domestic bank, which negates the cost savings and still requires this entity to onboard at a Tier-1 bank. The FSC treats unlicensed cross-border payment service providers as prohibited banking-like activities; therefore the treasury blueprint must route all statutory capital and trade flows through licensed FX banks.

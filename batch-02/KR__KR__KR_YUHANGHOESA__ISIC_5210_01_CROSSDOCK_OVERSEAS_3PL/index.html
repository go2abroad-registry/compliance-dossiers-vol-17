Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act; however, all subscribed cash capital must be fully paid into a branch of a Korean commercial bank before the incorporation registration, and the bank must issue a capital deposit certificate. The certificate is submitted to the Registry Court; there is no statutory post-registration blocked escrow. In-kind contributions must be appraised by a court-appointed or statutory appraiser, and title must be transferred to the Company prior to registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal 'HomeTax' at www.hometax.go.kr; export customs declarations and UNI-PASS filings are administered by the Korea Customs Service.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must obtain a Korean Business Registration Number (BRN) under the Framework Act on National Taxes. The entity must appoint a tax representative in Korea if it has no domestic place of business; register with the competent tax office via HomeTax within 20 days of commencing a taxable supply. Required documents include the corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, branch registration certificate (if applicable), power of attorney for the tax representative, and non-resident identity/holding structure. The BRN is a 10-digit number that serves simultaneously as the VAT registration number, corporate income tax ID, and employer payroll account.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for corporate entities: every business operator must obtain a VAT registration (BRN) within 20 days of commencing business. For individual small merchants, simplified VAT filing applies when annual taxable revenue is below the statutory simplified-tax threshold (currently KRW 80 million).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for 0% VAT once an Export Declaration is accepted by Korea Customs Service and the goods are actually removed from Korea. Services supplied to a non-resident recipient outside Korea (including cross-border logistic, warehousing, and delivery facilitation) are zero-rated under the Korean VAT Act as 'export services' when the recipient is located outside Korea and the service is wholly performed abroad or exported. The exporter must hold contractual evidence and payment in foreign currency.

  • Export Declaration Receipt issued electronically through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system
  • Commercial Invoice with overseas consignee and FOB/CIF value
  • Packing List / Weight List
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill (AWB) evidencing cross-border carriage
  • Foreign Currency Acquisition Confirmation (Certificate of Receipt of Export Proceeds) issued by a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank, or bank remittance statement matching the invoice

Mandatory e-Tax Invoice issuance through the NTS HomeTax portal. Each VAT invoice must be issued electronically and transmitted to the NTS by the 20th day of the month following the taxable supply. There is no real-time pre-approval block; however, the NTS operates automated electronic invoice matching and risk-scoring. Businesses may also integrate ERP/accounting systems with HomeTax APIs for electronic submission. Fiscal anti-fraud digital stamping is not required for standard 3PL/export services.

If the NTS determines that zero-rating was claimed without proof of actual export/repatriation or the transaction is fictitious, the 0% rate is retrospectively revoked; the taxpayer is assessed omitted VAT at 10%, plus additional tax penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Penalty rates escalate by violation type (up to 40% of the unpaid VAT), and criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act (including fines or imprisonment) may apply. The NTS also has authority to cancel the initial tax refund and impose statutory interest from the date of repayment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a managed float and the Korean won is freely convertible for current account payments and receipts such as cross-border logistics fees, cargo transportation charges, and warehousing revenue. Capital transactions are not unrestricted: an inbound equity investment, a loan, or a profit distribution above the current account framework must go through a licensed foreign exchange bank and be filed on the Foreign Exchange Information System under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. There is no foreign currency surrender requirement for export sales, and the entity may maintain foreign exchange deposit accounts in the bank where the export receipt is collected. Dividend and branch remittances by a foreign-invested Yuhan Hoesa are permitted after domestic tax clearance, audited financial statements, and a shareholder resolution, but the bank must receive a payment purpose code and supporting documents. In practice, the main operational restriction is documentary rather than quantitative: each outward remittance must be accompanied by a real underlying trade invoice or service contract. The MOEF/BOK retains emergency stabilization powers, but ordinary current account transactions remain settled on a same-day or T+1 basis through bank-owned clearing rails.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must generally be collected within six months from the export declaration date unless the Korean foreign exchange bank approves an extension based on the goods movement, letter-of-credit tenor, or supplier payment terms. The collection is reported through the bank to the Bank of Korea's Foreign Exchange Information System and to the Korea Customs Service to close the underlying export declaration. No fixed percentage of export foreign currency receipts must be converted to KRW. The exporter may retain 100 percent of such proceeds in a domestic foreign currency deposit account or convert them to Korean won at the prevailing market rate; only the reporting of receipt and legal custody of the foreign exchange within a licensed bank is mandatory.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Foreign Exchange Administration of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), acting through licensed foreign exchange banks in South Korea.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as the sole treasury rail. While Airwallex and global EMIs are accepted as a supplementary settlement engine for collection from overseas marketplaces and are useful for non-KRW multi-currency balances, a foreign EMI without a Korean Electronic Financial Business Registration cannot hold KRW current accounts on behalf of a Korean legal entity. The Korean entity must clear inbound share capital, export surrender reporting, and KRW liquidity through a licensed Tier-1 bank; the EMI channel is legally acceptable only for ancillary FX conversion and offshore pooling when routed through partner banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident investors should first file a foreign investment report with the Ministry of Economy and Finance or Invest KOREA under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, then remit investment cash through the designated Korean Tier-1 bank using SWIFT MT103 with the FEIS purpose code covering foreign direct investment by share subscription. The remittance must name the Yuhan Hoesa as beneficiary, include the foreign investment report number if available, and identify the investing entity. The receiving bank should be instructed to credit a foreign-invested company capital deposit or escrow account and to issue a foreign investment capital deposit certificate; the company's auditor uses this bank certificate to complete capital verification for registration with the Korean court registry and tax office. After payment, the foreign investor must file the foreign investment report and obtain the Foreign Enterprise Registration Certificate within the statutory window. Fund release follows share issuance and is recorded on the Foreign Exchange Information System; all valuations should use the bank's telegraphed transfer rate or finance market average rate.Use SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border payments of overseas 3PL service fees and inventory purchases; for larger purchase orders use irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit issued through a Tier-1 Korean bank; for stable recurring supplier payments use Documentary Collections in D/P or D/A form. For domestic KRW treasury movements, use the KFTC ACH and BOK-Wire+; for customs-related export declarations and logistics evidence, use the electronic trade settlement system to reconcile physical cargo movement with the FX payment. All outbound payments must be supported by an underlying contract, invoice, tax invoice, and if goods are moved from Korea, the export customs declaration and bill of lading or airway bill. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Act on External Audit of Companies, a Korean Yuhan Hoesa is subject to mandatory independent CPA audit if total assets at the end of the immediately preceding fiscal year are at least KRW 12 billion (approximately USD 9 million). Additional mandatory audit triggers apply to listed companies, companies preparing for listing, financial institutions, and companies whose industry or parent-company status is expressly designated by the Financial Services Commission. Headcount is not a stand-alone statutory audit trigger in Korea; the decisive quantitative tests are asset-based. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Progressive rates under Article 55 of the Korean Corporate Tax Act: 9% up to KRW 200 million; 19% above KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion; 21% above KRW 20 billion up to KRW 300 billion; and 24% above KRW 300 billion (headline top statutory rate 24%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding under Article 156(1) of the Corporate Tax Act, plus 2% local income tax surcharge on the national tax, producing a 22% effective statutory rate on outbound cash dividends to non-resident shareholders not entitled to treaty reduction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 40% of the underreported tax (20% where the failure to file is negligent) under Article 47-2 through 47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, plus daily late-payment surcharges and, if fraudulent, criminal fine under the Punishment for Tax Evasion Act of up to 3 times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be subjected to joint and several liability for the corporate tax under Article 42 of the Commercial Act and civil asset restitution to the NTS; willful evasion triggers imprisonment for up to 3 years, travel blacklisting through the NTS international tax cooperation procedure, and freezing of personal bank accounts, with no suspension of the limitation period until the tax is paid.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Mandatory tax adjustment using the best arm’s length method and an added penalty of 10%-20% of the understated tax under Article 12 of the LCITA, in addition to a fine up to KRW 100 million for non-submission of the required documents; in egregious cases the NTS may apply a broader anti-avoidance recharacterization.

Director personal liability: Directors owe a statutory duty to complete and sign the transfer pricing documentation; failure can make them personally liable for penalties and accrued interest, and they may be exposed to criminal sanctions if they certified false information; NTS may issue a forced collection attachment against director personal assets and impose international travel bans pending resolution of the adjustment.
Shell or Economic Substance Abuse and Tax Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits under anti-abuse provisions and possible unilateral recharacterization of all Korean-source income as taxable PE income of a foreign parent; civil penalties for failure to maintain meaningful substance may include cancellation of corporate identity under court orders and AML/CFT reporting fines for unexplained clearing transactions of up to KRW 500 million.

Director personal liability: Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil when the company is a sham or may be equated with the foreign parent’s alter ego, making directors jointly liable for all underpaid enterprise-scale taxes; criminal exposure reaches imprisonment for up to 10 years under Article 355 of the Criminal Act for breach of duty or embezzlement where logistics business assets were diverted, and directors may be blacklisted from leaving Korea and have their passports suspended.

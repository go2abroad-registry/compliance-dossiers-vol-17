Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For the domestic Yuhan Hoesa company, the procedure is: (1) incorporate under the Commercial Act and obtain the corporate registration extract from the court registry; (2) file an Application for Business Registration with the competent district tax office through the NTS HomeTax portal before starting business; (3) attach the articles of incorporation, commercial registration certificate, evidence of registered head office or place of business, and the representative's proof of identity and authority; (4) receive the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the unified TIN for corporate income tax, VAT, electronic tax invoice filing, and withholding; (5) if the same company acts for foreign non-resident principals, a foreign non-resident entity required to file Korean VAT or corporate income tax must register through a Korean tax representative and submit an official certificate of foreign incorporation, articles, and a board resolution appointing the tax representative; (6) obtain an exporter customs code through UNI-PASS in parallel. There is no separate VAT ID; the same 10-digit Business Registration Number is used for all indirect and direct tax filings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary threshold for Korean VAT registration; every person making taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea must register upon commencing business. A simplified taxpayer threshold of KRW 80,000,000 in annual turnover applies to small businesses, but it does not exempt registration. Separate mandatory registration applies to foreign suppliers of electronic services without any threshold effective 1 July 2021.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 21 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act for: (i) exported goods where the exporter submits an accepted export declaration to Korea Customs Service and the goods are physically removed from Korea; (ii) services supplied to non-residents where the services are deemed supplied or used outside Korea and are paid in foreign currency; and (iii) international carriage of goods. For the AdTech DSP/SSP scope, programmatic advertising brokerage, demand-side platform, supply-side platform, and related optimization services rendered to non-resident customers and used outside Korea are eligible as foreign-exchange-earning services when invoiced in foreign currency and actually repatriated to Korea. Qualifying services must be documented to show the recipient's non-resident status and offshore place of use. A Korean exporter with zero-rated export supplies may claim early refund of input VAT under the same evidence bundle.

  • Accepted electronic Export Declaration transmitted through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS, reflecting the exporter of record, customs code, commodity code, quantity, FOB value, and destination country
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the international carrier naming the Korean exporter as shipper and showing the Korean port/airport of loading and the foreign port/airport of discharge
  • Foreign Exchange Purchase/Settlement Certificate or bank-produced inbound remittance record showing that export proceeds denominated in foreign currency were received through an authorized Korean foreign exchange bank within the repatriation deadline
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin if required, retained for NTS and Korea Customs Service verification

All VAT-registered entities must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax national e-tax invoice system. The electronic tax invoice must be transmitted to NTS by the day after issuance; transmission is automatic when the invoice is issued through HomeTax or through approved API/EDI integration. The system uses a national electronic tax invoice number and digital signature for authentication; there is no physical clearance stamp, but NTS continuously receives transaction data to support cross-matching against customs and bank foreign-exchange records. Fraud-risk industries are subject to enhanced real-time monitoring and strict electronic tax invoice issuance controls.

The NTS electronically cross-matches zero-rating claims against Korea Customs Service export declarations, electronic tax invoices, and bank foreign-exchange records. If export status is not substantiated because goods were not exported, services were not actually used offshore, required documentation is missing, or foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, the zero-rating is reversed and Korean VAT at 10% is assessed, together with statutory additional tax for underdeclaration and irregular electronic tax invoice reporting. Intentional false documentation or fraudulent evasion is subject to criminal enforcement under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment and fines, with the statute of limitations for fraudulent evasion extended to ten years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberal current account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, administered jointly by the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the central policy authority and the Bank of Korea as the monetary and statistical guardian. All current account transactions, including the remittance of programmatic advertising service fees, software licensing royalties, and SSP platform settlement flows, are freely convertible through designated foreign exchange banks, provided the remitter submits the standard underlying transaction documentation and selects the correct balance-of-payments purpose code. Capital account transactions are governed by a reporting-and-notification system rather than blanket prohibition: foreign direct investment exceeding KRW 100 million in subscribed equity must be registered with the Ministry of Economy and Finance, typically through a domestic bank or KOTRA, and the committed capital must be wired via a foreign exchange bank into the investor's escrow account. Profit remittances by a non-resident shareholder are permitted once the Korean entity files corporate income tax returns, settles tax liabilities, and presents a tax clearance certification to the remitting bank together with audited financial statements and board minutes approving the dividend. The principal practical hurdles for an AdTech vehicle are the documentary burden for outbound service disbursements (invoice, contract, and proof of actual service delivery), the requirement to report receipts into Korea within 30 days for statistical entry, and the need to maintain a clear separation between the platform's own revenue and funds passed through on behalf of advertisers or publishers, as commingled flows may be reclassified as financial intermediation and trigger additional licensing scrutiny.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 19-3 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export settlement for both physical goods and invisible trade services must be collected within 18 months from the date of export declaration or service delivery; the receipt must be credited to a domestic foreign-currency account at a designated foreign exchange bank, and the bank must file the receipt in the Foreign Exchange Information System no later than the next business day. There is no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio: the historical export surrender duty was abolished decades ago, and a Korean exporter may retain foreign currency in an onshore foreign-currency deposit account or offshore subject to reporting. For an AdTech platform selling DSP/SSP services, the 18-month deadline starts from the invoice date on the platform settlement statement, and an extension for longer credit terms may be granted only upon application to the Ministry of Economy and Finance before the lapse date. Failure to collect within the statutory period without a permit constitutes a foreign-exchange violation subject to administrative fines.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea as the central monetary and statistical authority, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the principal foreign exchange policy and administrative body under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a replacement primary clearing rail; Medium as a supplementary offshore collection account. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Real-Name Financial Transactions Act, only institutions licensed by the Financial Services Commission, or entities registered as electronic financial businesses, may lawfully open KRW settlement accounts and execute fund transfers for a Korean resident legal person. Established offshore EMIs such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, or Airwallex do not hold a Korean banking or e-money license and therefore cannot maintain a KRW account-and-routing clearing facility for a Korean LLC, cannot support FEIS filing, and cannot substitute for a designated foreign exchange bank in the annual foreign-exchange reporting duty. The permissible alternative is to keep the primary operational current account at a Korean Tier-1 bank and to open a foreign-currency collection account at an overseas EMI purely for receiving USD advertising receipts from global counterparties; however, every onward remittance from that EMI into Korea must be declared through the designated Korean bank, the EMI account must be disclosed in the entity's annual foreign-exchange status report, and the bank may request a written explanation demonstrating that the EMI arrangement does not circumvent the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

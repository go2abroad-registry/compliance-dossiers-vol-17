Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is imposed for a Yuhan Hoesa. The Articles of Incorporation must state the total capital, which is the aggregate of contributions subscribed by shareholders. In standard registry practice, cash contributions are fully deposited into a domestic bank account opened or designated in the name of the company before the incorporation registration is filed; no blocked governmental escrow account is required. In-kind contributions must be inspected by a licensed expert and completed before registration.

Funding & unlock path: The company must obtain the Fire Safety Completion Inspection Certificate for any warehouse or bonded storage facility, verify the structural load capacity of the building through the official building register, install automatic fire protection systems, emergency exits, ventilation, and ignition-source controls as required by the Act on Fire Prevention and Installations, obtain a Hazardous Materials Storage Permit or submittal if the baijiu inventory in bonded or unbonded warehouses exceeds the statutory thresholds, and file the initial Occupational Safety and Health Report with the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency if the company employs five or more workers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None - VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply by any person commencing a business in Korea under Article 8 of the Value-Added Tax Act, regardless of turnover. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21(1) of the Value-Added Tax Act, supplies of goods that are exported are zero-rated at 0% VAT. The VAT-registered exporter must substantiate that Korean customs accepted the export declaration and that the goods physically left Korea. The claim must be supported by an export declaration number shown on the 0% export VAT invoice, a Korea Customs Service export clearance certificate, and bank evidence of actual receipt of foreign currency export proceeds. Qualifying offshore digital services may also be zero-rated when the place of supply is treated as outside Korea and the supplier maintains contemporaneous records showing the non-resident customer, foreign use or consumption, services supplied outside Korea, and payment received from abroad.

  • Signed commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing the physical export of the Chinese Baijiu from Korea
  • 0% VAT export tax invoice transmitted to the NTS through HomeTax
  • Foreign Exchange Bank certificate / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent foreign exchange settlement receipt
  • Certificate of Origin, liquor export permit or liquor license evidence under the Liquor Tax Act, and certificate of analysis or alcohol content documentation if required by the destination customs authority
  • Any destination-country import permit or health certificate required by the importing country before release of the alcohol goods

All registered businesses must issue tax invoices, and corporations must issue and transmit electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-invoice system no later than the day following issuance. Korea does not operate a real-time per-transaction physical fiscal stamp or cash-register fiscalization system. Instead, the NTS performs central e-invoice clearance and data matching through mandatory electronic tax invoice transmission; the NTS-KCS data interface validates export declarations against export VAT invoices, and any mismatch can trigger an automatic compliance audit. Anti-fraud controls are embedded in the mandatory e-invoice data trail, the export declaration number reconciliation, and the KCS UNI-PASS customs clearance records.

The NTS and KCS may audit export zero-rating claims by matching the export declaration, e-tax invoice, bill of lading or air waybill, and foreign exchange settlement records. If a zero-rating claim is unsupported, excessive, or fraudulent, the NTS will claw back the VAT at the standard 10% rate, add late-payment and reporting additional taxes under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, and impose the statutory penalties for false or unissued tax invoices, including the penalty based on the supply value. Criminal liability for tax evasion may also be pursued under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Export proceeds must be repatriated within 180 days of export clearance; failure to do so without prior regulatory approval can result in reversal of the 0% rate, foreign exchange penalties, and customs or tax corrective assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberal current-account convertibility regime. Trade-related payments, service fees, and ordinary profit remittances can flow without transaction-by-transaction approval, provided they are routed through a licensed foreign-exchange bank and the underlying purpose is properly declared. Capital and financial account transactions are more controlled: non-resident equity subscriptions, loans, and cross-border settlements must be notified/reported under FETA, and any inbound foreign investment for Yuhan Hoesa must be filed through the Foreign Investment Promotion Act process before/around capital wiring. Korea does not impose a general surrender requirement on export receipts, but export proceeds must be collected through an authorized foreign-exchange bank within the statutory period. The KRW is not fully internationalized: non-resident access to onshore KRW liquidity, offshore KRW clearing, and speculative local-currency activity is restricted, so cross-border trade settlements are usually denominated in USD or another freely convertible currency. Profit remittances to overseas shareholders are allowed after corporate tax and audit, provided the entity submits an External Transaction Report showing distributable reserves, withholding tax evidence, and board approval. In sum, the currency is operationally convertible for genuine trade and dividends, but compliance with declaration, documentation, and licensed-bank routing is mandatory; no parallel-market or fintech bypass is tolerated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA Article 28, export proceeds must be received and deposited into a domestic foreign-currency account (or an overseas account pre-approved by MOEF) at a licensed foreign-exchange bank within 18 months from the date of export declaration. There is no fixed statutory percentage that must be converted to KRW; 100% of proceeds may be retained in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account as long as the bank records the inflow and matches it to the Korea Customs Service export declaration. Any voluntary conversion into KRW must be executed at the prevailing market rate through the same licensed bank and reported as a foreign-exchange transaction. Documentation for each collection should include the original export LC/contract, invoice, packing list, bill of lading, customs export clearance, and the bank's External Transaction Report entry.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the primary foreign-exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA); operational control is delegated to licensed foreign-exchange banks in Korea.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Revolut, Payoneer, or other foreign payment e-money institutions are not licensed as foreign-exchange banks under Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and cannot lawfully maintain multi-currency operating accounts for a Korean tax-resident company. Domestic fintech banks, such as KakaoBank or Toss Bank, may offer convenient onboarding, but their cross-border trade services and escrow functions are still dependent on a licensed bank or require separate authorization from the Financial Services Commission. For this spirits-export company, the compliance risk of using an unlicensed EMI is unacceptable: AML regulators would question the absence of a Korean External Transaction Report trail, and the bank account would fail to satisfy customs verification and VAT/export evidence requirements. Consequently, the blueprint mandates a Tier-1 commercial bank or its fintech subsidiary that acts merely as a technology front end, never as a licensed settlement bank substitute.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The investment must be disclosed under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before the transfer. For a non-resident shareholder injecting equity into Yuhan Hoesa, the recipient company opens a dedicated Capital Escrow Account at a licensed foreign-exchange bank, and the investor wires USD or another convertible currency using SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code declared as inbound foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Notice. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Revolut, Payoneer, or other foreign payment e-money institutions are not licensed as foreign-exchange banks under Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and cannot lawfully maintain multi-currency operating accounts for a Korean tax-resident company. Domestic fintech banks, such as KakaoBank or Toss Bank, may offer convenient onboarding, but their cross-border trade services and escrow functions are still dependent on a licensed bank or require separate authorization from the Financial Services Commission. For this spirits-export company, the compliance risk of using an unlicensed EMI is unacceptable: AML regulators would question the absence of a Korean External Transaction Report trail, and the bank account would fail to satisfy customs verification and VAT/export evidence requirements. Consequently, the blueprint mandates a Tier-1 commercial bank or its fintech subsidiary that acts merely as a technology front end, never as a licensed settlement bank substitute.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in total international related-party transactions during the fiscal year for Local File; KRW 100 billion in consolidated revenue of the multinational group and the Korean entity's related-party transaction amount exceeding KRW 10 billion for Master File; KRW 50 billion for CbCR; even where the mandatory threshold is not breached, the NTS may request TP documentation when a tax risk is identified.

Economic substance scope: Korea has not enacted OECD-form Economic Substance Regulations, but the substance-over-form principle and anti-abuse rules apply under the Corporate Tax Act and International Tax Adjustment Act. A liquid export distributor must demonstrate active economic substance through its employees, office premises, warehousing, logistics functions, customer contracts, invoicing, inventory movement, and risk-bearing capacity. If a non-resident parent or Chinese affiliate controls sales, orders, pricing, and risk, the Korean entity may be deemed a limited-risk distributor and its profits adjusted to the arm's length remuneration. The NTS may also disregard shell entities for treaty purposes under the Limitation of Benefits and Principal Purpose Test provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Sham / shell company arrangement

Statutory sanction: Under the Corporate Tax Act assessment procedures, the NTS will deny deductions, recharacterize transactions, impose the 40% dishonest underreporting penalty, and disallow treaty benefits under the International Tax Adjustment Act LOB/PPT provisions. The local tax surtax penalty is also assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly establish or operate a sham entity may suffer piercing of the corporate veil under commercial and tort principles, becoming personally liable for unpaid corporate tax, local tax, and penalties where assets insufficient. Criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act carries imprisonment up to 5 years; where tax evaded exceeds KRW 500 million, imprisonment up to 7 years. Directors may be subject to travel bans, blacklisting, and disqualification from serving on any Korean board.
Transfer pricing manipulation / base erosion

Statutory sanction: The NTS can make a transfer pricing adjustment under the International Tax Adjustment Act, impose a 20% penalty on unjustified underreporting (or 40% if fraudulent), add daily late-payment interest, and disallow tax treaty benefits. If the transaction is not disclosed, a 10% negligence penalty applies on the transaction amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed off on contrived intercompany contracts and knowingly caused the understatement are jointly and severally liable to the extent of corporate underpayment. Criminal tax evasion sanctions apply: imprisonment up to 5 years or fine up to 5 times the tax; in severe cross-border schemes, up to 7 years. Directors can be blacklisted and temporarily prohibited from departing Korea.
Beneficial ownership / treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: The NTS will deny the treaty rate, collect the full 22% statutory WHT, impose underreporting penalties of up to 40%, and apply daily late-payment interest. The payor may also be liable for failure to withhold.

Director personal liability: Directors who arranged the conduit structure may be criminally prosecuted for fraud in withholding remittance, with imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil liability extends to the withholding shortfall, penalties, and tax attorney costs. A director who is a non-resident may be issued an Interpol red notice or extradition request where fraud is established.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating through the HomeTax System electronic portal (hometax.go.kr), with customs-side processing via the Korea Customs Service (KCS) UNI-PASS system

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing or owning a Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) in Korea: (1) complete corporate registration under the Korean Commercial Act at the competent registry and, where applicable, file a foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; (2) apply for business registration at the district tax office with jurisdiction over the domestic place of business; execute the statutory business registration application under the Framework Act on National Taxes; (3) submit the corporate articles of incorporation, certificate of corporate registration, proof of local establishment or place of business, identity documents of the statutory representative, and any foreign investment registration certificate or equity subscription evidence; (4) the NTS shall issue the 10-digit Business Registration Number (TIN), which is the unified identifier for Corporate Tax, Value-Added Tax, withholdings, and other national tax filings; (5) for a foreign enterprise with no Korean place of business that nevertheless supplies taxable services in Korea, a simplified VAT registration under the Value-Added Tax Act must be made through a statutory tax representative, with a separately assigned foreign business registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for a Yuhan Hoesa: no check-the-box or passthrough election is available under Korean tax law. A Yuhan Hoesa is a resident domestic corporation taxable on worldwide income under the Corporate Income Tax Act. The Korean group tax consolidation regime under the Corporate Income Tax Act is available only to qualifying Korean corporate groups and is not a passthrough election; capital gains, income, and losses remain recognised at entity level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for a domestic Yuhan Hoesa: VAT registration is mandatory from commencement of business under Article 5 of the Value-Added Tax Act, irrespective of anticipated turnover. For a foreign non-resident supplier of electronic services without a Korean place of business, a simplified registration obligation applies, with an annual sales threshold of KRW 30,000,000 for mandatory registration; this electronic-services rule is not relevant to a domestic marine insurance surveying company.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rate eligibility is governed by Article 20 of the Value-Added Tax Act and its Enforcement Decree. Exports of goods qualify when the goods are delivered overseas or under customs export procedures. Services qualify for 0% VAT when performed outside Korea, or when rendered in Korea to a non-resident recipient in consideration of foreign currency and the service is actually used or consumed overseas. For the marine insurance surveying scope, zero-rating requires the services to be supplied to a foreign underwriter or principal, the contractual consideration to be received in foreign exchange, and the survey work to be evidenced as related to export-bound cargo, ocean-going vessels, or international marine transportation. Domestic survey services for Korean-resident principals are taxable at 10%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (KCS UNI-PASS export filing with export declaration number and validation result; the Korean statutory equivalent of an export bill of entry, applicable to goods movements)
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list identifying the supplier, recipient, export cargo, vessel, and stated zero-rated supply value
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical transportation of the goods out of Korea
  • Certificate of receipt of foreign currency issued by a foreign exchange bank (Korean statutory equivalent of an inward remittance certificate), documenting the inward remittance or foreign currency purchase supporting the export service proceeds
  • Marine survey report, vessel particulars, cargo damage documentation, and the insurance contract or engagement letter demonstrating that the recipient is a non-resident underwriter and that the claim inspection service was performed in connection with export/international marine insurance risk

Under the Korean electronic tax invoice regime, all VAT-registered businesses must issue Electronic Tax Invoices through the NTS HomeTax system for taxable supplies to other registered businesses, and transmit the invoice data to the NTS no later than the day following the statutory time of supply. Since 2021 the e-tax invoice mandate applies regardless of the supplier’s annual turnover. There is no physical fiscal stamp or Latin American style real-time fiscal clearance API; the anti-fraud control is effected through the NTS’s central e-tax invoice database and data matching. For retail cash sales, businesses must operate the cash receipt system, issuing cash receipts and transmitting transaction data to the NTS to preserve input tax credits for consumers and suppress hidden turnover.

The NTS may retrospectively assess the 10% VAT with penalty taxes where a zero-rated export claim is unsupported, the required customs or foreign-exchange documents are missing, or the export proceeds are not collected within the statutory repatriation period of 180 days, unless an extension is approved by the foreign exchange bank or the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Penalty tax exposure includes understatement and non-payment penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes, with aggravated penalties for deliberate concealment or the use of false tax invoices. In fraud or evasion cases, the taxpayer is subject to criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment, fines, and mandatory recovery of any VAT refund together with interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a market-determined foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decrees. The Korean won is freely convertible for current-account items such as marine insurance surveying fees, vessel hull and cargo damage inspection reimbursements, royalties, and professional fees, but every inward or outward remittance must be declared through a resident foreign exchange bank using standard transaction codes. Capital-account transactions are more structured: a foreign shareholder injecting equity into the Korean LLC, or the LLC extending or repaying shareholder loans, must first file a foreign investment notification or capital transaction declaration, after which the bank can lawfully convert the inbound currency into KRW. There is no statutory surrender quota applicable to service export proceeds, but the bank must verify the underlying contract, invoice, and business relationship to prevent disguised financing flows. Profit remittance from the LLC to an overseas shareholder is permitted on current-account grounds once distributable reserves are proven by audited financial statements, a board resolution is passed, and applicable Korean corporate tax and withholding obligations have been settled. Offshore KRW clearing is limited: the onshore market is ring-fenced, and non-resident access generally occurs through non-deliverable forwards rather than free third-country KRW settlement. Overall convertibility is high for genuine trade and service flows, but banks will require documentary substance and will reject speculative or undocumented capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Korea Customs Service settlement guidelines, general merchandise export proceeds must be received by a Korean foreign exchange bank within 15 months from the export declaration date unless the settlement term is extended due to letter-of-credit, project, or ship-construction conditions. Marine insurance surveying receipts are service-export earnings; they must be collected on the invoice due date, which the Korean bank will encode in the Foreign Exchange Information Network as a current-account service receipt. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0 percent for ordinary export and service receipts. South Korea does not impose a forced surrender requirement; a resident company may keep foreign currency in a licensed foreign exchange bank deposit account, convert to KRW at market rates, or use it for permitted external payments, but the funds must enter the republic through a designated Korean bank and remain traceable through SWIFT and Foreign Exchange Information Network reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (the central bank), with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the foreign exchange control authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; Korea Customs Service supervises trade settlement reporting and the Foreign Exchange Information Network is the electronic reporting gateway

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms, including Wise, are not licensed banks or registered foreign exchange businesses under the Korean Banking Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. A Korean-resident LLC cannot lawfully rely on such an EMI for inbound equity subscription, marine insurance claim settlements, or trade disbursements because all capital-account and current-account foreign exchange transactions must be routed through a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank. For the LLC, using Wise as a treasury rail would expose the client to unregistered money transmission risk and will cause a Korean relationship bank to refuse onboarding. The entity should use only SWIFT MT103 transfers from the licensed bank accounts of foreign underwriters, shipowners, and reinsurers; Wise may be relevant only for personal or non-corporate small-value scenarios and should not appear as a corporate treasury counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inward capital must be sent from the overseas investor's licensed bank to the Korean LLC's designated foreign currency account at a licensed Korean bank. The remittance should be executed as a SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary being the LLC, a clear shareholder-loan or equity-investment reference, and the Korean foreign exchange transaction purpose code approved by the receiving bank. Upon receipt, the Korean bank will credit the capital account, issue an inward remittance certificate, and verify the contribution against the filed foreign investment notification or capital transaction declaration. KRW conversion is then executed onshore at market rate through a bank FX desk. No statutory minimum capital exists, so formal CPA capital verification is not mandatory, but the bank-issued capital remittance certificate should be retained for KOTRA, audits, and future dividend repatriation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% national corporate income tax (CIT) plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the CIT, yielding a combined top marginal rate of 27.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax on dividends paid to a non-resident corporate shareholder, plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the national WHT (aggregate 22%); the rate may be reduced under an applicable bilateral DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50 billion in foreign-related intercompany transactions triggers full Local File and Master File documentation obligations; a simplified transfer pricing disclosure may still be required for transactions above KRW 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); substance is enforced through the General Anti-Abuse Rule under the National Tax Basic Law (Art. 14-3), CFC rules under the International Tax Adjustment Act (Arts. 17–22), and the beneficial ownership condition in treaty relief. A Korean marine insurance surveyor LLC must have actual employees, office facilities, and decision-making on core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Korea; paper-only arrangements are disregarded and profits are reattributed to the board in Korea.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Additive tax of 40% of the evaded national and local income tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes (Art. 47-3); criminal sanction under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (Art. 9) with imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine up to twice the evaded tax, aggravated to 3 years when the evaded tax exceeds KRW 5 billion; local surcharge penalties under the Local Tax Act.

Director personal liability: If a court pierces the corporate veil, directors are jointly and severally liable for the full evaded tax amount and penalties; criminal conviction additionally triggers personal asset seizure and an overseas travel ban under the NTS delinquency blacklist program.
Withholding Tax Default on Outbound Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent is liable for the unwithheld tax; additive tax of 20% of the under-withheld amount after 3 months, plus 0.022% per-day overdue interest; intentional failure may become tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and other statutory withholding officers bear personal, joint liability for the full unwithheld national and local taxes (Corporate Income Tax Act Art. 127-5; Local Tax Act Art. 103-22-2); NTS can attach personal bank accounts and real estate, and the individual may be blacklisted and barred from overseas departure until the debt is secured.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Thin Shell Substance

Statutory sanction: Unilateral TP re-assessment under the arm's-length principle; 10% under-reporting surcharge; 30% penalty for failure to submit or providing international transaction documentation under International Tax Adjustment Act (Art. 11-5); automatic audit of all Korean-source activities.

Director personal liability: Although a genuine TP miscalculation alone is an administrative issue, an intentionally false TP documentation used for tax evasion exposes directors to civil fraud compensation claims, disqualification from serving as directors, and criminal liability if book entries are forged; exit bans under the overseas travel restriction notice apply for unpaid reassessed taxes.

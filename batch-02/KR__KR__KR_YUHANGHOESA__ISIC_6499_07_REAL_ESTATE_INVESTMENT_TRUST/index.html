Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Corporate Registration Section of the Local District Court Registry Office, which operates under the Supreme Court of Korea and has territorial jurisdiction over the registered office of the company.

Governing statute

Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea, Act No. 12102, as amended, Part III, Chapter I, Articles 287 through 317 (limited liability company/yuhan hoesa), including Articles 54, 55 and 289 regarding registration, legal seat, and corporate name; together with the Real Estate Investment Company Act, Act No. 16022, as last amended, Articles 8, 9, 11 and 16, and the Enforcement Decree of the Real Estate Investment Company Act, as applicable to the REIT authorization sought after incorporation.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of Incorporation of a Yuhan Hoesa (Limited Liability Company) submitted to the court registry, together with the notarized Articles of Incorporation, capital deposit certificate, consent declarations of the first manager and auditor, corporate seal registration data, and all statutorily required annexes.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

For the Yuhan Hoesa at Stage 2, the Commercial Act prescribes no minimum paid-up capital; however, the total contribution stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully subscribed and actually paid in before incorporation registration. The paid-in capital must be placed in a blocked capital subscription escrow account at a licensed bank in Korea, and the bank must issue a deposit certificate expressly blocking withdrawal until the court registry issues the certificate of incorporation registration. If the entity is later authorized as a real estate investment company under the Real Estate Investment Company Act, the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport will impose a separate minimum equity requirement in the authorization order, and that equity must remain unencumbered and supported by the same escrow or blocked-account confirmation until the REIT license is issued.

Funding & unlock path: A fire safety certificate must be obtained from the local fire station for the registered office, certifying that the firefighting equipment, emergency exits, evacuation signage, and alarm systems comply with the Firefighting Facilities Installation and Maintenance Act. A structural load inspection and building operating certificate must confirm that the office premises safely accommodate the number of employees and electronic equipment. The workplace must also be cleared under the Occupational Safety and Health Act through a labor office inspection that covers electrical safety, fall prevention, confined spaces, and general accident-prevention measures. These certifications must be posted at the registered office and renewed at each renovation, lease renewal, or official inspection interval prescribed by the local authority.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the foreign non-resident entity maintains a domestic place of business in Korea under the Corporate Tax Act. A foreign REIT holding Korean income-generating real property either directly or through a domestic trust generally has a fixed place of business in Korea. Step 2: If a domestic place of business exists, the foreign entity must appoint a domestic tax representative and file a Foreign Corporation Domestic Place of Business Registration with the competent district tax office. Supporting documents include: certificate of incorporation and trust deed or articles of incorporation, certificate of foreign registration, power of attorney in favor of the Korean tax representative, evidence of Korean real estate ownership or lease/trust relationship, and proof of REIT license or authorization under the Real Estate Investment Company Act. Step 3: Upon approval, the district tax office issues a 10-digit Korean Business Registration Number, which operates as the corporate TIN and VAT identification number. Step 4: If the foreign non-resident entity does not have a domestic place of business but supplies VAT-able electronic services to Korean consumers, it must complete the simplified foreign business VAT registration through the HomeTax portal and obtain a simplified VAT registration number. Step 5: A foreign REIT with Korean-source income but no domestic place of business must still appoint a Korean tax representative for filing corporate tax and withholding obligations. Step 6: For VAT refund or direct pay purposes, the foreign entity may separately register as a registered foreign business where the Value-Added Tax Act grants that right.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for US-style check-the-box entity classification because Korea does not offer check-the-box elections. However, a qualifying Korean REIT may elect the special tax treatment provisions under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act and the Real Estate Investment Company Act, which permit a deduction for mandatory dividend distributions from corporate taxable income subject to statutory distribution ratios and other qualification conditions. Such election does not change the entity-level taxation classification of the Yuhan Hoesa limited liability company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold applies to corporations; every corporation engaged in business in Korea is required to register. A simplified VAT taxpayer regime is available only to eligible individual businesses whose annual taxable turnover is below KRW 80 million, and this regime does not apply to a Yuhan Hoesa LLC or a REIT. Foreign suppliers of electronic services must register for Korean VAT regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate when the goods are physically exported from Korea and evidenced by an export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service, with risk and possession transferred to a foreign buyer and the export payment received in foreign currency or Korean won in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Services and intangibles qualify for zero-rating only when they fall within the statutory categories under the Value-Added Tax Act Enforcement Decree, including services or digital content supplied to a non-resident client that are actually used outside Korea. A Korean REIT supply of Korean real property or lease of Korean real estate is a domestic supply subject to 10% VAT and cannot be zero-rated because the place of supply and use are in Korea.

  • Customs export declaration (export Bill of Entry / proof of export) issued by the Korea Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export and matching the export declaration
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and signed export contract or purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Bank settlement certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of sales proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • For exported services or intangibles: signed service supply contract, documentation of overseas use, and foreign currency settlement certificate

Korea mandates electronic tax invoices for B2B supplies. Tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS through the HomeTax electronic tax invoice system, generally by the day after issuance. The system does not use physical pre-approval or fiscal stamping; instead, NTS operates a centralized data matching and risk-scoring platform that reconciles electronic tax invoices with cash receipts, bank settlements, and customs declarations. Businesses must use NTS-approved API, EDI, or HomeTax direct filing protocols and digital certificate authentication. Failure to issue or transmit an electronic tax invoice exposes the seller to statutory penalty surcharges.

Where the NTS disallows an export zero-rating claim because the statutory documentation bundle is incomplete, false, or inconsistent, the supply is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply and reassessed at the standard 10% VAT. The taxpayer will be liable for late payment surcharges and statutory additional taxes, which can range from 40% to 100% of the understated VAT depending on whether the deficiency is attributable to negligence or deliberate fraud. Where intentional false export declarations are used to obtain a VAT refund, the NTS may claw back the full refunded amount together with input VAT reversals and refer the matter to prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act evading tax duties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a fundamentally liberalized and largely convertible foreign exchange regime for current-account transactions, while capital-account transactions remain subject to mandatory reporting, notification, or licensing through authorized foreign exchange banks and the Bank of Korea. The Korean won is freely convertible into foreign currencies for trade, dividend remittance, and foreign direct investment, but every transaction must be reported or handled via a bank that is designated as a foreign exchange bank. For a non-resident-owned Yuhan Hoesa real estate investment trust, the main convertibility concern is not the is not the existence of surrender requirements but the need to satisfy the real-demand rule and documentary evidence for every cross-border transfer. Foreign capital injected for the REIT must be declared under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and subsequent profit distributions and capital repatriations can be converted through a domestic bank provided that corporate tax, withholding tax, and foreign exchange reporting obligations are settled. There is no dual exchange rate and no restriction on the remittance of net profits for an entity with proper statutory audit and tax clearance, but the bank will impose an enhanced review when the underlying investments are real estate because of money-laundering typologies involving asset value manipulation and shell holding structures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected and settled through a Korean foreign exchange bank within 18 months of the export declaration date under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into Korean won; exporters may retain the foreign currency proceeds in a foreign-currency corporate account or convert them at the market rate. Any extension beyond the statutory deadline requires prior notification or approval from the Ministry of Economy and Finance, depending on the reason for the delay.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI and fintech platforms are not viable as the primary treasury or clearing rail for a Korean-resident entity. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only domestic banks designated as foreign exchange banks may perform Korean won conversion, foreign exchange reporting, and foreign investment registration services. Non-bank electronic financial businesses are not licensed to operate as foreign exchange banks for resident corporates, and they cannot legally open KRW deposit accounts, issue foreign-investment registration certificates, or act as settlement banks for dividend remittances and capital repatriation. A fintech EMI is therefore limited to a supplementary expense-card or invoice-correspondence role, and the treasurer should reject any attempt to route staging or disbursement liquidity through an unregulated offshore platform because the Korean bank would reject the downstream funds as non-compliant with source-of-funds rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Investors must remit share capital or subscription proceeds by SWIFT from their originating bank directly to the Korean entity's foreign-currency account at an authorized domestic foreign exchange bank. Before the remittance, the foreign exchange bank must receive a foreign direct investment notification on the appropriate FDI report so that the entity obtains a Foreign Investment Registration Certificate under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The bank will assign the official foreign exchange transaction purpose code for foreign direct investment and record the capital transfer in the Foreign Exchange Information System. After settlement, the bank converts the USD or other foreign currency into Korean won at the market rate, and an external CPA verifies the paid-in capital against the bank receipt and the issued certificate. The verified capital may then be released for the purchase or development of income-generating real estate assets, but every disbursement must be traceable to the registered purpose of the real estate investment trust.Trade and real-estate disbursements should be executed through domestic corporate internet banking using SWIFT MT103 for ordinary property acquisition payments and high-value wire transfers. For larger construction or escrow-type transactions, the Korean buyer should issue an irrevocable documentary letter of credit or use documentary collections with title documents through a domestic bank to ensure that payment occurs only upon presentation of compliant shipping or closing documents. Dividend payments to foreign shareholders are made by SWIFT MT103 with the foreign exchange purpose code for direct investment income remittance, accompanied by the bank's withholding certificate and evidence that the dividend was approved by the board and disclosed to tax authorities. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% maximum marginal CIT rate; progressive schedule 9% up to KRW 200 million taxable income, 19% up to KRW 20 billion, 21% up to KRW 300 billion, 24% above KRW 300 billion

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% statutory withholding on dividends paid to a non-resident corporation or non-resident individual, composed of 20% national income tax and 2% local income tax surcharge. Dividend payments to Korean resident shareholders are subject to 15.4% withholding under the Income Tax Act. A qualifying REIT distribution does not eliminate shareholder-level withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File preparation and filing are mandatory where cross-border related-party transactions of the Korean entity exceed KRW 50 billion in a fiscal year. A Country-by-Country Report is required where the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent reaches KRW 100 billion. Even below these thresholds, the Korea National Tax Service may require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where margin anomalies or financing arrangements are present.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply Gulf-Caribbean style Economic Substance Regulations, but it applies strict economic substance principles through the Framework Act on National Taxes, the Corporate Tax Act anti-avoidance rules, beneficial ownership tests, and OECD BEPS anti-conduit doctrines. A Yuhan Hoesa REIT must maintain real presence in Korea: registered Korean office, local directors or officers, physical or verifiable operating records, independent financial statements, actual investment and asset-management decisions, legal title to Korean real estate, and compliance with the Real Estate Investment Company Act. Substance-less structures will be denied treaty benefits and may face reassessment, penalty tax, license revocation, and criminal investigation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act on National Taxes, fraudulent underreports trigger a 40% penalty additional tax and late payment surcharge of 1.2% per month; under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years or a criminal fine of up to KRW 100 million, with aggravated economic crime penalties possible for large amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow beneficial owners may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties; the corporate veil may be pierced where the company is an alter ego or nominee vehicle. Personal consequences include civil restitution, asset attachment, travel bans, mandatory appearance orders, and imprisonment.
Shell Company, Economic Substance Deficiency, and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service will deny treaty withholding reductions, impose the full 22% statutory withholding rate, disallow the REIT income distribution deduction, and may assess up to 40% additional tax plus overdue surcharges. The Financial Services Commission may revoke the REIT registration or license and order termination of unlawful trust activities.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute false substance certifications, sign inaccurate resident certificates, or authorize conduit structures are personally liable for the resulting tax deficits and penalties. They face civil restitution to investors, travel bans, disqualification from serving as licensed REIT directors, and criminal prosecution.
AML/CFT and Unlicensed Real Estate Investment Trust Activity

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act, unlicensed REIT/trust activity is punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years or by a criminal fine of up to KRW 200 million, or both; failure to comply with the Specified Financial Information Act STR regime may result in imprisonment up to 3 years and fines up to KRW 50 million.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly manage an unlicensed REIT or permit money laundering through the trust are criminally liable, individually subject to asset forfeiture, freezing orders, travel bans, and imprisonment; they may also be required to personally compensate investors for losses caused by unlawful trust management.
Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure and Misallocation of Income

Statutory sanction: Korean tax authorities may reassess related-party income under the arm's length principle, disallow inflated deductions, impose interest and additional tax for underreported taxable income, and penalize failure to submit mandatory transfer pricing documentation. The penalty and interest can reach 40% of the adjusted income when combined with fraudulent understatement rules.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve transfer pricing policies without adequate documentation may be personally liable for the resulting corporate tax shortage if they knew or recklessly disregarded the non-arm's length structure; they face civil liability to the company and shareholders, arrest and travel bans, and criminal exposure for falsifying tax documents.

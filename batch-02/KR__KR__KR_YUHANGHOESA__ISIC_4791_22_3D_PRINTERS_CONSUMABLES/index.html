Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Korean courts and the National Tax Service accept a virtual office or commercial mailbox for corporate registration only if it is a real domestic address where statutory notices, tax invoices, and court documents can be personally served and where the company maintains some physical indication of business activity such as work space, telephone service, or inventory records. For an online retail seller of desktop 3D printers and resin filaments, no special industry license is required, but a virtual office is frequently rejected by Korean banks during corporate account opening because Financial Intelligence Unit customer due diligence standards require verification of the actual place of business and physical control over the registered address. National Tax Service audit officers also may disallow certain VAT-deductible expenses or issue a business-suspension notice if the address is only a mail-forwarding box with no human presence. A serviced office with a dedicated address, lease agreement, utility invoice, and access-controlled workspace is recommended to reduce bank, tax, and registry friction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of goods are zero-rated under Article 24 of the VAT Act when the goods physically depart Korea as evidenced by an accepted KCS export declaration, the purchaser is a foreign person/entity, and export proceeds are received in foreign currency and repatriated/settled in accordance with Korean foreign-exchange rules. For online retail of desktop 3D printers and resin filaments/consumables, export-by-parcel clearance must be made through UNI-PASS with confirmed foreign delivery; qualifying exported B2B services are also zero-rated, but the present scope is physical goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate) accepted by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / export parcel consignment evidence
  • Export Sales Contract or Purchase Order
  • Foreign Exchange Purchase and Settlement Certificate / bank remittance advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Proof of actual physical export including KCS customs examination report and carrier departure proof

Mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) issuance through NTS HomeTax e-Bill/e-Invoice interfaces for every VAT-registered business. Invoices must be issued to all VAT-registered customers no later than the day following the supply and transmitted digitally to the NTS via certified ERP/API integration where applicable. Korea does not employ real-time pre-clearance fiscal stamping; the NTS instead performs automated cross-matching of e-tax invoice data against VAT returns and customs records for post-filing anti-fraud control.

If the NTS determines that export documents are false, goods did not actually leave Korea, or the foreign-currency proceeds were not received within the 180-day repatriation deadline (or an approved extension), the zero-rating is denied: 10% VAT is assessed on the full export value plus statutory additional tax under the VAT Act and Framework Act on National Taxes (including civil penalties of up to 40% for fraudulent evasion and 20% for negligent underreporting), late-payment interest, and potential criminal liability for tax evasion; the exporter may additionally lose UNI-PASS fast clearance and export-zero-rating privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a liberalised but regulated foreign exchange regime rather than a strict capital-control system. The Bank of Korea, through authorised foreign exchange banks, requires declarations/reports for significant cross-border transactions, but does not routinely approve every remittance. The Korean won (KRW) is convertible on current account: export/import payments, royalties, service fees, and dividends may be settled freely if supporting documentation is retained. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity investment, loans, and sureties, are reportable and need a genuine economic purpose. Export proceeds are not forcibly surrendered; exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic foreign exchange accounts. However, profit remittance and dividend payments require the bank to hold audited financial statements, tax payment confirmation, withholding tax certificate, and a valid foreign investment report to prove access to the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) benefit. In practice, banks enforce local substance checks, customer due diligence, and BOK reporting, making onboarding dependent on clean documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export receipts must be received within six months (or such extended period as approved by the Bank of Korea) after the export declaration date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to Korean won; the full export proceeds may be retained in a domestic foreign currency account, but the receipt must be reported to the licensed foreign exchange bank and preserved as evidence of export performance.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), as the central bank and foreign exchange authority, acting together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Financial Services Commission (FSC) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the electronic financial supervision framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs domiciled outside Korea are not licensed under the Korean Banking Act or Electronic Financial Transactions Act and cannot serve as the principal clearing bank for a Yuhan Hoesa. They cannot provide a domestic KRW account for tax payments, VAT, payroll, or local commercial settlements. A registered domestic specialised electronic financial business may provide foreign exchange payment services only with a licensed bank settlement account. Therefore, cross-border EMIs are legally barred as primary treasury rails and should be used solely as payment-acquiring aggregators (Stripe, Shopify Payments). Viability rating: Low for a corporate bank account; Medium as a payments aggregation layer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward foreign direct investment must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's corporate account to the LLC's domestic foreign-currency account at a Tier-1 Korean bank. The remittance must carry a genuine purpose code for equity acquisition or foreign direct investment under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the bank files the foreign-exchange declaration and the investor reports under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act if the investment is reportable. After registration, the bank issues a Certificate of Foreign Investment. The foreign currency is converted to KRW at the market rate, and capital verification is performed by the bank or CPA. For e-commerce receivables, export card settlements from Stripe/Shopify are reported as current-account receipt through the online payment provider and swept into the operating account at the local bank.Trade-related payments for 3D-print resins, spare parts, filaments, and freight are executed through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account purchases; irrevocable commercial letters of credit for high-value or first-time supplier purchases; and documentary collections (D/P or D/A) when the bank needs to hold title documents until payment or acceptance. Export trade settlement can be under T/T open account for repeat buyers, and the local bank handles BOK declarative reporting.

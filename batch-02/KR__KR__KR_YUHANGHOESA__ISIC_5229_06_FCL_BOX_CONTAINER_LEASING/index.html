Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists under the Commercial Act, but the entire subscribed capital must be deposited and blocked in a capital subscription escrow account at a Korean bank before the court will register the company. For an intermodal container leasing company, a paid-in capital of KRW 100,000,000 is recommended to establish commercial creditworthiness. The state filing burden consists of registration tax of 0.4 percent of subscribed capital, an education surtax of 20 percent of the registration tax, and a court collection fee; for the recommended KRW 100,000,000 capital subscription, the aggregate official fee is approximately USD 650.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, electronic portal Home Tax Service at www.hometax.go.kr; export customs matters are administered by Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance portal at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must appoint a domestic tax representative recognised by the NTS, file the official Business Registration Application in the prescribed NTS form through Home Tax Service or at the competent district tax office, and submit statutory attachments comprising certificate of incorporation, foreign registration and corporate existence documentation, constitutional documents, domestic business office or lease evidence, and a power of attorney appointing the tax representative. After verification, the NTS assigns a ten-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the unified TIN, VAT registration number, and corporate income tax identifier. A domestic Yuhan Hoesa completes the same BRN procedure within twenty days of business commencement. The entity must additionally register as a trading entity in UNI-PASS with the same BRN before making export declarations. The BRN certificate is retrievable electronically through Home Tax Service and is the sole statutory tax identity credential for indirect tax transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every business operator, including a foreign-invested Yuhan Hoesa, must complete VAT registration before commencing taxable activity. The mandatory economic turnover threshold distinguishing general VAT taxpayers from simplified taxable persons is annual taxable supplies exceeding KRW 80,000,000 in the preceding calendar year; an entity supplying FCL container leasing services with expected consideration above that threshold must register as a general VAT taxpayer, issue standard tax invoices, and file regular VAT returns. In all cases, a newly incorporated Yuhan Hoesa must file the Business Registration Application within 20 days of business commencement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, zero-rating applies to goods that are actually exported under a customs export declaration. For cross-border FCL box container leasing, zero-rating is available if the container lease is characterised as an exported service or as an export of goods depending on the contractual and physical facts. The statutory criteria require that the supply is made to a non-resident lessee, the container is used outside Korea or in international shipping, the risk and benefit of the lease are offshore, and the consideration is paid in foreign currency. The taxpayer must retain complete transactional, movement, and foreign-exchange evidence to prove that the benefit of the supply is realised outside the Korean VAT territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued by Korea Customs Service showing the container export, export date, and classification
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing the international movement and foreign destination or offshore use of the leased container
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank proving receipt of foreign-currency lease consideration
  • Container leasing agreement and ISO container identification list demonstrating the foreign lessee, lease term, and offshore utilisation
  • Commercial invoice for the lease rental or export value prepared in foreign currency
  • Packing list or cargo manifest cross-referencing container numbers with the export declaration

Mandatory electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) must be issued for B2B supplies and transmitted through Home Tax Service or an NTS-approved electronic service provider no later than the day following the supply date. The NTS receives and cross-matches e-tax invoice data in real time to validate input VAT claims. For B2C transactions, the mandatory KCash Cash Receipt System must issue cash receipts. There is no separate anti-fraud digital stamping system in the Mexican or Italian sense; the anti-fraud mechanism is the mandatory real-time transmission to NTS, electronic invoice matching, periodic VAT reconciliation, and cash receipt surveillance.

The NTS may perform in-depth zero-rating audits and retrospectively disallow the 0% rate if the customs export declaration, Bill of Lading, lease documentation, or foreign-currency repatriation evidence is absent, inconsistent, or fabricated. Disallowed zero-rating is clawed back by assessing the standard 10% VAT on the supply value, denying associated input VAT refunds, and applying statutory imputed additional taxes for non-issuance, false e-tax invoices, underreporting, and late filing. Export proceeds must be repatriated within 180 days under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; failure to timely repatriate without a lawful extension can itself invalidate the zero-rating treatment. Fraudulent zero-rating claims may be referred for criminal prosecution under the Framework Act on Taxes and the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, with fines and imprisonment exposure in addition to full VAT recovery, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won (KRW) is convertible for current-account transactions, including merchanting, services, and ordinary lease payments, subject to submission of underlying trade or transaction documents through a licensed foreign-exchange bank. Capital-account convertibility is more constrained: foreign direct investment requires a notification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, typically processed through KOTRA or an authorized bank, while portfolio investments and external borrowings require prior declaration or approval. Profit remittance and liquidation proceeds are generally free once taxes are cleared, but each payment must be documented using statutory purpose codes and validated by a designated foreign-exchange bank. Korean residents, including domestic LLCs, may maintain foreign-currency accounts, but spending within Korea is mandatory in KRW; non-resident KRW accounts remain highly restricted and cannot be used for capital transactions absent licensed bank intermediation. Major practical hurdles include the strict documentary audit trail for cross-border rental income, the requirement to match foreign-currency receipts to export declarations or service invoices, and an anti-abuse scrutiny of corporate structures backed by foreign shareholders. The BOK and MOEF jointly enforce capital-flow management measures, ensuring that all outward remittances of dividends or capital reduce the entity's registered investment amount and must be supported by audited financial statements and a certificate of investment completion. The overall regime is liberal for trade and leasing, but the Korean user of an offshore fintech EMI cannot access onshore KRW clearing; all local payment and collection must transit a regulated Korean bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export and cross-border service proceeds must be collected within 15 months of the export declaration or shipment date, unless extended by the foreign-exchange bank. Exporters may hold the proceeds in a domestic foreign-currency account or deliver them to a licensed foreign-exchange bank; there is no mandatory percentage conversion for ordinary trade proceeds, but any conversion to KRW must be executed through a licensed bank and reported using the appropriate export settlement code. For leasing income from intermodal containers, the rental invoices and equipment sub-headers must demonstrate a valid service export so that the remittance can be classified as current-account earnings and not as an unapproved capital inflow.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the foreign exchange information system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Presidential Decree

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and not legally viable for onshore KRW operations. An offshore digital EMI, even if licensed in Singapore, the EU, or the UK, is not permitted to open KRW clearing accounts or provide local payment rails in South Korea. Korean banking law requires all inbound and outbound KRW flows to be settled through domestic licensed banks, and a foreign LLC entity cannot hold a virtual account without a real bank partnership. The EMI can only serve as a technical layer for foreign-currency collection, but it cannot satisfy capital verification, tax withholding, or statutory FX reporting obligations. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must designate a Korean Tier-1 commercial bank as the primary settlement rail and may use EMIs merely for incidental foreign-currency receivables if the EMI has a licensed Korean bank sponsor; in practice, no such arrangement is available on a compliant basis.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Comprehensive Local File and Master File mandatory if intercompany transactions exceed KRW 10 billion per year (or KRW 5 billion for transactions with parties in tax havens). Country-by-Country Report required if aggregate group revenue ≥ KRW 300 billion. Penalty for non-submission: up to 3% of the transaction amount and criminal liability for willful omission.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply under the international anti-BEPS framework and the Korean tax reform incorporating the OECD 'Core Income-Generating Activities' (CIGA) test. A container leasing entity must demonstrate that the leasing activities, management decisions, asset maintenance, and risk control functions are actually performed in Korea (or in a qualified foreign jurisdiction) through qualified employees and premises. If the entity is a 'managed holding company' with no substantive local staff, the National Tax Service may recharacterize income as attributable to a foreign permanent establishment or, under the dividend stripping rules, impose 22% withholding on deemed repatriation and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act Article 118 (Penal Provisions): imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine of up to KRW 100 million; additional 40% evasion penalty on understated tax; for amounts exceeding KRW 1 billion, aggravated punishment up to 10 years imprisonment under Article 18-2 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Director personal liability: Under the Corporate Tax Act and the Commercial Act, directors who knowingly participate in tax evasion are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax plus penalties. The courts can pierce the veil to impose personal liability for the full tax liability. Directors may be blacklisted by the National Tax Service, restricting travel, obtaining government contracts, and financial transactions; criminal conviction results in a criminal record and possible imprisonment, as well as asset seizure to cover unpaid taxes.
False Claim of Tax Treaty Benefits / Abuse of DTA

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 54-2, abusive treaty claims are denied, and the full 22% withholding tax plus 40% negligence penalty is imposed. The act may also trigger criminal penalties under Article 118 of the Corporate Tax Act for false declarations, and the tax authorities will issue a 'badge of fraud' determination leading to the 10-year statute of limitations (ordinary statute is 5 years).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the false treaty claim are personally liable for the additional tax and penalties. Under the ‘piercing the corporate veil’ doctrine, a director may be held criminally liable for fraud, and the court may order restitution plus unpaid taxes. Travel bans (blacklisting) and entry restriction to all OECD member states are possible after Interpol red notice for cross-border tax fraud; imprisonment up to 5 years is commonly applied.
File Fabrication and False Accounting (AML Integration)

Statutory sanction: The Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Economic Crimes imposes imprisonment of 1 year to life (up to life for amounts ≥ KRW 5 billion) and fines of up to 5 times the laundered amount. Under Article 102 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, illegal cross-border transfers result in penalties up to 70% of the transferred amount. The National Tax Service will impose the 40% perjury penalty and also report to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) under the Specialized Financial Supervision Act.

Director personal liability: Directors are individually liable for money laundering offenses; the corporate veil is pierced because the company is the instrument of crime. Personal assets can be seized at the criminal confiscation stage. Directors face a mandatory travel ban, entry to South Korea and treaty partners is restricted, and personal criminal liability includes imprisonment from 3 to 10 years, with a life sentence possible for aggravated amounts. The Financial Supervisory Service can also impose a lifetime business restriction on the director from serving as a corporate officer in any financial or leasing business.

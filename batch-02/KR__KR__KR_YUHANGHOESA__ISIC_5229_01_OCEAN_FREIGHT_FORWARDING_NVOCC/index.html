Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic portal (www.hometax.go.kr); customs and export declaration filing is administered by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS system (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Korean Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) must first complete incorporation under the Korean Commercial Act and register the corporation at the local court registry. The foreign investment must then be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, usually through a KOTRA investment support center or the Trade-Korea single-window portal. Tax registration is obtained by filing a business registration application with the district tax office of the National Tax Service having jurisdiction over the registered head office. The application must be submitted within one month of the commencement of business and accompanied by the articles of incorporation, certificate of corporate registration, evidence of the domestic place of business (lease agreement or title deed), the foreign parent entity's certificate of incorporation or equivalent legal existence document, board resolution appointing a domestic tax representative or statutory representative, and a power of attorney if using a certified tax agent. The NTS then issues a Business Registration Certificate containing the unique Business Registration Number (TIN), which also functions as the value-added tax (VAT) registration number. For a non-resident seller of services or digital goods without a domestic fixed place of business, a domestic tax representative must be appointed to complete VAT registration and file returns through HomeTax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A Korean domestic Yuhan Hoesa is treated as a domestic corporation taxed at the entity level under the Corporate Tax Act. There is no statutory mechanism to elect pass-through treatment or territorial scope for a domestic LLC. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Adjustment of International Tax Act apply to address double-deduction and deduction-without-inclusion outcomes, but no elective safe harbour is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover exemption threshold for VAT registration. Any person making taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea as a business owner must register with the National Tax Service. A simplified taxable person regime applies when the previous calendar-year taxable turnover is below KRW 30,000,000, but registration remains mandatory and the simplified regime affects remittance and invoicing mechanics only, not the existence of a tax registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to exported goods where the goods are removed from Korea under an accepted export declaration; services performed wholly outside Korea; international transportation of goods or passengers; and other foreign-exchange-earning services provided to non-residents, including qualifying international freight forwarding. For an NVOCC ocean freight forwarder, the freight forwarding commission or net fee qualifies as zero-rated only where the service is genuinely connected to international ocean carriage, evidenced by a carrier master bill of lading or a recognized house bill of lading issued by the NVOCC, proof of actual international movement of the cargo, and a direct payment or invoicing link to the foreign counterparty. Merely incidental inland handling, warehousing, or port services that are not internationally connected remain subject to standard-rate VAT at 10%.

  • Export Declaration submitted to the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS (the statutory Korean export declaration equivalent to a Bill of Entry)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or NVOCC House Bill of Lading evidencing actual international carriage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Bank Receipt Certification showing the export sales proceeds have been received from outside Korea

All registered Korean VAT entrepreneurs must issue electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoices) through the NTS HomeTax electronic invoicing system. The e-Tax Invoice must be transmitted to the National Tax Service by the end of the day following the date of supply. There is no general third-party anti-fraud digital stamping regime in Korean VAT; transaction integrity is protected by NTS-issued digital signatures, the mandatory electronic transmission channel, and the HomeTax cross-matching database. Taxpayers may use the NTS HomeTax web interface or the NTS Open API for machine-to-machine electronic invoice filing and clearance-related data exchange.

The National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service may jointly audit export transactions and require production of the export declaration, bill of lading, invoice, and foreign exchange receipt documentation. If zero-rated export treatment is found to be fraudulent, unsubstantiated, or not supported by actual foreign reimbursement within the statutory period, the NTS will retroactively assess the unpaid 10% VAT, impose additional tax penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and may impose criminal fines or imprisonment for intentional tax evasion. The taxpayer bears the burden of maintaining a complete documentary chain for at least five years from the date of export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a broadly liberalised but report-heavy foreign exchange control regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is fully convertible for most current-account transactions, including trade in services, freight forwarding, and remittance of dividends, provided the transaction has a genuine underlying commercial purpose and is reported to the registered foreign-exchange bank. Capital-account transactions are free in principle under the positive system but require prior or post facto reporting, as applicable. Export proceeds do not have to be surrendered into won; however, they must be collected through a licensed foreign-exchange bank within statutory periods and either held in a domestic foreign-currency account or converted to KRW. Profit remittance for non-resident shareholders is generally permissible after tax clearance, but banks will require documentary evidence of underlying earnings, certified financial statements, tax payment confirmations, and board resolutions. Practical hurdles are not regulatory convertibility limits but strict AML/KYC documentation, beneficial ownership certification, tax withholding, and Korea's real-name financial transaction verification. Overall, South Korea is assessed as a highly convertible jurisdiction with high compliance intensity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received within 18 months from the date of export declaration; if the goods are shipped after the export declaration, the period runs from the shipment date. Extension of up to 6 months is available with the approval of the relevant foreign exchange bank for justifiable causes. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency, but must deposit them in a domestic foreign-exchange bank account and report settlement through the Korea Foreign Exchange Information System (KFIS).

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), operating in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low - not viable as a standalone banking relationship. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, foreign fintech EMI companies without a domestic bank license cannot open Korean-won settlement accounts or act as licensed foreign exchange banks. They may only provide payment aggregation if they partner with a licensed Korean bank; in practice, no standalone foreign EMI account will satisfy statutory requirements for corporate FX reporting or capital-account movement. Direct Tier-1 domestic bank onboarding is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the company's designated capital escrow foreign-currency account at a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank. The remittance instruction must carry the correct KFIS foreign-exchange purpose code corresponding to inward direct investment or equity acquisition. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the funds and provides a certificate of inward remittance / foreign investment registration. Under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the foreign investor must file the investment report through KOTRA or the bank, and the company must obtain a Certificate of Foreign Investment Registration before converting the capital into Korean won for operational use. All payments must be matched to the appropriate KFIS foreign-exchange purpose code for service payments or import settlement and backed by invoices, bills of lading, contracts, and customs declarations. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation and submission is mandatory when aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; Master File is required when such transactions exceed KRW 50 billion or where the consolidated Korean/MNE group revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion, subject to exceptions in the International Tax Adjustment Act.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a standalone ESR code, but the NTS applies the equivalent substance-over-form doctrine, the General Anti-Abuse Rule, CFC rules, and OECD BEPS principles to deny treaty or transfer-pricing benefits to shell freight-forwarding entities. For an NVOCC, Core Income-Generating Activities are the negotiation of freight contracts, booking of vessel space, container consolidation, issuance of house bills of lading, invoicing cargo customers, and managing foreign-agency relationships. These CIGA must be performed by qualified employees with actual decision-making power in Korea; if a director signs a tax return asserting more than this, the false representation supports piercing the corporate veil.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Violation of Articles 3 and 14 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and Articles 47-2/47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes: imprisonment up to 3 years (up to 5 years where evaded tax exceeds KRW 300 million), plus a fine up to 3 times the evaded tax; administrative additional tax of 40% of the unpaid tax and daily late-payment surcharge.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced because the Yuhan Hoesa is used as a cloak for fraud; the director is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, subject to civil asset attachment, NTS overseas-travel blacklisting, disqualification from management of foreign-invested enterprises, and a personal imprisonment sentence.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Fraud / Offshore Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Violation of Articles 4, 5 and 12 of the International Tax Adjustment Act: the NTS re-determines the arm's-length price, disallows the deductions, and adds a 30% additional penalty on the adjusted tax, plus daily overdue interest; criminal referral applies if the transfer-pricing adjustment is accompanied by fraudulent declarations.

Director personal liability: Piercing the veil arises where the interposed shell lacks commercial purpose; the director must restore diverted profits from personal assets, faces a travel ban and IVTS Blacklisting as a high-risk tax delinquent, and may be sentenced to imprisonment for the false transfer-pricing declaration.
AML / Sanctions and Shell-Company Abuse in Ocean Freight Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Violation of Article 4 of the Act on Regulation and Punishment of Criminal Proceeds Concealment and Article 9-2 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act: imprisonment up to 5 years, confiscation of freight proceeds, fines up to KRW 300 million, revocation of the NVOCC license by the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries, and financial sanctions by the Financial Services Commission.

Director personal liability: The company is deemed a facade used solely for sanctions evasion; directors face personal criminal conviction, civil forfeiture of assets located in Korea, overseas-travel blacklisting, disqualification from any maritime transport license, and imprisonment from 1 to 5 years depending on the amount laundered.

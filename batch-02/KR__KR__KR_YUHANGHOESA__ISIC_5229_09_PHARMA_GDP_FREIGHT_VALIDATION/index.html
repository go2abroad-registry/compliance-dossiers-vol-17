Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for non-resident suppliers. Any business that makes taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea, including via cross-border electronic or digital services, must register with the NTS regardless of turnover. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act Article 0.0 (Zero-rating of exported goods and services), exports of all tangible goods are zero-rated when: (a) the goods are shipped or transported out of the Republic of Korea within the statutory export period; (b) the export is evidenced by a Customs Export Declaration (수출신고필증) issued under the Customs Act; (c) title and risk have passed to a non-resident purchaser; and (d) the foreign exchange has been received in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Additionally, qualifying offshore digital services (e.g., B2B digital services performed entirely outside Korea) may be zero-rated if the recipient is a non-resident business, the service is supplied to a foreign territory and the NTS Foreign Exchange Transaction Certificate shows inward remittance from abroad. The claim must be supported by standard export documentary evidence and made in the quarterly VAT return. For goods, the zero rate applies both to outright exports and to sales of goods to be delivered in Korea but then moved abroad under customs supervision (bonded export transactions).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate / 수출신고필증) issued by the Korea Customs Service (UNI-PASS)
  • Original or electronic commercial invoice with the statutory zero-rating indication
  • Bill of Lading (for sea shipments) or Airway Bill (for air shipments) showing the Korean exporter and foreign consignee
  • Packing list and certificate of origin where required
  • Supplier's VAT registration certificate and tax representative appointment deed for any third-party handlers (e.g., GDP freight forwarder)
  • Proof of foreign exchange repatriation either via the institutionally designated foreign exchange bank's 'Import/Export Exchange Payment Confirmation' (수출대금 입금증명) or a Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation Certificate
  • Freight forwarding contract and customs broker power of attorney evidencing physical outbound logistics for pharmaceutical GDP cold chain

Mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-invoice) issuance through HomeTax, with real-time data transmission to the NTS under the 'Electronic Tax Invoice Mandatory Issuance System'. Since 2023, e-invoices are compulsory for all B2B transactions where the total invoice value exceeds KRW 100,000, and for B2C transactions regarding simplified tax invoices; issuance must be transmitted to the NTS within 3 days after the supply tax point (day after the statutory invoice date for daily aggregation). Failure to transmit falsifies the claim base. The system requires API integration or the HomeTax batch upload portal, and all invoices must contain an electronic serial number (codeferred by NTS), the VAT payer's registration number, recipient's registration number, date, item description, net amount, VAT amount and grand total. No separate cryptographic fiscal stamping exists, but NTS data validation is automated and anti-fraud matching uses cross-referencing between customs (UNI-PASS) filings, bank remittance evidence and e-invoice data.

The NTS retail VAT audit unit and Korea Customs Service jointly inspect export zero-rating claims. If documentary evidence (particularly the Export Declaration Certificate and foreign exchange remittance certificate) is fraudulent, incomplete, or the goods are later found not to have physically left Korea, the NTS will assess the full 10% VAT on the export value (denying the zero rate) and impose an additional 10% of the assessed tax as an administrative fine under the Framework Act on National Taxes (equivalent to under-reporting penalties). If the false claim is deemed tax evasion ('severe negligence' or 'fraudulent evasion'), a penalty of 40% of the unpaid tax may be imposed, plus a 20% overdue charge for unpaid periods. Criminal prosecution under Article 10 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act can lead to imprisonment not exceeding 2 years or a fine not exceeding two times the evaded amount. Furthermore, the NTS will claw back any previously refunded input VAT directly attributable to the erroneous zero-rated exports, and the tax representative becomes jointly liable for the unpaid VAT, penalties and interest. For pharmaceutical GDP logistics, additional regulatory sanctions under the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act (suspension of the GDP license) may be triggered if the export documentation falsification also violates the Good Distribution Practice certification requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized, largely market-determined foreign exchange regime, managed not by exchange controls but by a reporting and verification framework. Current-account transactions, such as export/import settlements, freight and logistics fees, royalties, and ordinary service payments, are freely convertible through designated foreign exchange banks after the submitting entity provides a proper transaction classification and evidential documents; no prior central-bank consent is normally required. Capital-account transactions, however, are subject to a stricter architecture. An inbound equity subscription to a Korean limited liability company is a foreign-direct-investment capital transaction that must be reported or approved under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; the funds must be remitted by the identified shareholder and received into a Korean bank account in the name of the resident entity. Profit remittances are legally current-account-like but are treated as current-account transactions requiring strict verification: the bank requires audited financial statements, shareholder and board resolutions, tax clearance certificates, and proof of distributable retained earnings, and the remittance may be stopped where dividend documentation is incomplete. Export proceeds are not subject to a mandatory surrender ratio; the exporter may keep the foreign currency in a foreign-currency account or convert it at market rate. The practical convertibility hurdles are therefore not formal restrictions but customer due diligence, transaction code accuracy, economic-purpose validation, and the de facto in-person KYC requirements for foreign directors and shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, there is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the Bank of Korea and no fixed conversion ratio to KRW. Exporters must obtain payment through one of the accepted settlement methods in the underlying contract (advance payment, sight L/C, usance L/C, D/P, D/A, or open account). For ordinary pharma freight and logistics services, the standard statutory collection expectation is that the full invoice payment is received within 90 days from the date of export declaration or service completion; a longer collection window may be declared and approved for project-type contracts or capital goods with deferred payment terms, but the complete proceeds must be repatriated by the confirmed deadline and either credited to the selling entity's domestic foreign-currency account or converted to KRW at the market rate. Any overdue non-collection must be explained to the designated foreign exchange bank and the Korea Customs Service, with enforcement reflected in the entity's trade statistics and compliance profile.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, exercised jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MoEF) and the Korea Customs Service as the statutory foreign-exchange control apparatus of South Korea.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for entity domiciliation and primary treasury. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by the Financial Services Commission nor registered as designated foreign-exchange businesses to hold KRW or execute Korean payments; they cannot provide local corporate bank accounts, IBANs, or permitted capital-verification evidence. They may only be used as an ancillary non-KRW expense channel for small operational payments to foreign suppliers where the EMI is fully licensed in its home jurisdiction. All equity injections, export collections, tax payments, and outbound trade settlements must flow through a domestic designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Using an EMI as the main operating bank would trigger automatic compliance rejection and may invalidate capital verification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound equity wire must be sent by the actual shareholder identified in the Korean LLC's articles and foreign-investment notification; third-party payment is prohibited. The funds should be routed via SWIFT through the remitter's home bank to the Korean entity's capital escrow account at a designated foreign exchange bank. On the wire, the remitter must include the Korean foreign-exchange transaction purpose classification for equity subscription/investment, the name of the target Korean entity, and the investment registration or notification number where required. The receiving bank will match the wire, remitter name, amount, and purpose code against the board resolution and investment report before crediting. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% national CIT on first KRW 200,000,000 taxable income; 19% above KRW 200,000,000; 21% above KRW 20,000,000,000; 24% above KRW 300,000,000,000, plus 10% local income tax surtax on the national CIT, producing a 9.9% aggregate rate on the first bracket

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax on gross dividends paid to non-residents, plus 10% local income tax surtax on the withholding amount, giving an aggregate statutory rate of 22%; a Double Tax Treaty may reduce the rate to 5-15% depending on shareholding and treaty terms.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required for a South Korean Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. There is no mandatory blocked escrow account. However, the initial share capital must be fully paid into a temporary capital deposit bank account before the court registration, and a bank capital deposit certificate must be filed with the court. If the foreign investor needs foreign investment status under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the investment amount must satisfy the statutory reporting threshold of KRW 100,000,000, but this threshold is not a general paid-in capital formation minimum.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Confirm the existence of a Korean place of business; if none, appoint a domestic tax representative under Article 82 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. Step 2: Prepare the corporate formation dossier, including certificate of incorporation and articles of association (with apostille/consular legalization), board resolution appointing the local officer or tax representative, power of attorney, and passport copies of authorized signatories. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT (0%) applies to the export of tangible goods under Article 21 of the VAT Act, including wholesale distribution of clinical diagnostic ELISA assays and reagents, provided the goods are definitively exported from Korea to a foreign destination. Qualifying offshore digital services or export-related services may also be zero-rated. The exporter must maintain contemporaneous documentation demonstrating physical export and receive the export proceeds in foreign currency within the statutory repatriation window.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Electronic Export Declaration Receipt / Export Declaration Confirmation)
  • Commercial Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill) evidencing physical movement from Korea to the foreign destination
  • Foreign Exchange Bank transaction statement / Certificate of Export Proceeds (equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate) evidencing foreign currency receipt
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • KCS EDI export clearance report and customs broker confirmation

All VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices (ETIs) through the NTS Hometax platform. Detailed supply data must be transmitted to the NTS within one business day after the supply. Real-time validation, anti-fraud analytics, and digital signature authentication (using an authorized public certificate) are mandated. ERP/API integration is supported. Violations of electronic invoicing obligations attract penalty taxes and rejection of input VAT deductions.

Fraudulent or unsubstantiated export zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective VAT assessment at the standard 10% rate, plus additional tax charges under Article 60 of the VAT Act (up to 40% for intentional misdeclaration and 10% for negligent understatement) plus daily late-payment surcharges. Criminal penalties may be imposed under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, including imprisonment and fines for tax evasion. Customs violations may trigger additional penalties under the Customs Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and any refunded VAT amount is clawed back with accrued interest. The NTS may also invalidate the relevant e-tax invoices, disallow input VAT credits, and pursue criminal referrals for serious offenses.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a free-floating exchange rate regime under a broad current and capital account liberalization framework. The Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, profit remittances, dividends, and royalties, subject to filing and verification through designated foreign exchange banks. Capital account transactions require a more formal reporting or licensing framework; while portfolio investment and inbound equity ownership are largely open, resident and non-resident entities must route transactions through a domestic foreign exchange bank and within the prescribed purpose codes. Surrender requirements are no longer a blanket mandatory conversion rule; however, exporters must convert FD or KRW proceeds within a settlement period under a 'received foreign exchange holding' system unless a deferral or exemption is granted by the bank. Profit remittance hurdles are predominantly documentary: audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, pay-in evidence of subscribed paid-in capital, and a draft remittance application under purpose code C-I-1 (dividend distribution). In practice, the capital verification requirement of the Foreign Investment Promotion Act for foreign-invested enterprises requires a certificate of completion of external capital introduction before any outbound capital transfer. The overall convertibility is characterized as high, but the reverse regulatory requirement to repatriate export proceeds within a designated time horizon, and the need to obtain advance notification for aggregate cross-border borrowings above KRW 30 billion equivalent, constitute nuanced operational frictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Bank of Korea's Foreign Exchange Operation Regulations, exporters must sell or convert export foreign currency receipts into KRW within the settlement due date, generally the foreign exchange receipt date plus a statutory tolerance of 30 calendar days, unless the exporter has applied for and received an extension or qualifies for an exemption under the 'export proceeds reinvestment' or 'offshore trade receivables offset' provisions. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% of the export proceeds received in foreign currency, as Korean law requires the repatriation and settlement of trade receivables in Korea unless a specific exemption is granted. For a distributor of diagnostic reagents, the invoice currency may be USD but the equivalent KRW must be accounted for and the trade settlement through a domestic bank is mandatory, with proof of settlement submitted for export tax rebate and customs clearance.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central monetary authority, operating in tandem with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) through the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, delegated to the Foreign Exchange Information System and the Korea Customs Service for trade settlement monitoring

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions and neobanks have a legally constrained viability in South Korea. Any financial services entity, including a transfer-only EMI, must obtain a license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act or be registered as a small-amount electronic payment service provider with the Financial Services Commission. A non-resident overseas EMI, particularly one operating solely through a remote account opening interface without a physical Korean branch, is legally barred from maintaining a domestic won settlement account or holding a foreign exchange business license. Consequently, offshore EMIs cannot be used as primary treasury rails for a Korean corporation; they may only act as a supplementary document repository or source of reference, never as the regulated clearing bank. The recommended treasury route is through a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank acting as the designated foreign exchange bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound capital injection, the funds must be routed via MT103 SWIFT wire directly to the capital escrow account of the Yuhan Hoesa at a designated foreign exchange bank. The wire must carry the correct foreign exchange purpose code under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, most appropriately 'FD-1' for acquisition of shares or capital investment by a non-resident, or 'FD-2' for acquisition of contribution certificates. The bank is required to verify the foreign investor's identity, the foreign investment claim (under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act), and the receipt of the funds. Once the capital is credited, the bank issues a certificate of foreign exchange purchase, and the company must register the investment with the Korean Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (MOTIE) within 30 days. The capital verification process for statutory purposes likewise involves the bank confirming that the remitting party is exactly the shareholder, and the payment purpose is exclusively an equity injection; no personal or third-party payments are accepted unless structured as a subordinated loan with prior approval.Outbound trade disbursements for the distribution of IVD diagnostic reagents are facilitated through the following rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct delivery-based trade settlement with pre-approved foreign beneficiaries, always referencing the relevant export/import contract and the harmonized system code 3002.12 for diagnostic reagents; (2) Commercial letters of credit (L/C) are used for high-value repeat orders from hospitals and clinical reference laboratories, where the issuing bank confirms the documents, especially the certificate of analysis and cold-chain temperature logs, with a maximum tenour of 90 days under documentary credit terms; (3) Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) are suitable for lower-value transfers or for markets with hard currency deficiency, but the foreign exchange bank will require the submission of the original transport documents and a proof of delivery receipt before releasing the proceeds. All outbound disbursements require the submission of a 'payment order to foreign country' under the designated purpose code, including 'S-3' for purchases of goods or 'T-1' for trade-related services. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital electronic money institutions and neobanks have a legally constrained viability in South Korea. Any financial services entity, including a transfer-only EMI, must obtain a license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act or be registered as a small-amount electronic payment service provider with the Financial Services Commission. A non-resident overseas EMI, particularly one operating solely through a remote account opening interface without a physical Korean branch, is legally barred from maintaining a domestic won settlement account or holding a foreign exchange business license. Consequently, offshore EMIs cannot be used as primary treasury rails for a Korean corporation; they may only act as a supplementary document repository or source of reference, never as the regulated clearing bank. The recommended treasury route is through a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank acting as the designated foreign exchange bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

South Korean national Corporate Income Tax under CITA Article 55 is progressive: 10% on tax base up to KRW 200,000,000; 20% above KRW 200,000,000 up to KRW 20,000,000,000; 22% above KRW 20,000,000,000 up to KRW 300,000,000,000; and 24% above KRW 300,000,000,000. A 10% Local Corporate Income Tax surcharge is added to the national corporate tax, producing an aggregate top effective rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate on outbound dividends paid by a Korean corporation to a non-resident shareholder: 20% national Corporate Income Tax withholding under CITA plus 2% Local Corporate Income Tax surcharge. A DTA may reduce the rate, commonly to 10% for beneficial corporate shareholders owning at least 10% of voting capital and to 15% otherwise.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Korean transfer pricing documentation thresholds under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs require a Local File when aggregate annual foreign related-party transactions exceed KRW 50,000,000,000; a Master File when aggregate annual foreign related-party transactions exceed KRW 100,000,000,000; and a Country-by-Country Report when the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least KRW 1,000,000,000,000. Even below these thresholds, contemporaneous OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation is strongly advisable for a wholly foreign-owned Korean IVD reagent distribution entity.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a zero-tax Economic Substance Regulations regime like the UAE, but economic substance doctrine is enforced through OECD BEPS standards, the Korean general anti-abuse rule, and treaty limitation-on-benefits and principal-purpose tests. The Korean Yuhan Hoesa must prove Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea: local distribution decisions, reagent inventory ownership and risk, qualified sales and regulatory personnel, independent board meetings, and the required IVD wholesale license. If the Korean entity is a registered shell with no commercial activity, no employees, no local decision-making, and no regulatory substance, NTS will disregard the entity, deny treaty benefits, and reattribute profits to the foreign shareholders.

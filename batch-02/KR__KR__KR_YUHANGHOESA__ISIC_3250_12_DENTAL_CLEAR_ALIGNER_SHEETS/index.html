Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea operates an open current-account convertibility regime under Article 6 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act: trade-related settlements, service payments, royalties, and dividend distributions may be freely converted and remitted through a licensed foreign exchange bank once the underlying transaction, tax clearance, and anti-money laundering declarations are evidenced. Capital-account transactions, by contrast, remain subject to notification, declaration, or post-verification; foreign-direct-investment capital subscriptions, earn-out payments, and equity purchases require FDI registration, bank confirmation, and statistical reporting to BOK through the Foreign Exchange Information System. KRW is not an entirely unrestricted international settlement currency: cross-border capital transfers at unusual velocity or value invite anti-round-tripping scrutiny. Profit remittance for a Yuhan Hoesa is the least obstructed route when corporate income tax is settled and audited financial statements support distributable earnings; the standard friction is withholding tax documentation and proof that the recipient is the ultimate beneficial owner under the Korea tax treaty network. The practical picture is a moderately liberal capital account where compliance documentation, not discretionary approval, is the true control mechanism.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No mandatory conversion ratio: effectively 0% surrender. Export receipts must be collected through licensed FX banks within the agreed trade credit period, generally not exceeding one year from export declaration unless a deferred payment plan is approved; each receipt must be reported under a foreign exchange transaction code using the export declaration and invoice. The exporter may keep foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account and draw down or convert later, subject to annual reporting and not solely financing unlawful capital outflow.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the monetary and foreign exchange authority, acting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and supervised by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) through Korea Customs Service and licensed foreign exchange banks for reporting and declaration enforcement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local File required when annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion; Master File required when aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion; simplified documentation for cross-border transactions between KRW 1 billion and KRW 10 billion.

      
Economic substance scope: Korea has no standalone economic substance regulation (like the UAE's ESR), but the substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 2-10 of the Framework Act on National Taxes apply to disregard transactions lacking genuine commercial purpose or economic substance. For tax treaty purposes, Korea applies the OECD Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under the MLI, and a paper company without substantive activity in Korea cannot claim treaty benefits. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis (e.g., strategic commercial decisions on aligner manufacturing, staffing, inventory risk) must be demonstrably performed in Korea to maintain residence and avoid a PE charge elsewhere.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting
        
Statutory sanction: Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to 3 times the amount of evaded tax. The NTS will further impose a 20% negligence penalty on the underpaid tax plus daily late-payment interest.

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where fraud is found; directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid tax, interest, and penalties. Criminal conviction results in imprisonment up to 5 years and loss of internet banking privileges; the director is placed on the NTS 'High-Value Delinquent Taxpayer' blacklist, triggering travel bans, asset seizure, and public naming.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Arm's-Length Violation
        
Statutory sanction: Under Articles 4-6 of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA), the NTS can adjust taxable income based on the arm's-length price and add a 10% surcharge on the under-declared adjustment. Failure to produce documentation triggers a penalty of up to KRW 50 million and permits the NTS to use its own comparables and estimation methods.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly executed the non-arm's-length transactions are personally liable for the additional corporate tax and interest if the company is impaired; in egregious cases, criminal charges for tax evasion are prosecuted and directors may be barred from re-entering Korea until taxes are settled.

      

      
      

        Withholding Tax Failure on Non-Resident Payments
        
Statutory sanction: Under Article 126-1 of the Income Tax Act, the payer (company) is personally liable for the withheld tax not collected, plus a 10-20% penalty, and monthly interest at 0.025% per day. The NTS will levy a pre-final assessment and may impose criminal liability under the Tax Act for willful violation.

        
Director personal liability: Directors serve as statutory withholding agents; where failure is intentional or reckless, the director may be held personally liable for the unpaid WHT and penalties. Criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine up to KRW 10 million, and the director may be banned from serving as a director for up to five years.

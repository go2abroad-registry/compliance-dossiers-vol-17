Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). Korea Customs Service (KCS) via UNI-PASS (www.unipass.customs.go.kr) for customs border procedures and import VAT collection.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity forming a Yuhan Hoesa limited liability company in Korea must first complete incorporation registration with the Korean court registry and obtain the certificate of incorporation and corporate seal. Within one month from business commencement, the entity must file a Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office through the HomeTax portal or in person, submitting the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, shareholder register, registered office lease contract, and proof of identity for the statutory representative. The NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Number that serves simultaneously as the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Foreign non-resident enterprises without a Korean establishment that engage in VAT taxable supplies must appoint a Korean tax representative and register for VAT through HomeTax in order to obtain a separate VAT identification number.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold; registration is mandatory from the first taxable business supply. A simplified taxpayer regime exists for eligible small businesses with annual turnover below KRW 80 million, but a wholesale green coffee bean distribution operation is outside the simplified regime and must register as a general VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act for (a) supply of goods actually exported from Korea to a foreign destination and (b) supply of services used or consumed outside Korea. The export supply must be supported by an accepted Korea Customs Service export declaration, evidence of shipment, and evidence that foreign currency proceeds were received through a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank. Imported green coffee beans are subject to normal 10% VAT at the import stage, and those import VAT amounts can be credited as input VAT against output VAT on subsequent taxable domestic supplies. If imported green coffee beans are later re-exported from Korea, the re-export supply may be zero-rated provided the full export documentation package is preserved.

  • Customs Export Declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS, containing the validated export declaration number and HS classification
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment from Korea to the foreign buyer
  • Commercial Invoice signed by the Korean exporter and foreign purchaser
  • Packing List
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank, functionally equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), evidencing foreign currency repatriation
  • Electronic tax invoice issued through the NTS HomeTax system for the zero-rated export transaction

All VAT-registered entities must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax electronic tax invoice system. The e-tax invoice must be transmitted to the NTS in real time via the HomeTax API by the day following issuance. Each transmitted invoice receives an NTS-assigned receipt number for central cross-matching and anti-fraud reconciliation. Digital certificates are required for the person issuing the invoice. This government-coupled electronic clearing and reconciliation mechanism replaces any form of paper-based invoice clearance.

The NTS cross-matches electronic tax invoices, Korea Customs Service export declarations, and Korean Foreign Exchange Bank settlement data. If export zero-rating is not substantiated, or if foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the applicable statutory deadline without an approved extension, the zero rate is retroactively invalidated and VAT is reassessed at 10%. Additional tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act may be imposed, including fraud penalties, interest, and criminal liability for false export declarations or fabricated e-tax invoices. The statutory assessment period is normally five years, extended to ten years where tax evasion or fraudulent documentation is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a historically liberalized foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is convertible on the current account without repatriation limits for trade transactions, subject to the Real Name Financial Transactions Act. On the capital account, the Foreign Exchange Transactions Law enables free capital flows but imposes a post-event declaration (or pre-event notification) requirement for selected transactions including foreign direct investment and cross-border loans. There is no requirement to surrender export proceeds to the central bank; exporters may retain hard currency or sell it to licensed banks. Profit remittances in the form of dividends are permitted after statutory tax withholdings and audit, and the commercial bank requires a shareholders' resolution, certification of tax clearance, and proof of internal accounts. For foreign investors, the Foreign Investment Promotion Act governs FDI reporting; proceeds from equity liquidation can be remitted abroad after submission of a report. Practical blockers remain document-heavy onboarding, and all conversions must be executed through licensed foreign exchange banks rather than third-party EMIs. Any capital or current transaction must be underpinned by genuine trade or investment documentation, with KRW conversion performed only at a bank holding foreign exchange dealing authority.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Korea does not impose a mandatory conversion ratio or surrender requirement. Export proceeds must be received and deposited within six months from the date of export clearance, extendable to twelve months with prior bank or MOEF approval. The exporter may retain the funds in a foreign currency account or voluntarily convert to KRW at a licensed commercial bank. The statutory surrender ratio is 0%.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Very Low Feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs are legally barred from acting as deposit-taking institutions in South Korea under the Banking Act, and unregistered foreign payment service providers cannot lawfully provide domestic treasury or foreign exchange settlement services to a Korean corporate entity. A Yuhan Hoesa must maintain its operating and escrow accounts with an FSS-licensed domestic bank. Offshore EMIs may only serve as auxiliary invoicing channels outside Korea, but cannot be used for statutory capital injection, FX conversion, import settlement, or corporate liquidity management in KRW. Attempting to route capital through an unlicensed EMI would be considered a violation of the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and would block the required capital verification for the foreign-invested entity.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in annual international related-party transactions (including cross-border green coffee purchase, ocean freight cost plus, marketing fees, and intercompany loans) triggers the statutory obligation to prepare and file a Korean Local File and Master File under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs. Lower attachment thresholds may be imposed for short-term intercompany financial loans of KRW 1 billion or more, and even below the threshold a taxpayer must maintain contemporaneous arm's length workpapers when claiming a DTA benefit or avoiding TP penalty exposure.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply the Gulf Cooperation Council Economic Substance Regulations by that name, but the substance-over-form principle under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes requires the Korean LLC to exercise Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Korea: container negotiation, green coffee purchase sourcing, cupping and quality certification, LC issuance, freight management, customs clearance, storage, insurance, invoicing to wholesale roasters, and treasury management. A Yuhan Hoesa entity that merely holds Korean bank accounts and contracts while all coffee business decisions, coffee inventories, and sales revenue flows are controlled and earned by a foreign parent, sister company, or non-resident director will be disregarded for Korean income tax purposes; its profits are re-attributed to the substantive foreign company and the Korean presence may be treated as a mere permanent establishment, triggering branch-profit remittance and WHT liabilities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion and False Revenue Reporting

Statutory sanction: Article 121 of the Corporate Income Tax Act punishes tax evasion with imprisonment for up to three years and a fine of up to three times the evaded tax; the Framework Act on National Taxes adds a 10% non-filing penalty, a 20% to 40% under-reporting penalty, and daily overdue surcharges at approximately 0.025% per day. The Korean tax authority may issue an immmediate seizure of bank accounts and a 'public delinquency' blacklisting of the entity.

Director personal liability: Corporate limited liability is pierced where a director intentionally converted coffee sales into personal foreign deposits. The director is jointly and severally liable with the LLC under National Tax Collection Act Article 39 for the unpaid CIT, penalties, and interest; the National Tax Service may attach the director's personal real estate, issue a travel ban / departure restriction, and the controlling director may face criminal imprisonment up to three years plus civil asset restitution.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's Length Remuneration Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Article 13 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the tax authority may re-determine the arm's length gross margin and impose a 10% penalty tax on the adjusted amount, increased to 20% if the taxpayer has no justifiable basis; the Framework Act on National Taxes adds a 20% understatement penalty and a 10% non-filing penalty. Diesel companies can be named in public non-payment lists, and the National Tax Service may demand full tax payment without waiting for the return.

Director personal liability: A director who signs the false intercompany purchase, service, and procurement agreements owes restitution to creditors under Commercial Act Article 399 and can be joined personally by the National Tax Service for the TP adjustment and penalties where the profit was intentionally pushed out to a non-resident affiliate. Travel bans and blacklisting under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the National Tax Collection Act apply, and deliberate TP manipulation is prosecuted as fraudulent tax evasion, with imprisonment up to three years for willful participation.
Shell Company, Treaty Shopping, and DTA Withholding Abuse

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service will deny treaty benefits under the principal-purpose and substance-over-form rules of Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and Articles 4 and 17 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, re-classify the dividend as subject to the full 22% aggregate withholding, and impose a 20% to 40% improper under-reporting penalty on the difference, plus daily overdue surcharges; the LLC, as withholding payer, is itself liable for the insufficient WHT amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised dividend distribution without collecting the minimum treaty evidence are deemed grossly negligent withholding agents, personally liable to the National Tax Service for the tax shortfall, interest, and penalty; the controlling foreign shareholder and director can receive departure restrictions, asset seizure orders, and criminal liability as accomplices to tax evasion under the Corporate Income Tax Act; courts have disregarded the corporate veil where the shell structure had no real economic substance and served as a device to evade Korean taxes.

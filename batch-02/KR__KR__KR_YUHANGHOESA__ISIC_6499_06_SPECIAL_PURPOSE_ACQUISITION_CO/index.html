Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity with a domestic place of business in Korea: (1) register the foreign company's Korean branch/domestic place of business with the relevant Commercial Court registry and obtain the Foreign Company Domestic Place of Business Registration Certificate; (2) within 20 days of actual commencement of taxable business, file the statutory Application for Business Registration with the NTS district tax office having jurisdiction over the Korean place of business, using HomeTax or in-person counter service; attach the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, evidence of the Korean place of business, representative identity documents, and a power of attorney if represented by a tax agent; (3) the NTS will issue the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves simultaneously as the Corporate Tax TIN, the VAT registration ID, and the withholding-tax registration identifier; (4) for a foreign entity with no fixed Korean place of business that nonetheless has Korean-source tax obligations, the NTS may assign a withholding/corporate taxpayer number through HomeTax, but a Business Registration Number is still mandatory before making any domestic taxable supply or claiming export zero-rating.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or an elective hybrid-mismatch classification. A Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily taxable at entity level on its worldwide income under the Corporate Tax Act. The only available group-level mechanism is the statutory consolidated corporate tax filing for qualifying Korean parent-subsidiary groups, which is not a pass-through or hybrid-mismatch election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary registration threshold; VAT registration is mandatory for any person who commences a taxable business in Korea, regardless of turnover, and must be completed within 20 days of business commencement. A simplified taxable person filing threshold applies at KRW 80,000,000 of annual taxable supplies, but that threshold does not relieve a business from registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, the 0% export rate is available where: (i) goods are exported to a foreign country and the export is evidenced by a customs export declaration; (ii) services are supplied to a person located outside Korea and the service is actually performed, used, or consumed outside Korea; or (iii) the supply is international maritime or air transportation. For a SPAC entity, issuance of shares or securities is not a taxable supply; any cross-border financial or advisory services may be zero-rated only if the place of supply is outside Korea and the recipient is a non-resident, supported by contemporaneous service-use documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (statutory export declaration lodged via Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic ocean bill of lading) or Air Waybill
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation / Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-to-bank SWIFT credit evidence for repatriated export proceeds)
  • Export Contract or Purchase Order evidencing the cross-border sale to the foreign counterparty
  • Service-consumption dossier for qualifying cross-border services, evidencing offshore place of supply and actual use by the non-resident recipient

All VAT payers must issue tax invoices. Electronic tax invoices must be generated through the NTS HomeTax electronic tax invoice system or an NTS-certified ERP/API integrator and transmitted to the NTS at issuance; the NTS maintains a centralized transaction-clearance database. There is no separate physical digital stamp; anti-fraud controls include automatic cross-matching of electronic tax invoices against customs export declarations, mandatory cash receipts for qualifying B2C sales, and real-time audit flags for unmatched or irregular invoices.

Substantiation is scrutinized through triangulation of HomeTax zero-rated VAT returns, Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declarations, and foreign exchange bank settlement records. If the 0% export claim is not supported or export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory settlement period, the NTS reverses the zero-rating, assesses VAT at 10% on the export selling price, and imposes additional taxes under the Framework Act on National Taxes, including additional tax for underreporting (generally 10% for non-fraudulent underreporting and 40% for fraudulent or unlawful underreporting), daily late-payment interest, and withdrawal or clawback of any prior refund. Intentional evasion also carries criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and may extend the statutory assessment window for retrospective correction.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won (KRW) operates under a managed floating exchange rate regime with liberalized current account and capital account transactions, but significant verification and reporting obligations remain in force. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree require that all cross-border settlements be routed through authorized foreign exchange banks, with transaction-level purpose codes and supporting documentation. For a special purpose acquisition company (SPAC) structured as a Yuhan Hoesa, the capital account liberalization allows inbound equity injections, but the initial share subscription proceeds and subsequent trust/escrow funding must be accompanied by a certificate of foreign investment registration, capital contribution receipt, and business plan validation when remitting into a designated capital escrow account. Current account transactions, including dividends and liquidation proceeds, are freely repatriable after corporate tax withholding, but profit remittances require audited financial statements and tax clearance certificates from the National Tax Service (NTS). The Korean authorities do not impose a mandatory surrender of export proceeds, yet all non-KRW receipts must be reported to the reporting bank within 30 days of receipt, and exporters must either retain the foreign currency in a domestic foreign currency account or convert to KRW within the statutory settlement timeline. Derivatives and hedging instruments are permitted for trade and debt servicing purposes, but speculative FX transactions by non-financial entities require prior approval under the soundness guidelines. Overall, the KRW is convertible for bona fide business operations, but administrative compliance and documentary evidence burdens create material friction for SPAC treasury management, particularly for ring-fenced acquisition funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border fintech EMIs present low viability for a South Korean Yuhan Hoesa SPAC. The Banking Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act grant deposit-taking and foreign exchange brokerage authority exclusively to licensed banks and authorized foreign exchange institutions. An offshore digital EMI without a Korean banking license cannot operate settlement accounts, issue local IBANs, or process KRW clearing. Although certain fintech companies offer multi-currency wallets, they act as resellers of banking infrastructure and require a domestic banking partner for each transaction, adding counterparty risk and compliance opacity. The Financial Services Commission (FSC) prohibits unlicensed providers from undertaking foreign exchange purchase and sale notifications, which are mandatory for every inbound and outbound cross-border transfer. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely exclusively on Tier-1 commercial banks with full foreign exchange dealing licenses, global trading platforms, and established capability to service blank-check acquisition vehicles. Any alternative payment institution must obtain a registered foreign exchange business license under Article 8 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, a process that is operationally prohibitive for a newly formed SPAC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the SPAC must first be registered with the Korean government via the Foreign Investment Promotion Act by filing a foreign investment report with a domestic bank or the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA). After receiving the foreign investment registration certificate (IRC), capital can be remitted through SWIFT MT103 messages with the purpose code '7120' (equity subscription) or '7810' (other foreign direct investment), referencing the IRC number. The receiving bank will verify the IRC, confirm the exchange rate margin, and issue a certificate of foreign investment capital receipt, which is mandatory for capital verification (proof of paid-in capital) for corporate registration. All funds must be settled through the Korea Communication Settlement System (BOK-Wire+) to the designated capital escrow account. The bank will apply a standard T+1 value date, and any KRW conversion of foreign currency capital must be executed at the interbank market rate with a bank spread capped under the Foreign Exchange Regulations. The capital verification report must be filed with the court registry within two weeks of receipt.For trade settlements related to future acquisitions and operating expenses, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 via the corporate banking platform, using the bank's electronic file transfer (EFTS) requesting the appropriate foreign exchange purpose code (e.g., '6220' for professional and business services, '7130' for offshore investment, or '7110' for acquisition of securities). Documentary collections (D/P, D/A) are permissible for import trade purchases, while commercial letters of credit are advised for high-value acquisitions to mitigate counterparty risk. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Cross-border fintech EMIs present low viability for a South Korean Yuhan Hoesa SPAC. The Banking Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act grant deposit-taking and foreign exchange brokerage authority exclusively to licensed banks and authorized foreign exchange institutions. An offshore digital EMI without a Korean banking license cannot operate settlement accounts, issue local IBANs, or process KRW clearing. Although certain fintech companies offer multi-currency wallets, they act as resellers of banking infrastructure and require a domestic banking partner for each transaction, adding counterparty risk and compliance opacity. The Financial Services Commission (FSC) prohibits unlicensed providers from undertaking foreign exchange purchase and sale notifications, which are mandatory for every inbound and outbound cross-border transfer. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely exclusively on Tier-1 commercial banks with full foreign exchange dealing licenses, global trading platforms, and established capability to service blank-check acquisition vehicles. Any alternative payment institution must obtain a registered foreign exchange business license under Article 8 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, a process that is operationally prohibitive for a newly formed SPAC.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax top marginal rate plus 10% local income tax surtax, producing a combined top effective rate of 26.4%; progressive brackets are 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 21% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion, and 24% above KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% effective rate for non-resident shareholders, composed of 20% national dividend withholding tax plus 10% local income tax surtax, unless reduced by an applicable DTA; general DTA dividend rates are 10% to 15%, with lower rates of 0% to 5% for substantial corporate shareholdings depending on the treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Annual cross-border related-party transactions must be reported on the corporate tax return when the amount exceeds KRW 100 million for a transaction category; mandatory Local File documentation is required when intercompany transactions exceed KRW 50 billion; Master File is required when Korean consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country Report applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of KRW 100 billion or more under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs and its Enforcement Decree.

Economic substance scope: The Republic of Korea does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but Korean tax authorities apply beneficial ownership tests, place of effective management analysis, Korean GAAR, and OECD core income-generating activities or CIGA principles. A SPAC should maintain substantive local governance in Korea, including Korean board meetings, minutes, general meeting records, bank and trust accounts, manager residence, and real decision-making, to avoid foreign residence recharacterization and to sustain treaty protection for dividends, interest, and royalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, a violation is punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding twice the evaded tax; administrative penalties include a 20% non-filing penalty, a 10% under-reporting penalty, and daily overdue surcharge on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: A representative or active director who knowingly causes the false return may be personally liable under Article 401 of the Korean Commercial Act to the company and third parties; the court may order asset restitution, and the tax authority may impose a travel ban or immigration blacklisting until liabilities are paid; criminal conviction can result in imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Unjustified Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 4 and 11 of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the National Tax Service may reallocate income and impose corresponding income adjustments, with under-reporting penalties of 10% of the additional tax assessed under the National Tax Basic Act and late payment surcharge interest; failure to produce required transfer pricing documentation triggers separate statutory per-document penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false transfer pricing documentation or intentionally erase intercompany transactions face joint and several compensation liability under Commercial Act Article 401; if documentary fraud is used to evade tax, the directors are subject to criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and travel bans as a collection enforcement measure.
AML and Abuse of SPAC Trust Assets Through Concealed Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Capital Markets Act and the Act on Regulation and Punishment of Criminal Proceeds Concealment are punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years, fines up to KRW 100 million, confiscation of criminal proceeds, and violations of the Real Name Financial Transaction and Guarantee Act can separately generate criminal fines and revocation of the SPAC listing.

Director personal liability: Directors may have the corporate veil pierced under Korean Supreme Court alter-ego principles when trust assets are treated as personal assets; they are subject to civil liability under Commercial Act Articles 399 and 401, asset freeze orders, Korean immigration blacklisting, and criminal imprisonment terms of up to 5 years for concealment of criminal proceeds.
